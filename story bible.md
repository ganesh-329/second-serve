## Writing guide: interactions and culture
 
**Workflow:** before every chapter, run 3 research agents: (1) canon events and character voices, built from fetched wiki pages with every beat sourced; (2) THE INTERACTIONS AGENT (mandatory, never dropped): drafts the chapter's main conversations in full, following "How these people talk" below; (3) one chapter-specific topic. Write only after all 3 report back.
 
### How these people talk (rewritten Oct 30)
 
The old version of this guide was a list of comedy templates and scene formulas. Followed together, they turned every conversation into a sitcom bit: polished one-liners, perfect comebacks, callbacks and counters, everybody bargaining. The user called the result artificial. Haikyuu doesn't talk like that, and neither do teenagers. These rules replace it.
 
1. **Most lines are plain.** People say what they need. "Did you eat?" "Yeah." "Where's the cart?" "Club room." Plenty of exchanges have no joke in them at all, and that's normal.
2. **Only the sharp ones are sharp.** Tsukishima is dry. Suga teases gently. Oikawa and Kuroo needle. Everyone else is blunt, loud, awkward or earnest. Nobody wins every exchange, Sho included.
3. **Humor comes from situations and reactions.** Hinata panicking, Kageyama's face, Tanaka's intimidation face, a misheard word, somebody crying over a manager saying good luck. Verbal wit is the rare case. 1 or 2 real laughs in a scene is plenty, and some scenes have none.
4. **People don't answer perfectly.** They mishear, ignore the question, answer something else, repeat themselves, trail off ("..."), say "eh?", "ha?", "oi", "un". A reply that mirrors the last line's wording and tops it is the most reliable sign of fake dialogue.
5. **Sincere moments land.** Canon is earnest. People say thank you, apologize, cry and yell encouragement. Keep it short and in the character's own words, and let it sit. Don't undercut every one with a joke. Avoid speeches, therapy language and soft-voiced reassurance no 16-year-old would say.
6. **Callbacks are rare.** Use one only when a real person would remember it. No running counters every chapter (wipe counts, debt ledgers, "retroactive"). 1 recurring bit per chapter at most.
7. **Kids talk like kids.** No adult or analyst words in teenage mouths (escrow, sample size, peer review, inflation, consistency as a stat). Mio is 10: demanding, blunt, sometimes illogical, and not a stand-up comic.
8. **Volleyball talk is functional.** On court it's calls: "Nice receive!", "One more!", "Cover!", "Chance ball!", "Mine!". When Ukai explains something, he explains it properly, and the scene can just be an explanation.
9. **Care shows through ordinary things.** Food, a towel, a ride, a covered plate, someone waiting. Nobody needs to comment on it, and nobody needs to deflect it either.
10. **Not every conversation needs an agenda fight.** Some are just 2 people passing time, or one person helping another with something.
### Artificial tells to cut on sight
 
- Every line is a setup or a punchline.
- Comebacks that reuse the previous line's words ("That's off topic." "It's always on topic.").
- Analyst or adult vocabulary from teenagers.
- Characters explaining their own joke, or the narrator explaining it.
- Every scene ending on a snappy last line.
- Everybody bargaining, keeping score or trading favors.
- Numbers and counting used as a gag.
- Two-word deadpan replies from everyone, in a row.
- A simile on every gesture ("like a man who...", "the way a cat...").
- The same joke shape repeated across scenes (A says X, B says "that's not X", A doubles down).

### Culture cheat sheet
 
- **Club:** practice right after homeroom until about 6 p.m.; first years fetch balls, mop (it turns into a race), carry the ball cart with its jammed wheels, fix the net antenna. Greetings: a squashed "chiwassu" to senpai, "onegaishimasu" when entering the gym. Shouts: "nice kill", "nice cut", "chance ball", "one touch", "koi!", "mou ippon" (one more, never just one). Smells: knee pads, floor wax. Red, then purple forearms that sting in the bath.
- **School:** shoe lockers and white indoor shoes, gakuran uniform, bento in the classroom, bread sells out first at the school store, vending-machine carton milk, students clean the school themselves.
- **Family:** "ittekimasu / itterasshai" leaving, "tadaima / okaeri" coming home. Mom up at 5 for a bigger bento; a cling-wrapped plate and miso to reheat after late practice; laundry always full; shared bath water, wash before getting in; dad who never comments turning up at the back of the stands.
- **Miyagi in April:** about 6°C mornings, 15°C afternoons, light jacket; Sendai cherry blossoms around mid-April; konbini nikuman at the tail end of their season; local food: gyutan, zunda mochi, sasa-kamaboko; icsca card for Sendai buses and subway.
## Sho's place on the team
 
**Plan:** Sho is Karasuno's sub ace until Nationals. He comes in for Asahi or Tanaka at specific moments to change the pace, so he plays as both ace and wing spiker without taking anyone's spot.
 
Nishinoya stays the only libero. Sho's past life as a college ace ties him to Asahi: Satoshi knows what it's like to get blocked over and over and lose your nerve.
 
Early on his shifts are short, a few rotations at a time. His body can last, but his technique is raw and his eye can't be counted on.
 
### When Sho comes in
 
| Situation | Who goes out | Why Sho |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blockers have read Karasuno's attack rhythm | Tanaka | Different tempo and angles reset the block |
| Asahi gets stuffed and needs to reset | Asahi | Sho takes the ace sets for a rotation or two |
| A tall block sits on Asahi (Date Tech, Shiratorizawa) | Asahi | Before Nationals: a flicker sometimes shows the gap. After: he reads it on command |
| Sho's serve lines up at a key moment | Either | Pinch serve, then stays on for the rally |
 
After the eye awakens at Nationals, Sho's minutes grow. Whether he becomes a starter at Nationals is still open.
 
### Rules for the rivalry
 
- Tanaka keeps his full canon arc. The Inarizaki slump and comeback stay his.
- Sho pushes Tanaka to train harder, and Tanaka gets better because of it.
- The competition stays healthy: no sabotage, no bitterness, and nobody gets written down to make Sho look good. Sho's shamelessness is aimed at himself, never at teammates.
- Asahi's big moments (beating Date Tech's wall, his return) stay his. Sho can be on court beside him or on the bench, never in place of him.
### All-rounder and the libero role
 
Emperor's Eye and Meta Vision make Sho a natural back-row defender. In matches he plays as a receiving outside hitter: he takes serve receive with Noya and Daichi, then attacks.
 
He can't switch between libero and attacker inside one match. Under FIVB rules, a player re-designated as libero stays libero for the rest of that match ([Rule 19.4.2.1](https://vbahk.org.hk/items/media/Refereeing/202528_Volleyball_Rules/VB/fivbvolleyball_rulesmodification20252028.pdf)), and a libero can't attack above the net or block.
 
So the libero role shows up in 2 places:
 
- **Practice and camps:** Sho trains as libero next to Noya, and later Yaku, Watari and Komori. That's where his receiving grows fastest.
- **Emergency:** if Noya can't play in a match, Ukai can re-designate Sho as libero for the rest of that match. That never happens in canon, so it's your call if and when to use it.

### Body
 
Sho has a perfect athletic body: a scaled-down Toji in conditioning, meaning strength, stamina, durability and recovery. His speed and reflexes are good for a human and nothing more. He can play 5 sets and still run Karasuno's hill afterwards, but he can't react his way out of a bad read.
 
His early weakness is skill. Sho's body has never really played. Satoshi's brain knows ace technique cold, and Sho's hands, timing and approach don't. He shanks passes and nets serves while knowing exactly what he did wrong.
 
### Personality: shameless
 
Modeled on Lloyd Frontera from The Greatest Estate Developer.
 
- Zero shame. He begs for help without pride, flatters coaches to their faces, haggles Ukai for free meat buns, and takes credit loudly.
- "Stealing is learning." He copies anyone's technique and tells them so.
- He complains like he's lazy, then outworks everyone when it pays off.
- He runs every situation through a 29-year-old analyst's brain.
- He thinks in anime references and only sometimes says them. The eye is "Observation Haki", a tight 3rd set is "Bankai time".
- **Hard limit:** shameless about himself, never cruel to teammates. Rivalries stay healthy.
**Under the jokes:** Sho is cold, calculating and deep. A shock rattles him for a beat, never longer, and he's never scared of his own power. He treats it as data: rattled, then analyze, then plan, then act. After the 3v3 he trains twice as hard, and he keeps doing it.

## Dialogue playbook (rewritten Oct 30)
 
Conversations are still the user's top priority. This replaces the Oct 29 playbook, whose templates (self-praise audit, rule-of-three invoice, snark duel with a score, third-wheel button and the rest) produced quippy, artificial dialogue. The rules in "How these people talk" (Writing guide, above) come first. This section adds scene rules, Sho's voice and canon voice notes.
 
### Scene rules
 
- Each chapter is built around 2 or 3 main conversations. They can be long, and they should feel like people talking, with plain lines, false starts and things left unanswered.
- Write the conversation first as what these 2 people would actually say in that room, at that time of day, then cut anything a real person wouldn't say. Don't start from a joke structure.
- A scene doesn't need a joke, an agenda fight or a closing line. It can end mid-task, on a plain line, or when someone leaves.
- Feelings can be said plainly when the character would say them (Tanaka, Hinata, Noya and Takeda say a lot out loud; Kageyama and Tsukishima don't). Narration still never names them.
- Keep the pace calm (see the handoff: chapter 3 is the model). Stay in a scene; give it air, sounds, physical business.
### Sho's voice
 
- Out loud, Sho is a polite 15-year-old with an odd vocabulary. Short sentences to seniors, -san and -senpai, shameless asks said plainly ("Please toss me a few." "Teach me that."), not elaborate pitches. He sometimes gets ignored, told off or out-talked, and takes it.
- Analyst jargon stays mostly in his head. Out loud he says what he saw in ordinary words ("He goes with Hinata every time").
- Inner voice: mostly plain thoughts. Anime references 1 or 2 per chapter, stats only when he's actually analyzing something. Italics, sparingly.
### Narration
 
- Plain. "He said." Simple actions. One simile in a while, not one per gesture.
- Still avoid the user's listed tics: "didn't look at", "without turning his head", "For a moment", "Something in his chest", "That was all", "filed that", stoic 1-line paragraphs after every exchange, the narrator explaining subtext. Don't replace them with ornate substitutes.
- No em dashes, no negative parallelisms ("Not X. Y.", "It wasn't X, it was Y"), numbers as digits, no banned AI words.
### Canon voices (short notes)
 
- **Hinata:** loud, short bursts, sound words ("the ball went whoosh"), lots of questions and "amazing!", honest, scared then fired up. Polite to seniors.
- **Kageyama:** blunt and short. "Oi." "Hinata, boke." Explains volleyball clearly and everything else badly. Goes stiff and quiet when praised. Never quippy.
- **Tsukishima:** calm, polite-sounding sarcasm, "King", minimal effort. The one Karasuno player allowed to be dry.
- **Yamaguchi:** "Tsukki!", laughs along, "Sorry, Tsukki!", shy, gets firmer later.
- **Tanaka:** yells, "Ha?!", intimidation faces, protective of juniors, cries easily, worships Kiyoko. Rough speech, big feelings said out loud.
- **Nishinoya:** declarations. "Leave it to me!" "I've got your back!" Simple, loud, absolute.
- **Daichi:** calm, short instructions, warm, and a smile that ends arguments.
- **Sugawara:** gentle, cheerful, perceptive, encourages people by name, teases Daichi. Says sincere things plainly.
- **Asahi:** hesitant, "um", apologizes, self-deprecating, big and gentle.
- **Ennoshita:** calm, reasonable, mature, the sensible second year. Few words, no counting gags.
- **Ukai:** gruff, short barks, mild swearing, and real coaching explanations when it matters. Cares through instructions.
- **Takeda:** polite, earnest, long sentences, a little poetic, admits what he doesn't know.
- **Kiyoko:** very few words.
