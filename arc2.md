## Chapter 16: The Map

Ukai came into the gym on the Monday after Nekoma in sandals, with a cigarette he wasn't allowed to light tucked behind his ear, and announced it like he was reading a weather report.

"I'm staying. Coach. Officially. Through the prelims and after."

Tanaka screamed. Nishinoya screamed on top of Tanaka. Takeda clasped his hands together and said "Ukai-kun..." in the voice of a man at his daughter's wedding.

*Satoshi had watched 40-something head coaches sign contracts,* Sho thought. *Press conferences. Club scarves. Flash photography. Not one of them did it in sandals.*

He raised his hand.

"Don't," said Ukai.

"I haven't said anything, Coach."

"You were gonna say 'I told you so.' At Golden Week you asked if I'd ever thought about doing this professionally."

"I was going to say congratulations."

Ukai squinted at him. "Were you."

"Congratulations first. Then 'I told you so.' In that order." Sho put a hand on his chest. "I have manners."

"He has NO manners," said Tanaka.

"He has manners," said Sugawara, kindly. "He just uses them as weapons."

Daichi smiled. It was the smile.

"Kiyose. 20 laps."

"For manners, Captain?"

"For timing."

Sho ran 20 laps. On lap 7, Hinata joined him for no reason at all, and on lap 12 Hinata started racing him, and on lap 13 Kageyama joined in to race Hinata, and by lap 20 Sho was the only one still running at a normal speed while the other 2 were arguing about who'd lapped who.

*Not my race,* he thought, and then, because he couldn't help it: *I would've won, though.*

---

The bracket went up on the whiteboard that afternoon in Ukai's handwriting, which looked like it had been written on a moving bus.

"Day 1, Tokonami." Tap. "Win that, it's Date Tech." Tap, harder. "Day 2, if you're still breathing, probably Seijoh. And up here," he drew a big lopsided circle, "Shiratorizawa. Ushijima. They win Miyagi every year. It's boring."

"Date Tech!" Nishinoya slapped Asahi on the back hard enough to move him. "We're gonna SMASH them, right, Asahi-san?"

"Y-yeah," said Asahi. "Smash."

*2 syllables of hope,* Sho noted, *and about 9 of stomachache.*

Ukai pointed the marker at the Date Tech box. "Their middle's huge. 190-something. Blocks like..."

"Read and commit, Coach," said Sho. "He watches the setter's hands, picks a hitter early and goes all in. The outside blockers close to him like a door. If you get them to commit to the wrong man, the seam opens. Once a set, maybe twice."

The gym got quiet in the specific way it got quiet when Sho said something a 15-year-old shouldn't know.

Ukai lowered the marker. "Who told you that?"

"Television."

"...Television."

"The local channel reran last year's prelim highlights at 5 p.m. I taped it." Sho considered. "I also taped 20 minutes of a cooking show by accident. Do you want to know how to make nikujaga?"

"Do I look like I want to know how to make..."

"I'd like to know," said Takeda, sincerely.

"Sensei, NO," said Ukai.

He turned back to Sho, and his eyes went narrow, the way they did when a delivery van parked in front of his shop.

"Don't be good at things I didn't teach you," Ukai said.

"Then teach me faster, Coach."

Tanaka made a noise like a balloon losing air. Hinata looked between them with his mouth open, as if he were watching a car crash and a magic trick at the same time.

Ukai jabbed the marker at him. "You. Receive. After practice, every day until the prelims."

"Ennoshita-san's already feeding me."

"Then do it MORE."

"Teacher's pet," Tsukishima murmured, from the back.

"Pets get fed, Tsukishima-kun," Sho said. "It's strategy."

Yamaguchi snorted, then clapped a hand over his mouth. "Sorry, Tsukki."

---

Ennoshita fed him after practice with 1 basket of balls, from a box by the net, in a voice that never went up or down. Narita stood behind Sho to chase the shanks. Kinoshita sat on the bench with his chin on his fist, counting.

"Shoulders," said Ennoshita, and fed one.

Sho passed it. "My shoulders are perfect."

"Your shoulders are early." Another ball. "They turn before your feet get there."

"He's said 'shoulders' 30 times," Narita reported from behind him.

"9 shanks," said Kinoshita.

"Kinoshita-san," said Sho, between balls, "are you counting my sins?"

"Somebody has to."

"Ennoshita told us to count," said Narita.

"I didn't tell you to tell him I told you," said Ennoshita, and fed a short one that Sho had to dive for.

Sho came up off the floor with his chin stinging. "Ennoshita-san. That one was personal."

"That one was a free ball." Another. "You'll get 4 of those against Date Tech. Shoulders."

They did it for 20 minutes. By the end, Kinoshita had stopped counting out loud and was just holding up fingers, which was somehow worse.

"11," Kinoshita said, at the end.

"Narita," said Sho, wiping his face on his shirt, "you're my favorite."

"I didn't do anything," said Narita.

"That's why."

Ennoshita laughed, once, through his nose. Then he picked up a ball from the floor and turned it over in his hands while the other 2 collected the rest.

"I watched you take my spot," he said. "Against Seijoh."

Sho stopped with the basket on his hip.

"You were stiff the whole first set," said Ennoshita, in the same voice he'd used to say *shoulders*.

"I was," said Sho. "My hands were wet the whole time. I wiped them on my shorts 14 times."

"13."

Sho looked at him.

Ennoshita put the last ball in the basket. "I had a lot of time on the bench," he said. "Eat more. You're getting thin."

"You sound like my mom," said Narita.

"Your mom's right too," said Ennoshita, and went to get his bag.

---

Sho got to the gym at 6:10 on Wednesday to teach Tanaka the foot thing. Tanaka was already at the far net, alone, swinging at nothing, sweating through a shirt that said FIGHTING SPIRIT in English with one of the letters backwards.

"You're late," said Tanaka.

"It's 6:10, senpai."

"You're LATE."

Hinata and Kageyama were shouting at each other at the other end of the gym. Nobody paid them any attention. That was just the weather now.

Sho picked up a ball. "Okay. The foot thing."

"The foot thing," said Tanaka, very seriously, like it was a kata.

"Your left foot is the liar. Plant it square, the same as if you're going cross, so the blocker's eyes say cross. Same approach. Same shoulders. Then, at the top, the left arm drops and the hand goes to the line." Sho mimed it, slowly, twice. "Foot says cross. Hand says line."

"Foot lies. Hand tells the truth."

"...Sure, senpai. That's a better way to say it."

Tanaka tried it. Into the tape.

"Again," said Tanaka, before Sho could.

Into the tape. Then long. Then wide, into the bleachers, where it hit a folded chair with a sound like a gong. Then, on the 9th one, straight down the line, a hand's width inside, a perfect hard slap on the varnish.

Tanaka landed and stared at the spot.

"Oi," he said. "Did that count?"

"Line. A hand inside. It counts."

"Say it louder."

"It counts, senpai."

Tanaka turned to face the empty bleachers, threw both fists in the air, and roared, "IT COUNTS!"

At the other end of the gym, Hinata and Kageyama stopped arguing to look. Then they went back to arguing.

The next day Sho came at 6:00. Tanaka was already there, already sweating.

"Senpai. Do you sleep here?"

"Ha?! I LIVE here now."

On Friday, Tanaka decided it was his turn. He set up on the other side of the net and blocked Sho for 20 minutes straight, hands up early, eyes on Sho's shoulders, and he stuffed him 4 times in a row.

"Senpai," said Sho, picking himself up, "you're being mean."

"I'm TEACHING. Get your hands up. You hit like a pancake."

"A pancake doesn't have hands."

"EXACTLY."

On the walk to the club room afterward, Tanaka slung his towel over his shoulder and said, to the hallway ceiling, "Don't think I'm thanking you for the foot thing."

"I would never dream of it, senpai."

"Good."

They walked a few more steps.

"Thanks," said Tanaka.

"What was that?"

"NOTHING. Change your shirt, you smell like a gym."

---

At lunch on Thursday, Sho found Asahi on the concrete steps behind the gym with a bento he'd opened and then forgotten about.

Sho sat 2 steps above him and held a meat bun over his shoulder.

"Oh," said Asahi. "No, Kiyose, I'm fine, really, I..."

"It's the cheese kind," said Sho. "They're 20 yen more. I want you to understand what you mean to me."

Asahi laughed despite himself, a big helpless huff, and took it.

They ate. A crow landed on the railing and looked at them like it was considering a robbery.

"Kiyose," Asahi said, after a while. "Have you ever been blocked so many times that you..." He stopped. He turned the bun around in his hands. "Never mind."

*Here's the door,* thought Sho. *Satoshi had a whole speech for this. Fear is information. Walls are just geometry. Very inspiring. 0 percent of it is mine to say.*

"Once," said Sho. "In middle school. A kid named Kobayashi. He was 160 centimeters tall and he blocked me 6 times in a row and then asked if I was okay. It was the worst day of my life."

Asahi blinked. "Why was that the worst..."

"Because he asked if I was okay." Sho took a bite. "Very kind boy. I hated him for a month."

Asahi laughed again, smaller this time, and looked down at the bun, and finally ate the rest of it.

*There was no Kobayashi,* Sho thought. *Sorry, Kobayashi.*

When he got up to go, Asahi said, "That's 3."

Sho turned around. "You're counting too?"

"Noya says debts are sacred." Asahi rubbed the back of his neck. "He has a notebook."

"Noya-senpai has a NOTEBOOK?"

"It's mostly drawings of Kiyoko-san."

"That's fair," said Sho, and went inside.

---

Yamaguchi had been doing a thing all week where he left with his bag like everyone else, said goodbye to Tsukishima at the gate, and then came back 10 minutes later when the gym was empty.

Sho, coming back from the vending machine with a melon soda, caught him at it on Thursday.

Yamaguchi was at the far end line, alone, serving floats into the empty court. Toss. Pause. Hit. The ball wobbled over the net like it was drunk and dropped on the end line.

Yamaguchi saw him, and went red to the ears.

"Kiyose-kun! I wasn't... I was just..."

"Serving," said Sho. "Into a court. With a ball. Very suspicious."

"You won't tell Tsukki?"

"Tell him what? That you've been seeing an older man every Sunday?"

"IT'S SHIMADA-SAN!"

"I know. I'm the one who introduced you."

"...Oh." Yamaguchi deflated. "Right. Sorry."

Sho popped the tab on his soda. "Your toss is the same height every time. I watched 20 in a row. Same height, same little stop at the top."

"Is that... good?"

"It's the hardest part. Most people's tosses wander around like Hinata in a supermarket. Yours doesn't. I'm going to steal it."

Yamaguchi stared at him. "You're not making fun of me?"

"I don't lie about data," said Sho. "I lie about everything else. Ask anyone."

Yamaguchi laughed, a startled little sound, and picked up another ball. Sho drank his melon soda and left him to it, and somewhere behind him a ball hit the end line again, on purpose.

---

On Saturday, Kageyama stood at the net after practice holding a ball and staring at Sho until Sho understood he was being invited.

He set him 10 in a row on the left. High, perfect, the kind of set Satoshi used to dream about at 20, when his knee still worked.

"2nd step," said Kageyama, after the 10th. "Better."

"Thank you, Kageyama-kun."

"It was still late on the 6th one."

"Thank you, Kageyama-kun."

From the doorway, Hinata, who had been on his way home, stopped dead. "Wait. WAIT. You're setting HIM extra?"

"He asked," said Kageyama.

"He DIDN'T ask, I was RIGHT THERE!"

"He asked with his face."

"I have a face! I'm asking with my face RIGHT NOW!"

"Your face is always asking."

"Set me one too!"

"You have a ball."

"I can't SET MYSELF, BAKAGEYAMA!"

Kageyama set him 15. Sho sat on the bench with his towel over his head and listened to the 2 of them argue about every single one.

---

Sugawara caught him in the club room afterward, folding practice bibs into the bin.

"Same lineup as Nekoma," Sugawara said. "Ukai told me."

He said it like he was reading the lunch menu. He folded a bib into a perfect square.

"So it's you and me on the bench, Suga-san."

"Mm. You'll go in more than me, though." Another perfect square. "Pinch hitter, change of pace. That's a real job. Backup setter is more like... being a fire extinguisher. Very important. Everybody hopes they never need you."

"Fire extinguishers get checked every year," said Sho. "By law."

Sugawara laughed. "Is that supposed to make me feel better?"

"It's supposed to be true." Sho picked up a bib and folded it into something that was not a square. "I used to watch volleyball for a living. In a dark room. On a screen. For years. You know what the best seat was?"

"Courtside?"

"The bench. You see the whole thing. Nobody's looking at you, so you can look at everybody."

Sugawara stopped folding.

"For a living," he repeated.

"In a past life."

"You're so weird, Kiyose."

"Thank you."

Sugawara took the lopsided bib out of his hands, refolded it into a square, and gave him the bigger half of the pile.

"It bothers me a little," Sugawara said, lightly, to the bin. "The bench."

"Good," said Sho.

Sugawara looked up.

"If it didn't bother you, you wouldn't be ready," said Sho. "You'd just be sitting."

Sugawara looked at him a while longer. Then he flicked him in the forehead, hard.

"Fold faster," he said, smiling. "You're terrible at this."

---

On the last day before the prelims, Kiyoko asked them to wait.

She came back with Takeda and a long roll of black cloth, and they knelt on the gym floor and unrolled it carefully, the whole length of the court. It was old. Somebody had washed it and mended the frayed edges with small, neat stitches.

Across it, in huge white characters:

> 飛べ

Fly.

The end of the banner kept curling up off the floor. Sho crouched and pressed the corner flat with his palm.

Kiyoko looked up at him. "Thank you."

*Satoshi had a joke for funerals,* Sho thought. *He'd had a joke ready for his own funeral. Turns out he'd died before he could use it. And he had nothing, absolutely nothing, for this.*

He nodded and kept his hand on the corner.

Kiyoko stood, looked at all of them, and said, "Good luck."

Tanaka sat down on the floor like his strings had been cut. Nishinoya sat down beside him. Both of them were crying, openly, enormously, clutching each other's sleeves.

"Senpai," said Sho, "breathe."

"She SAID it, Kiyose." Tanaka grabbed Sho's shirt. "She said good luck. To US."

"Write it down!" Nishinoya pointed at him, sobbing. "In your notebook! Date and time!"

Sho took out the notebook. "6:42 p.m."

"PUT IT IN BIG LETTERS!"

He wrote it in big letters.

Daichi pinched the bridge of his nose. Sugawara laughed so hard he had to hold onto Asahi, who was also crying a little and pretending he had something in his eye.

---

At home, Mio was crossing out June on the fridge calendar in red marker. Every day. Including the ones that hadn't happened yet. Her magnet sat on August like a flag.

"Summer starts in July," she informed him. "You still have time to quit."

"Technically," said Sho, "the solstice is June 21st."

Mio turned around with the marker. "...Teacher says July."

"Then July."

"Are you going to win?"

"Probably not all of it."

"Then quit."

"That's not how it works."

"Mio," said their mother, from the stove.

"I'm helping him," said Mio, and went back to crossing out days.

At dinner, his father passed the rice and asked one question.

"10 a.m.?"

"10, Dad."

"Mm."

At 4:15 the next morning, the sports page was taped to the fridge under the spare magnet, the one shaped like a fish. The Miyagi prelim schedule, in tiny print. Karasuno's first match was circled once, neatly, in pen.

His mother handed him his bento. It was noticeably bigger than usual.

"Is this 2 bentos, Mom?"

"It's 1 bento," she said. "It's just ambitious."

---

That night, at his desk, Sho drew the bracket in the notebook.

He wrote every team's name, and everything he knew next to each one. Tokonami's serve. Date Tech's wall. Seijoh, with a little star next to Oikawa's name and the word *dangerous* underlined twice. Shiratorizawa at the top, in slightly bigger letters, because Satoshi had never been able to help that.

Then he got to Karasuno's box.

He knew more about Karasuno than about anyone. He could have filled 3 pages. Tanaka's backwards T-shirt. Asahi's 9 syllables of stomachache. Ennoshita counting his hand wipes from the bench. Yamaguchi's toss.

He tapped the pen in the middle of the box, once, and left a small dot.

*You don't scout your own team the night before,* Satoshi would've said. *You'll just find things to worry about.*

*Shut up, Satoshi,* thought Sho, and set his alarm for 4:15.

## Chapter 17: First Official Serve

At 4:15 the sky behind the house was the gray of dishwater.

Sendai got its sunrise early in June and its rain late, and the rainy season hadn't been declared yet. The air coming through the kitchen window screen was mild and wet and smelled like asphalt that had been rained on in the night. Overnight, the tape holding Dad's prelim schedule to the fridge had curled up at 1 corner, right next to Mio's magnet, still parked on August, and the fish magnet had slid down 2 centimeters.

Sho came down the stairs with his bag on his shoulder and his shoes in his hand. The plan was out the door by 4:30 and at the gym by 4:50, because Tanaka had beaten him there 6 mornings in a row, and on prelim day that streak was going to end.

The kitchen light was on. Oil popped on the stove.

His mother stood at the burner in her cardigan, turning something breaded with a pair of long chopsticks. Mio sat at the table in her pajamas, her hair flattened on one side, both hands around a cup of barley tea filled to the brim.

"Why are you up," said Sho.

"Thirsty."

"Sit down," said his mother. "5 minutes."

"I'll eat on the walk."

"You'll eat on the walk and see it again at 10. Sit."

He sat on the front 3 centimeters of the chair with the bag still on.

"That's hovering," said Mio. "Mom. He's hovering."

"Sho."

He took the bag off and sat like a person. A bowl of rice landed in front of him, then miso, then 3 slices of tamagoyaki nobody had promised him. The breaded thing in the pan hissed as his mother lifted it onto the rack.

He leaned over to see. "Is that katsu?"

"It's pork."

"It's katsu," said Mio. "Because *katsu*. Mom, that's so embarrassing. Everybody's mom does that for exams."

"It was on sale."

"At 4 in the morning?"

"Yesterday." His mother cut the cutlet into strips with 4 hard knocks of the knife. "Eat your rice."

He ate. The miso was the dark red kind she usually saved for New Year's.

*Carbs front-loaded, protein on top, red miso for sodium,* Satoshi's half of his head reported. *Mom is running a pre-match nutrition protocol at 4 a.m. and calling it a sale.*

Mio watched him chew. "So if you lose today. Does it count?"

"Count as what?"

"Quitting. Losing is quitting, except the other team does it for you."

"The bet says quit. Losing is a separate category. Read the contract."

"There's no contract."

"Then the terms are whatever I remember."

"Then I remember 3 puddings."

"That's inflation, Mio."

"It's interest." She finally drank some of the barley tea, made a face at how warm it had gone, and set it down. "What time do you even play? I can't read Dad's circle. It's too small. It could be an 8."

"10. At the City Gymnasium."

"Is that far?"

"Why?"

"So I know how far away you'll be when you lose."

Their father came in, in his undershirt, the newspaper folded under one arm. He went to the fridge, pressed the curled tape flat with his thumb and held it there, reading the tiny print as if the schedule might have changed in the night.

"Humid," he said.

He poured a glass of milk, drank it standing at the sink, and rinsed the glass. On the way out he stopped behind Mio's chair.

"Bed. The train isn't till 8."

"I don't have a train," said Mio.

"Mm," said their father, and took the newspaper into the living room.

Mio picked up the barley tea and drank the whole cup in one go, with her eyes on the window.

His mother tied the bento into the blue furoshiki. Then she wrapped a frozen bottle of barley tea in a hand towel and wedged it against the box.

"The bottle keeps the katsu cold till lunch. Drink it when it melts."

"Pork," said Mio.

"Pork," his mother agreed.

The clock on the rice cooker said 4:33. Somewhere across town, Tanaka was already unlocking the gym and grinning at the empty parking lot.

In the genkan, Sho stepped into his shoes. Beside them, a pair of small sneakers was lined up with the toes pointing at the door, laces tucked in.

"Ittekimasu."

"Itterasshai," his mother called. "Umbrella."

"It's not raining."

"Umbrella."

He took the umbrella.

From the kitchen, Mio's voice followed him out. "If it rains and you slip and break your leg, it doesn't count. I want to win fair."

---

Tanaka was already there.

He sat on the concrete step outside the Second Gymnasium with the key around his finger and a face that said he'd been waiting since the Edo period.

"4:52," said Tanaka.

"Senpai. What time did you get here?"

"Early."

"How early?"

"EARLIER THAN YOU."

Inside, Kageyama was already on the court, and Hinata came through the door at a sprint 30 seconds later, already shouting.

"I WIN! I'm first!"

"You're 4th," said Kageyama. "That's 31 wins for me. 30 for you. 1 draw."

"32! It's 32 for ME! You can't count the day you slept at school!"

"I slept at home. I was here first."

"That's what sleeping at school MEANS!"

Sho sat on the step, unwrapped one of the 3 onigiri his mother had slipped into the side pocket of his bag without telling him, and listened to them argue about a scoreboard nobody else kept. Salmon. They had 2 hours until the bus, and Tanaka made them spend 1 of them stretching.

At 7, the bus came. Ukai stood at the door counting heads with the unlit cigarette behind his ear. Takeda stood beside him holding the team's entry form in both hands, as if somebody might try to steal it.

Hinata got on looking fine. By the second traffic light, he looked like a boiled egg.

Tanaka, without looking up from his phone, reached across the aisle and held out a paper bag.

"Senpai," said Sho. "You brought a bag?"

"I brought 3." Tanaka kept scrolling. "Seijoh taught me things."

Hinata held the bag in his lap for the whole ride and didn't use it, and at the end he gave it back, folded into a neat square. Tanaka unfolded it and checked inside.

---

Sendai City Gymnasium was bigger than Sho's memory said a prefectural gym should be.

The lobby was full of school jackets. Navy, maroon, green, a white one that looked expensive. Every school had a first year holding a stack of bags and a third year reading the board. The air smelled like floor wax and Salonpas, so strongly that Hinata stopped in the doorway and sniffed like a dog.

"It smells like my grandpa's shoulders!"

Across the net, behind the end line, Oikawa was bouncing the ball. He watched Sho walk all the way to his spot. He smiled.

"Tall-kun," he said. He said it to the ball.

Sho got low.

He put his feet a little wider than Noya had told him, and his weight on the front of them, and his hands loose in front of his knees. He could hear Daichi breathing next to him, on his right, and past Daichi the shuffle of Noya's shoes. Tanaka was in front of him at the net with his back to him. Hinata was in the middle, on his toes. Kageyama was at the right, front row, waiting to run to wherever the pass went.

*He's going to serve me,* thought Sho.

There was no question about it. He was the newest thing on the court. He'd come on cold, off a bench, with a reputation Oikawa had personally watched him earn: the 1st year who couldn't pass. Oikawa had spent 3 serves hunting Asahi because Asahi was the softest spot in Karasuno's receive. Now there was a softer one, standing exactly where Asahi had been.

So he was the target. And if he was the target, then the only questions were where on him, and how fast.

He knew things about this serve. He'd written them down yesterday from the rail, and again this morning, and he could see the page without opening it. Oikawa tossed with his left hand, high and out in front. When the toss drifted right, the serve went cross. When he wanted the seam he looked at it, once, before he started his walk in, and then never again. He hit the top of the ball and it dove late, in the last 3 meters.

At the net, Kageyama's right hand went behind his back and closed. A fist. The plain one.

Oikawa bounced the ball. He looked up. His eyes went to the space between Sho and Daichi, for half a beat, and then went to nothing.

*The seam. On my side of it.*

The whistle went. Oikawa walked in, 1 step, 2, and threw the ball up.

The gym went silent.

Sho knew the silence. It had come in the 3v3 on Hinata's first freak quick, and at Golden Week, and twice since, always on its own, never when he asked. The crowd and the drums and the squeak of shoes all went somewhere far off, as if somebody had shut a door. Oikawa turned into points of light. Shoulder, elbow, wrist, hips, knees, a skeleton made of light hanging in the air over the end line, and the brightest point of all was his right hand, burning white.

A ghost peeled off the hand, 1 blink early, and swung. Sho saw its path. It went across the ball and down, and the line it drew pointed at his own right hip, half a step from the seam, deep.

He moved before Oikawa hit it.

He took 1 step back and to the right and dropped his hips. He kept his arms still and put them where the ghost had pointed and let the platform sit there. Then the sound came back, all at once, the smack of the serve and the drums and someone on the 2nd floor screaming, and the ball was already on him. It dove in the last 3 meters exactly the way it did on the page, and it dove into his forearms because they were already there.

It came up high. A little toward the net, a little right of the middle. High.

"UP!" Noya shouted.

Kageyama was there. Hinata ran in, and Kindaichi went up with him, because now he went up with Hinata every time. This was the plain one. Kindaichi was at the top of his jump when the ball left Kageyama's hands, and coming down when it reached Hinata, and Hinata, still rising, put it over his falling hands and into the floor inside Seijoh's 3-meter line.

18-20.

The bench was on its feet. He could hear Tanaka yelling his name as if it were a curse word. Noya slammed into his back with both hands. "KIYOSE! THAT'S IT! THAT'S IT!"

He could feel it thinning out, the way the afterimage of a bright window thins out when you close your eyes, and the court was coming back to ordinary depth.

He held on to it. He didn't know how. He just kept hold of it, the way you'd keep a tune in your head by humming it before it faded. He kept it there, the faint burning outline of things, and then he did the other thing, the thing he'd been doing all day without the eye: he put the court together in his head, from above. Where every Seijoh player was. Who was front row. Who'd hit cross from which spot. Kindaichi always with Hinata. The end guy moving his outside hand when the hitter looked at it. Kunimi waking up when it was even. All 4 lines in the notebook and everything he hadn't written down. He laid the map out flat, and he put what was left of the eye inside it.

It held. It hurt. A thumb pressed from inside his skull, right behind his eyes, and pushed.

*Byakugan,* thought Sho. *The discount version.*

He knew what this was. He knew exactly what this was. A 20-year-old in Osaka with a knee that clicked every time he landed, taping it tighter, telling nobody, playing through it for 3 months because the team needed him and it was only pain. He'd sworn twice as hard and twice as smart. He'd meant it.

*1 set,* he thought. *3 months is what took the knee. This is 1 set.* He didn't tell anybody.

That was the rule: when you won back the serve, everybody moved 1 spot clockwise, and the back-left player moved up to the front left. He was a hitter now. At the serve Tanaka was in the middle and Hinata on the right, and once the serve was over they'd switch, Tanaka out to the right side and Hinata into the middle.

Ukai was looking at Sho, with his arms folded.

Asahi sat back down. He had his towel in both hands and was twisting it, and his eyes stayed on the rotation. Suga said something to him, and he nodded without taking his eyes off the court.

Across the net Iwaizumi was on the right side of Seijoh's front row, straight across from Sho. In this rotation he was the one who'd block Sho, and the one who'd hit at him.

Kageyama served. It was a good serve, deep, and Seijoh's libero passed it a little off the net. Oikawa ran it down. Sho watched him, and only him, and the burning outline came up around Oikawa's body like a light switching on. His hands. The ghost of his hands went *back*. Over his own head, toward his right side.

Oikawa's set went exactly where the ghost had gone, back over his head to Iwaizumi, and Iwaizumi came in hard from the right and hit, and the ball hit Sho's hands and Hinata's together and went up off them, high, back toward Karasuno's end line.

Daichi ran it down. He got it up with 1 arm, the ball spinning off it toward the middle. Kageyama was under it.

"Kiyose!"

He was calling him. Sho came off the net, back behind the 3-meter line, turned, and ran in.

*When you look at the hand, they'll move it.*

Oikawa had said it at the sink with a can in his hand. He'd meant it as a warning, or a joke, or a thing to get stuck in Sho's head. All 3, probably.

Sho looked at the hand. He turned his chin to it, down and to the side, so that anyone watching his face would know where he wanted to hit.

Iwaizumi saw. He pulled his outside hand in toward the middle, to take away the ball off the hand, the shot Sho had just told him was coming. It was a good block. It was the right thing to do. It closed the angle off the hand and opened the line beside it, down the sideline, a ball's width inside the antenna.

Sho hit the line.

It went past the edge of Iwaizumi's hand without touching it and landed on the sideline, inside, 2 meters from the end line. The line judge's flag pointed down. In.

19-20.

Iwaizumi came down and looked at his own hand. Then he looked at Oikawa. Oikawa was standing in the back row with his hands on his hips, and his smile had gone. He was looking at Sho the way Sho had been looking at him all day.

"WHAT WAS THAT?!" Tanaka grabbed Sho by both shoulders and shook him. "You LOOKED at him! You looked right at the hand and then you hit somewhere else! What was THAT?!" He slapped Sho's back hard enough to rattle his teeth and ran back to his spot, still yelling it.

Kageyama served again.

The pressure behind Sho's eyes had gone from a thumb to a fist. He let it. He kept the map up and the eye inside it and watched the court, and every rally, at the moment that mattered, the outline came up around whoever had the ball.

Seijoh passed. Oikawa set the left, to Kunimi. Sho was blocking on the far side from him, too far to get there, but the eye put its outline around Kunimi as he went up, and his arm was slowing. The ghost of it went soft, a short swing that stopped early, a tip over the top of Tanaka's block.

"TIP!" Sho yelled, before Kunimi touched the ball. "Short! Behind Tanaka-san!"

Noya was already running when Kunimi's fingers touched it. He got there with his whole body flat out on the floor, and his hand went under the ball like a lid, and the ball popped up. Kageyama put it up to Tanaka on the right. Tanaka hit it so hard into the middle of Seijoh's court that the libero ducked.

20-20.

Noya came up off the floor shouting. "How'd you know?! Kiyose! How'd you *know* that?!"

"He slowed down," said Sho. His voice came out ordinary.

"Do it again!"

"Okay."

Kageyama served. Seijoh passed. This time Oikawa set the right, to Iwaizumi again, and Sho saw Iwaizumi's ghost go up and swing down the line, toward Karasuno's left sideline, where Sho was standing.

"The 4! Line!"

Hinata came across fast, faster than anybody Sho had ever seen move on a court, and they went up together on the line side. Sho knew where the ball was going and when, and he put his hands there and held them still, and the ball hit them and came straight down on Seijoh's side, between Iwaizumi's feet.

21-20.

Hinata landed and screamed, a noise with no word in it, and grabbed Sho's arm with both hands. "WE BLOCKED HIM! Did you see?! We blocked the 4!"

"Yeah," said Sho. "We did."

He couldn't quite see Hinata's face. It was there, bright and close, but it was flat, like a photo of a face held up in front of him. The lines on the floor had doubled at the edges. When he looked at the net, the white tape had a second, fainter tape beside it.

*Kakashi,* he thought. *Kakashi always fell over afterwards. Every single time. I used to think that was the writers being lazy.*

Kageyama served. It came back. Seijoh got a big dig on Tanaka's spike, and the ball came over high and easy, a free ball, floating down short toward the 3-meter line right in front of Sho.

It was the easiest ball of the set. A free ball. Satoshi could have passed it at 10 years old.

He watched it come. It was close. It was further. It was close again. The depth went in and out like a camera that couldn't find focus, and he stayed when he should have stepped. He reached. The ball dropped a step in front of his hands and bounced on the 3-meter line.

21-21.

"Don't mind!" Daichi called. "Don't mind!"

"Sorry!" Sho's hand went up. "Mine. Sorry."

He blinked hard. The gym came back in pieces. He looked at the bench. Ukai was looking straight at him, and Ukai had unfolded his arms.

---

Seijoh rotated, and Iwaizumi went back to serve.

His serve was a jump serve, all power, and it went at Sho, because Sho had just shanked a free ball and the whole building had seen it. The eye didn't come. He reached for it and there was nothing to reach for, only the fist behind his eyes. He had the map. Iwaizumi was a big hitter who served the way he spiked, straight, at the body. So Sho didn't move his feet. He stood square, low, with his arms out in front of his chest, and let the ball come to him.

It hit him in the forearms and the chest at the same time and went up. Ugly. Up. Kageyama ran it down at the sideline and put up a finger behind his hip, and Hinata was already running. The ball got to him at the top of his jump, and he hit it straight down past Kindaichi's arms.

22-21.

Karasuno rotated. Hinata went back to serve, and Sho moved from the left side into the middle of the front row. Hinata's serve was a plain one, safe, and Seijoh passed it easily. Oikawa set Hanamaki on the left, and Sho went up to block him a beat late. The ball went past his hands, cross, and Daichi got a forearm on it, and it flew off into the boards.

22-22.

It was high, higher than anything he'd given Tanaka all day, because Tanaka had told him *higher* before the set started. Tanaka hit it down the line with his whole body behind it.

23-22.

Karasuno rotated again. Tanaka went back to serve, and Sho moved from the middle to the right side of the front row. Tsukishima came back on for Noya, because Noya's spot had come round to the front and a libero can't play there. On his way past Sho to the left side of the net Tsukishima stopped for half a step.

"Your eyes are red."

"It's the lights."

Tsukishima looked at him a little longer than he looked at most things. Then he went to his spot.

Tanaka served. Seijoh passed, and Oikawa set their other middle, the number 2, on a quick right in front of Sho. Sho jumped. The ball was closer than he'd seen it, and he got there late, and it went off his fingertips and out past the end line.

23-23.

At the dead ball Kageyama came up to him. He stood a little too close, the way he did when he was going to say something he'd decided on in advance.

"Kiyose."

"Yes."

"My tosses. What do you want."

Sho looked at him. Kageyama's face was flat and photograph-thin, and there was a faint second Kageyama standing just to the left of the first one.

"Flat," said Sho. "Out to the antenna. Like Date Tech."

"Okay."

Seijoh served. Daichi passed. Sho was on the right side now. Across the net Oikawa had rotated up to the front row, on the left. A setter in the front row usually ran over to the right side of the net as soon as his team served, to be ready to set. Oikawa stayed on the left, right in front of Sho, and swapped with his hitter for this one rally. He was standing across the net with his hands up, smiling again, and he was looking right at Sho's chin.

*He knows the trick,* thought Sho. *It's his trick. So when I look at the hand, he won't move it. He'll hold it still and wait for me to hit the line.*

The eye didn't come. He could do this one without it, the old way, the film-room way: what does this player know, and what will he do because he knows it.

Oikawa's hand didn't move. He kept it out by the antenna with the palm turned in, sealing the line, and Sho could see in his face that he knew he'd won.

24-23.

"Rude," Oikawa said, across the net.

Sho couldn't answer. The fist behind his eyes had become a nail.

Karasuno rotated. Sho went back to the end line to serve.

Somebody bounced him the ball. He caught it on the 2nd try. The end line was a white stripe with another, fainter stripe beside it, and he put his toes behind both of them to be safe.

Set point.

The gym was loud enough to feel in his teeth. He could hear the Seijoh girls, and Karasuno's 2 rows of supporters, and Shimada's voice somewhere yelling his name, and all of it sounded far away. He could see that she was standing.

It went over the tape and wobbled and dropped toward Seijoh's libero, who passed it perfectly, because it was an easy serve.

Oikawa set.

Sho was in the back right now, running into his defensive spot, and he reached for the eye one more time and pulled. It was like pulling a nail out with his fingers. It came.

The gym went silent and the light came up, and he saw all of it. Oikawa in the front row with his hands over his head. Kindaichi going with a fake. Hanamaki running a fake on the left. And Iwaizumi, from the back row, behind the 3-meter line, coming through the middle on a back-row attack. Everybody looked good. Every hitter on Seijoh's side looked like he was getting the ball.

*When all of them look good, that's him,* Ukai had said about a setter.

The outline went up around Iwaizumi. His ghost swung down and across, at the right back corner. At Sho.

Sho was already there, standing where the ball was going to be, low, with his arms together, and Iwaizumi's spike came into them like it had been posted there.

It went up. High, clean, in the middle of the court.

"NICE!" That was Noya, from somewhere. "NICE KIYOSE!"

Kageyama got under it. He was in the front row on the left, and Daichi was on the right side, front row, coming in. Kageyama set Daichi, a plain, honest ball that sat up for him. Daichi hit it as hard as Sho had ever seen him hit anything, down past the end of Oikawa's block and into the floor.

The whistle went.

25-23.

---

The noise came back first. Then the bench, all of it, on the court at once. Hinata's voice cracked on whatever he was yelling. Tanaka was crying already, openly, with his arms around Daichi's neck. Noya jumped on Asahi's back, and Asahi, who'd come off the bench with everybody else, staggered and held him up.

Sho was still standing in the back right corner. His arms were still together in front of him.

Something warm ran over his top lip.

He touched it. This time his fingers came away red. A drop fell past his hand and hit the floor and spread, a bright coin on the yellow wood, and then another one landed next to it.

"Oi." Tanaka had let go of Daichi. He was 2 meters away and coming closer. "Oi. OI. Kiyose. Your nose."

"It's okay," said Sho. "It's just..."

"ARE YOU DYING?!" Hinata had both hands in his own hair.

"No."

The 2nd referee had blown a whistle and was pointing at the floor. Somebody ran over with a towel and a mop. Kiyoko was there. She held tissues in front of his face and he took them and pressed them under his nose and tipped his head forward, the way his mother had taught him when he was 6, and the blood soaked through the first tissue in a second. She gave him another.

Ukai walked across the court and stood in front of Sho, and he held out a bottle.

Sho reached for it. His hand closed on air a hand's width short of the bottle. He reached again, slower, and found it.

"How many fingers."

Ukai had put his other hand up. Sho looked at it. There were fingers. There were a lot of them, and they wouldn't stay still.

"It's fine. Coach, it comes back. In a few minutes it comes..."

"How many."

"...3. Or 4."

Ukai breathed out through his nose.

"You're done," he said.

"I can play set 3. I just need..."

"You're done for today." He said it the same way he'd said *not yet* to Asahi, as if there was nothing to argue about because it was already decided. Then, lower, so that only Sho could hear it: "You knew. Out there. You knew it was costing you, and you kept going."

Sho held the tissues under his nose.

"Yes."

Ukai looked at him. Then he looked at the spot on the 3-meter line where the free ball had dropped.

"I saw you miss that one," he said. "And I left you on." He rubbed his face with his whole hand. "So we're both going to talk about it. Later. Sit down."

---

They put him at the end of the bench with a cold towel folded over his eyes and a bag of ice from Kiyoko's cooler on the back of his neck. The blood had slowed. The pressure hadn't. The dark under the towel was much better than the light, and he sat in it with his elbows on his knees and listened.

He could hear Ukai with the line-up paper. Asahi going back in. Takeda saying *Kiyose-kun* to someone in a low voice and someone else answering. He could hear Kageyama ask Suga a question, and Suga answer it. Hinata came and stood in front of him, he could tell from the breathing, and stood there for a while, shifting from foot to foot so that his shoes squeaked, and then went away again.

Then a hand put something on his knee. It was cold and square. He felt it. Carton milk, with the straw already in it.

"Drink it," said Kageyama's voice. The bench creaked as he stood up again. Footsteps went away.

Sho held the milk. For 9 years he had sat in a dark room watching other people play. He'd come back from it, somehow, to a body that worked and a court with real light on it, and he'd sworn he would never sit behind the glass again.

*1 set,* he thought. *I said 1 set.*

The buzzer went for the end of the break. Close, above and behind him, at the rail of the 2nd floor right over Karasuno's bench, a long way round from where she'd stood all morning, over the drums and the girls and everything else, a voice he had known for 10 years shouted his name.

"SHO!"

It cracked in the middle.

Sho took the towel off. The light came in like a slap, and he couldn't find her. He could only hear her, and under her, lower, his father's voice saying, "Mio. Mio, wait," and the sound of someone small running down a flight of concrete stairs.

Takeda went. His footsteps went down the hallway quickly and turned a corner.

Sho turned the good tap on and bent to drink. The water came out slow, the way it always did. He drank until his mouth stopped being dry, turned the tap off and straightened up.

Behind him, a vending machine clunked.

Sho turned round. It took his eyes a while to sort out the layers: the machine, the wall, the person crouched in front of the machine, taking a can out of the slot. A white and teal jacket, zipped to the chin.

The person stayed crouched there with the can in his hand, waiting, as if to see whether Sho would speak.

Every time, Oikawa had spoken first. At his school's gate. At this sink. Across the net.

Sho stood with his back against the sink and his hand on the cold edge of it.

"Oikawa-san," he said.
