# Agent 6: Opponents and counters (Arc 3)

How the Tokyo teams notice Sho's reading, how they fight it, how he adapts, what each relationship is about, and what each person can teach him. Built on the fic's established facts (ch 13 to 28 continuity blocks) and canon research.

**Source key.** [web: X] = confirmed in a search result (links in "Sources" at the end). [memory] = from my knowledge of the series, not confirmed this session; check before it goes in a Continuity block. [fic] = already established in the fic. [proposal] = invented for the plan. WebFetch to the fandom wiki and to breezewiki was blocked, so canon detail is from search snippets only.

## 0. The big idea in one paragraph

Each Tokyo team attacks a different layer of Sho's reading, and none of them knows the eye exists. **Nekoma attacks the eye itself** (multi-attacker plays beat a 1-player lock; long rallies raise the price of every read; Kenma removes his own tells). **Fukurodani attacks the value of a read** (Akaashi hides the set; Bokuto hits so hard that knowing where it goes isn't enough; the supporting cast has no big tells). **Ubugawa attacks the body under the eyes** (jump-serve barrage at the D-rank passer; the eye likes to fire on serves, so this is where the hand goes up). **Shinzen is the control test** (a plain, sound team where the map should work without the eye). Every counter points Sho the same way the notebook's 1st page already points: *Build the map so I don't need the eye.* That's how the opponents become the arc's teachers without anyone being a prop.

## 3. Nekoma

Nekoma's identity in canon is defense and connection: the ball doesn't drop, rallies go long, Kenma is "the brain" and the others "the blood" [web: toy-people/Kenma profile; memory for Kuroo's pre-match chant "We are like the blood in our veins..."]. **That makes Nekoma the eye's natural predator.** The eye charges per read; Nekoma's whole style is making rallies longer.

### 3.1 Kozume Kenma (setter, 2nd year)

**Canon.** Calm, quiet, analytical; observes and predicts; directs the team as its "brain"; game sense and technique make up for low power, speed and stamina; usually carrying a handheld console; brought into volleyball by childhood neighbor Kuroo. [web: Sportskeeda "Who is Kenma", toy-people profiles, myanimepulse stats: intellect 5, technique 5, power 1, stamina 2] His setting doesn't sell the direction; he dumps when a team reads his sets (ch 14/15 in the fic follow this). He hates pointless effort and wasted energy [memory]; in the Nationals match he struggles to explain why he put in extra effort, and gets cranky from being tired [web: fandom "Trap" episode]. He thinks of opponents and matches like games he's playing through [memory; the "level up" phrasing was **not** confirmed by search, so keep it as his inner framing, not a quote]. He reads people's nerves (canon later: "Hinata will feel the confinement... get antsy to jump") [web: Goodreads Vol 35 status].

**(1) How he notices.** He already has T1 (ch 14). In Arc 3 he adds:
- T3/T4: that Sho sometimes goes still and is moving early. Kenma doesn't care what it is; he cares that **it isn't every rally**. He watches for when it happens.
- T8 and T10: the stillness is followed, a few rallies later, by the pale face and then by the hand going up and Sho leaving. Kenma connects them.
- His model (inner voice if there's a Kenma POV): a **passive skill that procs on its own, with a cooldown and a cost**. "It isn't something he turns on. It goes off. And then he pays for it."

**(2) How he counters.** In order of escalation across the camp:
1. **Kill his own tells.** Ch 14's snapshot read his shoulders ("square = dump"). At camp Kenma sets with square shoulders every time, front, back and dump. Sho's Kenma snapshot becomes worthless. [proposal]
2. **Make the 1-player lock worthless.** The eye shows *one* player. Nekoma's synchronized attack (every hitter approaches every time) means the brightest limb Sho sees may belong to a real swing that doesn't get the ball. Decoys really swing, so the ghost really shows a swing. **This is the clean, logical counter to the eye, and it's canon Nekoma.** [fic: Nekoma's synchronized decoys appear in ch 15; proposal for the logic]
3. **Dump when he goes still.** When Sho locks onto a hitter, Kenma tips into the space Sho just gave up (the ch 14 trick, upgraded from neck checks to the stillness).
4. **Make rallies long.** Kenma tells Yaku and Kai to keep Sho's attacks alive and send easy balls back to Karasuno, so Sho keeps having to read. "He gets tired if he has to look a lot. So make him look a lot." This is ordinary strategy, never cruel. [proposal]
5. **Serve him** (Kenma's own soft, placed serve, ch 14 canon, at Sho's weak side).

**(3) How Sho adapts.**
- Stops reading Kenma at all. Ukai's ch 20 advice, already in the notebook, is the answer: **"Not at him. At them. When all of them look good, that's him."** Sho reads the *pass* (by sound, T9) and the hitters' approach depth instead of Kenma's hands. A bad pass removes the quick; a pass that pulls Kenma off the net removes the back set. That's the Field map in its first form: cheap, every touch, no eye.
- Against synchronized attacks he gives up the 1-player read and calls zones for the blockers ("Left's deep. Middle's late.") which is information the map can hold and the eye can't.
- Against long rallies: he plays the 1st set with his eyes on the map and lets the eye fire only if it fires; he puts the hand up the 1st time the burning starts. Losing that match is acceptable. Playing all 3 sets is the goal.

**(4) Relationship beat.** Kenma is Hinata's friend; that stays theirs. With Sho the bond is **2 watchers who spend energy opposite ways.** Kenma saves everything. Sho spends everything (and Satoshi spent a knee). What Kenma wants from Sho: at first nothing; he finds him tiring ("You look at everybody. It's tiring to watch." from the bible draft). Later, a puzzle he hasn't finished. What Sho wants from Kenma: how to be lazy with his eyes. The soul note: Kenma is the first opponent who treats Sho's cost as a fact of the game, without pity or concern, and that's oddly a relief to Sho.

**(5) What he could teach.** **Economy.** "I don't look at everything. I look at what changed." Sho begs, politely and shamelessly ("Please tell me what you look at." "...No." "Then what you don't look at."), and Kenma, too tired to argue, gives him one thing: look at the receivers' faces after the serve; the scared one gets the next ball. That's a setter's eye given to a hitter, and it's cheap.

**Sample lines (plain).**
- Kenma: "You look at everybody. It's tiring to watch."
- Kenma (after a set where Sho went off with his hand up): "You came off when you were winning." / Sho: "Yes." / Kenma: "...Hm." (he goes back to his game)
- Kenma, later, the one real gift: "Don't watch me. I don't do anything. Watch the one who's scared."

**Surprise POV candidate.** Kenma, ch 33 (the Nekoma loss) or ch 39. His POV shows Sho from outside as a game character he can't fully read, with a "skill" that has a cost. Inner voice: short, flat, game words (cooldown, proc, grind), not jokes. Never 2 POVs in a row; the bible's ch 36 POV slot would then go to someone else or nobody.

### 3.2 Kuroo Tetsuro (captain, middle blocker, 3rd year)

**Canon.** Read blocker; provocateur with a lazy smile and nicknames ("Shrimpy"); Kenma's childhood friend who got him into volleyball; teaches Tsukishima read blocking and proper form at the 2nd camp's 3rd gym, after provoking him into joining; blocking as patience and timing. [web: Fanlore Kurotsukki, otakumode Kuroo profile, fanlore Kenma] His paused-approach quick is in the fic's ch 15 (canon).

**(1) How he notices.** Already has T1, T5 and the notebook (ch 13/15: "notebook", "You stopped looking around"). In Arc 3 he notices that Sho **lies with his face** now (Tanaka's lesson). A read blocker loves this: "you look where you're not hitting." He also notices the hand-up and comes over after, as captain-to-1st-year, half needling, half checking.

**(2) How he counters.**
- **Reads the hips, not the face.** Once he's caught the chin bait, he stops looking at Sho's face. "Your face is lying. Your hips aren't." That neutralizes T5 for good against Kuroo, and it's what he teaches Tsukishima too.
- **Gives Sho a block to read.** Kuroo shows line on purpose (done in ch 15) and escalates: shows line, moves late; shows nothing, then puts the outside hand up as Sho's eyes arrive. Kuroo is the one blocker who **wants** Sho to read, because he can set traps in what Sho sees.
- **Baits the eye.** Kuroo's paused approach (a real swing motion that stops) is exactly what a "ghost" reads wrong. If the eye shows Kuroo's arm coming and Kuroo stops, Sho commits early (T4 becomes a weakness). That's the counter that could make Sho *bite* once in a big way at camp. [proposal]
- **Provocation.** "Oi, notebook." Mild, steady. Not cruel.

**(3) How Sho adapts.** He learns there are blockers who read the reader. Answer: **hit the hand** (Sho's own lesson from Oikawa 24-23), and **hit before Kuroo has finished deciding** (faster arm, earlier contact), which is a body fix, not an eye fix. Off court: begs Kuroo for blocking (Block E).

**(4) Relationship beat.** Kuroo wants the "Battle at the Garbage Dump" for real, at Nationals; he wants Karasuno strong. So he helps them, needles them and cares more than he'll say. What Kuroo wants from Sho: to see the notebook. Sho lets him read the Nekoma page (shameless trust); Kuroo reads it, laughs once and hands it back: "You missed one." That's the trade. What Sho wants from Kuroo: to be blocked, a lot, by someone who can teach while doing it. Keep **Tsukishima's** lessons and moonrise his own: Sho is in the 3rd gym as the **hitter they practice on**, which is humbling and right for a 1st year with Block E.

**(5) What he could teach.** Blocking footwork and patience; and the idea that "a read is a bet, so make them bet first". Use Sho as a drill: "Tsukki. Watch his chin. ...He's lying. Now watch his hips." Sho learns his own tells from the outside by hearing Kuroo explain them to Tsukishima. That's the most efficient (and funniest, situationally) way for Sho to see himself.

**Sample lines.**
- Kuroo: "Oi, notebook. You looked at my hand again." / Sho: "I did." / Kuroo: "I know. That's why I left it there."
- Kuroo (3rd gym, to Tsukishima, Sho in the approach line): "Watch his face. Now don't. Watch his hips."
- Kuroo (after Sho's hand goes up and he comes off): "You hurt?" / Sho: "No. Rules." / Kuroo: "...Okay." He doesn't ask again. (Respecting the rule's "no questions" spirit without knowing it.)

### 3.3 Haiba Lev (middle blocker, 1st year, 194 cm)

**Canon.** Half Russian 1st year, joined late, huge reach, raw skill, can barely hit at first, Kenma ordered to practice with him, self-declared future ace of Nekoma; promises Hinata he'll be first to stop his quick; shows a whip-like spike vs Ubugawa at camp; at the 2nd camp he asks to join the 3rd gym. [web: fandom Lev Haiba; animeoshi episode summary] Yaku kicks him when he mentions Yaku's height [web: Yaku fan wiki summary].

**(1) How he notices.** He doesn't, at first. Then he notices the result: Sho digs him from the right spot every time. He asks out loud, in front of everyone.

**(2) How he counters.** He can't on purpose, and that's the point. **Lev is the eye's blind spot from the other side: the eye reads intention in the body, and Lev's body does things Lev didn't intend.** The ghost says cross; the ball comes off his fingers somewhere else. Raw players are hard to read because there's nothing consistent to read. On the block, his reach simply covers the line Sho likes. [proposal]

**(3) How Sho adapts.** Stops "reading" Lev and plays the percentages (map) on him; hits over Lev's hands high, or uses Lev's late outside hand (raw blockers drift). He also learns a humbling fact: the eye's read isn't the result. Note in notebook: *Lev: the body says one thing, the ball does another. The eye only sees the body.* (Plain words; Sho knows nothing about the eye's name.)

**(4) Relationship beat.** 2 1st years with big bodies they can't fully use yet. Lev wants to be the ace; Sho is a sub ace who used to be an ace in another life. Lev is loud and friendly and asks too much ("How did you know? Teach me!"). Sho, who begs everyone, gets begged at for once, and finds he likes it. Keep it light; Hinata and Lev's rivalry (the quick) stays theirs.

**(5) What he could teach.** Nothing technical. He gives Sho something else: 50 swings against a 194 cm block in an empty gym, which no Miyagi player can give him. Sho gives Lev the "your shoulder said cross" lesson, and Lev doesn't understand it.

**Sample lines.**
- Lev: "How did you know? I didn't even know where it was going." / Sho: "Your shoulder went cross." / Lev: "...It did?" / Sho: "Your hand didn't."
- Lev: "Again! One more! Kiyose, block me!" / Sho: "I'm worse at blocking than you." / Lev: "Then we're even!"

### 3.4 Yaku Morisuke (libero, 3rd year, 165 cm)

**Canon.** Nekoma's libero, the most consistent receiver; hardworking, honest, watches over the younger players (Shibayama, Kenma, Lev); dug Asahi's spikes having seen them once; short temper about his height. [web: Yaku MyAnimeList profile; fic ch 14] He drills Lev's receiving at camp [memory, unconfirmed by search; Lev's receive practice under Yaku is a canon running bit].

**(1) How he notices.** Already has T10 (ch 14: "You look pale."). He notices bodies, not minds: pale, swaying, late feet. A libero sees Sho's **feet start late after a long look**.

**(2) How he counters.** He fixes his own tell (Sho beat his half-step cheat in ch 14). At camp he cheats the other way on purpose once, then not at all. As a receiver, he reads Sho's hits like any good libero: Sho's line shot after the chin bait is something Yaku now waits for.

**(3) How Sho adapts.** He asks for receiving lessons instead of trying to beat him. Bible already plans libero work with Yaku (ch 36).

**(4) Relationship beat.** Yaku is the senior who takes care of people and hates being thanked for it. Sho's "senpai of the other school" line annoyed him (ch 14). He wants Lev to receive properly; Sho wants to receive properly. So Yaku ends up drilling 2 tall 1st years, kicking one and glaring at the other. Sho's Reception D is the skill under all the reading: **reading a ball and passing it are 2 different things**, and Yaku is who proves it.

**(5) What he could teach.** **Platform first, feet second, eyes last.** "You see it early and move late. You stand there looking." That's a direct, practical rebuke of the eye habit (T3): the eye makes Sho *watch* and then move, and Yaku tells him to move while he watches.

**Sample lines.**
- Yaku: "You see it fine. Your feet are late. You're standing there looking at it."
- Yaku: "Drink something." / Sho: "Yes, Yaku-san." / Yaku: "...Good. That's better than last time."

### 3.5 Yamamoto Taketora (ace, 2nd year)

**Canon.** Nekoma's ace; loud, Tanaka's mirror and rival, flees from Kiyoko; power hitter. [web: Wikipedia list, otakusnotes; fic ch 13] (A strong cross is the fic's bible trait; **cross-shot specialty not confirmed by search**.)

**(1) How he notices.** He notices that 13 dug him, and gets angry.
**(2) How he counters.** He hits harder. That's it. And it works: Sho reads Yamamoto every time and still can't hold the ball with a D platform.
**(3) How Sho adapts.** Moves earlier and sets his platform still (Yaku's lesson), and gets a few up by the end of camp. Lets Daichi take the ball when it's Daichi's.
**(4) Relationship beat.** Yamamoto is **Tanaka's** rival. Sho is a side character in it; his only beat is being the one Yamamoto keeps hitting at, and Tanaka's "I just hit it harder" (ch 27) coming true on the other side of the net.
**(5) Teach.** Indirectly: that reading isn't enough. Sho writes Tanaka's line in the notebook under Yamamoto's name.

**Sample line.** Yamamoto: "Oi! 13! Stop digging me!" / Sho: "Sorry." (He digs the next one too, badly, out of bounds.)

### 3.6 Kai Nobuyuki (vice-captain, wing spiker, 3rd year, #2)

**Canon.** Vice-captain, 3rd-year wing spiker, calm, dependable, considerate, keeps the team together; has his own one-shot ("Nobuyuki Kai's Nightmare") retelling the Karasuno match from his side. [web: Wikipedia list via search, Anibase profile, thegeekiary one-shot review] Excellent receiver per the bible [bible; not confirmed this session].

### 3.7 Nekomata Yasufumi (Nekoma coach)

**Canon.** Old coach who came out of retirement, rival and friend of Ukai Ikkei (the original "Battle at the Garbage Dump" rivalry), hates losing, values connection and long rallies; "It wouldn't be a practice match without a rematch." [web: MyAnimeList Nekomata; fic ch 15]

## 4. Fukurodani

Fukurodani's canon point is that it's a full team around an ace: when Bokuto sinks, the others carry on and Akaashi routes the offense elsewhere. [web: fmyly analysis; Konoha wiki]

### 4.1 Akaashi Keiji (setter, 2nd year, 182.3 cm)

**Canon.** Calm, composed, analytical, humble about his own limits (says he couldn't do what Kageyama does); handles Bokuto's moods and can anticipate them; called the only person who knows all Bokuto's moods and how to handle them (Quote the Anime cites ch 244); precise, reliable under pressure; capable of a calculated dump. [web: Sportskeeda setter rankings, fanlore Akaashi, quotetheanime] A list of Bokuto's weaknesses is fan summary; **"37 weaknesses" not confirmed** [memory]. Same height as Sho (182) [bible].

**(1) How he notices.** Akaashi watches Bokuto between rallies to manage him. He notices that **13 is watching Bokuto between rallies too**, and reading his moods (shoulders, voice, how he holds the ball). Nobody does that except Akaashi. He also notices Sho reading his own hands (T3 on a setter).

**(2) How he counters.**
- **Protects Bokuto's moods from being read and used.** If Karasuno starts serving Bokuto when he's sagging (Sho told Ukai the tell, Ukai decided: ordinary volleyball), Akaashi sets *away* from Bokuto for 3 rallies (Konoha, Sarukui, Washio) and lets Bokuto recover without the ball. The mood tell stops being useful because Akaashi takes Bokuto out of the attack exactly when it shows.
- **Disguises the set.** Same posture for every set and a late release; and a dump when he sees 13 locked on a hitter. Unlike Kenma he doesn't play games with tells; he just gives nothing.
- **Sets the player Sho isn't looking at.** Like Kenma, but with a team that has no weak attacker, so the map has to hold all of them.

**(3) How Sho adapts.** The eye on Akaashi's hands gives ~0.2 s, which is useless if Akaashi releases late. Sho moves his read from the setter to **the pass and the hitters' run-ups** again (the same lesson Nekoma taught, now confirmed with a second setter). He tells Ukai about Bokuto's mood tell **first** (rule: Tell Coach 1st), and then has to accept that Akaashi beats it.

**(4) Relationship beat.** **The deepest mirror at camp.** Akaashi is a quiet, same-height watcher who serves a louder talent and doubts himself. Sho, an ex-ace who became an analyst, knows exactly what it's like to be the person who sees the ace clearly. What Akaashi wants from Sho: for him to stop studying Bokuto (protectiveness), and, under that, someone who understands the job. What Sho wants: setting lessons. He is Set E, a backup all-rounder, and the 2nd ball comes to wings when the setter digs. Shameless, polite: "Please teach me to set. Badly is fine."

**(5) What he could teach.** **The 2nd ball for a non-setter**: high, to the antenna, square to the target, "a hitter can hit anything high and off the net." And, by example, **how to manage a person without them knowing**, which Sho will need for himself (managing his own eyes like Akaashi manages Bokuto). A good closing thought for Sho: *He handles Bokuto the way I have to handle my eyes. Early. Before it shows.*

**Sample lines.**
- Akaashi: "You watch Bokuto-san between rallies." / Sho: "Yes." / Akaashi: "Most people watch him during them." / (pause) "Please don't serve him when he's like that." / Sho: "I told my coach. He decides." / Akaashi: "...That's fair. Then I'll set someone else."
- Akaashi (setting lesson): "Higher. You don't need it fast. You need it where he can see it."

**Surprise POV candidate.** Akaashi, for the Fukurodani final match (ch 40 to 41) or the 3rd gym (ch 36). His POV would show Sho as someone reading Bokuto, from the person whose job that is.

### 4.2 Bokuto Kotaro (ace, 3rd year, 185.3 cm)

**Canon.** Top-5 ace; huge emotional swings ("emo mode" is the fandom term) that Akaashi manages; when his cross gets blocked he sulks; at the camp he develops/uses a **straight (line) shot** past blockers who shut his cross [memory: the line shot vs Tsukishima's block at the 3rd gym and in the final match]; "HEY HEY HEY"; invites Tsukishima into the 3rd gym with Kuroo; the "moment you get hooked on volleyball" talk with Tsukishima [memory; protected as part of Tsukishima's arc]. [web: fanlore BokuAka, fmyly analysis, fanlore Kurotsukki]

**(1) How he notices.** He doesn't notice tells; he notices that 13 **dug his spike**. That's all he needs.

**(2) How he counters.** Not by thinking. Bokuto's limbs are honest, so the eye reads him perfectly (cross or line, the ghost is clear). His counter is **power and joy**: knowing it's cross doesn't help when Sho's platform can't hold it. And when Bokuto is on, he hits both line and cross with the same approach. Sho reads him and still gets blasted.

**(3) How Sho adapts.** Gets *in the way* rather than *passing*: dig high to the middle of the court, not to the setter (Yaku's and Noya's lesson); and accepts some balls just aren't his.

**(4) Relationship beat.** **Bokuto is Satoshi at 18 before the knee:** pure joy at being the ace. Sho sees it and it hurts in a quiet way (never said). Bokuto wants a defender who digs him so he has something to beat. Sho wants to be hit at, by the best, 50 times. A perfect match of needs: the 3rd gym's late-night drills where Sho stands in the back row and Bokuto hits at him. Keep it light and loud on Bokuto's side and quiet on Sho's. Do **not** let Sho fix Bokuto's mood; that's Akaashi's.

**(5) What he could teach.** **"When they read your cross, hit the line."** Bokuto's lesson for hitters, plainly said. And the reverse, for Sho's eyes: an ace who doesn't hide anything still wins, so reading isn't everything.

**Sample lines.**
- Bokuto: "HEY! 13! You got that one!" / Sho: "Please hit me 50 more." / Bokuto: "50?! Akaashi! He wants 50!" / Akaashi: "Then hit 50, Bokuto-san."
- Bokuto (honest, quieter, later): "When they block my cross I get, like. Ugh. So I hit the line. Then I feel good again."

### 4.3 Konoha, Komi, Sarukui (and Washio, Onaga)

- **Konoha Akinori** (#7, 3rd-year wing spiker): versatile on offense and defense, fans call him the jack of all trades; Komi calls him essential to how the team works. [web: fandom Konoha page; MyAnimeList] **Use:** the "no tells" player. Solid, average motion, no big swing. The eye barely fires on him; the map has to. He's also the role Sho will fill at Karasuno: the guy who does everything well enough. Sample: Konoha (to Sho, after a 3-rally run of Sho reading Bokuto): "Everyone watches Bokuto. Watch me for a bit. I'm the one scoring."
- **Komi Haruki** (3rd year, the smallest on the team): teases Konoha and Sarukui. Search snippets call him a wing spiker in one profile and **didn't confirm libero**; canon has him as Fukurodani's libero [memory, high confidence; verify]. **Use:** a 2nd libero model for Sho's practice libero work (bible lists Yaku first). Light, teasing, quick hands.
- **Sarukui Yamato** (3rd-year wing spiker, all-rounder like Konoha, Bokuto nicknames him "Monkey"). [web: Anibase] **Use:** filler on court; the "set someone else" option Akaashi uses.
- **Washio Tatsuki** (middle) and **Onaga Wataru** (1st-year middle) [memory]: on court only.

**Counter as a group:** Fukurodani's depth means Sho can't win by finding the one weak hitter. The team has no single tell to exploit. That's where Sho learns the map must track 5 attackers, not 1.

## 5. Ubugawa and Shinzen

### 5.1 Ubugawa (jump-serve team)

**Canon:** jump serves drilled with 100 extra after practice every day; serve-and-block style. [web: fandom Ubugawa High] Lev shows his whip spike against them [web: fandom Lev Haiba].

- **(1) Notice:** a serve-and-block team scouts the receive line and serves the weakest passer. Sho is Reception D. They find him in 1 rotation.
- **(2) Counter:** every server serves 13. Hard, deep, to his left arm (the "flat sound" Sho heard in Tanaka's pass is also Sho's own weakness? [proposal: Sho discovers by sound that his own left arm is also lower; nice mirror with ch 27]).
- **(3) Sho adapts:** the eye **loves serves** (it fired on Oikawa's serve, ch 22; first misread was a serve, ch 5). A whole team of jump servers means the eye will try to fire again and again. **This is the natural site for ch 38 "Hand up": the eye fires on an Ubugawa jump serve, Sho passes it perfectly, the burning starts on the next one, and he puts his hand up while the pass was good.** That's the arc's spine moment: stopping while it's working. Then he learns to pass serves the cheap way: the server's toss and approach angle (map), the sound of the hit (a heavy, late "thock" is deep), and Noya/Daichi shifting to cover him (Ukai decides).
- **(4) Beat:** low on personality; Ubugawa matters as a test. Optional tiny beat: an Ubugawa server who notices Sho came off after passing his best serve, and serves the sub instead.
- **(5) Teach:** by force: Sho's serve receive improves from sheer volume. Sho begs an Ubugawa player to serve at him after practice (100 serves, their own ritual). [proposal]

### 5.2 Shinzen

**Canon:** captain Ogano Daiki (3rd-year wing). [web: Daiki Ogano] Little else confirmed.

If he can't win reads cheaply against Shinzen, the map isn't working yet. This is a good spot for the 1st time he plays all 3 sets at camp, quietly, without a big moment, so the Fukurodani final isn't the only test.

## 6. The adults who watch

### 6.1 Ukai Ikkei (Karasuno's old coach, Keishin's grandfather)

**Canon (ch 83-84 "Tempo" area):** teaches Hinata the 3 tempos and that in the quick the **spiker, not the setter, is the defining force**; reminds him a quick needs variety; has Hinata try blocking middle schoolers' spikes. [web: fandom "Tempo" chapter page; "How to Fly"] Retired after collapsing (fic ch 9 continuity: "old Ukai returned last year... collapsed"). Runs a local volleyball class [memory]. **Hinata's Ikkei lessons are protected.**

**(1) How he notices.** Bible plan: Ikkei is **the 1st adult who suspects the eye**. He sees T4 in a new form: Sho, standing at the side of Ikkei's class while Hinata trains, moves when a child spiker is still in the air, *before the hit*. Old coaches have seen thousands of players; he's maybe seen 1 or 2 who did that.

**(2) Counter.** None; he isn't an opponent. But he tests: has a kid feint, watches whether Sho bites.

**(3) How Sho adapts.** He doesn't hide it (rule: Listen). Ikkei says it in ordinary words and Sho hears his secret described by someone else for the 1st time.

**(4) Beat with soul.** Ikkei is old and was sick; Satoshi died young at a desk. Ikkei asks the one question nobody has asked: "What are you looking at, boy?" and doesn't accept the 1st answer. He shouldn't name the power (Sho never learns its real name). He can say: "I had a boy once who saw the hit early. He got headaches. Quit at 17." [proposal: invented story; keep it short and unconfirmed in canon]. That puts the stakes in the mouth of a man who knows what ends careers.

**(5) What he could teach.** **"An eye is a muscle. You don't run a muscle all day."** and **"Shut them for the ones you don't need."** Ikkei approves the sound reading, which nobody else knows about. This is the lesson that turns the eye from a curse into a resource to *budget*. Also possibly the hitter-side tempo idea for Sho's own attack, but only after Hinata's lesson is done and in a separate scene.

**Staging rule:** Sho does **not** attend Hinata's lessons as a participant. Options: Keishin sends Sho to deliver something to his grandfather's house a day after Hinata's 1st visit; or Sho is in the gym doorway at the end of Hinata's session and Ikkei calls him over as Hinata leaves. Ikkei's scene with Sho is short and separate.

**Sample lines.**
- Ikkei: "What are you looking at, boy?" / Sho: "Their shoulders." / Ikkei: "No. Before that." / (silence) / Ikkei: "You moved before he hit it."
- Ikkei: "Shut them for the ones you don't need. You'll still hear it."

## 7. Oikawa's "something strange" thread (how it continues)

**What's fixed.** Ch 25: Oikawa named T2 to T6 and has no name for the stillness ("I don't know what that one is."). His needle: "Now you'll be thinking about squinting." His parting: "Next time, Tall-kun, play all 3 sets. I want to see what you look like when you can see." Seijoh then lost the Inter-High final to Shiratorizawa (ch 28 paper). [fic]

**Canon hook.** In "Tempo" (ch 83-84 area), Kageyama goes to Oikawa for advice; he opens with "what-if" examples, Oikawa cuts him off and makes him say it straight; Oikawa's point is that Hinata (the hitter) drives the quick, and that if Kageyama doesn't get that, his dictator streak (the King) comes back. [web: fandom Tempo; breezewiki Tempo snippet] The fan summary of Oikawa's philosophy is that a setter draws out 100% of each spiker [web: fan wiki summary; exact chapter wording unconfirmed].

**Recommended continuation (proposal).** Keep Oikawa's canon advice to Kageyama exactly his. Then add **one exchange after it**, in the same scene, Kageyama POV-neutral (Sho isn't there; Kageyama reports it later in his own blunt way):

1. Oikawa, at the end: "Your 13. Is he playing all 3 sets yet?" Kageyama, who can't keep things in: "He's not allowed to look so hard. Doctor said." **That's a real leak, by a blunt teammate, to a rival.** Oikawa's smile goes off (callback to ch 25's tell for his own honesty). He now knows the strange thing has a **medical cost**, which he understands better than anyone (the knee brace).
2. Oikawa's own point, joined to his canon advice: **"Then that's your job too. A setter knows when his hitter can't see. If you keep setting him when his eyes go small, you're the King again."** That turns Oikawa's canon lesson (know your hitters, don't force them) into a Sho application without adding new philosophy.
3. And his pride: **"Whatever it is he does, don't tell me. I'll find it myself."** He wants to beat it by work, which is his whole soul ("Talent is something you make bloom"). The secret stays safe partly because Oikawa *refuses* to be told.

**What it changes at camp.** Kageyama, back in Tokyo, starts watching Sho's eyes. He doesn't set Sho when Sho squints; once he says "Your eyes. Hand." before Sho raises it. Sho is shocked a teammate saw it first. Kageyama tells him, flat: "The Great King asked if you squint still." Sho: "...What did you say?" Kageyama: "I said I'd check." This makes Kageyama an ally of the hand-up rule (healthy, character-true: Kageyama learning to read hitters is his canon arc) and keeps Oikawa present in Arc 3 without him appearing on court.

**Forward payoff (Arc 4).** At the Spring High prelims rematch, Oikawa's counter is ready and honest: **serve 13 in set 3, and make every rally long**, because he knows the squint comes late. Sho's answer is that he plays all 3 sets on the map with the eye as an extra, which is the arc-3 promise kept. Oikawa gets to "see what you look like when you can see". The rivalry stays mutual respect plus wanting to crush each other.

**Alternatives (if the user prefers less leaking).** (a) Oikawa asks, Kageyama says "I don't know," and Oikawa answers "Then watch him." (b) No Sho mention at all in the Tempo scene; Oikawa instead turns up at the Inter-High Nationals viewing / a Miyagi practice game later. Option 1 above is recommended because it uses Kageyama's bluntness and gives Kageyama a soul beat.
