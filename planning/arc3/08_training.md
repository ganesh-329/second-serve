## 0. The one idea that runs the whole training plan

**Train the map to need fewer looks.** The research gives Sho a real, non-magical way to obey page 1 of his notebook ("Build the map so I don't need the eye") and the doctor ("Stop at the burning") at the same time:

- Experts look *less and better*, not more. In volleyball serve reception, novices keep their eyes locked on the ball's whole flight; experts use "visual pivots", a few saccades that jump ahead of the ball to where it will drop, and respond faster with the same accuracy (Moreno Arroyo thesis, summary at [UMH](https://portalinvestigacion.umh.es/documentos/5da82caa2999523a1dc43c22?lang=ca); [sponet record](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4019218?lng=en)). Experts receiving spike serves look at the server's **shoulder and arm during the toss**, then pick up the ball faster in flight (same sponet record, Int. J. Applied Sports Sciences 2010).
- Experts park their gaze in **empty space between cues** and read the rest with peripheral vision: "gaze anchor" and "visual pivot" (Vater, Williams and Hossner 2019; summary at [Bern ISPW project page](https://www.ispw.unibe.ch/research/movement_and_exercise_science/research_projects/performance_enhancing_gaze_strategies_in_sports_games/index_eng.html) and [BORIS](https://boris.unibe.ch/142345)). Beach volleyball experts anchor between ball and attacker, on nothing at all.
- In football, Geir Jordet's scanning work shows that players who turn their heads more before receiving pass better ([Frontiers 2020, PMC7573254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7573254); [Sloan paper](https://sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/the-hidden-foundation-of-field-vision-in-english-premier-league-epl-soccer-players)), **but** Jordet himself says frequency is not the point; timing is ("no point scanning a lot if you're doing it the wrong way", [Training Ground Guru](https://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/geir-jordet-why-scanning-is-about-more-than-just-frequency)). And scanning can be trained: a 5-week, 1 to 2 sessions a week program raised scanning frequency in U19 players and it held 3 weeks later ("Heads Up Girls!", [Frontiers 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1602099/text)).
- Sound carries early information. Sors and colleagues (Trieste) found the sound of a volleyball serve or spike contact helps predict **serve length** and **smash speed**, sometimes better than vision ([PhD thesis](https://arts.units.it/retrieve/e2913fdb-8e06-f688-e053-3705fe0a67e0/Thesis_PhD_Sors.pdf); [Sors et al. 2017](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/244366/mod_resource/content/1/Sors%20et%20al.%202017.pdf); context effects in [Cognitive Research 2022](https://doaj.org/article/43a2bf0475594bc09334202796ac62f4)).

So Arc 3's training has a shape: **fewer fixations, timed checks, peripheral anchoring, sound, early cues.** Every one of those costs his eyes *less* than the ch 14 head-spinning (12 checks in 5 seconds) or the ch 22 forced fusion. Satoshi the analyst would love this, because it's the opposite of how he died: 9 years of staring at a monitor, rewinding at quarter speed. The new Sho trains to see the court by *not staring*. That's the thematic spine, and it's real sports science.

The Emperor's Eye is never trained. It's innate, it fires on its own, and the plan never pretends otherwise. Training is aimed at the map, and at needing the eye less. Training changes *when* the eye fires (rule in section 3.4) and how early Sho catches the burning. It never makes the eye stronger in Arc 3.

**Agreed camp rules (Ukai and Sho, ch 36)** [design]:
1. Map only. No eye on purpose. (He can't use it on purpose anyway; this means: if it fires, don't hold it.)
2. Hand up at the burning. After a hand-up: off for the rest of that set **and** the next set; eyes shut 10 minutes on the bench with a wet towel.
3. Takeda tracks checks per rally on Sho's full sets, and nobody tells Sho the numbers till dinner.
4. Penalty laps: Sho does all of them. (Physical fatigue is fine; he's the scaled-down Toji. That contrast is useful: his body never tires, his eyes and his head do.)

**How fatigue affects the eye and the map (rules for consistency)** [design, grounded in the fatigue and eye-strain sources]:
- **The burning comes sooner when:** it's hot and he's sweating (sweat and dry air in the eyes), he slept badly (classroom futons, Noya and Tanaka talking), he did near work (exam week), he received a lot of fast serves (an incoming fast ball is the heaviest load), and he already hit the burning earlier that day. A good night's sleep mostly resets it.
- **Numbers for the author:** fresh, no burning in a full set of map-only play. Camp day 1: burning after about 15 to 20 hard-reading rallies. Day 4 evening: after about 5 to 6. After a hand-up and 10 minutes eyes shut: he can play again next set, threshold about half.
- **The map gets worse with mental fatigue, the same way the research describes:** he decides faster and less accurately ([JSSM](https://jssm.org/jssm-08-259.xml-Fulltext)). On the page: late in a day he stops checking and guesses, and the guesses come early. Ukai sees it as "jumping the gun". Snapshot capacity drops by 2 or 3 players.
- **The eye fires more when the map breaks.** Rule for the author: the eye comes uninvited when Sho's map has a hole at a high-stakes moment and he's straining into it. Fatigue opens holes. So late in a tired day, the eye is likelier to fire and costlier when it does. That's the trap the ch 38 "Hand up" chapter springs.
- **The training fix is real:** fewer, timed looks and more sound make the map hold longer when tired, which means fewer holes, which means the eye fires less. By the Fukurodani final the eye doesn't fire at all for a full set. That's the earned payoff of "Build the map so I don't need the eye".

## 3. The drills

### 3.1 Meta Vision substages for Arc 3 (sub-steps inside "Scanning", leading to Field map Lv1)

The bible puts Field map Lv1 at the Spring High prelims. Arc 3 builds its parts. I propose 6 sub-skills the author can track without status screens [design]:

| Code | Sub-skill | What it is | Ends Arc 3 at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S1** | Timed checks | Look in the "free" windows, not at random; vary timing so it isn't a tell | 3 to 4 checks per rally, 80%+ in windows |
| **S2** | Snapshot capacity | Players he can hold from 1 look | 8 of 12 (Field map needs all 12) |
| **S3** | Anchor gaze | Park the eyes on a point, read the rest with the edges | Reads 2 hitters' approaches off 1 anchor |
| **S4** | Sound layer | Pass quality, which arm, approach footsteps, contact sound for serve length | Reliable in a 1-court gym; patchy in a loud 3-court gym |
| **S5** | Early cues | Server toss and shoulder, setter body, hitter arm, own block | Server and hitter reads solid; elite setters still unreadable |
| **S6** | Map under fatigue | The map holds in the 3rd set and on day 5 | Holds a full set at the end of camp (Fukurodani final) |

### 3.2 The 12 drills (Sho's names)

Each: name, what it is, where it comes from, Sho's anime reason for the name, substage, chapter, and how the author shows progress.

**1. Tosen** (Bleach: Tosen Kaname, the blind captain who fights by sound)
- **What:** eyes shut, Sho calls what he hears: which arm a pass comes off, clean or shanked; later, how many approach footfalls (2 hitters or 3), the "pop" of a set (clean vs doubled), and a serve's contact sound (deep or short).
- **Source:** ch 27-28 sound reads; Sors' auditory anticipation research.
- **Substage:** S4.
- **Chapters:** ch 29 to 30 (mornings with Tanaka); ch 35 (Ikkei at the door); ch 32 to 33 **fails** in the multi-court gym (setback 4); ch 40 a single sound read wins a point (he hears the set is doubled/tight before he sees it).
- **Marker:** correct calls out of 10. Tanaka keeps score on the wall in chalk. Week 1: arm 7/10. Week 2: arm 9/10, clean/shank 8/10. Camp gym: drops to 4/10. Final: only used for 1 cue (set contact), but that 1 is reliable.

**2. Byakugan Snapshot** (Naruto: the Hyuga's near-360 vision)
- **What:** 1 head turn, about half a second; turn back; place every player he saw. Home: Mio arranges 12 pudding cups and soy sauce bottles on the kitchen table, he looks once, she throws a dish towel over them, he places 12 coins on a drawn court. Gym: the 2nd-year benchers stand anywhere on the far court; Yachi's magnet board from ch 31.
- **Source:** multiple object tracking (capacity falls as targets rise); Jordet's snapshot idea. Note the honest limit: table games don't transfer by themselves ([ETS no-transfer study](https://pure.etsmtl.ca/en/publications/no-transfer-of-3d-multiple-object-tracking-training-on-game-perfo/)), which is why the court version matters more. Sho can know this (Satoshi read papers): "The table's for counting. The court's for learning."
- **Substage:** S2.
- **Chapters:** ch 29 (Mio invents the pudding edition after watching Saturday practice); ch 31 (Yachi's board); camp nights.
- **Marker:** players held. Buildup: 4. 1st Tokyo trip: 6. End of camp: 8 (only when fresh). Day 4 night: 5.

**3. Traffic Light** (his name for the Brock string because Mio's beads are red, yellow, green)
- **What:** the doctor's home exercise. 5 minutes, morning and night. Dad holds the far end.
- **Source:** clinic maintenance exercise ([U. Michigan handout](https://www.med.umich.edu/1libr/PMR/VisionExercises/BrockString.pdf)); CITT says home-only therapy is modest, so it's maintenance, not a cure.
- **Substage:** none; it's the health line under everything. It's how the author shows the *eye's* body.
- **Chapters:** ch 29 (introduced; 1 scene, Dad at the far end, "Mm."); ch 35 (pre-camp check); ch 38 (he does it in the camp corridor after the hand-up and the red bead won't go to 1, then does, 2 minutes later).
- **Marker:** where the near bead doubles. The doctor's pen-to-nose at clinic visits ("closer than last time"). Keep numbers out of the prose or vague.

**4. Mantra** (One Piece: the Skypiea word for Observation Haki before it had a name. Sho, who calls the eye "Observation Haki", calls the eyes-shut version by its older name)
- **What:** live temporal occlusion. Sho watches the setter (or hitter) and shuts his eyes **at a cue** (setter's hand contact; later, the hitter's arm at the top of the swing), then calls where it's going before he opens them. Suga sets; Sho calls "4", "back", "dump", "quick".
- **Source:** occlusion paradigms ([Bordini et al.](https://revistas.usp.br/rbefe/en/article/view/97346)); strobe training ([Duke 2012](https://today.duke.edu/2012/07/appelbaumstrobe)) as the expensive version Satoshi knew the V.League tried. Sho's version costs nothing and *rests* his eyes on every rep.
- **Substage:** S5 (setter and hitter cues), S1 (it forces him to take the information early).
- **Chapters:** ch 30 to 31 (Wednesdays with Suga, after English); ch 36 (with Akaashi, as Poker).
- **Marker:** calls right out of 10. Suga: 5/10 → 8/10 in 3 Wednesdays. Suga, the last Wednesday before camp, disguises a back set (ball overhead) and Sho calls it wrong 4 times in a row. Suga grins: he's getting something too.

**5. Filler Look** (anime filler: the episodes where nothing can happen to the main plot. "Look during filler. Never during the arc.")
- **What:** timed scanning. The windows when the ball can't hurt you: the opponent's pass in the air on their side, his own team's set in the air (when he's not the hitter), the ball going away from him. Never while a ball is coming at him. 3 checks max, at those windows, and *not always at the same beat*.
- **Source:** Jordet: timing over frequency; trainable.
- **Substage:** S1.
- **Chapters:** ch 30 (Sho explains it to Ukai as "only look when it's boring"); ch 31 (1st 6v6, Takeda's sheet); ch 33 **Kenma breaks it** (setback 6); ch 39 to 40 the varied version works.
- **Marker:** Takeda's tally: checks per rally, and how many fell in a window. Ch 14 baseline: 12 in 5 seconds, freeze. Ch 15 to arc 2: 2 checks max (too few). Ch 31: 5 per rally, half in windows. Final: 3 to 4, 80%+ in windows, 0 freezes.

**6. Jan-ken** (rock, paper, scissors)
- **What:** coach-on-box defense read. Rock = full back swing, hard hit. Scissors = bent elbow, roll or cut. Paper = straight arm, tip or soft. Sho calls it **before contact**, then digs. Ukai on the box in Miyagi; Bokuto at camp.
- **Source:** "back, bent, straight" ([Art of Coaching Volleyball](https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/arm-swing-reading-drill-back-bent-straight/)); Hebert's arm-lift advice ([Coaches Insider](https://coachesinsider.com/volleyball/teaching-players-how-to-read-article/)).
- **Substage:** S5.
- **Chapters:** ch 30 to 31 (Ukai, soft); ch 36 to 39 (Bokuto: 4/10, because Bokuto changes his mind in the air; Sho laughs out loud, Bokuto thinks it's a compliment).
- **Marker:** calls right before contact out of 10. Ukai: 6 → 9. Bokuto: 4, and that's the point (leads to drill 12).

**7. Iai Read** (Gintama and samurai films: the draw. "Everything is decided before the blade leaves the sheath.")
- **What:** serve receive. Read the server's toss and shoulder in the toss (float or topspin, short or deep, which seam), then the contact sound for length, then 1 jump of the eyes to where it will drop, then 1 long still look as it arrives (Yaku's "Look where it lands. Then look at it again.").
- **Source:** expert receivers fixate shoulder and arm in the toss; visual pivots ahead of the ball; quiet eye; Sors on serve-length sound.
- **Substage:** S5, S4, S3.
- **Chapters:** ch 35 (Asahi's raw jump serve, 20 a day); ch 36 to 37 (Yaku, mornings, with Lev); ch 40 to 41 the payoff: he passes Bokuto's jump serve clean, with no eye. (In ch 22 he only passed Oikawa's serve by forcing the eye.)
- **Marker:** clean passes (A or B quality) out of 10 vs jump serves. Asahi's: 3/10 (many of Asahi's are out; count only the ones in). Yaku's drill at camp: Yaku counts out loud. By the end: 7/10 vs Fukurodani's servers. Reception D → C on the arc card.

**8. Minimap** (Kenma's word, ch 33 or 36; before that Sho calls it "the Fridge")
- **What:** anchor gaze. Eyes stay on 1 point, and he reads things at the edges. Home: he stares at Mio's magnet on August on the fridge from across the kitchen (low near-focus load); Mio holds up fingers at the edge of his vision, or moves the salt shaker; he calls it without moving his eyes. Gym: he anchors on the net between the setter and the strong-side hitter and calls which hitter starts their approach 1st.
- **Source:** gaze anchor and visual pivot (Vater et al.); light-board peripheral training (UC Bearcats).
- **Substage:** S3.
- **Chapters:** ch 29 to 30 (the Fridge; Mio cheats by moving her fingers *behind* him and Sho says "That's not peripheral, that's the back of my head"); ch 33 or 36 Kenma's line gives it its real name; ch 39 (3v3 owls vs cats) it's how he plays.
- **Marker:** the number of moving things he can track at the edges off 1 anchor: 1 hitter → 2 hitters → 2 hitters and the setter's feet.

**9. Forecast** (One Piece: Nami, the navigator who reads the weather before the storm comes)
- **What:** reading the opponent's first contact. When their pass is tight to the net, the quick is on; off the net, the quick is gone and the outside is coming. As a back-row player he calls the *pass*, out loud, for his blockers: "Good!" / "Off!" / "Over!". He does **not** call the set.
- **Source:** pass quality gates the quick ([Art of Coaching](https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/slower-middle-attack/); [Smarter Volley](https://smartervolley.substack.com/p/sideout-by-pass-quality-pt2)).
- **Substage:** the seed of the Predictive map (Lv2) and of ch 22's calls, this time sustainable.
- **Chapters:** ch 35 (6v6, Miyagi; Tsukishima objects, setback 7, resolved: Sho calls only the pass, Tsukishima reads the set himself); ch 40 to 41 used in the final.
- **Marker:** how often his call matches what the setter then does. And whether the blockers use it. Tsukishima using one of Sho's calls without comment in ch 40 is the real marker.

**10. Poker** (no anime; a game Satoshi played with the V.League staff on away trips)
- **What:** reading setters. Akaashi sets 20; Sho calls each before the ball leaves Akaashi's hands; Akaashi tries to hide everything.
- **Source:** setter tells and disguise; front and back sets look alike in good setters.
- **Substage:** S5, and an insight that moves S1.
- **Chapters:** ch 36. Sho goes 6/20, a coin toss. Then Akaashi, asked how he hides it, says he doesn't hide anything, he just doesn't decide till late. Sho writes Ukai's line from ch 25 under it: *Not at him. At them.* He switches to watching the hitters' approaches (whoever's approach is "real" is the set), and gets 13/20. Kenma refuses to play Poker. Once, in the 3v3 (ch 39), Kenma plays it anyway, for 1 rally, and wins.
- **Marker:** calls right out of 20, and the switch from setter-reading to hitter-reading.

**11. Bankai Hour** (Bleach. Sho already calls a tight 3rd set "Bankai time")
- **What:** fatigue test. After the last penalty laps of the day, while soaked, he runs 10 Byakugan snapshots and 10 Jan-ken calls (from whoever is around; Lev volunteers, then Hinata). He logs morning vs night.
- **Source:** decision-making under mental and physical fatigue (faster, less accurate).
- **Substage:** S6.
- **Chapters:** ch 36 to 38. Day 1 night: 7 snapshot players, 8/10 Jan-ken. Day 4 night: 5 players, 3/10, and he notices he's calling *before* the swing even starts. This is the data that tells him ch 38's burning is coming, and he writes it down and doesn't act on it. That's the shadow (notebook as hiding place) coming back.
- **Marker:** morning-night gap. By the last day it narrows (8 morning, 7 night) because the timed, anchored, sound-assisted map costs less.

**12. Shadow Possession** (Naruto: Shikamaru's jutsu. "If I stand in their shadow, the ball has to come to me.")
- **What:** defensive positioning off his own block. Stand where the block's hands leave the hole, read the block from behind, and let the block tell you where the hitter can hit. For Bokuto (unreadable by arm), this is the answer.
- **Source:** standard block-defense relationship; Kuroo as the teacher.
- **Substage:** S2/S5 joined: his own team is part of the map, not only the opponents.
- **Chapters:** ch 36 (Kuroo in the 3rd gym); ch 39 (3v3, digging Bokuto behind Kuroo); ch 40 to 41 (behind Tsukishima and Hinata).
- **Marker:** Bokuto spikes dug in the 3rd gym per night: 1, 3, 6. Bokuto, offended: "AGAIN?"

### 3.3 The habit under all of it: the Bingo Book (Naruto: the ninja bounty book)

The notebook's back section becomes a ledger [design]. After every set, Sho logs his reads: **source** (map, sound, eye, guess) and **right/wrong**. He also logs every burning: the time, the rally count, what he was doing. It's the analyst's way to tell what the eye gave him from what he earned, and it's where Ukai can check his honesty.

- The notebook has **31 pages left** at the end of ch 28. Arc 3 eats them. Somewhere around camp day 3 he runs out and has to buy a new notebook at a konbini near Shinzen, and he copies the 1st page by hand: *Hand up at the burning. / Tell Coach 1st. / Build the map so I don't need the eye. / Listen. / Play all 3 sets.* That's a quiet chapter beat, no speech.
- The ledger also shows the shadow: on day 4 he logs the warning numbers and tells no one. Setback 8.

### 3.4 What training does and doesn't do to the eye (rules)

- Training never strengthens the Emperor's Eye in Arc 3. No new range, no command. It stays partial: ~0.2 s, uninvited.
- Training lowers how often it fires, because the eye comes when the map has a hole.
- Training makes him notice the burning sooner (the Bingo Book numbers, the Traffic Light bead), so hand-up becomes a skill.
- Mantra and Tosen are rest built into reps. Sho realizes on the page that shutting his eyes is training, not quitting. That's the line his soul needs: Satoshi kept going with his eyes open on the monitor; Sho trains with them shut.

## 5. Setbacks and failures that create drama

In rough order. Each comes from what Sho wants and costs something.

1. **The dinner table hand-up (buildup week 2, ch 29 or 30).** He stacks home drills: Traffic Light, the Fridge, pudding Byakugan, English homework, under the kitchen light. He wants 40 minutes; the doctor said short sessions. At dinner his eyes burn. He feels it, keeps eating, then puts his right hand up flat at shoulder height at the dinner table. Mom stops serving. Dad lowers the paper. Mio: "Does that count?" Sho: "It counts." Mio gets no puddings because he told. It's half funny, and it's the rehearsal for ch 38. Mom halves the drills without a word by taking the coins away.
2. **Exam week drops the threshold (ch 30).** Near work doubles. The 1st full 6v6 on the Monday after exams, the burning comes after 8 rallies. He's angry at a textbook. He realizes his eyes are 1 budget for school and volleyball, and he can't borrow.
3. **The pendulum (ch 31).** First 6v6 back: scared of the burning, he barely looks. Gets caught by a simple back set to the 2nd-year side. Kageyama: "You stopped looking at all." Too much looking froze him at Nekoma; too little now. Takeda's sheet shows 1 check per rally. The Filler Look drill comes out of this.
4. **Tosen goes deaf (ch 32 to 33).** The Tokyo gym has 3 courts going, whistles, Bokuto yelling, shoes squeaking from everywhere. His sound reads go from 9/10 to coin flips. He'd built a whole layer on a quiet 5 a.m. gym. Lesson: sound is a layer, never the base.
5. **No tape (ch 30 to 32).** Takeda gets Nekoma's spring tournament video. The screen rule says 1 hour, and Sho has exam reading. He asks Ennoshita to watch it and describe it out loud while Sho listens with his eyes shut. Ennoshita is a good describer, better than he thinks. A small Ennoshita scene with his own soul (the 2nd year who once walked away).
6. **Kenma reads his rhythm (ch 33).** Kenma remembers ch 14. Now Sho checks only in the windows, so Kenma makes fake windows: he slows the set, Sho looks, Kenma dumps into the spot. Sho's own scanning has become a tell. "You look at everybody. It's tiring to watch." Fix: vary the beat, and use the Minimap anchor so he doesn't need to turn at all. The eye fires once here, small, he steps toward the dump late, no real cost; he tells Ukai at the timeout and Mio that night (1st test of the terms, small).
7. **Tsukishima: "Don't call it for me." (ch 35).** Sho's Forecast calls from the back row land on Tsukishima, who's trying to learn to read on his own. Tsukishima: "If I'm going to be wrong, I'd rather be wrong myself." Sho hears it as fair. They split it: Sho calls only the pass, never the set. This is healthy rivalry and leaves Tsukishima's moonrise arc untouched. In ch 40 Tsukishima uses an "Off!" call with no comment.
8. **The Bingo Book lies by omission (camp day 4, ch 37 to 38).** The ledger shows his night numbers collapsing (5 players, 3/10 Jan-ken, calls before the swing). He writes them and tells nobody. That's the shadow from the Soul sheet: the notebook as hiding place, exactly what Satoshi did with the knee. Ukai doesn't know because Takeda's sheet only counts checks, not accuracy.
9. **Ch 38, Hand up.** A practice match the next morning, Karasuno winning for once (vs Ubugawa or Shinzen, so it doesn't touch canon results with Nekoma or Fukurodani). The map breaks late in the set, the eye fires into the hole, the burning starts on the same rally. Hinata and Kageyama are mid-fight territory; the team needs points; they're *winning*, which is exactly the case Ukai named ("especially if we're winning"). He puts his hand up. Ukai pulls him, no question. Tanaka goes on and keeps the lead (Tanaka's arc gets a small win). Sho sits with a wet towel over his eyes, listening (Tosen, on the bench: he can still play the match by ear in his head). Then he tells Ukai about the 4 nights of numbers. Ukai: furious in 2 lines, then decides. That night he calls Mio and tells her all of it. The Traffic Light red bead won't go to 1, then does.
10. **The rest set (ch 38 to 39).** Ukai's rule costs him the next set too, and it's a set vs Nekoma. He watches Yaku from the bench, and it's the same room as the monitor (the soul wound). He doesn't argue. This is where the arc's "hand up" becomes a cost he chooses, not a rule he obeys.
11. **Bokuto can't be read (ch 36 to 39).** Jan-ken fails on him, and Sho laughs. He learns to read Bokuto through the block (Shadow Possession). Satoshi's pride as an analyst takes a small hit: some players have no tells because they haven't decided yet.
12. **Akaashi has no tells (ch 36).** Poker 6/20. The analyst who built a career on tells meets a setter who decides late. He moves to the hitters. A loss that turns into a method.
13. **The final, and the limit (ch 40 to 41).** He plays the whole Fukurodani match on the map: Iai Read passes Bokuto's jump serve clean, Shadow Possession digs Bokuto behind Tsukishima's block, Forecast calls, no eye. The new quick lands, then fails (Hinata and Kageyama's, protected); Fukurodani wins as in canon. Sho's own limit: in the last set (or late in the final set if it's 2), the snapshot drops to 5, his checks start landing outside windows, and he puts his hand up **before** the burning even starts, because the numbers say it's coming. That's the earned version: Bingo Book used honestly. Whether that counts as "playing all 3 sets" is the question he takes home to Miyagi.

## 7. Guardrails for the author

- Every drill gets Ukai's yes on the page at least once. Sho proposes; Ukai decides.
- The eye stays secret. Drills are "reading" and "doctor's exercises" to everyone. Ikkei, Kenma and Kuroo can suspect; nobody names it.
- No drill becomes a superpower. Table drills don't transfer by themselves (say so through Satoshi); court drills do, slowly.
- Keep analyst words in Sho's head. Out loud: "Can I look once?" not "visual pivot".
- Each drill scene should be built around a conversation (user's top priority), not a training montage. One drill per scene; the numbers come out of someone's mouth (Tanaka, Yaku, Takeda, Mio).
- Protected moments untouched: Hinata/Kageyama's quick and fight, Hinata's Ikkei lessons, Asahi's jump serve, Tsukishima's moonrise and Kuroo's coaching of him, Tanaka's arc, Yamaguchi's float.
