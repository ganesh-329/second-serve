## 1. The decisions, up front

1. **Ch 29 "Invitation" stays the end of Arc 2, written as Arc 3's hinge.** Arc 2's question ("was it the knee again?") is answered by ch 28's notebook page and Ukai's "till January"; ch 29 closes the family thread (Mio at practice) and the stat ledger (status card), the way ch 15 closed Arc 1. But its hook IS Arc 3's question, so it is specced here in full. Arc 3 proper = **ch 30 to 45, 16 chapters** (17 counting the bridge).
2. **The arc's answer to "how does he use his eyes as a weapon":** in Arc 3 his eyes start as a *liability* (Tokyo setters read him, and the eye itself has a visible tell) and end as a *weapon that is his own*: a map built from his ears and from elimination frees his eyes from gathering information, so his eyes can **send** information, and he learns to make them lie. Emperor's Eye stays innate, involuntary and rare; Arc 3 gives it a doctrine, not a level: *the eye tells you what a body will do; the map tells you what the ball can do. When they disagree, trust the map. When they agree, go.*
3. **Camp results: canon shape stays, 1 small canon result flips, 2 losses get closer because of Sho.** Karasuno still loses almost everything, still runs flying falls, never beats Nekoma, and loses the final to Fukurodani 25-23 on Bokuto's point. The 1 flip: canon's 23-25 loss to Shinzen (ch 91) becomes a 25-23 Karasuno win on the last day. Justification in section 5.
4. **"Play all 3 sets" becomes literal on the last day.** Camp games are single sets, so the last day is built as 3 games (Nekoma, Shinzen, Fukurodani). Sho is on court in all 3 and is standing at the end of the 3rd. That is the arc's finish line.
5. **2 surprise POVs: ch 33 Kenma, ch 38 Ukai Keishin.** 5 chapters apart. Kenma shows the reader exactly how readable Sho is (the early wound, from outside). Ukai shows the hand going up from the man who made the rule and has to pull a kid while winning (the middle turn, from outside), and Sho's inside of it arrives the next chapter through what he tells Mio.
6. **Eye budget for the whole arc: 7 fires, 2 burnings, 2 hands up, 0 pressure, 0 depth loss, 0 forcing.** The discipline is the story. (Ledger in section 6.)
7. **Status cards:** ch 29 card: Reception D to C (earned across Arc 2). Ch 45 card: Mentality B to A (2 hands up while winning, chose the map over the eye under pressure, finished all 3). Meta Vision stays **Scanning**, Emperor's Eye stays **Partial**. The map shows itself top-down for 1 rally in the final as a flicker, with no rank change; **Field map Lv1 debuts at Ougiminami** per the bible.

**Proposed rule clarification (needs the user's yes): the 1-free rule.** A single fire leaves a dry sting that is gone by the next serve. A 2nd fire in the same game brings the burning, and the burning means HAND, every time. This fits the record (single fires vs Nekoma and Date Tech had "no noted cost"; the 3v3 double fire cost depth) and it makes the eye a resource Sho has to think about without letting him control it. He also invents a 2nd signal in ch 32 with Ukai: **1 finger** at the dead ball = "it came once, no burning". Flat hand = burning, out.

**The eye's limit, stated once and used twice:** the ghost shows what a body will do, never where the ball goes. A decoy who swings for real fools it (ch 33, Kenma's discovery from outside; ch 41, Sho beats it by trusting the map).

## 8. Showcase set pieces for the arc's peak, ranked

**1. "The lie" (vs Nekoma, last day, ch 41). Recommended.**
Sho fakes the stillness that Kenma learned to read in ch 33, sends Kenma's sets to the hitter his ears and map already picked, digs 2, forces Nekomata's timeout, then trusts the map over the eye at 24-24. Kenma adjusts and lies back to win 26-24.
Why it's 1st: it turns the arc's wound (being read) into his weapon; the weapon is literally his eyes, used as bait; it uses the eye's own tell, so it is only possible for Sho; it is built from things he made himself (the ear from ch 27, Yaku's line, Akaashi's warning, his own tape); it touches no protected moment; it costs him (overload) and keeps every canon result; Kenma stays brilliant.

**2. "Four into one" (vs Shinzen's synchronized attack, ch 42).**
EAR and ELIM cut 4 hitters to 2; a natural fire on the setter agrees with the map and cuts 2 to 1; burning, hand up at 24-23; the team closes 25-23 without him.
Strength: a visible win that only Sho's kit explains, plus the hand-up paying off. Weakness: the decisive read is the innate eye, so it's less "his own" than the lie, and it flips a canon result. If chosen as the peak, put it after the Nekoma game and make the Nekoma game a plain loss where the lie fails.

**3. "Open book" (vs Bokuto, mid-camp or in the final).**
The eye fires most on Bokuto because his body hides nothing; Sho stacks EAR and 1 natural fire to dig Bokuto 3 times; Akaashi has to talk Bokuto back up.
Strength: big, funny, physical, and Bokuto would love it. Weakness: it leans on a canon character's moods as the payoff (prop risk), it sits right on top of the new quick if placed in the final, and it pushes Sho toward using the eye more, against the whole arc's discipline. Best used in small form, as it is in ch 32 and ch 38.
