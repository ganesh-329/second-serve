## Chapter 7: Blue castle

The meat buns at Sakanoshita cost 120 yen, and Sho had been trying to get them for 100 since last week.

"No," said the man at the register.

He was maybe 26, blond, with a headband pushing his hair back and an unlit cigarette behind his ear. The shop was at the bottom of the hill below the school, and the whole club stopped there after practice, and he rang all of them up with the same face.

"I'm a loyal customer," Sho said. "I've been here 4 times."

"So you've paid full price 4 times. You'll manage a 5th."

"What if I buy 2?"

"Then it's 240." The man held out his hand.

Sho paid. The man took a bun out of the steamer with the tongs, and put it back, and took out the 1 next to it, which was bigger, and dropped it in a paper sleeve.

"Thank you very much," said Sho.

"It's 120, kid. Every time."

Outside, the club was gathered round the bench in front of the shop, eating, with their bags in a heap against the vending machine. It was Monday, a little after 6, and the light was going orange on the hill. Daichi stood in front of them with a sheet of paper.

"Tomorrow's lineup," he said. "Kageyama sets. Hinata and Tsukishima in the middle."

Hinata's head came up.

"Hinata, you're the decoy."

Hinata's head went down again. "Decoy? That's the 1 who doesn't hit."

"That's the 1 everybody blocks," said Kageyama, with his mouth full. "If 2 of them jump with you, Tanaka-san's got 1. If 3 jump with you, he's got nobody. Every point he scores like that is yours. The best decoy on a court is the most dangerous player on it."

Hinata looked at him. His head came up again, slowly, a little at a time.

"Wings," Daichi went on. "Tanaka on the right. Me." He stopped for about as long as it took to turn the paper over. "Kiyose on the left."

Sho stopped chewing.

He knew where everybody was sitting. Sugawara at the end of the bench. Ennoshita next to him, with a bun in its sleeve that he hadn't started on.

"Tanaka and I take most of the receive," said Daichi. "Kiyose, you get the strip along your sideline and nothing else. If it's anywhere near me, it's mine. Understood?"

"Yes, Captain."

Then he looked at Ennoshita.

Ennoshita was already looking at him. He had a face that always seemed half asleep, and he was smiling a little.

"Left wing, huh," Ennoshita said. "Warm up your shoulders properly. Seijoh serves hard."

"Ennoshita-san, I..."

"Mm." Ennoshita took the 1st bite of his bun. "Eat. It gets cold."

He turned and asked Narita something about the English homework. Sho sat there with his own bun going cold in his hand and half a sentence still in his mouth.

---

The club went down the road in 2s and 3s. Sugawara dropped back beside Sho for a few steps at the bridge.

"Chikara's tougher than he looks," Sugawara said, low. Then he sped up and went to steal a piece of Daichi's bun.

Sho walked behind them to the corner, where the road split and the 3rd years went left. He'd been doing a sum since the bench, and he didn't like how it came out.

"Captain," he said. "Could I ask something?"

Daichi stopped. Sugawara looked at the 2 of them, and at the sky, and found that he needed to retie his shoe a little way up the road.

"Ennoshita-san's better than me," Sho said. "At receiving. At where to stand. At everything except 1 thing. He's been here a year. I've been here a week."

"That's right."

"So why..."

"Because of the 1 thing." Daichi put his paper away in his jacket. He didn't seem annoyed to be asked, and he didn't seem surprised. "Seijoh's blockers are tall. Tanaka can't carry the left and the right by himself, and I can't hit over a block like theirs. On Saturday you hit over Tanaka and through Hinata, on your 6th day, off Yamaguchi's sets. I want to know what you do off Kageyama's." He looked at Sho steadily. "It's a practice match. That's what they're for. I'm finding out."

"And if I'm bad?"

"Then Ennoshita goes in for you in the 2nd set, and I've found out." Daichi smiled, the ordinary 1. "He knows that. I told him before I told you."

Sho bowed. He didn't know what else to do with it.

"Kiyose."

"Yes."

"Don't play like you owe somebody. You'll swing tight." Daichi turned up the left-hand road. "Play like you did when you told me it was 4."

---

Hinata didn't sleep on Monday night. Sho knew, because Hinata told everybody at the bus on Tuesday afternoon, in detail, with gray under his eyes.

"My sister said I was being loud. I wasn't even talking! I was just lying there!"

The bus was a rented school minibus that smelled of old seats and air freshener. Takeda-sensei sat at the front with a notebook open on his knee, writing in it every few minutes. Daichi stood at the door and counted heads twice. The 3rd years took the back. Kiyoko sat alone on the 2nd row with the team bag on the seat beside her. Sho ended up across the aisle from Hinata and Tanaka.

Hinata had an onigiri in his lap. He was holding it in both hands and hadn't unwrapped it.

"Are you scared?" Hinata said, across the aisle.

Sho thought about it. "A bit."

"You don't look scared."

"I've been waiting a long time for a match."

"It's been a WEEK."

"It felt longer," said Sho.

Hinata looked at him as if that were a strange thing to say, and nodded hard, as if it were also true. "I waited 3 years," he said. "For a team. A real 1, with a setter, and people who..." The bus went over the railway crossing. He stopped. His face went from white to a very particular green.

Sho leaned across and took the onigiri out of his hands before it could go anywhere, and put it in his own jacket pocket.

There was nothing to do about the rest of it. It went into Tanaka's lap.

The bus was silent. Then Tanaka said, very calmly, "Hinata."

"I'm sorry I'm sorry I'm so sorry I'm..."

"Hinata. It's fine. I've got it. Sit still."

Sho already had his bag open. He took out a folded pair of practice shorts and a T-shirt and held them across the aisle.

Tanaka stared at them. "You carry spares?"

"2 sets. It's an away game."

Tanaka took them. He held them a second, looking at Sho, and then he reached across the aisle and hit him on the shoulder, much too hard, and went to change behind the back seat while Sugawara rubbed Hinata's back and Daichi opened every window on the bus.

At the front, Takeda wrote something down.

---

Aoba Johsai looked like money.

The gym was twice the size of Karasuno's and a lot newer, with tall windows and a floor that shone, and a banner along the far wall in white and pale green. 4 courts' worth of space, with 1 net up. The team lined up inside the door and bowed. "Onegaishimasu!"

On the way to the changing rooms, 2 Seijoh players passed them in the corridor, talking, and didn't lower their voices.

"...Karasuno? Aren't they the ones who used to be good?"

"They've got the King now. Should be fun to watch him order them about."

The 1 who'd said *King* was a 1st year, tall, with dark hair that stuck straight up. Tanaka turned round. His face did its thing, the eyebrows and the jaw. Kageyama had stopped walking. Tsukishima said, in his politest voice, "Please go easy on us. We're only crows," and Yamaguchi stood behind his shoulder and tried to look as if he'd said it too.

"Tanaka," said Daichi.

"Sorry about that," Tanaka said to the 2 of them, with all his teeth. It was the least sorry thing Sho had ever heard.

The tall 1st year looked past all of them, at Kageyama.

"I'm looking forward to it," he said. "Seeing what kind of team lets the King rule it."

"Yeah," said Kageyama, and kept walking.

Sho was watching the tall boy when he turned away. He'd expected him to be pleased with himself. His mouth had gone flat, and his jaw moved once, as if he'd bitten something sour.

*He isn't enjoying it,* Sho thought. *So why say it?*

---

Hinata spent most of the warm-up in the bathroom.

He came back from his 3rd trip looking worse. He'd met the tall boy at the sinks. The boy's name was Kindaichi, he'd been Kageyama's teammate at Kitagawa Daiichi, and he'd told Hinata that Kageyama saw his hitters as pieces on a board, and that Hinata would find that out. Tanaka had walked in on the end of it and said the match would do the talking.

"He's wrong," Hinata said. He was folding and unfolding the hem of his shirt. "Right? Kiyose. He's wrong."

"You hit his set with your eyes shut," said Sho. "On Saturday."

Hinata looked at his own right palm. That seemed to help a little.

Then Daichi came over and put a hand on Hinata's shoulder and told him, kindly, that it was only a practice match and nobody expected anything, and Hinata heard *nobody expects anything* and went a shade paler. And then Kiyoko, on her way past with the water bottles, stopped. She tapped Hinata on the other shoulder with 2 fingers.

"We're counting on you," she said, and went on with the bottles.

Hinata stopped moving altogether. His eyes had gone like a fish's. Tanaka, 4 meters away, had frozen too, staring at the shoulder Kiyoko had touched.

"Shimizu," said Daichi, tiredly. "That was the opposite."

When they lined up at the net to bow, Sho was across from Kindaichi.

Kindaichi looked him up and down. Tall, new, a plain practice bib with a 9 on it that had been Ennoshita's on Friday. Nobody he knew. His eyes went on along the line to Kageyama and stayed there. Next to him a pale boy with half-closed eyes glanced at Sho, and away again, as if Sho had been interesting for about as long as a blink.

"Onegaishimasu!"

They went to their places. Sho looked back once at the bench.

Sugawara and Ennoshita sat side by side in their jackets with their hands on their knees. Each of them had his eyes on the court. Ennoshita had a towel folded on his lap, and a bottle of water at his feet, and was sitting the way a person sits who is going to watch every point.

*Don't play like you owe somebody.*

Sho turned round to face the net.

---

Seijoh served 1st, and the 1st rally was over in 3 seconds.

The serve was a hard float to the middle of the court, Daichi's ball, called. Daichi had his arms out and his feet set. Hinata came across in front of him from the right, eyes on the ball and nothing else, and got 1 arm to it just as Daichi did, and it went off both of them sideways into the scorer's table.

0-1.

"Sorry! Sorry, Daichi-san!"

"It's fine. Call it next time. Or let me."

Hinata didn't call the next 1 either. He stood rooted and watched it land a meter from his foot. 0-2.

On the 3rd, Daichi passed it clean, and Kageyama set Tanaka on the right, high. Tanaka came in shouting. The tall middle, Kindaichi, slid along the net with his arms already up and stuffed it straight down at Tanaka's feet.

0-3.

On the far side of the net Kindaichi landed and looked at Kageyama. He didn't say anything. He just looked, with his mouth flat.

Kageyama's set to Hinata at 1-4 was the fast 1, the Saturday 1. Hinata jumped a full beat late with his eyes wide open, staring at the blockers, and the ball went past his ear.

"WHAT ARE YOU LOOKING AT?" said Kageyama.

"THEM! THEY'RE HUGE!"

Sho's own trouble was quieter.

His 1st set came at 2-6. He was in the front row on the left, and Daichi's pass was good, and Kageyama's hands went up and the ball came out to him. He'd spent 3 days learning to wait: watch the ball climb, watch it stop, count 1, go. That worked on Yamaguchi's rainbows. Kageyama's set to the left came flat and fast, and by the time the count in Sho's head said *go*, it was already dropping past the place he should have hit it.

He reached it late and low and hit it straight into Kindaichi's hands. It came back down on his own toes.

2-7.

"Mine," Sho said, before Kageyama could open his mouth. "Sorry. I was late."

Kageyama shut it again. He looked like somebody who'd had a shout ready and nowhere to put it.

"Your sets don't stop at the top," Sho said. "Yamaguchi's did. I was waiting for it to stop."

"Why would it stop?"

"His did."

"His are bad." Kageyama said it as a plain fact. He frowned, working at something. "It's the same set every time. Same height, same speed. If you go on my hands, it'll be there. Go when it leaves my hands."

"When it leaves your hands."

"That's what I said."

It was. Sho went back to his spot and said it to himself twice.

---

On the next dead ball he walked over again. Hinata had just jumped for a ball that was Tsukishima's, and Tsukishima was telling him so, at length, and there was time.

"Kageyama-kun. Their tall middle. Does he watch you, or the hitter?"

"Me." Kageyama answered before the question had finished. "Kindaichi watches the setter. He always did. At Kitagawa Daiichi, in practice, he'd stand across from me in 6 on 6 and watch my hands, every day, for 3 years. He knows which way my wrists turn before I do. If I show him the direction early, he's there. He was the best blocker we had. He jumped for everything, he never..."

He stopped.

His mouth stayed a little open. It was more words in a row than Sho had ever heard from him, and they'd run straight up to something and halted at the edge of it. *He jumped for everything.* And then, 1 day, on a ball Kageyama had set, he hadn't.

Kageyama looked across the net. Kindaichi was at the far post, drinking, with his back to them.

"He watches me," Kageyama said, flatly. "That's all."

"Okay," said Sho. "Does anybody watch me?"

Kageyama looked at him. "Nobody knows who you are."

"Good."

He went back to the left side. The whistle went.

It didn't save the set. Seijoh's servers had found the strip along his sideline by the 3rd rotation, and they put 4 serves into it. He passed 2. 1 went into the bleachers. 1 he thought was Daichi's and Daichi thought was his, and it dropped between them, and Daichi said "Mine, next time, all of them," in a level voice and moved half a meter closer to him.

He went on Kageyama's hands. At 6-15 he was early, and swung at air. At 8-17 he was nearly right, and hit it cross, hard, a real swing, and Seijoh's libero took 1 step and dug it as if it had been tossed to him underhand. It came back over. They lost that rally too.

That was the other thing he was learning, on the left side of a full-sized court, across from a top 4 school. Their number 4 was a 3rd year with spiky dark hair and thick arms, and he hit from the same side as Sho, across the net. He'd scored 6 already. He went up the same way every time, no hurry in it, like somebody who'd been given the ball at the end of every rally of his life and had never once minded. When he hit it, it stayed hit. Daichi got his arms to 1 and it took him backward off his feet.

Satoshi had hit like that at 20. Sho watched every 1 of them from under the net, each time, with his hands open at his sides.

*That. I want that back.*

---

13-24.

Karasuno had just got the serve back on a Tanaka spike off the block, and the rotation brought Hinata to the end line.

Sho was at the front right now, with his back to him. He turned his head to look. Hinata was holding the ball in both hands, and his hands were shaking, and so were his knees. His eyes were on the other side of the net and didn't seem to be seeing it. Kageyama stood in the middle of the back row, 4 meters in front of him, hands on his knees, waiting.

The whistle went. Hinata threw the ball up before it had finished, much too low, and swung at it with his eyes half shut.

Sho saw the toss go up crooked and knew where the swing would send it. He had time to wince.

The ball hit Kageyama square in the back of the head.

It made a sound like somebody knocking on a door.

The gym went silent. The ball rolled off toward the sideline. Kageyama didn't move. Hinata's face went the color of paper. On the Seijoh side, Kindaichi had both hands over his mouth, and the pale boy beside him had his eyes fully open for the 1st time all day.

Then Tanaka made a strangled noise and folded in half, and Tsukishima turned his back to the court and put his face in the crook of his elbow, and his shoulders shook.

13-25.

Kageyama turned round very slowly.

Sho kept his own face still. It took some doing.

Kageyama walked back to Hinata. Hinata was backing away with his hands up, saying something that was mostly vowels. Kageyama stopped in front of him. He reached up and slapped the back of his own head, hard, twice, right where the ball had hit.

"Is there anything," he said, "scarier than what you just did?"

Hinata stared at him. Sho watched him actually think about it.

"...No?"

"Then there's nothing left to be scared of. You already did the worst thing. So play normally." Kageyama's voice went up. "OR I'LL HIT YOU WITH 1!"

"YES!"

---

They had 3 minutes. Tanaka used all of them.

He sat down on the bench next to Hinata with his elbows on his knees and talked to the floor between his feet, and he wasn't loud.

"You think you have to be good," Tanaka said. "Right now. Today. Or we'll pull you off and you'll never get back on. That's what's in your head."

Hinata's hands closed on his knees.

"Listen. Everybody here already knows you're bad. We watched you all week. You can't receive, you can't serve, you run into people. Nobody put you on this court thinking you wouldn't mess up." He turned his head. "And whether you come off isn't for you to decide. It's for Daichi-san. And he hasn't. So what are you carrying it for?"

Hinata opened his mouth.

"I'm not done. When you mess up, somebody covers you. Me, or Daichi-san, or that glasses bastard, or the new guy. That's what we're there for. That's the whole reason there's 6." Tanaka sat up and jabbed a thumb over his shoulder at the net. "Everybody on this side of it is on your side. So quit playing like you're out there by yourself."

He stood up, and stretched, and went to get his water as if he'd said nothing much.

Sho had been standing a few steps off with a towel round his neck, keeping out of it. Hinata sat on the bench, a little straighter than before, with his eyes very wide and wet at the edges. Sho went over and took the onigiri out of his jacket pocket and put it on Hinata's knee.

Hinata looked at it. He looked up.

"You said you waited 3 years," said Sho. "Eat."

He went and sat down at the other end of the bench.

Somebody pushed a water bottle into his hand. He twisted the cap, and it turned easily. It had already been loosened.

Ennoshita was beside him, with his eyes on the court. On the Seijoh side the tall middle was drinking from a bottle of his own, and a 2nd year was saying something to him that he wasn't listening to.

"You were late on the 1st 1 and early on the 2nd," Ennoshita said. "The 3rd was all right."

"Their libero dug it."

"Their libero's good. It was still all right." Ennoshita turned the towel over on his knee. "Kindaichi. Their tall middle. When a rally goes long, he drifts. A step, sometimes 2, toward wherever Kageyama is. Every time. He wants to be the 1 who stops him."

Sho sat with the open bottle in his hand.

He'd been on the court the whole set, 3 meters from that blocker, with 9 years of tape in his head, and he hadn't caught it. Ennoshita had sat on a bench in a jacket, on the day somebody else had his spot, and watched every point, and found it.

"If he drifts toward Kageyama," Sho said slowly, "he's leaving somebody."

"The left side. Yours." Ennoshita still had his eyes on the court. "The 1 next to him doesn't jump much. He's saving his legs for something."

"Thank you, Ennoshita-san."

"Mm." The whistle went for the 2nd set. Ennoshita picked up his own bottle. "Use it."
