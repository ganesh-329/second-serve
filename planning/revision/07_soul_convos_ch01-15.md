Built from: BRIEF.md; the bible's Soul sheet, Writing guide, Dialogue playbook, Sho sections and POV rules; arc3.md ch 41 to 45 (voice model), ch 33 (Kenma) and ch 38 (Ukai); arc1.md ch 1 to 15 in full; planning/arc3/09_continuity.md; greps of arc2.md and arc3.md for every arc 1 callback.

Line refs: `a1:L`, `a2:L`, `a3:L` are lines in the CRLF-stripped copies (same numbering as the files). Sample lines are proposals in canon voice; "KEEP" marks lines already in the text that should stay word for word or close to it.

**How to read the sample lines.** They are texture, not scripts. Each set is the spine of the talk; the writer fills in air, business and the plain lines around them. The ch 45 model (a3:5470 to 5526, Hinata on the step) is the target: plain questions, a pause the narration doesn't explain, an answer that doesn't top anything, a hand left where it is.

**What's wrong with the current talk, in one paragraph.** Sho wins almost every exchange with a polished line ("Spite.", "Testing the ball's density.", "It's a medical condition.", "It is in my heart.", "Budget cuts, senpai.", "Earn your bread."). Side characters exist to feed him setups. Several scenes are Sho overhearing a canon scene from behind a wall and then nodding. Home scenes are short and comic when they should be where the chapter's feeling sits. The fix is the same everywhere: give the other person a want of their own, let Sho lose or go unanswered sometimes, and let the joke be a reaction instead of a comeback. Sho stays shameless; he just asks plainly ("Please toss me a few.") instead of pitching.
