## 0. Cross-chapter findings (read first)

These problems run through all 5 chapters. The per-chapter sections cite instances, but the fix is global.

1. **The chapters were written before the house style existed.** There are about 140 spelled-out numbers and ordinals across ch 1 to 5 ("one rule" 11, "first year" 73/131/1013, "The first one... The second one... The 3rd" 249 to 255, which mixes both styles in 1 paragraph, "third year" 121, "a hundred" 37/470/672). Arc 3 writes "1st year", "3rd years", "the 1". Every chapter needs a full numbers pass.
2. **The narrator is a stand-up comic.** Almost every paragraph ends on a quip or a simile. There are 53 lines with a "like a / as if / the way a" simile in about 12,400 words. Dogs alone: "stray dog guarding a sandwich" (322), "a dog that has just opened the fridge" (342), "a dog trying to climb stairs" (430), "a dog that had learned to talk and was using it to ask for ham" (1031). Arc 3's narrator (ch 41) carries 1 simile in a page and lets plain action do the work.
3. **Sho wins every exchange.** He gets the last word in nearly every conversation in ch 1 to 4, usually with a polished line ("Your victim", "Spite", "Shamelessness. It's my best quality.", "It's a medical condition", "It is in my heart."). The bible's Sho is "a polite 15-year-old... sometimes gets ignored, told off or out-talked, and takes it" (Dialogue playbook, Sho's voice). Out loud, ch 1 to 5 Sho is a wisecracking adult.
4. **Running bits and callbacks** that the Writing guide bans (rule 6):
   - "investment" 225, 646, 768
   - "Theoretical" 143, 426
   - the "memo" joke 251, then 414
   - "He waited. Gravity didn't." 690, then 1157
   - Daichi's "fridge look" 342, 496, 844
   - Tanaka yelling at politeness: "STOP BEING POLITE ABOUT IT" 856, "I DID NOT TEACH YOU THAT" 1223, "STOP ASKING NICELY" 1235
   - "the heat in his chest" 91, 135, 273
   - "kettle" sound 993, then ch 6
5. **Canon conversations are summarized in indirect speech exactly where a scene belongs:**
   - Suga and Hinata on the step (748 to 752)
   - Tsukishima's evening taunt (868 to 872)
   - Suga's sideline talk (1323 to 1327)
   - Kageyama's "I envy you" to Hinata (1337 to 1339)

   These are the 4 most important talks in this stretch of canon, and the reader gets each one as a précis. The fix is to put them in direct speech with invented wording (the ch 26 model: "Takeda's speech in invented words"), at whatever distance Sho actually hears them.
6. **"Satoshi saw this on tape" as a reflex.** Nearly every character gets introduced through a Satoshi tape memory:
   - Kageyama's hands (672) and face (756)
   - Tsukishima (1121)
   - the setter problem (1315)
   - Suga's smile (1329)
   - Hinata's trust (1427)

   Once a chapter is a voice. Six times in 2 chapters is a template.
7. **The family are joke machines in ch 1 and turn into the Soul-sheet family in ch 3.** Ch 3's kitchen is the right register. Ch 1's family is out of voice: Dad bantering about a red car, Mom feeding setups ("Who won?"), Mio doing bits.
8. **The calendar is broken (continuity).** Ch 3 ends "3 days until Saturday" (942), so ch 1 = Monday, ch 2 = Tuesday, ch 3 = Wednesday, and the 3v3 is on day 6. But ch 6 has Sho say "my 8th day of volleyball" (1773) and "you won the mop race on Tuesday" (1803), and the mop race happens in ch 3, on Wednesday (864). Ch 2 also has an unmarked jump from a 6:50 a.m. start (300) to "the last of the light" (538). Pick 1 timeline and write it into the Continuity blocks.
9. **Satoshi's years don't add up.** "about 12 years of it" (141) against "Satoshi had done it for 15 years" (654, the bowing). He played until 20. If 12 years is right, he started at 8. Fix 654.

## Chapter 1: Ball to the face (lines 9 to 296)

### 1. Soul

**Scene A: hallway, then the gym door (11 to 51).**
- **Sho** wants to go home. That's a real want, and a good one: a boy who quit everything. The scene does show it (flyers in the bin, "keep walking"). Then the narrator jokes over it: "like a boxer with a very short career and very soft hands" (15), and Mio "had no mercy and an excellent memory, which is a bad combination in a little sister" (27).
- The real "before" never shows: the flatness, the boredom, a 15-year-old who isn't happy. Ch 26 needs that. Mom says "I didn't know you could be happy like this. You weren't, before." (a2:5126). Ch 1 currently shows a cheerful smart-mouth before the hit, so the change has nothing to change from.

**Scene B: waking on the concrete (55 to 161).**
- This is the most important moment of the novel, and it gets about 150 words of processing: "did the math" (71), "2 tapes on the same shelf. Neither felt borrowed. That was the strangest part." (77). A man has just learned he died alone at a desk at 3:14 a.m., and within 20 lines he's doing a bit ("Your victim", 103; "Compensation", 109).
- Under the soul sheet he'd be shameless. But shamelessness comes from "a man who has already lost everything once has nothing left to protect" (Soul sheet), and the page never lets him register that loss. Grief, vertigo and the body's strangeness (his hands, his height, the taste of blood in a 15-year-old's mouth) are all skipped.
- "it scared him a little how good it felt" (93) names a feeling and goes against the bible ("never scared").
- **Hinata** wants to apologize, then to get Sho's height, then to play. He acts ("bowed so fast he nearly headbutted Sho's knee", 79). His canon want on day 1 is to be Karasuno's ace (wiki `Tobio_Kageyama_1097.txt` line 52: "he yells... that it is he, that will be Karasuno's ace"; goal field in `Sh_y_Hinata_816.txt`: "To become Karasuno's Ace"). - **Suga** wants members (the club is short-handed; `Tobio_Kageyama_1097.txt` line 54). He acts on it (towel, form, "Bring your form tomorrow"). He's the only person in the scene who behaves like himself.

**Scene D: wall passes and the form (243 to 277).**
