## 0. The 5 principles every match below follows

1. **Ukai decides, and the reader hears why.** Every time Sho goes in or comes out, Ukai has a reason a volleyball coach would give: the opponent, the rotation, the serve, the knee, the length of the day. Sho tells him what he saw (from ch 22); Ukai checks and decides. Nobody else makes the call.
2. **Sho's minutes grow in steps across the arc, never in a jump.** Ougiminami: 1 set. Kakugawa: back row. Johzenji: a few rotations. Wakutani: back-row receive in a crisis. Seijoh: pieces of all 3 sets. Shiratorizawa: the biggest role yet (see section 8 for the 3 options). That's the wing spiker Ukai's long plan promised for Arc 4 (bible "The ace path"), earned one match at a time.
3. **Every Sho point costs something, and every Sho bend is paid for.** If Sho makes a set easier, something else in the same match gets harder (an opponent adapts, he gets blocked, Oikawa reads him). That's the anti-cheap rule.
4. **The last point of every match belongs to its canon owner, and Sho is off court or in the back row for it.** Hinata's block-out vs Kakugawa, the quick vs Seijoh, Hinata vs Shiratorizawa at 21-19.
5. **The ace line in every match.** Even when Sho plays back row, the chapter carries one ace beat: an approach, a swing, a bad ball asked for, a reach number, a jump counted for the knee.

## 2. Before the first whistle: the knee moment sets the load

The ace path puts the knee moment in Arc 4 ("a sore left knee after plyometrics, and he tells Ukai before practice. Nothing dramatic happens."). The best place for it, from a minutes point of view, is **the week before the 1st stage**. It gives Ukai a plain, physical reason for the small day-1 role, and it lets the knee rule pay off with no drama: Sho tells, and the cost is a few rallies.

- Sho tells Ukai before practice (Coach 1st, same day). Ukai: no plyos for a week, no depth jumps, ice, and "you play 1 set on Saturday. 1. Then we look at it."
- Day 1 minutes are therefore set by the knee, not by a lack of trust. The reader sees a coach managing a body, and Sho accepting it.
- Sample:
  - Ukai: "Which bit."
  - Sho: "Under the kneecap. Left. After the box jumps. 3 out of 10."
  - Ukai: "...You came and told me."
  - Sho: "You said same day."
  - Ukai: "Saturday you get 1 set. Don't argue."
  - Sho: "I wasn't going to."
  - (He was going to. He writes *1 set* in SHO 2 and doesn't cross it out.)

If agent 8 (training) puts the knee moment elsewhere, keep the principle: Ukai's reasons for Sho's minutes on day 1 are physical and spoken.

- The knee cap from section 2: 1 set, this one.
- Ukai tells Tanaka on the bus, not on the court (Rules for the rivalry: no surprises, nobody written down). Tanaka's reaction is his: loud first, then quiet, then useful.

**What Sho does:** This is where the **Field map Lv1 forms** (bible timeline): a whole set on court, the same 6 rotations coming round twice, Ougiminami's habits repeating, and the map stops being snapshots and holds. He calls it once, to Daichi, in plain words.

**Protected:** Tsukishima's habit-read block (set 1 in the fic, so Sho isn't on court and can't be thought to have called it). Put it in set 1.

**Bend:** set 2 goes **25-19** (canon 25-13). Reason: Karasuno's first-choice wing is on the bench, Sho makes 2 hitting errors and gets stuffed once in his 1st official full set (the ace is raw: Power C), and Ougiminami's float server aims at him for 3 rotations. Karasuno still wins comfortably. The bend makes Sho look new, which protects the later matches from feeling cheap.

**Ace line:** he gets 1 high ball out of system at about 17-12, swings at full reach, and it goes in off the block. 1 kill out of 1 bad ball. He counts jumps for the knee (Ukai asked for a number): *31 jumps. Knee: 0.*

## 5. Johzenji (final stage, day 1)

**Canon:** 2-0; Terushima's chaos; **Kageyama's face-block nosebleed; Suga in; Narita in for Hinata (Suga combos); Hinata learns from the bench; Kageyama returns.** [web snippet]

**Why Ukai decides it:**

**What Sho does:** the map runs into a team with almost no patterns. Johzenji's "fun" volleyball breaks the map's elimination (they set whoever is laughing). This is the 1st clear limit of Field map Lv1, right after it formed: it needs habits to rule things out, and Johzenji has fewer. The eye still works on Terushima's wrist (hitter), so Sho digs Terushima once with the eye and then gets beaten by a no-look tip from a player the map had ruled out. Good honest loss of a point.

**Protected, and where Sho must NOT be:**
- **Kageyama's nosebleed** and his blocking of Futamata.

**Bend:** none to the result; set 1 maybe a point wider because of Sho's 2 rotations resetting the block, then Johzenji claws it back with a tip run. Recommend: keep canon set scores.

**Canon:** 2-1 (25-20, 20-25, 25-23); **Daichi and Tanaka collide in set 1, Daichi out; Ennoshita in, frozen trying to be Daichi; Tanaka gets through to him**; Nakashima's block-outs. [web snippet; bible scores]

**Where Sho plays:** **back-row receive for Asahi in set 3**, 3 rotations, about 15 rallies (the bible's planned hook: "Sho takes back-row receive"). Not set 2.

**What Sho does:** receives 7 of 9 serves aimed at him cleanly enough (Reception C: early, on balance, not always perfect); 1 shank. Wakutani adapts: they serve at Ennoshita again, and Ennoshita has to pass his way through it. Good: the leadership test comes back to its owner.

**Protected, and where Sho must NOT be:**

- **Tanaka getting through to Ennoshita.** Tanaka collided with Daichi; Tanaka's guilt and his push to Ennoshita are Tanaka's (and canon's). Sho is not in that conversation.
- **The last points of set 3.** Sho is **off court** at 23-23 (Asahi back in at the front). Whoever scores in canon scores.

**Bend:** set 3 stays 25-23, but the path changes: Karasuno leads by 3 during Sho's rotations, Wakutani gets it back when they move their serve back to Ennoshita, and the end is Ennoshita's. Recommend no score bend: the closeness is the point of Ennoshita's set.

### The 3 options for Sho's role in the final

#### Option A: "Stints" (small)
- **What:** Sho plays in sets 3, 4 and 5, in pieces: back row for Asahi when Ushijima serves (set 3 and set 4), 1 front-row stint of 2 rotations for Tanaka in set 3, 1 pinch serve in set 5 before Tsukishima's injury. About 25 to 30 rallies.

- **"1 more":** has to happen earlier (Seijoh set 2, after Iwaizumi stuffs him, or not in Arc 4 at all).
- **Pros:** zero risk to protected moments; Tanaka's spot untouched; the win stays 100% canon-shaped; Sho's step up from Seijoh is gentle.
- **Cons:** the ace spine of Arc 4 ends small. The author's notebook line and the "1st real 1 more" (ace path: "a big set late in a tight game") lose their best stage. The final would read as "canon with a guy on the bench", the exact thing "Canon and divergence" ended.
