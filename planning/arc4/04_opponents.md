## 0. The big idea in one paragraph

Arc 3's opponents attacked the reader. Arc 4's opponents mostly attack the **ace**, and the ones who attack the reader do it with information Sho handed them himself. The bracket climbs a ladder that matches the ace program: **Ougiminami** is plain and gives him a quiet gym to see the whole floor (Field map Lv1). **Kakugawa** puts a 2 m 1st year in front of him: reach beats reading at the net. **Johzenji** has no patterns to rule out and doesn't care if they're read, and teaches him that an ace is allowed to enjoy it. **Wakutani South** is a mirror: Nakashima is a small ace who lives by hitting off hands, which is the trick Sho has been using all year, done as a whole identity. **Seijoh** is the only team with tape, memory and a theory of Sho, and Oikawa uses the tell Sho gave him at the sink as bait, while Iwaizumi keeps his promise and leaves his hand there, and Kyoutani arrives with no file at all. **Shiratorizawa** doesn't care what Sho sees: Washijo believes in height and power, Shirabu sets Ushijima anyway, Tendou guesses before anyone's body knows, and Ushijima's ball goes through a correct read the way Bokuto's did. Every counter points the same way as page 1 of *SHO 2*: *Arms. Enough to hold Bokuto's line. Then enough to hit it.* The opponents turn the reader into an ace by making reading insufficient.

## 3. The 1st stage

### 3.1 Ougiminami (round 2)

**Canon.** Round 2 of the Miyagi Spring High prelims and Karasuno's 1st match; Karasuno won in straight sets, **25-16, 25-13** [web: 1, 2]. Captain **Towada Yoshiki** meets Hinata in the hallway before the match [web: 2]. Ougiminami had lost to Shiratorizawa at the Inter-High, and Towada is discouraged by it [web: 2]. Their former captain **Akimiya Noboru** comes to the venue to cheer for them [web: 2]. A plain, honest, mid-level team with 3rd years playing what may be their last tournament [memory for the tone].

**Style.** Sound and ordinary: high outside sets, a libero who's fine, no tricks [memory, unsure on players]. That's why the bible puts Field map Lv1 here: a loud official gym kills Sho's sound layer, and a plain team gives the map clean data on every touch (05 section 3).

**How they notice Sho.** They don't scout. They notice the way plain teams notice: their outside hitter gets dug 3 times by a 1st year in the same spot and starts looking at him before he swings. By set 2 their setter avoids his side. That's the whole counter [proposal].

**How Sho adapts.** He doesn't need to. This is the match where he learns he can play a whole set without the eye and know where everyone is (Kagura Shingan, 05). The ace content: Ukai uses the blowout. At about 15-8 in set 2 Sho goes in for Tanaka on the wing and gets 3 sets: 1 kill line, 1 tooled off the end blocker, 1 hit into the net because he swung for the reach mark instead of the ball [proposal]. Tanaka on the bench: "You swung like you were measuring something." "I was." "Measure it in practice."

**What Ougiminami teaches him.** About time, more than volleyball. At the handshake Towada is crying and Akimiya is at the rail. Sho thinks of Suga's and Daichi's last tournament (Soul sheet: "he counts time like money"). He doesn't say it. It sets up the decision round: every match from here is somebody's last.

**Sample lines (plain).**
- Towada (handshake, wet face, to Daichi, overheard): "Win it. All of it." Daichi: "We will."
- Akimiya (at the rail, to his old team, overheard by Sho on the way to the bench): "You did good. You did good."
- Their libero to his setter, after Sho's 3rd dig: "Don't give it to that side. The tall 1st year's always there."

**Protected moments.** Hinata and Towada in the hallway (Hinata's). The 1st quick of the tournament (Hinata and Kageyama's). Field map Lv1 is Sho's own and can happen here.

**Arc 5 carry.** None needed. Towada's tears are a seed for the 3rd years' story in Arc 5, which Sho watches with the same eyes.

### 3.2 Kakugawa (group final)

**Canon.** The last match of Group 4; Karasuno won in straight sets [web: 3]. Set 1 **25-22**: Hyakuzawa pulls Kakugawa within 2 at 24-22, then Hinata's quick closes it [web: 3]. Set 2: a roster listing gives **25-19**; Tanaka's spike makes 24-19 [web: 3]. The match ends on **Hinata blocking out off Hyakuzawa's fingertips**, a skill he took from Bokuto's training [web: 3]. **Hyakuzawa Yudai**: 1st year wing spiker, about 2 m (one wiki says 201 cm) [web: 5]. Kakugawa's early advantage was his height, and Hyakuzawa believed his height and power were enough [web: 5]. Episode title: "A Simple and Pure Strength" [web: 5]. My memory: Hyakuzawa started volleyball recently because he was tall, felt he was valued only for being tall, and Kakugawa's offense was mostly high balls to him over the block [memory]. He later attends the Miyagi 1st-year selection camp [memory, unsure].

**Style.** "Give it to Hyakuzawa." High sets, he hits over the block. His body is raw and honest: his swing is the same every time, and he hits where he looks because nobody ever taught him not to [memory + proposal].

**How they notice Sho.** Hyakuzawa doesn't think about opponents; he thinks about whether he's more than tall. What he notices is that the 183 cm number 13 keeps measuring him: Sho blocks him, then backs off and digs him, then tools his hands. Kakugawa's coach sees Sho dig the big ball and tells his setter to send Hyakuzawa to the other wing [proposal].

**How they fight him.** Height. That's it, and it works. When Sho blocks Hyakuzawa, Hyakuzawa's hand is above Sho's hands; when Sho attacks, Hyakuzawa's hands are above Sho's ball. **The eye reads Hyakuzawa clean and it doesn't matter at the net** (05: "Height beats reading at the net"). Sho's attempts:
1. Blocks him straight: the ball goes over his hands, Noya digs it, barely. Block D at 183 cm against 201 cm.
2. Stops blocking him and stands where the ball goes: Shunpo (05): dig, 4 steps out, a full transition approach. That's how he gets a swing at all.
3. Hits at the 2 m block: stuffed once, flat. Then hits high off the top of Hyakuzawa's hands and out (a tool off the highest hand in Miyagi), which only works because his contact point is higher than it was in August. The reach number earns its page.

**What Hyakuzawa teaches him as an ACE: reach.** Reach is the 1 thing reading can't buy. Sho's reach (05 and the bible: about 318 cm in August, 325 by the prelims) against a beginner who outreaches him without knowing how. The soul beat: Hyakuzawa has never been measured.

**Sample lines (plain).**
- Sho, at the vending machines between matches (shameless, polite): "Excuse me. What's your spike reach?" Hyakuzawa: "...My what?" "How high you can touch. Running." "I don't know. Nobody measured it." Sho (thought): *Nobody measured it.* Out loud: "You should. It's a good number."
- Hyakuzawa, later, quietly: "Everybody just says I'm tall." Sho: "You are tall." Beat. "Your approach is 2 steps. You'd get more with 4." Hyakuzawa looks at his own feet.
- (Flag: Sho giving an opponent a note fits his shamelessness and his old job, and he already traded Oikawa a tell. Keep it after the match and keep it to 1 sentence. Hinata's block-out finishes their match; Sho's note must never touch Hinata's moment.)
- Tanaka, after the stuff: "That guy's a building." Sho: "Yes, senpai." "So go round the building."

**Protected moments.** Hinata's block-out off Hyakuzawa's fingertips to end the match, and that it comes from Bokuto's lessons (protected list: Bokuto's lessons to Hinata). Hinata's quick to close set 1. Sho has no part in the last 2 points of the match.

**Arc 5 carry.** Strong, if the author sends Sho to the Miyagi 1st-year camp (section 6). Hyakuzawa is there in canon [memory, unsure]. He shows up with a number written on his hand: his reach. "I measured it." That's the whole payoff.

**Canon.** Karasuno won in straight sets [web: 4]; one roster page gives **25-21, 25-20** [web: 4] (the bible's timeline says only "Won 2-0"; use 25-21, 25-20 unless a canon check disagrees). **Kageyama's nosebleed:** he blocks a spike from **Futamata** with his face; the block works, he bleeds, Takeda calls a timeout [web: 4, season 2 episode 15]. **Narita** comes on, scores a quick in set 1, and reminds Tanaka he's the same way sometimes after Terushima's ace celebration angers him [web: 4]. **Terushima Yuji**: captain, wing spiker, 2nd year, about 178 cm, flashy offense-first play [bible Noun KB]. Other Johzenji names [unsure]: I won't name the setter.

**Style.** Chaos with skill: tricky combinations, unplanned plays, setters who aren't setters, a celebrating server. **The anti-map**: the map rules options out by what a team usually does, and Johzenji don't usually do anything. The eye still reads them (honest bodies, they don't hide) but 05 has the right counter: their 2nd hitter really swings, and while the eye is on 1 body the board freezes and the other one scores (05's fire log: doubled ghost, wrong).

**How they notice Sho.** Terushima notices immediately because he likes noticing people. Sho digs his 1st line shot standing still. Terushima laughs at the net.

**How they fight him.** They don't scheme. They **make it a game**: once Terushima figures out 13 knows where he's going, he starts hitting stupid shots on purpose: a backhand tip, a one-handed push, a ball he swings at full and then rolls. The eye shows the swing honestly; Terushima changes the shot in the hand (the eye never shows the ball after contact or a tip that changes in the hand: 05 rule). Sho loses 2 points to "I didn't know either." [proposal]

**How Sho adapts.** He stops trying to win the reading game and plays volleyball: wide gaze, a step back, cover the tip with his feet instead of his eyes. As an ace, he answers with his own serve (05: Mirage Tempo's match debut here, 1 ace and 1 net).

Terushima reminds him that a big swing is also a joy. After a kill Sho laughs on court for the 1st time in an official match. Tanaka: "WHY ARE YOU LAUGHING?" "It was fun, senpai." "IT WAS. DO IT AGAIN."

### 4.2 Wakutani South (quarterfinal)

**Canon.** Quarterfinal; Karasuno won after a close 3rd set [web: 6]. The bible gives **25-20, 20-25, 25-23** [bible timeline; not confirmed by search]. **Hanayama** serves 1st; the 1st quick scores, then **Nakashima** knocks a ball off Hinata's hands for a point [web: 6]. **Nakashima Takeru**: 3rd year captain, wing spiker, about 173 cm, the core of Wakutani's tenacity, wants to be the next Little Giant; his spiking is close to the Little Giant's [web: 6; bible Noun KB]. His trick is the **block-out** (hitting off the blockers' hands) [web: 7]. **Daichi's injury**: after a Nakashima block-out, Daichi chases the ball and collides with Tanaka, who was going for it too [web: 7]; he leaves the match. **Ennoshita** comes in for him, doesn't feel he can fill Daichi's shoes, and leads anyway; he later tells the team he'll go after every ball Nakashima deflects off the block [web: 7]. Oikawa (watching) comments on whether Daichi can come back [web: 7]. My memory: Wakutani are a tenacious, defensive, never-drop-it team; Daichi returns for the end [memory, unsure]; Hinata and Nakashima battle in the air [web: 6].

**Style.** Tenacity and the tool. Every hitter, and Nakashima above all, aims at the blocker's hands and sends the ball off them out of bounds or into the back of the court. Long rallies. They chase everything.

**How they notice Sho.** Nakashima is the 1 player in Miyagi who recognizes Sho's attack, because it's his own: the tool off the outside hand (Kuroo in Arc 1, Oikawa at 24-23, Aone in Arc 2, Bokuto's little finger in ch 44). He sees Sho's chin turn toward the end blocker's hand and his shoulder open. He doesn't see a reader at all. He sees a hitter who uses hands.

**How they fight him.**
- **Pull the hands.** When Sho aims at Nakashima's hand, Nakashima drops it out of the way; Sho's tool becomes a ball hit out with nobody touching it. The counter to a toolman is to give him nothing to tool. (It's what Nakashima would hate having done to him.) Sho hits 2 out this way [proposal].
- **Tool Sho's block.** When Sho blocks (Block D), Nakashima hits off Sho's hands. Sho's eye shows the swing honestly and the swing *is* the tool; the eye can't tell him which way the ball leaves his own fingers (05: "the eye shows the swing, the block decides where it goes").
- **Serve the seam** between Sho in the back row and whoever replaced Daichi.

**How Sho adapts.** This is the match where Sho's **block** learns something earned (Block stays D in 05's card plan; this is texture, no rank): against a toolman, take the hands down at the last instant or turn the outside hand in, toward the court. He learns it by being tooled 3 times. And the map wins where the eye can't: where tooled balls go off which hand (05). In the back row, after Daichi's injury, Sho is behind Tanaka on Ennoshita's order, and Ennoshita's plan is the one that works. As an ace, Getsuga (05: the back-row attack's match debut here, set 3, Ennoshita calls it).

**What Nakashima teaches him as an ACE: the mirror.** Nakashima is what a hitter becomes when he can't hit over people: he hits *through* them. Sho at 183 has been using the tool as a trick, an analyst's shot. Nakashima at 173 uses it because it's all he has, every ball, for 3 years. Sho sees the cost and the pride of it in the same body. The lesson is plain: the tool is a shot, and an ace needs the shot that goes through the hands too, and the arms to hit it. Sho has an option Nakashima never had. He doesn't say any of this.

**Sample lines (plain).** Nakashima: earnest 3rd year, a little stubborn, respectful.
- Nakashima, at the net after pulling his hand: "You aim at the hand before you jump." Sho: "...Yes." "I do too. That's how I knew."
- Nakashima, at the handshake, holding on 1 second: "Hit over it next time. You can."
- Ennoshita, in the huddle, to Sho, after Daichi goes off: "Kiyose. You're behind Tanaka. Anything off his hands is yours. Don't think about the rest." "Yes." (Ennoshita's leadership is his. Sho's job is to obey it perfectly.)
- Ennoshita, later (05 has it): "You didn't go still that time."
- Tanaka, after the collision, shaken, between rallies: "Is Daichi-san..." Sho: "He's sitting up. Ukai-san's with him." Tanaka nods hard and slaps his face once.

**Protected moments.** Daichi's injury and the collision with Tanaka. **Ennoshita's leadership** (protected list): his entrance, his doubt, his plan for the deflected balls, his calls. Hinata vs Nakashima in the air. The quick. Daichi's return if canon has one. Sho's back-row attack has to be called by Ennoshita, so it serves Ennoshita's moment.

**Arc 5 carry.** Nakashima graduates. The carry is to Hinata (his Little Giant thread) and, for Sho, a line in *SHO 2*: *Nakashima. Pulls the hand. Hit over it.* The relationship that grows here is **Ennoshita and Sho**: the 2nd year who counted his wipes and saw him go still, now giving him orders. That carries into Arc 5 and into Ennoshita's captaincy.

### 5.3 Oikawa's plan (in order, as the match escalates)

05 already has the core ("Oikawa stops showing him things": sets from the same body every time, serves Sho so he can't run Getsuga, sets Kyoutani when Sho's back row). I add the bait and the order.

1. **The gap tell, used as bait.** At the sink Sho told Oikawa that he looks once at the gap he'll serve. Oikawa said he'd fix it. He did better: he keeps it, and lies with it. His 1st serve at Sho: Oikawa looks at the seam between Sho and Noya, walks in, and serves the line behind Daichi. Ace. He smiles at Sho. It's Sho's own lie (ch 42) done to him by the person who taught him that faces can be read. [proposal]
2. **Serve him every rotation he passes.** So the eye fires (it likes servers he's studied, and he's studied Oikawa more than anyone) and so Sho is passing instead of approaching for the pipe.
3. **Make the 1-player read worthless.** When Sho's eye is on Oikawa, Oikawa's body is the same for every set (his best skill, 05). Sho reads the setter correctly and learns nothing.
4. **Set whoever Sho isn't watching.** If Sho locks on Iwaizumi, Oikawa sets Kunimi. If Sho reads Kunimi's waking up (he did in Arc 2), Oikawa sets Kyoutani.

## 9. Pitfalls

- **Opponents explaining Sho.** Max 1 near-miss line per chapter (05's rule). Nobody ever says "eye", "future", "see it before".
- **Everybody talks to Sho.** Canon opponents talk to Hinata, Kageyama and their own teams. Each opponent gets 1 or 2 exchanges with Sho for the whole arc. Ushijima maybe 1 line. Kyoutani 1 grunt.
- **Quips.** Tendou and Oikawa are the only playful ones. Terushima is loose and friendly, not witty. Iwaizumi, Ushijima, Washijo, Nakashima, Hyakuzawa, Shirabu are plain.
- **Reading wins matches.** It never decides a set in Arc 4. Arms and the map do the scoring; the eye is small (05).
- **Sho's notes to opponents.** He gave Oikawa a tell in Arc 2 and it came back as an ace against him. That's the lesson: he can give Hyakuzawa 1 sentence after the match, and that's the only note to an opponent in Arc 4.
- **Repeating Arc 3's Bokuto ball.** Use it once, at Ushijima in set 1, and say so in Sho's head in 1 line. After that the ball is different because the arms are.
