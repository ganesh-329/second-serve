## 0. The one idea that runs the whole calendar

**The ace program is a ledger, and every line of it has a person holding the pencil.** Arc 3 trained the map to need fewer looks. Arc 4 trains the body the map belongs to, and the rule that keeps it honest is the rule Satoshi broke: *write the numbers, and say the bad ones out loud.* So every marker in this calendar is counted by someone other than Sho, out loud or on paper, in front of him:

| Counter | What they count | Where it lives | Why it's theirs (their want) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Takeda** | Weight room: loads, sets, reps, Nordic reps; in the knee week, Sho's morning pain scores | A clipboard with a Karasuno sticker, and the library's sports-science book with bus tickets as bookmarks | He wants to be useful to the club in volleyball terms. The weight room is the first part of volleyball he can learn from a book faster than anyone else |
| **Mio** | Spike reach on the garage wall (*REACH* page), band rotations at home, and later the *KNEE* column | *THINGS HE DOES*, the *REACH* page; Dad's chalk and tape measure | She wants him to keep the thing that made him come alive. Measuring is how she watches over it without saying so |
| **Dad** | Height on the kitchen door frame | Pencil marks, *183. Aug.* | "Mm." He builds things instead of talking: a plyo box, later a wedge |
| **Mom** | Body weight, on the fridge calendar, Sundays | Small numbers in the corner of each Sunday square | She notices everything ("You're thinner", ch 45) and acts instead of asking |
| **Tanaka (chalk)** | Jump floats in, then in the target zone, out of 10 | Chalk tallies on the gym wall by the storage room door, the same wall his sound-read scores went on in Arc 3 | His own line shot and his own pride: he wants the kid to earn the wing, and he wants to be the one who saw it first |
| **Yachi (camcorder)** | Landings held 3 seconds with the knee over the toes (left and right), back-row takeoff foot faults | The *KARASUNO H.S. AV ROOM* camcorder, played back on the step by the side door | She wants to be useful and to belong. Frame-by-frame was already her job in ch 34 |
| **Ukai** | High bad balls killed out of 10 | In his head, then out loud, then once on the whiteboard | He wants to be a real coach. Planting an ace is coach's work his grandfather would respect |
| **Noya** | Receive upkeep: passes onto the tape in a row (Yaku's 10, brought home) | Out loud, every number, while claiming he isn't counting | He wants the back row to be a floor nothing falls through, and Yaku did something he respects |

**Scene rule (the guardrail made into a format):** each training scene is built on **1 exercise, 1 number, 1 counter**, inside a conversation. If a scene needs a second number, it's a second scene or it waits a week. Weeks that don't earn a scene get a single line in a chapter that is about something else (a match, school, family).

## 2. Research notes the program leans on (with honest limits)

| Topic | What's real | Limits | In the fic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Youth strength training | Supervised resistance training is relatively safe for youth; technique over load, never trade technique for added resistance; beginners do few reps with feedback on every rep of multi-joint lifts; 1RM testing unnecessary for low training age; later 2-4 sets of 6-12 at low-moderate intensity [web: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19620931/ ; https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/why-youth-strength-and-conditioning-matters ; summary of the 2014 consensus, https://g-se.com/uploads/blog_adjuntos/br_j_sports_med_2014_lloyd_498_505.pdf] | Position statements, not one program | Ukai and Takeda's rules: no maxes, 3 sets, the bar alone for 2 weeks, every rep watched |
| Plyometrics and jump | Adolescent plyometric training gives a large jump benefit; volleyball subgroup SMD about 0.90; biggest gains over 10 to 26 weeks; about 4 sets linked to the best jump results in male adolescents [web: https://www.jssm.org/jssm-24-406.xml-Fulltext ; https://peerj.com/reviewer-match/129815]; a 2025 meta-analysis: countermovement jump +2.2 (cm, units not shown in the snippet) [web: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1639477/pdf] | Effect sizes, not centimeters for volleyball | 10 weeks is enough for a real but small change. **318 to 325 is honest only if the 7 cm come from 3 places:** about 1 cm from growth (183 to 184), about 2 to 3 cm from strength and plyos, about 3 cm from approach technique (penultimate step, arm drive) [design] |
| Jump float progression | Standing float first; add a small hop, then a full jump; 3-step footwork like an attack but subdued; the toss is the biggest factor; same contact as the standing float [web: https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/how-to-teach-the-jump-float-serve/ ; https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/footwork-for-the-jump-float/ ; https://coachingvb.com/serving-stand-or-jump] | Coaching blogs | Weeks 3 to 6 and 8 to 10 |
| Growth-related anterior knee pain | Sinding-Larsen-Johansson (lower pole of the kneecap) and Osgood-Schlatter (top of the shin) are growth-related traction problems linked to jumping sports including volleyball; worse during and after jumping; relative rest and activity modification are the core; ice for flare-ups; complete rest generally discouraged; most resolve [web: https://www.fittoplay.org/body-parts/knee/osgood-schlatter-and-sinding-larsen-johansson-syndrome ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137138 ; https://www.sfh-tr.nhs.uk/media/3768/osgood-schlatters-disease.pdf] | SLJ is commonest at 10 to 13 | At 15 and 184 cm, the honest label is "the tendon below the kneecap is irritated where it attaches" (early jumper's knee in a growing boy). Never named on the page |
| Patellar tendinopathy management | Most common in young jumping men 15 to 30; pain-monitoring model (activity allowed if pain stays low and is back to baseline within 24 hours); single-leg decline squat on a wedge as the daily test; isometric holds (about 5 x 45 s at 30-60 degrees knee bend) used for short-term pain relief, small studies [web: https://physiotutors.com/conditions/patellar-tendinopathy/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40814-021-00792-5 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528703/table/i1938-162X-57-7-621-t02 ; https://www.sportsinjurybulletin.com/jumpers-knee-an-in-season-management-approach/] | Mostly adult data; isometric pain relief evidence is mixed | Ukai's knee plan, section 5 |
| Sleep, protein | Teenagers need about 8 to 10 hours; protein about 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg for young athletes | [memory] | Bed 22:00, up about 6:00; Mom's extra onigiri and eggs |

**Weight room session shape (40 minutes, Takeda's clipboard)** [design, grounded in the youth guidelines]:
1. 8 min warm-up and **knee armor**: lateral band walks, single-leg landings held 3 seconds (step off a low box, land on 1 leg, freeze; Yachi films the 1st session of each week so Ukai can see whether the knee caves in), step-downs.
2. 20 min main lifts, alternating A and B days: **A (Mon, Fri):** front squat, Romanian deadlift, push-up to bench press, rows. **B (Wed):** split squats, hip thrusts on the bench, pull-ups, calf raises, medicine ball rotational throws against the old gym's concrete wall.
3. 8 min **Nordic hamstring curls** (Takeda holds his ankles, kneeling on a folded gym mat) and shoulder armor (Y-T-W raises with 1 kg plates, band external rotations).
4. 4 min: write the numbers. Sho reads them out, Takeda writes, Sho checks Takeda's writing (shameless) and Takeda checks Sho's arithmetic (Takeda is right once, and that's a scene beat).

**The jump budget** [design; this is what makes the knee moment honest]: Sho counts jumps per day in the back of *SHO 2*: approach jumps in hitting lines, jump floats, back-row attacks, blocks, plyo contacts. Ukai sets a ceiling after week 3 (about 120 jump contacts on a practice day, 0 plyos on a day with 60 or more serves). The ledger exists so that the knee week has a cause the reader can see: in week 6 four things go up at once.

## 5. The knee moment: medical reality, and how small it stays

### 5.1 What happens in his body (for the author; never in prose)
- **Cause:** in 1 week, jump contacts go from about 100 to about 150 a day (depth jumps, the jump float finally working, back-row reps, 2 full sets on the wing), in a boy who grew 1 cm in 6 weeks. Bones grow first; the tendon attachment under the kneecap takes the pull. This is the textbook growth-plus-jump-load story [web: fittoplay; PMC7137138, section 2].
- **What it feels like:** a dull ache just below the left kneecap, at the bottom tip, where the tendon starts. Worst going **down** stairs and after sitting with the knee bent in class (it stiffens, then eases after a few steps). A little sore at the start of a jump, then warm and fine during, then it comes back afterwards. **No swelling, no clicking, no giving way, no pain at night, no lump on the shin.** On Ukai's scale: 2 out of 10 on the stairs, 3 on Dad's wedge squat.
- **What Satoshi knows:** this is a tendon talking. It isn't an ACL. The ACL in the other life tore in 1 landing, but the 3 months of pain before it made him land differently, protect, cut corners, and he told nobody. Sho's fear isn't that this pain is dangerous; it's that 2 out of 10 is exactly what he used to call nothing. **The rule says anything.** That's the whole internal scene: an analyst's brain saying "noise", and a rule written in pale blue notebook ink saying "same day".

### 5.2 The telling (Thu Sep 25)
- **When:** before practice, in his school shoes, in the gym doorway, before he's changed. Ukai has just come in from the shop with a carrier bag of meat buns.
- **Sample:**
  - Sho: "Coach. My left knee. Below the kneecap."
  - Ukai puts the bag down. "Since when?"
  - "This morning. The stairs. Going down."
  - "How bad?"
  - "2. Out of 10."
  - "Sit." Sho sits on the step. Ukai presses with his thumb, below the kneecap, then the bump on the shin, then the sides. "Here?" "Yes." "Here?" "No." "Swelling? Did it give?" "No. No." "Hurts at night?" "No."
  - Ukai sits back on his heels. "Okay." Then, after a bit: "Good."
  - "Good?"
  - "You told me. That's the good part. The rest is just a knee." He stands up. "Takeda-sensei. Write this down."
- No speeches about the past, no team gathering round, nobody else hears more than "knee, small". Tanaka, passing: "You okay?" "Yes, senpai. Tendon." "Okay." He goes. That's how small it is.

### 5.3 What Ukai does (a coach, plus Takeda's book; never a doctor)
1. **The red flags he checks for:** swelling, giving way, locking, night pain, pain at rest, a hot or lumpy shin. Any of those means a doctor the same day. None are there.
2. **Today:** no jumping at all. Receive and defense, full (nothing a libero does needs a jump). **Ice for 15 minutes after practice**, for comfort, on the bench; Takeda times it. At home, Mio brings the frozen edamame (her Arc 2 move, for his arm) and holds it on his knee herself.
3. **Days 2 to 4:** no plyos, no depth jumps, no jump float (standing float instead), no back-row attack, no hitting lines. **Swap the plyos for isometric holds:** a Spanish squat with a belt looped round the 1990 rack's post, 5 x 45 seconds, before practice and in the weight-room slot [web: isometric protocol summary, section 2]. Squats stay in at the same weight because they don't hurt over 3 and the next morning is back to where it was (the 24-hour rule) [web: pain-monitoring model]. Hamstrings, hips, upper body, shoulder armor: unchanged.
4. **Daily check, Takeda's clipboard:** 2 numbers every morning at 7:00, stairs and wedge squat. Dad builds the wedge (a 25 degree board, scrap wood, sanded) the evening Mom tells him, and leaves it by the genkan without a word [design; the decline squat is the standard daily test, web: physiotutors].
5. **The way back:** when both numbers are 0 or 1 for 2 mornings, approach jumps come back at half volume (15), then the jump float, then hitting lines. Back to full at about 8 days. **Depth jumps stay off until after the prelims** ("We'll see in November."). Ukai's jump ceiling drops to 100 on practice days for the rest of the arc.
6. **Numbers:** Thu stairs 2 / wedge 3; Fri 2/2; Sat 1/2; Sun 1/1; Mon 0/1; Tue 0/1; Wed 0/0 (approach jumps back); Thu 0/0 (jump float back).

### 5.4 The clinic (optional, 1 short scene; recommended)
Mom hears it at dinner, says nothing, and on Saturday at 8:30 she's wearing her going-out coat. Orthopedic clinic by the station [design]. The doctor presses the same spot Ukai pressed, bends the knee, has him do a single-leg squat, maybe 1 X-ray because he's growing. "You're still growing. The tendon's irritated where it pulls on the bone. You jumped more this month?" "Yes. About 50 a day more." The doctor looks at him over his glasses (a teenager who knows his own jump count). "Then jump less for a while. Keep the strength work. Ice if it's sore. Come back if it swells or it hurts at night." That's all. No MRI, no brace, no crutches. On the way out, Mom buys 2 cans of warm corn soup from the machine and gives him 1.

**What the scene does:** Satoshi, at 20, never sat in this chair at 2 out of 10. He sat in it at the end, at 10. The reader can feel the difference without a line of explanation. Keep the doctor bored. Boredom is the healing.

### 5.5 Mio
That night he tells her (the Arc 2 terms: all of it). Sample:
- "Did you tell Coach?" "Before practice." "Before?" "Before." She writes. Then she turns to a new page and writes *KNEE* at the top and draws 2 columns: *Stairs* and *Wedge*. "I'm doing this 1 too." "Takeda-sensei's doing it." "Then there's 2."
- Next morning at the genkan, she checks his knees the way she always does, and for the first time she does it openly, crouched, looking, instead of pretending to tie her shoe. He stands still for it. That's the chapter's end hook, out of Mio's soul: her secret check has become a job he lets her do.

### 5.6 Rules for the author
- Never dramatic: no music in the narration, no flashback in the doorway, no "his heart stopped". The knee gets 1 scene (the telling), 1 optional scene (the clinic), 1 home scene (Mio), and then numbers in 1 line a day until they're 0.
- Never a setback that costs a match. The knee week sits in late September, a month before the representative round, so it can't threaten the final and nobody pretends it might.
- Never cured by talent. It gets better because he did less and told someone.
- The knee never recurs in Arc 4. If the author wants a callback, it's Sho writing *0/0* in the ledger the morning of the final, and Takeda reading it.

## 7. Eye and map upkeep (what the rules are now)

- **The screen rule, checked against the chapters:** Arc 2's doctor limited screens to 1 hour a day, never before bed, which meant no match tape for the ex-analyst (Arc 3 report, section 1). At the pre-camp check (ch 35 to 36) it rose to **2 hours a day, still not before bed** (arc3.md: "You can have 2 hours of screens now. Still not before bed."). Nekomata showed him tape at camp and Ukai said "Don't use them all." So **match tape is allowed now, within 2 hours, never at night.**
- **Arc 4 rule (Sho's own, written on page 2 of *SHO 2*)** [design]: *Tape: 20 min a day. Kitchen timer. Never after 9.* The danger isn't his eyes any more; it's 3:14 a.m. and the rewind button (ch 34: he dismissed his own alarm without noticing). Mom's rule covers the night; the timer covers him. Takeda brings Inter-High footage of Seijoh and Shiratorizawa in October; Sho watches it in 20-minute pieces at the kitchen table where Mom can see the screen.
- **The eye itself:** no training. It's Conditioned (end of Arc 3): it comes most rallies when he's settled, he can invite it, tired eyes after a long day and nothing more. The ace seed (reading blockers' hands a blink early) stays rare and uninvited in Arc 4 (bible). No drill targets it.
- **The map:** Field map Lv1 forms at Ougiminami (week 2). After that it's kept by playing: 6 v 6 and scrimmages. No more pudding cups.
- **Traffic Light (Brock string):** down to 2 minutes, only on long-screen days, Dad at the far end. 1 line in the whole arc is plenty.
- **Sunday walks with Dad:** the hill road every Sunday, about 1 hour. Dad walks; Sho walks. In Arc 3 he counted cars at the edge of his eyes; in Arc 4 the walk becomes the knee's test (week 6) and one conversation, the only one in the arc where Dad starts it. Sample: Dad: "Your sister measures you." "On Sundays." "Mm." A long stretch of road. "She keeps the chalk in her pencil case." That's the scene.

## 11. Guardrails

- **1 exercise, 1 number, 1 counter per scene.** No montages, no "over the next weeks", no lists of lifts in prose. Takeda's clipboard can hold the list; the page holds 1 line of it.
- **Ukai decides.** Sho proposes (shameless: "Please cross out the stupid ones"); Ukai crosses out, sets ceilings, decides minutes and the knee plan.
- **The knee stays small.** Told before practice, the same day, at 2 out of 10; nothing happens; that's the point. Never explain the Satoshi parallel.
- **Protected moments:** Yamaguchi's float (Sho's jump float is flatter and faster and moves less, and it's never in Yamaguchi's pinch slot); Asahi's jump serve; Tsukishima's blocking (Sho never calls it, including in the bad-ball drill); the team's synchronized attack is the team's, and Sho's back-row hit lives inside it; Hinata and Kageyama's quick (no notes); Tanaka's arc (the wing rivalry stays loud and generous; Sho doesn't take Tanaka's spot in this calendar except in practice 6 v 6).
- **1 line of the ace per chapter**, even in match chapters: a jump count in the ledger, a reach number, a log line.
- **Numbers as digits, said by people.** The reader learns 318, 320, 321, 323, 325 from Mio's mouth, never from narration.
