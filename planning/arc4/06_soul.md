## 1. The arc's questions

**Surface question:** *Can he play all 3 sets in a match that counts?* It is written at the bottom of SHO 2, and Oikawa said it at the sink in June. It points straight at the Seijoh rematch. Readers can follow it without any soul at all: will Ukai play him, will his body hold, will Oikawa break him.

**Real question:** *Will he say it out loud: "it hurts" and "give it to me"?*

Arc 3 asked what he becomes when the talent is his. The answer was an all-rounder who can be on the court and enjoy it. Arc 4 asks the ace's question, which is about **asking**. Satoshi's whole wound is 2 things he never said: that the knee hurt, and that he wanted the ball. He went quiet on both at once. Sho's shamelessness has always been about cheap asks (meat buns, tosses in practice, "teach me that"). He has never yet made an expensive ask: one that can fail in front of everyone, in a match that counts.

**The answer, and the twist that keeps it from being predictable:**
1. **The knee turns out to be the easy one.** Readers have been primed since ch 1 to expect the knee to be the climax. It comes in week 5 of the program, on a Thursday, on the stairs. He tells Ukai before practice. Ukai says "Show me", looks, says no jumping till Monday, and goes to put the net up. Nothing happens. That is where Satoshi's wound heals, and the narration never says so.
2. **The ball is the hard one.** At Seijoh, late in set 3, Oikawa has made Sho's bad balls the weak link on purpose and Iwaizumi has stuffed him twice. The 3rd bad ball goes up. Every reason in the world says let Tanaka have it. His hand goes up and he shouts "KAGEYAMA! 1 MORE!" This is the 1st real "1 more", and it is the climax of his arc, a full 2 matches before the climax of the team's.
3. **The final belongs to other people, and he's fine on the bench.** Against Shiratorizawa he plays a smaller job (back-row receive, a pinch serve, 1 rotation on the wing). His one big play is a dig on Ushijima where his arms hold. Most of the 5 sets he is on the bench, shouting, holding bottles. "Every minute on a bench feels like that room" [Soul sheet] has quietly stopped being true. Mio sees it (POV option).

**What changes in him (checklist for the outline):**
- Tells the body's truth -> it becomes ordinary (no one praises it; it's a line in the notebook between squat numbers).
- Shameless about small things -> shameless about the 1 thing that costs: the ball after failing.
- Watches the ace's ball go to others (ch 41 Tanaka's, ch 40 "we can't both have it") -> gives Hinata his bad ball freely, then asks for his own.
- The notebook as hiding place -> the notebook as a log someone else also writes in (Takeda writes the knee; Tanaka's squat numbers go in it).
- The analyst at night -> closes the camcorder at 11 because sleep is training.

**What does not move:** cold, counting, polite, a sub, shameless, never scared of his power. Power C to B by the end (earned in the weight room). He still can't hit through Ushijima's block. "Then enough to hit it" stays open for Arc 5.

## 2. Design principle: 1 gesture, 2 directions

The hand carries 3 meanings in this story and nobody ever spells them out [bible: The hand, meaning 2]:
1. Satoshi's hand that stayed down in Osaka.
2. Sho's flat hand at shoulder height to come off (Arc 3, now barely used; his hand went halfway up "scared" in ch 38; Mio: "tell it it's fine").
3. Asahi's hand up to ask for the ball: "SUGA! ONE MORE!"

Arc 4 uses the hand twice, in 2 directions:
- **The knee:** the hand goes up to say *stop*. It's quiet and nearly invisible: he doesn't even raise his hand; he just walks up to Ukai at the gym door. The rule has become a conversation.
- **The ball:** the hand goes up to say *give it to me*. Loud, in a full gym, in Oikawa's face.

The 1st is told at the door of the 2nd gym with 2 adults present. The 2nd is shouted across a tournament court. Both are the same muscle. The prose never says so; the reader feels the arc close because the hand goes up literally on the "1 more" (his right arm up from behind the 3 m line, calling).

**Layer for the attentive reader only (optional, never stated):** "1 more" was also what Satoshi told himself at 3:14 a.m. with his thumb on the rewind button ("1 more clip"). The night before Seijoh, Sho catches his thumb doing it over Yachi's camcorder, stops, and goes to bed. Next day he says the same 2 words for the ball. If the author finds this too neat, cut the night scene's thought and keep only the action (he shuts the camcorder at 11:00).

## 4. The knee moment, designed scene by scene

**Placement:** week 5 of the program, a Thursday in late September, about 4 weeks before the tournament. Far enough that the tournament isn't about the knee; close enough that readers feel a small chill.

**Cause:** Wednesday was the 1st session of box jumps off Dad's 40 cm box at volume (or the 1st depth jumps Ukai approved). Thursday morning the left knee is sore just below the kneecap, going downstairs. It's the patellar tendon, the most ordinary complaint a jumping 15-year-old has. Sore, 2 out of 10, gone in days with less load. The ace agent can confirm the realism. It must never be the ACL and never be dramatic.

**Scene A. The stairs (6:40 a.m.)**
- The 3rd step from the bottom tells him. 1 beat of interior: a concrete stairwell in an Osaka dormitory, and 3 months of going down it sideways. 1 sentence, then gone.
- The analyst's report forms by itself, correct: load response, tendon, reduce volume, ice, 48 hours. He could manage it himself, and he'd be right.
- He doesn't think a decision. He puts his shoes on 15 minutes early. (Action only.)
- Mom at the table puts the miso down and looks at his left knee, then his face.

> "Left," said Mom.
> "It's sore. From the box. I'm telling Coach before practice."
> Mom nodded and went back to the rice cooker. When he left, the bento was heavier.

**Scene B. The gym door (4:20 p.m., before practice).** Who is there: Takeda unlocking, Ukai arriving with the ball bag, Tanaka coming round the corner at the end. Nobody else.

> "Coach."
> "Mm."
> "My left knee. Just below the kneecap. It's sore going downstairs. Since this morning. It's fine walking. I think it's Wednesday's box jumps. I think it's the tendon. It's load, so if I drop the volume for..."
> "Show me," said Ukai.
> He put the ball bag down on the step.
> "Stand on the left. Squat. Slow."
> Sho stood on his left leg on the concrete and went down slowly. Ukai crouched in front of him with his forearms on his knees and watched the knee, not his face.
> "Again."
> He went down again.
> "Where's it hurt?"
> "At the bottom. 2 out of 10."
> Ukai put his thumb under the kneecap and pressed. "There?"
> "Yes."
> Ukai stood up and picked up the ball bag.
> "No jumping. No boxes, no blocking, no hitting till Monday. You do receive from the floor. Ice after, 15 minutes. If it's there Monday, we go to the clinic."
> "Yes, Coach."
> Takeda had his notebook open against the door. He wrote, and then looked up. "Below the kneecap... since this morning?"
> "Since this morning."
> Takeda wrote the date.

Tanaka, arriving:

> "What's wrong with his knee?"
> "It's sore," said Ukai. "He told me. Go put the net up."
> Tanaka looked at Sho. "Sore how?"
> "Just sore, senpai."
> Tanaka looked at the knee as if it had insulted him. "...Okay." He went in.

Ukai turns to go in, stops:

> "Kiyose."
> Sho waited.
> Ukai looked at him for a second as if he were going to say something else. "Ice. Not heat."
> "Yes, Coach."

**What is said:** 2 lines of report, cut off. "Show me." A plan with a date. A practical correction.
**What isn't said (and must not be):** "Good, you told me" (Ukai said it at camp in ch 42; now it's ordinary and needs no praise). "ACL." "3 months." "I was scared." "Thank you" in any big way. Sho's 2-minute report (Ukai doesn't need it; a coach looks). Anything about Satoshi. The word "heal." Ukai's almost-sentence stays unsaid.

**Scene C. Practice.** He does floor receive at the side, ice afterward with Kiyoko's ice pack (no words). The gym sounds like a gym. Practice goes on. Nobody makes a fuss. That is the whole effect: readers waited 50 chapters for this and nothing happens.

Hinata, mid-practice, sweating:

> "Why aren't you jumping?"
> "Knee's sore."
> Hinata looked at his knee. He looked at it for a long time, for Hinata, which was about 2 seconds. He opened his mouth and shut it.
> "Then don't jump," said Hinata, and ran back to the net.

(Hinata, who hates the bench, telling someone not to jump. He remembered "His knee." Never stated.)

**Scene D. The genkan (7:30 p.m.).** Mio on the step. He speaks before she looks.

> "My left knee's sore."
> Her hands stopped on the notebook.
> "From the box. I told Coach before practice. No jumping till Monday. Ice."
> "Before practice."
> "Yes."
> "Same day."
> "Same day."
> She got up and went to the freezer, and came back with the edamame, and pressed the bag on his knee with both hands, harder than she needed to. They sat on the step.
> "Does it mean you stop?"
> "It means I stop jumping till Monday."
> "And Monday?"
> "If it's still sore, Coach takes me to the clinic. And I tell you what they say."
> "All of it."
> "All of it."
> She wrote. He read it upside down: *Knee (left). Sore. Told Coach. SAME DAY.* She underlined *SAME DAY* twice.
> "Is that all?"
> He checked, properly. "That's all."

**Scene E. SHO 2, that night.** 1 line among the squat numbers and Tanaka's bigger numbers: *Thu. Knee (L), below the kneecap. Told Coach before practice. No jumping till Mon.* Takeda's notebook has the same line in nicer handwriting. The narration notes only that 2 notebooks have it. Optional: Satoshi at 20 kept a notebook too, and the knee was never in it. 1 clause at most, or cut.

**Monday (Beat 9):** nothing. Stairs 2 at a time. Dad's 2nd box: "For landings."

**Never** make the knee come back in the tournament. **Never** let it be a threat in the final. The 1 allowed echo: in the final, landing after the Ushijima dig, his left knee tracks over his toe "the way it did now on its own" (ch 43 phrasing). Nothing more.

**Where:** vs Aoba Johsai, the semifinal, set 3, late (about 21-22 to 23-23; the final points are canon Kageyama and Oikawa territory [protected], so this point comes before them and keeps Karasuno alive). Canon set 3 is 26-24 [bible timeline].

**Why Seijoh and not the final (argued):**

**Alternative (weaker):** the final, set 4, against Tendou's guess block. Flag: it would crowd Asahi's and Tanaka's moments.

**Set-up across the match (tactics agent fills mechanics):**
- Sho is on court in all 3 sets: back-row sub for Asahi in each set (Ukai's ch 22 echo: on Oikawa's serve rotation), plus a front-row stint for Tanaka in set 2 when Seijoh has Tanaka's timing (Tanaka comes back in and scores, per beat 6's promise).

So Seijoh pushes their free balls and digs to make Karasuno's 2nd ball go bad toward Sho's side, and Iwaizumi and Kindaichi (and Kyoutani, wild) wait for it.
- Set 3: 2 bad balls to Sho, 2 stuffs. The 2nd at a bad moment. Oikawa holds up 2 fingers at the net, smiling. Ukai looks at the paddle. Asahi on the bench, bottle with the cap on.

**The rally:**
- Seijoh serve; Karasuno attack; Seijoh dig; a long rally; the ball comes back ugly. Kageyama has to dig or move; it goes up high and drifting, out of system. Tanaka at the left calls for it: "KAGEYAMA!" (He's earned the right; his want is real.)

- His right arm goes up. "KAGEYAMA! 1 MORE!"
- Kageyama sets him. The ball Sho asked for in September: higher, off the net. He decides at the top (map; whatever the tactics agent gives him: Kyoutani off his position, a seam).
- It goes in.

**Why it costs (all of it shown, none of it explained):**
- 2 public failures in the 3rd years' last tournament, in a single-elimination match, in front of his family.
- It's the ball Oikawa is baiting him into. Asking is playing into the plan.
- Tanaka called too. Sho takes the ball from the senpai who said "I'm not giving it you." Tanaka's reaction (his own want): 1st to hit Sho on the back, then, low: "Next 1, I'm yelling louder."
- Ukai had looked at the paddle. Asking is also asking Coach to leave him on.
- Satoshi's hand stayed down at 20, in the last 2 months, when he was scared.

**Reactions (small, plain):**
- Kageyama, walking back to his spot: "Oi." "...Yes." "Louder. Next time." (He wants hitters who call; the King's wound, never said.)
- The bench: Asahi stands up. Suga, beside him, looks at Asahi. Asahi's ears go pink. Nobody says anything. (Suga was the one Asahi shouted at, in ch 12. The mirror stays in their faces.)
- Oikawa: the smile goes. He looks at Kageyama, not at Sho. (The setter story stays the centre: "Tobio chose him.")
- Mom, Dad and Mio in the stands. Dad says, much later, on the walk home: "The 1 where you shouted." "Yes." "...Mm." That's all.

**What must not happen:** no one names it "like Asahi"; no narration line "for the 1st time he asked"; no slow-motion speech; no tears from Sho. He lands, knee over the toe, and goes back to his spot.

## 8. Where tension comes from (so chapters never lag)

Every chapter carries at least 2 of these engines, and each engine is a want against a want:

1. **Minutes (Ukai vs Sho).** Sho wants the wing; Ukai keeps Tanaka starting and says why. Every lineup read is a small stake.
2. **Tanaka (rival in the room and on court).** Who lifts more, who gets the bad ball, who comes back in.
3. **The body clock.** Numbers that go up slowly; the knee week; Power C to B earned rep by rep. A training chapter always has 1 number someone counts and 1 person counting it.
4. **Single elimination and the 3rd years' clock.** Any lost match ends Daichi's, Suga's and Asahi's high school volleyball. Sho is the 1 who sees it clearly and stops himself counting.
5. **Hinata's claim and Hinata's hunger.** The bad ball promised, given, then open. Hinata wants to be the ace too.
6. **Opponents who want things.** Oikawa's last chance; Kyoutani's wildness; Terushima's fun; Wakutani's pride; Ushijima's contempt for Karasuno [memory]; Tendou's joy in guessing.
7. **The shadow.** The analyst at night with the camcorder; sleep vs tape.
8. **The family's stake.** Mio's fear about knees; the magnet; the family in the stands.
9. **The untested ask.** From beat 7's cheap "1 more, please" to the expensive 1. Readers know the notebook line *1 more.* and wait for it.

**Lag risks and fixes:**
- Training chapters (Act 1) are the danger. Rule: each has a conversation with an agenda (Tanaka on the stairs, Asahi on the floor, Ukai's lineup, Kageyama's ball) and a hook from someone's want. No montage, 1 exercise, 1 number, 1 counter [bible Guardrails].
- Easy matches (Ougiminami, Kakugawa) carry a soul beat each (Hinata's bad ball; Sho vs a 2 m reach) so they earn their length.
- The knee chapter must not be padded with dread. It's short in effect and ordinary in tone; give the chapter another engine (the wing test aftermath, Tanaka) so it doesn't lean on the knee.

## 14. One-paragraph pitch (for the bible)

Arc 4 is where the ace is built and where Satoshi's 2 silences end. The surface question is SHO 2's last line and Oikawa's dare: can he play all 3 sets in a match that counts? The real question is whether he'll say it out loud: "it hurts" and "give it to me". In a room under the old gym, with a rack from 1990, Ukai makes him a wing spiker and Tanaka turns up uninvited and lifts more. Dad builds a box nobody asked for; Mio keeps a REACH page. In week 5 his left knee is sore on the stairs, he tells Ukai before practice, Ukai says "Show me" and goes to put the net up, and nothing happens: the wound Satoshi carried for 9 years closes without a word. The hard one is the ball. He gives Hinata the bad ball he was promised, defers to Ennoshita, plays all 3 sets against Oikawa, gets stuffed twice on the bad balls Oikawa set up for him, and puts his arm up anyway: "KAGEYAMA! 1 MORE!" Against Shiratorizawa his arms hold Ushijima's spike, Tendou's guess beats him, and he spends most of 5 sets on the bench shouting for other people, and the bench isn't the monitor room anymore. Mio carries the magnet in her pocket, and at home she puts it on a real January.
