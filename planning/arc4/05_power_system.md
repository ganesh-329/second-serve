## 0. The 1-paragraph version

Arc 3 made the eye cheap. Arc 4 makes it **ordinary and small next to the ace.** The Conditioned eye stays exactly what the ch 45 card says: about 1 blink ahead (author: about 0.2 s), 1 player, hitters and setters only, mostly on his own when he's settled and the player is studied, invitable only in dead time (between rallies). It costs nothing in a 2-set match and costs **time** in a 5-set final: the ghost arrives later and thinner as his eyes tire, so the lead shrinks toward nothing by set 5 and he stops asking. That is the arc's quiet engine: **the eye runs out, the arms don't.** Meanwhile the map grows a level. At Ougiminami, in a loud official gym where his ears are useless, Half map becomes **Field map Lv1**: both sides of the net in 1 silent top-down picture, updated on every touch. He discovers it by its absence: the eye fires, the board freezes, and in the afterglow he realizes there was a board. For attack the eye does almost nothing directly (blockers stay unreadable by rule, with a mechanical reason: at 1 blink, a blocker's ghost arrives after Sho has already left the floor). It helps the ace indirectly: an early dig buys the steps for a full transition approach. The map is the ace's tool (where the hole is); the weight room is what goes through it. Arc 4 ends with Power C to B and Serve C to B on the card, the eye still Conditioned, the map at Field map Lv1, and 3 notebook lines that make Inarizaki's on-command awakening feel inevitable: *Blockers decide after I go. I see them too late.* / *Asked it in a rally. It came. Late.* / *3 good ones a set. Then it's tired.*

**What changes from Arc 3 to Arc 4** is only 3 things, and all are behavioral, not power:
1. **Who the eye goes to.** In Arc 3 it went to whoever was loudest (Kenma's bait). In Arc 4 the Field map chooses the player and Sho invites the eye on that one between rallies. "The map picks; the eye confirms."
2. **What he does with the time.** In Arc 3 the early read became a dig. In Arc 4 the early dig becomes a **swing**: the steps he saves go into his transition approach (section 2).
3. **How often he leaves it alone.** On purpose, more often, every time the ball is the ace's job. Ch 44's last ball (map only, eye left alone) is the Arc 4 default for big swings.

## 2. The eye and the ace

### 2.1 The rule in one line

**It reads hitters and setters, not blockers** (author's rule, kept for all of Arc 4). The bible's ace-path row that seeds "the blockers' hands a blink early" in Arc 4 is **superseded** by this report's recommendation: keep blockers dark in Arc 4, and earn the change in Arc 5 with the 0.5 s lead (section 6). Flag for the author: if the author wants 1 blocker flicker in Arc 4, the only place it costs nothing is the last 2 points of set 5 of the final, and I recommend against it.

### 2.2 Why blockers stay dark (author mechanics, never stated in prose)

A blocker decides after the setter, and Sho's own swing is decided at the plant. At 1 blink of lead, a blocker's ghost would arrive after Sho has left the floor with his arm already back. The information exists too late to use. So at Conditioned the eye doesn't bother: it lands on whoever's body is deciding the rally's next touch, and when Sho attacks, that body is his own. This gives the Arc 5 change a real reason: at 0.5 s on command, a blocker's ghost arrives during Sho's approach, in time to change the shot. The author never has to explain this; the reader feels it when the Inarizaki moment works and nothing in Arc 4 did.

### 2.3 How the eye serves an attacker anyway (3 indirect ways)

1. **The early dig is a head start for the swing (weapon: Shunpo).** A dig 1 blink early leaves him balanced and nearer the line he needs. He's out to the antenna, or back behind the 3 m line for the pipe, before the setter touches the ball. Concretely on the page: dig, 4 steps out, open, and Kageyama's set finds a hitter who's already loaded. Kill counts in transition are the marker (Takeda or Yachi counts: transition swings with a full approach, 2 of 10 in September to 6 of 10 by the final).
2. **Serve receive to attack.** A clean first pass off an invited read on a scouted server means he's not the passer who arrives late to hit (Ukai's ch 38 lesson, now used against him by Shiratorizawa: they serve him to make him pass). The eye buys back that half step.
3. **Reading his own setter? Never.** He doesn't need to: he asks Kageyama for a set in words (canon, Kageyama asks hitters what they want). The eye never touches Kageyama or Hinata and Kageyama's quick.

### 2.4 What the ace actually uses

| Tool | What it does on attack | Arc 4 page proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field map Lv1 | Shows the block's shape after the set (where the hands *are*, seen live with wide gaze) and the floor that's empty | The seam, the empty chair, the line left open by a cheating libero |
| Arms (weight room) | Goes through the hole, hits the high bad ball, tools a real block, holds a platform | Power C to B |

## 3. Meta Vision: Field map Lv1 at Ougiminami

### 3.1 What Half map was (end of Arc 3)

A wordless picture of the **opponent's side**, with dark places where things can't happen (Ikkei's ruling out), the **own side by ear** (passes, sets and hits by sound), a running file on every setter and hitter. Visual: the far court with parts gone dark. Own side: sounds.

### 3.2 What Field map Lv1 is

| Field | Half map | Field map Lv1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | Far side by sight; own side by ear | **Both sides by sight, as 1 picture** |
| Viewpoint | From his own eyes | From above: the court as a board, the way Satoshi drew 10,000 plays on a magnet board in a dark room. Both nets' worth of people as dots with a facing |
| Update | Between touches, by memory | **On every touch.** A soft "tick" (a felt click, no sound) |
| Own teammates | Heard | **Known without looking**, "the way you know where your own hand is" |
| Ruling out | Yes | Yes, both sides: he also knows where his own side can't cover |
| Future | None | **None.** It shows now. Where players are about to go is Lv2 (Predictive, Nationals) |
| With the eye | The switch (Kai): drop the map, take the ghost, rebuild by ear | **The freeze:** the board stops on its last tick while the eye fires and through the afterglow, then picks up again. It doesn't have to be rebuilt; it has gaps |
| Cost | Little | Mental fatigue in long rallies (every touch is a tick). Shrinks back to Half map when tired (own side goes 1st) |

### 3.3 Why it forms at Ougiminami (the conditions, all on the page)

1. **His ears die.** The 1st official gym of the autumn [unsure which venue for round 1; canon agent to confirm], cheering, drums, a 2nd court's whistles, the echo. Arc 3 proved the sound layer breaks in big gyms (ch 32 at Fukurodani). Without ears, the own side has to come from somewhere.
2. **The wide gaze is ready.** Ikkei's lesson ("Look at the middle. Let the edges come to you.") was "slow for a month, maybe 2" in July. September is month 2.
3. **The match is easy.** 25-16, 25-13 (bible canon row). An easy match gives a quiet head and long stretches on court. The map can't form under pressure; it forms when nothing is at stake and he's been playing long enough to stop narrating.
4. **September training earned it** (counted markers, so it's no gift):
   - **Tanaka's pointing drill (5 a.m., chalk scores; replaces the eyes-shut sound reads):** Sho faces the far side; Tanaka moves anywhere on Sho's side and freezes; Sho points without turning his head. 5/10, then 7/10, then 9/10 the week before the prelims. Tanaka: "How are you doing that?" "You breathe loud, senpai." (A joke that's half true; the reader knows the ears are being retired.)
   - **Noise:** Takeda borrows the brass band's practice room tape, or the neighborhood association's drum [invented; Shimada and Takinoue's cheering is canon later, confirm]; Ukai plays it on a radio in the 2nd gym during 6-on-6. His sound reads drop to 3/10. Correct: the drill is meant to kill them.
   - **Yachi's tape:** she films the 6-on-6 from the gallery rail (a high angle). Sho watches 1 rally from above, once, within his screen limit, and the picture stays in his head.

### 3.4 How it looks the 1st time (scene sample, prose guidance)

Karasuno lead set 2 by a lot. Sho is back row, right. Noya is behind him, left, and the gym is too loud to hear anyone's feet. A free ball comes over. Sho invites the eye on Ougiminami's setter during the dead ball before it (it comes, thin, no file on this setter). The eye fires on the next set: the setter's ghost goes back-set. And for the length of the afterglow **something stops**: a picture he didn't know he was holding, Noya a step left of him, Tanaka at the net, Kageyama running in, every Ougiminami player, all of it frozen like a paused screen. Then the ghost fades and the picture starts again, tick, with Noya already moving. He moves out of Noya's way without looking. Noya digs. Later, between rallies, he tries to see it on purpose and can't (it's wordless, it doesn't come when you look at it). He finds it again by not looking: it's there whenever he stops checking.

Sample notebook line that night, the only explanation the reader gets:
*Ougiminami. Couldn't hear anything. Knew where Noya-senpai was anyway. / It stopped when the eye came. That's how I knew it was there.*

Sample dialogue (plain):
- Noya: "You moved before I called it." "You were there." "I didn't say anything!" "I know."
- Ukai, after the match, to Takeda, not to Sho: "He didn't look behind him once." Takeda: "Is that good?" Ukai: "It's IQ." (Ukai's word, used once in Arc 4 and no more.)

### 3.5 What Field map Lv1 does for the ace

It shows where the block's hands are and where the floor is empty in one picture, live. In Arc 3 the empty chair came from memory and ruling out; in Arc 4 it's simply seen. That's why Blind Spot (Arc 3's off-speed into the empty chair) gets better without a new name, and why the high bad ball becomes his: out of system, no setter, he can see the whole floor from above while running at the ball.

### 3.6 Limits shown on the page

- It freezes when the eye fires: once in Arc 4 a 2nd hitter moves during the freeze and Sho misses that he's the one now unguarded (Johzenji, who love 2nd hitters).
- It has no future: Johzenji's improvising and Kyoutani's madness both happen "inside" a correct map.
- It costs in long rallies: a 30-touch rally at deuce leaves him a beat slow on the next serve receive.
- Shrinks when tired: own side goes dark first, back to Half map (section 4).

## 4. Fatigue in 5 sets (the final vs Shiratorizawa)

Canon scores kept (bible): 16-25, 31-29, 18-25, 29-27, 21-19 [bible canon timeline; set scores from canon per bible]. Sho's body (Stamina B, Toji-style) is fine the whole match. **The eyes and the head are what tire.**

### 4.1 The curve (author mechanics)

| Set | Eye (lead / how it comes) | Map | What it means on court |

**Why time and not pain:** the user's rule is that the eye is talent and doesn't hurt. A muscle that's tired doesn't hurt; it's slower. A slower eye is the cleanest visual cost: the ghost and the body arrive closer together until they're 1 thing. In prose: "The ghost came. It was barely ahead of him now, like a shadow at noon." Nothing more.

### 4.2 The "1 more" sits on top of the eye running out (recommendation)

The ace path plans Sho's 1st real "1 more" late in a tight big set. I recommend **set 4 of the final, at deuce (between 25-25 and 29-27)**, after the mid-rally ask came late. The eye is done for the set; the map is down to half; he's just been aced or stuffed. And his hand goes up to ask for the ball. The power system's job here is only to make sure the reader feels that nothing extra is helping him: the next kill is weight room, approach and the map he has left. Never state the symmetry with the hand-up rule. (Final placement is the structure agent's call; Seijoh set 3 at 26-24 is the 2nd-best slot. Flag: Asahi's canon "1 more" moments stay his; Sho's is a separate ball.)

Sample, after the point (plain):
- Kageyama, at the next dead ball: "Again?" Sho: "Yes." (Kageyama's "Again" from ch 43, now a question.)

### 4.3 The eye in set 5: one clean ghost with no lead

Once in set 5 the eye comes on its own on Ushijima, with nearly no lead. The ghost and Ushijima's arm are the same arm. Sho is already where he decided to be from the map and Shiratorizawa's file (Ushijima's best ball in a tight 5th). The arms hold: weapon Tekkai, the pay-off of "Enough to hold Bokuto's line". The dig goes up. Whether Karasuno score off it is the match agents' call; my recommendation is that it doesn't score directly (a teammate's ball does), so the dig is an ace's arms, never a cheat.

### 4.4 Ukai sees the curve

Takeda keeps a tally Ukai asked for in September ("Write down when he's early.") without knowing why. Set 1: 5 marks. Set 3: 2. Set 4: 1. At the set 4 to 5 break: Ukai: "You're later on him than in the 1st." "Tired eyes." "Eyes." Beat. "Then hit." That's the closest Ukai comes (section 7) and it's also the coach choosing the ace over the reader.

### 4.5 After the match

Tired eyes, the kind that make the scoreboard lights soft. No clinic, no drama. At home: Mio's *FAST-FORWARD* page gets a column per set (5, 3, 2, 1, 1) because he tells her all of it (the bet). Mio: "It got tired." "Yes." "Did you?" "No." She writes *ARMS: did it* on the *REACH* page, not the eye page.

## 5. The road to Full awakening Lv1 (Inarizaki, Arc 5): set up, never spent

Full awakening Lv1 = **on command, mid-rally, 1 player, about 0.5 s ahead; cost: about 3 clean reads per set before it blurs** (bible ladder). Arc 4 must make each piece feel near without delivering it.

| Lv1 piece | What Arc 4 shows | Where | Notebook line (the only explanation) |

**What Arc 4 must never do:** an on-time mid-rally command; any lead longer than 1 blink; 2 players at once; a blocker's ghost; a fusion of eye inside the full map (that's pro era; ch 22's forced fusion stays the only one).

**The emotional set-up:** Arc 4 ends with the ace winning with arms while the eye fades. So when the eye arrives on command at Inarizaki, it reads as Sho finally getting a tool he's earned the right to, after proving he can win without it.

## 10. Pitfalls and guardrails

### Power creep
- **Hard caps for Arc 4:** lead 1 blink, never more; 1 player; hitters and setters only; invitation in dead time only (1 late mid-rally exception in the final); map tops out at Field map Lv1; 3 cards.
- **The eye loses on the page in every match after Ougiminami:** noisy ghosts (Kakugawa, Johzenji, Kyoutani), the tool (Wakutani), the late body (Oikawa), the wall it can't see (Tendou), the power it can't stop (Ushijima set 1), fatigue (sets 3 to 5).
- **Canon results stand** (bible): Karasuno win all 6, Seijoh 2-1, Shiratorizawa 3-2 with canon set scores. Sho changes rallies, never the winners.
- **Every rank change needs a counted marker and a match moment** (section 8). No growth during a montage.

### Eye as a cheat
- **Never** shows the ball after contact; never shows a blocker; never decides a match point alone. Big points are arms plus map.
- **Never** touches Hinata and Kageyama's quick, Tsukishima's blocks (his Ushijima block above all), Hinata vs Ushijima, Yamaguchi's floaters, Asahi's jump serve, Ennoshita's leadership, Kyoutani's debut, Oikawa and Kageyama's setter story. Sho can stand at the edge of Tsukishima's shadow (Kagemane, Arc 3), but he never calls the block.
- Ushijima's clean ghost must be shown as useless without arms (set 1) before it's useful with them (set 5).
- Opponents stay smart: Oikawa adapts, Tendou guesses right more than wrong, Washijo's plan (serve the passer, feed Ushijima) works on Sho more than once.

### Explaining too much
- No numbers for the lead in prose ("a blink", "barely ahead", "a shadow at noon"). No "0.2 s". No talk of why blockers are dark.
- New ideas arrive as a rally told in order first, then 1 notebook line at most.
- Weapon names: 1 per chapter, as a thought, never aloud, each once.
- The Field map is wordless: describe what he knows (Noya's position), never a diagram in prose beyond the 1st freeze.
- Never explain the hand (off for the eye in Arc 2 and 3, up to ask in Arc 4), the Satoshi parallels, or the Hinata mirror.

### Secrecy
- Nobody names it; nobody sees the ghost; 1 near-miss per chapter; every near-miss off court, ending on a question.
- Ukai's "IQ" once in Arc 4 at most; "Tired eyes." "Eyes." "Then hit." is his whole step forward.

### The ace wins the arc
- Every chapter has 1 line of the ace (gym number, reach mark, a swing). The eye can go whole chapters without appearing.
- The knee moment (told to Coach 1st, the same day) never touches the eye and never gets a weapon name.
- The eye's arc in Arc 4 is to step back so the ace can step forward. If a draft has the eye winning a big point, rewrite so the arms win it.
