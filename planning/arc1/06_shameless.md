## 0. The diagnosis in 1 paragraph

In Arc 1, "shameless" was read as "has a comeback". Sho's asks are buried inside pitches ("Think of it as an investment"), his failures are covered with a joke before anyone can see them ("Testing the ball's density"), and almost every exchange ends with Sho holding the last line. That is the opposite of the bible's idea. Lloyd's shamelessness is that he doesn't protect himself: he asks plainly, gets refused, asks again, fails in front of everyone, complains, and works until 3 a.m. Sho's quips are armor. A man with nothing left to protect doesn't need armor. In Arc 2 the quips mostly stop after ch 17, which is good, but the shamelessness goes with them: Sho turns quiet and grave, and from ch 18 to 29 there are only about 8 real asks in 12 chapters. Arc 3 gets it right: "Tomorrow too. Please. And Friday." [a3:277], "Please bully me too. The same as Lev." [a3:2462], "Can I start on Monday?" [a3:5259]. Plain words, a real want, and the other person's face carries the comedy.

### 1.2 How the shamelessness works (reading of the series) [memory]

1. **He asks for everything, out loud, at once.** Money, help, a better price, a favor from someone who outranks him. There's no lead-up and no shame in it. He'll grovel the second he's outmatched, because pride costs more than kneeling. [memory]
2. **He haggles over small things and is generous about big ones.** He fights over a coin and then spends a fortune on the project that matters. The pettiness is the visible part; the investment is the real part. [memory]
3. **He complains he's lazy and frail, and then outworks everyone.** He moans that he wants to retire rich and do nothing, and then he's up all night drawing plans. The complaint is sincere. So is the work. Nobody (including Lloyd) treats the 2 as a contradiction. [memory]
4. **He takes credit loudly.** When something works, he says so, and the people around him groan because it's true. [memory]
5. **He copies and uses whatever exists.** His whole skill set is someone else's knowledge (modern engineering) applied without apology. [memory; matches the premise in the snippet]
6. **The comedy lives in other people's faces.** The panels that land are reaction panels: his knight (Javier) going flat and exhausted, nobles steamrolled into a deal they didn't mean to make, family members horrified at his manners. Lloyd's own lines are often blunt, even boring: a price, a request, a complaint. The art does the joke. [memory]
7. **People end up respecting him against their will.** Exasperation turns into grudging trust, because he keeps delivering. That turn is slow and earned. [memory]
8. **It hides something serious.** He knows how the original story ends for his family (ruin, death) and he's racing it, with a debt hanging over the house. The greed and the shamelessness are a man buying time for people he's come to love. The series rarely says so out loud; you see it when he goes quiet, or when he risks himself for someone. [memory; the debt is in the snippet, the plot-ending motive is memory]

### 1.3 What to take for Sho, and what to leave

Take: plain asks, asking again after a no, haggling over tiny things, complaining then doubling the work, taking credit out loud, copying openly, zero embarrassment at being bad, and comedy that comes from other people's reactions.

Leave: Lloyd's intimidation, threats, scams and the menacing grin. Lloyd bullies people into deals. The bible's hard limit forbids that: Sho is shameless about himself, never toward a teammate. Also leave Lloyd's greed as an end in itself. Sho's currency is court time, and he spends real money (meat buns, melon bread, New Year's money) freely on other people.

The key transfer: **Lloyd's lines are mostly plain, and the scene is funny around them.** That is exactly the bible's rule 3 ("Humor comes from situations and reactions") and exactly what Arc 1 got backwards.

## 3. What shameless is for Sho at 15 (the definition writers use)

### 3.1 What it is

1. **Plain asks to seniors, said politely.** "Teach me that." "Could you toss me a few?" "Can I stay 20 more minutes?" "Serve at me." Short sentences, -san, -senpai, Coach, "please". The more he wants it, the more polite he gets (Soul sheet: "When he wants something badly, he gets more polite.").
2. **Asking again after a no, once or twice, then stopping.** "5. Underhand." "3?" "Stage." He goes back to the stage. The refusal is final when the other person makes it final (a smile, "Stage", "Out."), and he never sulks.
3. **Copying openly and saying so.** "Your toss is the same height every time. I'm going to steal it." to Yamaguchi (a2:293). He tells the person he's copying, often while he's doing it. He credits the source when it works ("Noya-senpai taught me").
4. **Taking credit out loud when it's his.** "Mine." "I read his hand." "That was mine, senpai." Said flatly, the way you'd report a fact. It's funny because 15-year-olds don't do it and Tanaka can't stand it.
5. **Complaining, then doing double.** "4:15 is too early." Then he's there at 4:15. "I hate the hill." Then he runs it twice. The complaint is real and short, and nobody comments on the contradiction.
6. **Zero embarrassment at failing in front of people.** "4. You're being generous." "41. Out of 100." "Today I hit 9. 3 went in." He reports his failures accurately to the people who could use them against him, and eats the sponge rice.
7. **Asking the people who just beat him.** "Please teach me that one too, senpai." (a1:1233) "Thank you for the lesson." (a1:1623) He bows to the man who blocked him.
8. **Asking anyone, regardless of rank or team.** Oikawa, Shimada, Yaku, Kuroo, Nekomata, the opposing captain, the coach's grandfather's friend. Pride about who to ask doesn't exist.
9. **Haggling over tiny things, never over big ones.** 20 yen on a meat bun, yes. His New Year's money for the Tokyo trip, offered at once (a2:6287).
10. **Visible wanting.** Calf raises in Ukai's eyeline (a2:842). Standing at the substitution zone before he's called. His hand up first (a1:840). The body asks before the mouth does.

### 3.2 What it isn't

- **Clever lines.** No toppers, no "technically", no "in my heart", no "it's strategy". At most 1 real joke out loud per chapter, dry, about himself, and allowed to land flat.
- **Pitches.** No lists of offers, no "I'd like to make an argument", no "Sensei, if I may".
- **Roasting people.** No "Ku. Suffering. Very fitting." No "8 out of 10, the hair costs you 2." No needling opponents (that's Oikawa's, Kuroo's and Tsukishima's register).
- **Cruelty or manipulation.** No "some people are easy", no using Noya's love of "senpai" as a lever, no invented Kobayashi to manage Asahi.
- **Winning every exchange.** He loses about half. The sharp ones (Tsukishima, Kuroo, Oikawa, Ukai) win nearly all of theirs.
- **Naming it.** He never says "shameless", "I'm never embarrassed", "I'm petty", "Shamelessly weird". Only another character may say "shameless", and only once in Arc 1 (Tsukishima, ch 4).
- **Adult or analyst words.** Investment, collateral, manifesto, budget, contract, inflation, peer review, sample size, consistency, strategy, protocol. Satoshi thinks them; Sho says "Can I?" and "He goes with Hinata every time."
- **A cover for failure.** The joke never arrives before the failure is seen.

### 3.4 The test for any line

## 4. The underneath: what the shamelessness is hiding

The Soul sheet: "a man who has already lost everything once has nothing left to protect. Asking for a toss costs nothing compared to dying at a desk." This must never be said in prose. Here is how it shows.

### 4.1 The 3 sources, and the behavior each one produces

**The bench (9 years behind a monitor).** For 9 years Satoshi watched other people play and nobody watched him. Being watched failing is better than not being watched at all. So Sho will shank 19 of 25 in front of the whole gym (a1:3181) and ask for the 26th.
- Shows as: he volunteers for the hardest drill; he stands where the coach can see him (a2:842); he asks to go in; he never pretends he didn't want it.

**The knee (3 months of pain, told nobody).** Satoshi's silence cost him everything. The opposite of that silence is asking. So the shamelessness is Satoshi's lesson in action: say the embarrassing thing out loud.
- Shows as: plain asks for help with technique, at once. And here is the tell: **he asks for everything except the one thing that costs him.** He'll beg Noya for a receiving lesson and Ukai for tosses, and he won't say "my eyes burn" (a2:3519, 11 rallies in ch 22). The shamelessness has exactly 1 blind spot, and it's the knee. Arc 1 should plant this without comment: he asks for 10 things, and the 1 thing he doesn't mention is the eye's cost (a1:1595 to 1601 "Your eyes are red." "Allergies."). In the rewrite, that "Allergies" is the 1 place a deflection belongs, and it should read as the only time he dodges in the chapter.

**The 3:14 desk (dying at 29).** He counts time like money. Later doesn't exist for him. So he asks now, asks 3rd years first, and asks the busy people anyway.
- Shows as: he asks Suga to toss before Kageyama (Suga has months; a1:3235); he takes the 5 a.m. slot; he counts down (3 days to Saturday, a1:944); he can't wait until he's ready to ask Oikawa. "Can I start on Monday?" (a3:5259)

### 4.2 How to show it without explaining

- **The ask's timing.** The best asks come straight after a loss or a humiliation (a1:1233 after being burned; a1:3047 after Noya eats his best spike; a1:2631 after watching Oikawa ace Tsukishima twice). A man with something to protect would wait. Sho asks while it still stings.
- **The refusal costs him nothing visible.** "Stage." He goes back to the stage. No narration about how he feels. The reader who knows about the desk does the math.
- **He gets more polite, never louder, when it matters.** "Please" appears in the asks that matter most. In the joke-ask ("100?") there's no please.
- **What his hands do.** He's always holding something when he isn't playing (Soul sheet). During a refusal, his hands go still or hold the bottle tighter. A writer can put the bench in his hands without a word.
- **1 flash per arc, no more.** a1:4031 to 4035 (the hand on the healthy knee, "A setter calling his name and him not raising his hand") is the right size. The narration in a1:2627 ("it would cost him nothing he cared about") and a1:3410 to 3412 ("That was all Sho gave him") explains the underneath and should be cut or reduced to action.
- **Other people say it, sideways.** Suga: "You really want Tokyo." Lev: "You play like an old man." Kageyama: "You talk like a coach." Nekomata: "You'd make a fine analyst." "I'd rather play." These are the only places the reader gets told, and each is someone guessing at the edge of it.
- **The contrast with Asahi and Tanaka.** Asahi stopped asking for the ball (a1:3265). Sho asks for everything. Satoshi stopped asking once (a1:3275). Put an ask of Sho's next to Asahi's silence in the same scene and say nothing.

### 4.3 The line it must never cross

The shamelessness can't become bravado. Bravado protects an image ("Remember it. It'll be on the scoresheet a lot." a1:2138). Shamelessness has no image to protect. If a line makes Sho look cool, cut it. If it makes him look a little ridiculous and sincere, keep it.

## 6. Rules for keeping it alive across the series

### 6.1 Frequency
- **1 to 3 shameless beats per chapter**, at least 1 of them a plain ask. Match chapters may have 1 (an ask to go in, or "Mine.").
- **At least 1 in 3 asks gets refused** on the page, and the refusal stands.
- **At most 1 out-loud joke per chapter from Sho**, dry, about himself, allowed to land flat. Inner voice can be funnier (that's "where Lloyd lives", 04_soul), and it should be cold and short.
- **The word "shameless"**: never in narration, never from Sho. Once per arc from someone else. Arc 1: Tsukishima, ch 4. Arc 2: nobody, or Iwaizumi about Oikawa and Sho together. Arc 3 has used "old man" and "You really want Tokyo" instead, which is better.
- **No ledgers, counters or running debts** as gags ("That left 1 meat bun outstanding", "That's 3", Noya's debt notebook). 1 count per arc at most, and only from the other person.

### 6.2 Escalation (what he asks for grows with him)
1. **Arc 1: things and lessons.** A ball, shoes, 20 minutes, a lesson, a toss, a serve aimed at him. From teammates, the shop guy, a rival setter.
2. **Arc 2: court time and trades.** "Put me in." Tosses while benched. A trade with Oikawa. The blind spot shows: he won't ask for the hand up until ch 28.
3. **Arc 3: help with the cost.** "Please bully me too." "Tomorrow too. Please." "Can I start on Monday?" He tells Ukai 1st ("Coach. 3 lines."). Asking for help with the eye, and saying it burns, is the hardest ask and the real growth.
4. **Arc 4 and after: the ball in a match.** The ace's ask. "Give it to me." to Kageyama, at set point, said plainly. Satoshi stopped asking for the ball; the series ends that sentence. Save it; the first time Sho calls for the ball the way Asahi did should be a chapter's hook.

### 6.3 Who calls him on it
- **Tsukishima** names it (once an arc) and wins.
- **Ukai** charges him: full price, a tab, "Stage", 20 more reps. Ukai's refusals are the spine of the comedy; his yeses come later and unasked.
- **Daichi** ends it with the smile.
- **Suga** sees under it, once an arc, in 4 words or fewer ("You really want Tokyo.").
- **Oikawa** hates the begging and loves the trade.
- **Kuroo** provokes, refuses, and keeps the last line.
- **Mio** refuses everything and wins every bet, and she's the only one he lets win on purpose.
- **Tanaka** never calls him on it. He copies it (a2:5439), which is the best compliment the series can pay it.

### 6.4 Keeping it from becoming a running gag
- **Never the same shape twice in a chapter.** If one ask is "Please teach me", the next is a silent one (holding out the milk) or a physical one (calf raises in the eyeline).
- **Never a callback to an earlier ask as a joke.** "I said I'd remind you" (a2:744) is fine because it's a promise kept plainly; "My block was prettier"-style callbacks aren't.
- **Let it cost something real at least once an arc.** Arc 1: Ennoshita's spot (he wants to apologize and isn't allowed). Arc 2: the 11 rallies (asked for everything, not for the hand up). Arc 3: the 5 puddings. The cost is what keeps it from being a bit.
- **Let it change other people.** Yamaguchi gets Shimada because Sho asked. Tanaka learns to ask. Kageyama starts asking hitters what they want (canon set 2; Sho can't take credit for that one). Each arc, someone does a small shameless thing they wouldn't have done before, and nobody points at it.
- **Keep the 15-year-old.** Polite words, short sentences, -san, a little odd. When he slips into Satoshi's register out loud, someone looks at him (Ukai: "Who told you that?"), and he answers with a plain lie ("Television.") or "I watch a lot of volleyball.", and that's the only joke.
