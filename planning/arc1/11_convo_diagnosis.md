## 0. Verdict in 1 paragraph

The quips are gone, and that part of the revision worked. What's left is a Sho who mostly *receives* conversations: he asks, then answers with "I know", "Yes" or "Okay" while other people talk. The other speakers mostly recite canon or serve Sho's scene. In Arc 3, Sho *brings* something into every key talk (a fact he noticed, a failure he reports flatly, a confession, a plain ask that gets refused), and the other person answers from their own soul with a line that isn't about Sho at all. Arc 3's own conversations also get a second turn, a question after the first answer that sends the scene somewhere new, and they end on the other person's move. Ch 1 to 5 are already at Arc 3 level wherever they do this (the milk, Dad at the red light, Mom at 4:40, Hinata's "I got good at jumping", Tsukishima's plan). The fix is to spread that method to the 20 or so places where it's missing. A rewrite isn't needed.

## 2. The conversation diagnosis (side by side)

### 2.1 Sho's half of the exchange: acknowledgments where Arc 3 puts content

Counts (dialogue paragraphs that are only the reply):

| | ch 1 to 5 (23,900 words) | ch 39, 41, 43, 45 (16,700 words) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I know." | 10 | 4 |
| "Yes." / "Yes, senpai." / "Yes, Captain." | 17 | 6 |
| Dialogue paragraphs per 1,000 words | 18.8 | 13.3 |
| Average words per dialogue paragraph | 9.6 | 12.1 |

So ch 1 to 5 have more lines, shorter lines, and Sho's share of them is mostly closing the exchange.

**Arc 1 (a1:1626 to 1634):**
> "Saturday," said Kageyama, "I'm serving at you."
> "I know."
> "Every time."
> "I know."
> Kageyama looked at him a little longer, as if he'd expected something else.

**Arc 3 (a3:3476 to 3486):**
> "Why him?"
> "So he has to pass," said Sho. "Then he's late to hit."
> ...
> "Who taught you to wait?"
> "Nobody yet, sir. I'd jumped too early the time before."

Both Sho lines are as short and plain as "I know". The Arc 3 ones carry a fact and a failure reported flatly (the bible's shameless rule), and that is what makes Nekomata laugh. Kageyama, in Arc 1, is left holding a conversation that has nowhere to go, which the text even notices ("as if he'd expected something else"). Ch 4 does this 3 times in a row: Kageyama (a1:1628, 1632), Hinata (a1:1638), Tsukishima (a1:1674), all about the same serve.

**Rule:** every short Sho line should be a fact, a failure, an ask, or a deliberate refrain. "I know" may appear when it's the point.

### 2.4 Long speeches belong to canon in ch 1 to 5; in Arc 3 they belong to the fic's hard truths

The 60-plus-word spoken paragraphs in ch 1 to 5 are all canon recitals with Sho standing silent: Daichi a1:446 (76 words) and a1:456 (79), Hinata a1:1176 (85), Tsukishima a1:1790 (62), Suga a1:2377 (60). Arc 3's long lines are Akaashi teaching hands (a3:3632, instruction, allowed by the playbook) and Sho's confession to Mio (a3:3730, 62 words). Arc 3 handles a protected canon moment by giving it to its owner in 1 or 2 sentences and moving on ("the 2 of them went off to a corner of the main gym to run the new 1, eyes open, and it went into the net, and they shouted at each other, and ran it again", a3:3566).

The canon speeches in ch 1 to 5 should stay (the author wants canon talks spoken). Each needs a listener's beat inside it: a question, a line nobody answers, someone else's want. Fixes 2A, 3D, 4E and 5F do that.

### 2.5 Head vs mouth: Arc 3's head holds the wound; ch 1 to 5's head holds a résumé

"He'd seen X on tape N times" lines: **15 in ch 1 to 5, 1 in the 4 Arc 3 chapters.** Examples: "He'd seen it in 100 locker rooms" (a1:598), "on tape from national team setters, 1,000 of them" (a1:1050), "written it in 100 scouting reports" (a1:1588), "Sho had seen 1,000 of them" (a1:2149), "on tape on 50 different blockers" (a1:2205), "He'd seen that shoulder on 10,000 hitters" (a1:2247).

**Arc 3 (a3:3526 to 3540):**
> ...that he was enjoying himself. Enjoying it the way he'd enjoyed it for 9 years, alone, at night, with a cold coffee going colder beside the keyboard.
> His thumb was moving against his knee. ... It was the movement his thumb made on a rewind button.
> He sat back in the chair, and put both hands flat on his thighs.
> ...
> "Thank you, sir," said Sho. "I'd rather play."

The memory is sensory, it costs him, and the mouth says the plain decision. In ch 1 to 5 the memory is a credential: it makes Sho the expert in the room and costs nothing. A second pattern: at a1:1538 the head and the mouth say the same thing (*Because I promised a short kid at 6:20 this morning.* / "I promised Hinata I'd try"). In Arc 3 the head holds the real reason and the mouth gives the presentable one.

The inner wit itself is fine and the author wants it kept (the Naruto fox a1:163, Rock Lee a1:2091, "Teachers." a1:2019, the futon a1:2305, "shown it to his mother" a1:2213). The fix is to cut most credential lines, and to give each chapter 1 Satoshi memory that hurts or warms.

### 2.6 Humor: Arc 3 gets it from situations and people's sincere logic; Sho's asks get refused

**Arc 3 (a3:5554 to 5562):**

> "I've been away for a week. I've lost a lot of weight. For the team."

Elsewhere the laugh is carried by narrator similes about Sho ("like a man waiting to be told he'd parked in the wrong place", a1:1989) or by Sho talking to himself out loud ("Disappointing," he told the streetlight, a1:167), which puts the wit in his mouth.

**Arc 3:**

### 2.8 Pauses get a sound; refrains turn

Arc 3 fills a pause with the room before the reply comes: "The air conditioner hummed. In the corner, Kenma's console made a small chiming sound and stopped" (a3:3538); "He could hear the television in the living room at home, low" (a3:3746); the cicada at a3:5480 and 5512. Its accidental refrain ("I know" x4 in ch 4) never turns.

## 3. Non-dialogue causes (real, but smaller)

1. **The credential tic** (2.5): 15 against 1. It reads as narrator bragging, and it slows every rally it appears in.
2. **Ch 5 plays every point as a list.** a1:2101 ("Tsukishima's serve. He floated it at Hinata... 4-5. He floated another... 5-5.") and a1:2153 to 2167 are score ledgers. Arc 3 picks 5 or 6 rallies, gives them air, and summarizes the stretch between them ("It went the way games against Shinzen went: slowly, 1 point at a time, never more than 2 apart.", a3:4166). The handoff records "every point played" as a decision, so this is only a flag: if the author is open to it, a1:2101 and a1:2153 to 2167 could each fold into 1 sentence with the scores kept.
3. **Soul cost in ch 4 and 5.** Ch 1 to 3 each have a Satoshi moment with weight (the dinner, Tokyo 40 times, the umeboshi). Ch 4 and 5 have none until the end of ch 5; the corner Sho stands in during the 3v3 is the bench on the court, and the text plays it as a joke (a1:1989). Fixes 4B and 5A put 1 line of cost back.
4. **Feeling adverbs and narrator explanations** in a few spots: "asked softly" (a1:207), "He said it gently." (a1:2377), "He wondered who had taught this kid..." (a1:1999, explains Tsukishima's subtext, which the bible forbids).

## 6. Checklist for every later chapter (10 items)

1. **Wants first.** For each main conversation, write down what each speaker wants from the other right now. Every line comes from that, Sho's included.
2. **Sho brings something.** In every key talk Sho gives a fact he noticed, a failure reported flatly, a plain ask, or a confession. Cap bare "I know / Yes / Okay" at 2 per scene unless it's a refrain on purpose.
3. **Everyone gets 1 line of their own.** Each other speaker says 1 thing from their Soul sheet that isn't about Sho (Tanaka's "no kind way to laugh at somebody", Dad's "Do you need someone there?").
4. **1 second turn per chapter.** In at least 1 fic conversation, after the first answer, someone asks the next question or admits something, and the scene changes direction ("Is that all?" "No.").
5. **No unbroken speech over about 50 words.** Break canon speeches with a listener: a question, a line nobody answers, a body.
6. **Head vs mouth.** Mouth: plain, polite, short. Head: the real reason, 1 or 2 jokes or anime refs, and at most 1 "he'd seen this N times" per chapter. Give each chapter 1 Satoshi memory that hurts or warms (a smell, an object, a sound).
7. **Humor from people.** Get laughs from the situation or from someone's sincere logic. Sho's shameless asks are plain and often refused flatly, and he takes it (the 120-yen meat bun).
8. **Pauses have a sound.** Before an important reply, let the room make a noise (the TV, a truck, the air conditioner), then the answer.
9. **Refrains turn.** If a line or question repeats 3 times, the 3rd time changes (Dad's "Mm" after "What time?", "Well?" until it stops).
10. **Endings belong to the other person or an object.** Close on their move, a plain exit, or a thing on a table. Never close on the narrator explaining how Sho felt, and never on Sho winning the last word.
