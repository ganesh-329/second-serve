## 0. Diagnosis: why the soul reads corrupted

The plot beats of Arc 1 are mostly right, and several scenes already carry real soul (the 4:40 onigiri, the milk at the vending machine, Daichi under the towel, Ennoshita on the bus, Noya at the door, the hand on the knee, the payphone, "13." "13."). The corruption is in 4 places.

1. **Sho performs instead of wanting.** His first words after remembering his own death are a serve critique and a demand for "compensation" [a1:99-109]. In ch 2 he flatters Tanaka with an "aura" speech [a1:328-340]. He calls Tanaka, Kageyama and Ukai "investments" [a1:646, 768, 1044]. He pitches Daichi, Shimada and the alarm [a1:526, 4275, 4423]. The bible's Sho gets *more polite* when he wants something badly, and his asks are plain ("Please toss me a few."). Right now he gets *wittier* when he wants something, which turns every want into a bit.
2. **Every exchange ends on a topper.** "That's not a compliment." "I know. I took it anyway." [a1:1073-1075]. "Testing the ball's density." "Spite." "Allergies." "To what?" "Volleyball." "Nice receive. Wrong body part." "Great aim. Wrong target." "Budget cuts, senpai." "Stealing is my line." "My block was prettier." "Report. I've found my limit. Please clap." The same shape (A says X, B tops it) repeats in nearly every scene, which is the bible's #1 artificial tell.
3. **The conversations that should carry the arc are summarized.** Suga's envy speech [a1:1325-1327], Kageyama's answer to Hinata [a1:1339], Hinata and Suga on the step [a1:748-752], Ennoshita's bus reads [a1:2697] and Suga's confession [a1:3622-3624] are all reported speech. Some of that is right (Sho overhears, and those moments belong to their owners), but the conversations Sho himself is *in* are short and quippy while the ones he only overhears are the earnest ones. Arc 1 needs Sho's own conversations to be the long, plain, meaningful ones.
4. **Family is a laugh track.** Ch 1's dinner is sitcom ping-pong (the red car, "I want both", "purely for research") [a1:199-239]. Mio is written as a 10-year-old stand-up ("I would rather die" [a1:2897], "Of my enemies." "Am I in it?" "Page 1." [a1:1974-1978]). Mom and Dad exist mostly to deliver a line. So ch 8's door check, ch 12's silent pudding, ch 13's magnet and ch 15's "You haven't quit" arrive with no ground under them; they are good beats with nothing set up.

Also, in narration: "filed" 13 times, "didn't say anything" 22 times, "for a long moment" 11, "for a second" 13, "without looking" 11, "Satoshi's brain" 11, a dog simile 7 times, the "memo" joke twice [a1:251, 414]. These are the style agent's job, but they also flatten soul, because the narrator keeps telling us Sho noticed and stored something instead of letting us watch what he does.

**The fix in 1 sentence:** Sho wants 1 thing in every scene, asks for it plainly, often gets told no, and the scene ends on what that costs him or someone else, with the joke (if any) coming from the situation.

## 1. Sho's soul spine across Arc 1

### 1.1 The arc's questions

**Surface question (bible):** Will this kid who quit everything get on the court, and will the strange eye come back?

**Real question:** *Will his feet go before his eyes are sure?*

Satoshi spent 9 years making certain. He rewound rallies at quarter speed until he knew. Certainty was his job and his hiding place. A player can't wait for certainty: by the time you're sure, the ball's on the floor. Arc 1 already has this spine on the page in 4 lines spread across 10 chapters, and it only needs to be built toward:

- ch 5, Daichi under the towel: "You moved before he hit it." [a1:1641] (the 1 time he didn't wait)
- ch 9, Noya: "You're waiting to be sure!" [a1:3119] and the rule *Move first. Correct after.* [a1:3283]
- ch 14, Ennoshita after the freeze: "Your feet stopped before your eyes did." [a1:5237]
- ch 15, Ukai: "Feet were ready. Keep them ready." [a1:5530]

The freeze against Nekoma (ch 14) is the analyst coming back onto the court: 12 snapshots and a ghost, 2 tapes playing at once, and the feet waiting for someone to press play. Never say that in prose. The reader gets it because the 4 lines above have been planted in order.

### 1.2 Shameless: how it should read early vs. how it reads now

The bible's reason for the shamelessness: *a man who has already lost everything once has nothing left to protect.* Asking for a toss costs nothing next to dying at a desk. So the asks are plain, polite, repeated after a no, and he accepts the no when it's final. The comedy, when there is any, comes from how little it costs him (and how much it costs the person asked to say no to a bowing 182 cm 1st year).

Rules for early Sho, out loud: short sentences to seniors; -san, -senpai, Coach; "please", "could I", "teach me"; he repeats the ask once after a no and then stops; he thanks people for corrections ("Thank you for the lesson, senpai" is exactly right [a1:1623]); he is sometimes out-talked (Tsukishima, Ukai, Kuroo) and takes it with "Yes." He makes at most 1 real joke a chapter, dry and about himself, and it's allowed to land flat. Anime references stay in his head (1 or 2 a chapter). Inside his head he can be cold and funny; that's where Lloyd lives.

What "shameless" means to his teammates by ch 15: he asks everyone for everything and never minds being told no. Tanaka adopts him because he asks. Noya can't refuse him. Tsukishima can and does. Ukai makes him pay 120 yen. Oikawa hates it.
