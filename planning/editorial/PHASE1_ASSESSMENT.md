## A. Overall diagnosis

### What is working

- **The quiet family scenes are the best writing in the arc.** Mom pressing rice at 4:40 and answering "Salmon, then. You hate umeboshi." (ch 3); Dad reading the rules off his phone until Mom says "Eat" (ch 4); Dad's tape measure and the lines down the door frame (ch 2); Mio dropping the frozen edamame on his arm, "It's for the bet. If you get hurt and quit, it doesn't count. I want to beat you fair." (ch 3). These are plain, specific, funny and tender without announcing it. This is the voice to recover everywhere else.
- **The canon comedy lands because it is staged, not narrated.** The wig and Tanaka's "Technically" (ch 2), Takeda's "chemical reaction" speech ending in "That was too poetic" (ch 9), Hinata and Kageyama pounding on the door.
- **The first eye firing (ch 5)** is the most controlled prose in the arc: the gym going quiet "as if somebody had pulled the plug on a speaker", Hinata as a skeleton of points of light. It is short, concrete and unexplained.
- **The physical-progress thread (ch 10 to 13)** works: toss count 6, 9, 11; *shu* held for 3, 5, 6 balls; Ukai's tape reach test; "Your legs are a kid's who sat in a chair for 3 years." It is measurable and nobody comments on it.
- **A handful of small exchanges are exactly right:** Noya's "Did he eat it?" / "He eats when he's sad." (ch 11); Hinata's "I'm glad you didn't get in... I'd have nobody to set me." (ch 10); Kageyama's "...I watched." (ch 9); Sho's "It was your set, senpai." (ch 12).

### What feels artificial, ranked by how much it matters

1. **The narrator keeps stopping the scene to explain it.** The commonest form is the Satoshi aside. "Satoshi had/half/used/would..." appears about **40 times** in 13 chapters, in nearly every one. Some carry real weight (the 3:14 clock, the knee, the 22-22 memory in ch 10). Many are just analysis delivered as backstory: "Satoshi had seen it on tape in young defenders: a boy who'd been lucky once and spent the rest of the match waiting to be lucky again." (ch 5); "Satoshi had known pros like that, the ones who worked out to the gram what they needed to spend." (ch 8). The worst cases say the parallel outright: "Satoshi's knee had gone at 20. It had gone because a 20-year-old ace who thought he couldn't break trained through 3 months of a sore knee and told nobody, and did everything twice as hard and nothing twice as smart." (ch 6). That line breaks your own rule and tells the reader what the hand-on-the-knee image already said.
2. **Similes and "as if" on every gesture.** About **80** "as if/as though" and about **117** "the way" (30 of those in the simile form "the way he'd/you'd/somebody...") in 55,895 words. Ch 3 has 12 "as if it/he/somebody" constructions alone: "as if he'd lent somebody his bike and they'd ridden it better than him on the 1st try and he hadn't decided yet how he felt about it"; "took it, carefully, as if it might go off"; "as if somebody had just gone out of it". Individually most are fine. Together they put the author's hand in nearly every reaction, which is the "deliberately inserted detail" you described.
3. **Reaction choreography between lines of dialogue.** "Looked at / up / across / back..." runs about **7 per 1,000 words** (about 385 times), and the same stage business repeats with different characters:
   - Group stacks: "Daichi looked at the cart. Sugawara looked at the ball in his hands. Nishinoya kept looking at the wall with his jaw set. Tanaka opened his mouth and shut it." (ch 11); "Tanaka looked at him. He looked at Daichi, at Sugawara, at the girl with the clipboard, who was writing, and back at Sho. His mouth moved." (ch 2).
   - "Opened his mouth and shut it" (12 times), "pushed his glasses up" (9), "ears went red/pink" (12, spread over Tanaka, Kageyama, Hinata, Yamaguchi and Noya, so it stops being any one person's tell), "a beat longer" (10, twice on one page of ch 13).
   - "Hands" runs 6.4 per 1,000 words and peaks in ch 13 (9.4) and ch 8 (8.8): hands flexing, hands up, hands on knees. You named this one.
4. **Everyone is a little too articulate at the emotional moments.** The comic and plain lines are in voice; the sincere ones drift into writing: Suga to Kageyama in ch 4 ("You're cutting off the only things he has."), Kageyama's reply ("I'm jealous of you... So it's my job. As the setter. To use them."), Sho to Tanaka in ch 13 ("That we think he's going to break. That we're all being careful with him."), Mio's "That's ages" is fine but "Is it bad?" "No. It's good. Everything's good." (ch 1) is not how a 15-year-old answers his mother. Some of these are canon beats, so the fix is making them shorter and rougher, not cutting them.
5. **Sho's voice and "Mm."** Dad's "Mm." is a signature (7 uses). Ennoshita now says it 6 times (ch 7 to 13), Daichi and Suga occasionally. Two characters with the same sound is exactly the "interchangeable speakers" problem, in miniature.
6. **Chapters end on a quiet closing image, and most nights end in the notebook.** "...and every window was dark" (ch 11); "...as if it were hanging there on a thread, waiting for somebody to come and get it." (ch 9); "...the streetlight through the curtain caught the white letters across its back." (ch 6); "He lay there and counted Hinata's run-up steps until he fell asleep." (ch 3); "Tomorrow, Kageyama was going to ask for his match." (ch 2). The notebook closes ch 6, 9 and 10 and sits mid-chapter in 11 and 12. It is a good device used on a schedule.

### Structure and pace

- **Scene count is high for the length.** Chapters of 3,400 to 5,500 words hold **8 to 13 scenes** (ch 4: 13, ch 2 and 3: 11 each, ch 7 and 8: 12 and 11), about 400 words a scene. That produces a run of short vignettes that each need an entrance and a closing beat, which is where much of the "stretched yet choppy" feeling comes from. By a rough count (dialogue fragments included) the average sentence is only about 9 words, and about a third of all paragraphs are 7 words or fewer.
- **The morning ritual opens three chapters in a row (10, 11, 12):** 4:15, legs, porch light, toss count written on the hand. It is a good thread the first time and a formula by the third.
- **Where momentum is lost:** (a) ch 7 to 9, 13,900 words for one practice match, with set 1 and set 2 each preceded or followed by a bench conversation, a bus, a home scene and a notebook; (b) ch 11, which is mostly transitions between Thursday practice, the shop, the walk, home, Friday's landing, Suga's story and the shop again; (c) the Monday of ch 12, nine scenes of 300 to 600 words each before the match; (d) ch 4's middle, a score-by-score run (15-14, 16-14, 17-14...).
- **The pass over ch 7 to 13 on Nov 22 made things longer under the old rule.** Examples worth re-judging: ch 12's dawn scene and its Yamaguchi stretching scene (ch 13 opens with Yamaguchi again); ch 13's Hinata "Are you being mean to Asahi-san?" beat and the rally-by-rally score tracking early in a pickup game; ch 11's warm-up question "Would you come back?"; ch 10's guard bit.

### Sho's competence

Mostly in balance: his body fails on the page (shallow jump, 1-foot landings, fingertips only to the tape, legs gone by 8-7). The risk is that his *reads* are almost always right: he calls Tanaka's cross three times in ch 4, reads Kindaichi's drift and Iwaizumi's swing in ch 8, calls Asahi's tip in ch 13. That is not an error, but in Phase 2 I would let a read be wrong once or twice where match results allow it, instead of adding more.

- Keep about 1 Satoshi aside per chapter, only where it carries weight (the 3:14 clock, the knee, 22-22). Convert the analytic ones into what Sho sees.
