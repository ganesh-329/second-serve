## Chapter 1: Ball to the face

Kiyose Sho had 1 rule for club recruitment week, and it was to keep walking.

It was the 1st Monday of it, and Karasuno's corridors were full of people who wanted his name. A 2nd year with a trumpet case stood at the top of the stairs saying "Brass band, brass band" to every 1st year who passed. The calligraphy club had a table by the shoe lockers with a brush and an ink stone on it and a sign that said TRY IT! in very good handwriting. A boy in full kendo armor bowed at him from the doorway of the old judo room.

Sho bowed back. He took the flyer. He put it in the bin by the vending machines, folded once, on top of the brass band.

He knew how clubs went. Soccer at 9, because his friend Daisuke did soccer, until Daisuke moved to Nagoya. Swimming at 10, until the coach started timing them. Kendo at 11, for 4 months, until a 13-year-old hit him on the wrist so hard he couldn't hold chopsticks, and he understood that the older kids were allowed to do that, and would keep doing it, and that the point of kendo was to get better at being hit. He'd stopped going. Nobody had made a fuss. By 12 he'd stopped trying new ones, and by 15 nobody asked anymore except his mother.

She'd asked that morning, holding out his bento.

"Just try 1," she'd said. "1 thing. Something you'd actually like."

"I like going home."

"That's not a club, Sho."

Mio had looked up from her cereal. She was 10 and still had her pajama top on over her skirt, which was how she got dressed when she was running late, from the bottom up. "Do something cool," she'd said. "Kaho's brother's in a band."

"Kaho's brother is 19."

"So?"

He hadn't had an answer to that. The entry form had come home in his bag on Friday, and it was in his blazer pocket now, folded in 4, blank. Every 1st year got 1. You were supposed to hand it in by the end of the month.

He went out the back of the main building, the long way round to the gate. It went past the gyms and the bike sheds and the incinerator, and nobody stood on that path with flyers, because nobody walked it. The cherry trees along the fence had buds on them, tight and dark pink, about a week from opening. The air was cold in the shade and warm in the sun, and he had his hands in his pockets and was thinking about whether the konbini by the station would still have nikuman this late in the season, or whether they'd switched the steamer over to the summer things.

Shouting came out of the 2nd gym.

"Receive it properly!"

"I AM receiving it properly!"

"Then why is it going over there?"

"BECAUSE YOU SERVED IT WEIRD!"

The big sliding door was half open. Sho slowed down, because it was the way to the gate and the voices were in the way, and because 1 of them sounded about to cry.

He looked in.

The gym was the old kind, wooden floor gone honey-colored, the paint on the lines worn through in the places people stood most. A net was up across the middle. On the far side, behind the end line, a dark-haired boy in a gym shirt was bouncing a ball with the flat of his hand, hard, the same height every time. On the near side a small kid with orange hair stood in a crouch, knees bent, arms out in front of him, his whole body pointed at the server.

"I'm going to beat you," the orange kid said. "I'm going to beat you and I'm going to be Karasuno's ace."

"You can't even receive."

"I CAN RECEIVE."

The dark-haired 1 tossed the ball up and stepped into it and hit it. It came over the net hard and dipping, with a lot of spin on it, and the orange kid got his arms under it, and it went off his arms sideways into the wall.

"See?"

"That was a practice 1!"

Sho stood in the doorway and watched them do it again. Serve, receive, wall. He'd seen volleyball in PE. He'd been on a team of 6 boys who all stood still and let it land between them. This looked about the same, louder, and he was mostly thinking that if he went round by the bike sheds he'd still make the 4:20 light at the crossing.

The dark-haired 1 served again. Harder.

The orange kid's elbows bent as the ball came in. It hit the inside of his left forearm and came off it at an angle, fast, low, straight at the door.

Sho saw it come. He saw the blue and the yellow turning over each other, and had about enough time to think *oh*.

It hit him in the face.

---

White.

Then a gym. Bigger, with a curved roof and windows high up full of gray Osaka sky, and the floor wax smell so strong it was almost a taste. A setter's voice, a man's voice, shouting "Matsumoto!" and the ball going up over the net, high and soft, hanging at the top of its climb right where he wanted it, the way it hung when the setter had looked at you and decided you were the 1. His own arm going back. His own hand.

A sound in his left knee like somebody snapping a pencil, so loud the bench heard it.

Crutches in a hospital corridor. A rehab room with a mirror. The same knee, 6 months later, 12, and a doctor saying *recreational level* in the voice doctors use for the end of something.

A narrow room with 3 monitors and the blinds down. A hitter's shoulder on the screen, frozen. A setter's hips. A league's worth of other people's rallies, rewound and played, rewound and played, at quarter speed, for 9 years. A can of coffee gone cold on the desk. His thumb on the rewind button. The clock in the corner of the middle screen: 3:14 a.m.

His heart doing something it shouldn't, all at once, hard.

Then nothing.

---

"Hey."

Sky. Pale blue, with a long flat cloud across it.

"HEY. Are you alive? Hey!"

A face came into the sky. Orange hair, wide brown eyes, mouth open.

Sho lay on the concrete outside the 2nd gym and didn't move, because he was busy.

He was Kiyose Sho. He was 15. He lived in a 2-story house 6 minutes from the school gate with his mother, who packed too much rice, and his father, who hummed in the bathroom while he shaved, and his sister, who hid his phone charger whenever she was losing an argument. He was in class 1-5. He'd quit kendo. He knew all of that the way he knew which stair at home creaked.

He was also Matsumoto Satoshi. He'd been 29. He'd been a wing spiker at university until the knee, and then he'd watched tape for a living, and then he'd been sitting at his desk at 3:14 in the morning with his thumb on a button.

And then he hadn't been.

He looked at the cloud. He knew what had happened in that room. Nobody would have been there. The light from the monitors on his face all night, and the tape running, and the cleaner opening the door at 6 with the vacuum.

He'd died. He'd died, and here was a cloud.

Both lives sat in his head, side by side. They didn't argue about it. The 15 years were his, the mother and the rice and the stair, and the 29 years were his too, the knee and the room and the clock. It felt like remembering, the plain way you remember where you left your shoes, and that was the part he couldn't get his mind around, lying there on the concrete with a cloud going over.

Something warm ran out of his nose and over his lip.

"You're bleeding! Oh no. Oh no, oh no. I'm so sorry!" The orange kid dropped into a bow so deep and so fast that his forehead nearly hit Sho's knee. "That was my receive! I mean, it was his serve. But it was my receive. But his serve was really..."

"It was your receive," said a voice from the doorway.

"I KNOW IT WAS MY RECEIVE."

Sho sat up.

The world swung once and came back. He touched his upper lip, and his fingers came away red. He looked at his hand. It was a big hand, square in the palm, the nails short. A 15-year-old's hand, soft on the inside where Satoshi's had been hard as a shoe sole from 12 years of balls. It was the right hand, and it was his, and he turned it over to look at the back of it.

"Are you okay?" said the orange kid. "Can you see? How many fingers?" He held up 3.

"3," said Sho.

The dark-haired server had come out onto the concrete. He was about Sho's height, with a face that looked like it was frowning even when it wasn't. He stood over them with the ball he'd been bouncing still under his arm.

"Is he okay," he said to the orange kid, as if Sho were somewhere else.

"I don't know! I'm asking!"

Sho opened his mouth and found that what he wanted to say was *Your elbows bent. Lock them, and move your feet 1st*, and that it was in Satoshi's voice, a grown man's voice, tired, at 3 a.m. He shut his mouth again.

"I'm okay," he said, in his own. "Sorry. I was in the way."

The server looked at him a bit longer. Then he nodded, once, and went back inside.

The ball that had hit him was by his knee. It had rolled to a stop against his leg, and sat there, blue and yellow, the panels a little gray along the seams from a gym floor.

He picked it up.

It was the most ordinary feeling in the world. A volleyball, 260 grams or so, soft, the leather slightly tacky. His fingers went to the seams by themselves, the way your feet find the stairs in the dark, and the moment they did, he wanted to play so badly that his palms hurt with it.

Satoshi had loved volleyball carefully, from the edge of the room, for 9 years. Now a 15-year-old body that had never wanted anything wanted this, all at once, with the volume turned all the way up. 10 minutes ago he'd been thinking about nikuman.

He held on to the ball.

Under the wanting, the other half of his head was doing what it had done for 9 years, all on its own. It ran the last 10 seconds back. The serve: a hard topspin serve, tossed a little in front, hit right on top of the ball so it dipped. A serious serve for a 1st year. The receive: the orange kid's elbows had bent as the ball arrived, and the ball had hit the soft inside of his left forearm, off the edge of the platform, and skidded off at whatever angle it liked. Feet set too late. Arms swinging up at it. Every beginner did it, and every tired pro did it at the end of a long 5th set, and Satoshi had written *platform* in the margin of more scouting reports than he could count.

"Oh no. Was that our ball?"

A 3rd year had come round the side of the gym at a jog, carrying 2 more balls under his arm. He had soft gray hair and a beauty mark under 1 eye, and he took 1 look at Sho's face and winced.

"It was his receive," said the orange kid, pointing at himself. "Mine. I'm sorry. Sugawara-san, I'm so sorry."

"Hinata. Okay. Breathe." The 3rd year put the balls down and crouched in front of Sho. "Hi. Don't tip your head back. Forward. Like this." He put 2 fingers on the back of Sho's head and pushed it gently down. "Pinch here. The soft part. Hinata, there's tissues in my bag. Front pocket."

Hinata ran.

"I'm Sugawara," said the 3rd year. "I'm really sorry about this."

"Kiyose," said Sho, through his pinched nose. It came out *Kiyobe*. "1st year."

Hinata came back with a whole packet of tissues and gave him all of them. Sugawara rolled 2 into plugs and Sho put them in, 1 each side, and sat on the step of the gym with the tissue sticking out of his nose and the ball on his knee.

Hinata stood in front of him. He couldn't keep still. He bounced on his toes and then made himself stop and then bounced again.

"Are you joining?" he said. "Are you a 1st year? Me too. 1-1. I came from Yukigaoka. Nobody's heard of it. We didn't have a team, really. It was just me for ages and then 3 1st years joined and I borrowed 2 more and we played 1 match. 1. In 3rd year." He pointed through the door, at the dark-haired 1, who was back at the end line, bouncing his ball. "Against his school. Kitagawa Daiichi. They won 2-0. He was the setter."

"Ah," said Sho.

"So I came here to beat him, and he came here too, which isn't FAIR, and now we're on the same team. Nearly. If we're allowed." Hinata's eyes went up, to the top of Sho's head, and stayed there. "How tall are you?"

"182."

"182!" Hinata looked up at him, and then up at the place 182 would be if Sho stood, and his face did something complicated. "You'd be such a good middle. You should be a middle. You'd block everything." He stopped bouncing. "I'm going to be the ace, though. So you know."

"Okay," said Sho.

"I mean it!"

"I know."

Sugawara was looking at Sho over Hinata's head. His eyes went to the ball on Sho's knee, and to Sho's hand on top of it, and away again.

"Hinata. Go and get the mop. Kageyama's going to want to do 50 more."

Hinata went.

Sugawara sat down on the step.

"You're a 1st year," he said. "Are you looking at clubs?"

The form was in his pocket, folded in 4. Sho could feel the corner of it through his shirt.

"I wasn't," he said.

"Oh." Sugawara smiled. "Okay."

"I think I am now."

Sugawara turned his head and looked at him properly. He had a way of looking that took its time.

"Do you have any experience?"

Sho thought about how to answer that. Yes. 12 years. A university gym in Osaka and a knee and 9 years of tape. And no, none. This body had touched a volleyball in PE, badly, and once more just now, with its face.

"Some," he said.

Sugawara waited, and Sho had nothing else he could say, so they sat there. Inside, the ball hit the floor, the same sound every time.

"We're a bit short this year," Sugawara said. "We'd be glad to have you. Practice is in the morning before school and after school till 6. Club doesn't properly start till the forms are in. Those 2 just couldn't wait." He tipped his head at the door. "Bring yours tomorrow, early, if you can. Our captain's Daichi. Sawamura. He'll want to meet you."

"Thank you," said Sho. "I will." He looked at the gym. "Can I sit here and watch? Till you finish?"

"With your nose like that?"

"I'll sit still."

Sugawara laughed. "Sure. Till 6. Then go home and put ice on it."

---

He sat on the step till 6.

The sun went down behind the clubhouse and the light in the gym went from gold to the flat white of the ceiling lamps. The cold came up out of the concrete through his trousers. Sugawara went back in, and set balls for the 2 of them, high ones, easy ones, and Hinata hit them into the net and over the end line and once, by luck, into the court, and screamed. Kageyama served, and Hinata received, and the ball went everywhere.

Sho watched it all. He watched the way Kageyama's whole body went still before he served, like a held breath. He watched Sugawara's hands. He watched Hinata's feet, which were fast and in the wrong place, every time, and his arms, which were wrong in a new way every time. His nose throbbed.

At 10 to 6, Kageyama walked to the net and tossed a ball straight up, high, for nobody in particular.

Hinata went after it.

He ran from the 3-meter line, 3 steps, and his last 2 were so fast they blurred, and he left the floor, and kept leaving it. Sho watched him go up. 163 centimeters of kid, maybe, and his hand went up past the tape, and past it, and his whole head came above the net, and at the top his hand came round and slapped the ball down into the floor on the other side with a bang that went round the roof.

Satoshi had spent 9 years watching V.League middles on a screen. He knew what a jump looked like when it was good. He sat on the step with the tissues in his nose and tried to work out where that 1 had come from, and the numbers wouldn't add up.

Hinata landed and screamed "YES!" at the ceiling. Then he turned round, to the door, and pointed at Sho.

"DID YOU SEE?"

"I saw," said Sho.

"Stop showing off and get the net," said Kageyama.

"I was showing HIM!"

They put the net down at 6. Sho stood up. The world swung again, a little, and settled. He went to the door with the ball still in his hands.

"Sugawara-san," he said. "Can I keep the ball?"

"No."

Sugawara held out his hand, still smiling, and waited. Sho looked at the ball, and at the hand. Then he gave it back. Sugawara took it with his fingers spread on the panels, the way setters hold a ball, and tucked it under his arm.

"Tomorrow," he said.

---

Home was 6 minutes. He ran it in 4.

His legs wanted to, so he let them, and he went up the hill past the dry cleaner's and the shrine with the 1 stone fox and the house with the dog that barked at everybody, and the dog barked at him, and he ran past it with the tissues still in his nose and his bag banging on his back. He wasn't out of breath at the top. He noticed that, and kept going.

At the corner by the vending machine the streetlight had just come on. He stopped under it and looked at his hands again. Normal hands. A bit big. No calluses. If this were Naruto there'd be a fox sealed in his stomach, and if he concentrated hard enough it would start talking to him. He tried it. A cat came out from under a car and looked at him and went back under the car.

He walked the rest of the way.

"Tadaima."

His mother came out of the kitchen before he'd got his 1st shoe off. She was wiping her hands on a towel, and she stopped in the hallway when she saw him.

"What happened to your FACE?"

Behind her, Mio came to the living room door in her socks. She looked at him, at the swelling across his nose, at the 2 plugs of tissue sticking out of it, red-brown at the ends.

"Did a girl hit you?"

"A boy with orange hair," said Sho. "With a ball. By accident. I was standing in the door."

"Why were you standing in the door?"

"I was watching."

Mio looked at the tissue as though it might explain better.

"Is it broken? Let me see." His mother came and took his chin in her fingers and turned his face to the light, the way she had when he was 6 and fell off the wall at the park. Her fingers were cold and smelled of onion. "It's swollen. Take those out."

He took the plugs out. His mother made a noise and took them off him with the towel, and went to the freezer. There was no ice in the tray, because there never was, so she came back with a bag of frozen edamame in a dish towel and held it out. He put it on his nose. It was cold enough to make his eyes water.

"Sit down," she said. "Your father made curry."

---

His father was at the stove in his work shirt with the sleeves rolled up, stirring the pot with a ladle. He made curry on Mondays because Monday was the day he got home 1st, and he made it the same way every time, out of the box, with extra potato, and it was always good. His newspaper was folded small on the corner of the table, the way he left it when he'd only read half. He looked round when Sho came in with the edamame on his face, and his eyebrows went up and stayed up.

"Volleyball," said Sho.

"Mm," said his father, and went back to the pot.

They sat. The table was the same table, with the same burn mark by his place from the hot pot 3 New Years ago, and the same 4 chairs, and the TV on low with the news. His mother put the curry down in front of him. The steam came up into his face, sweet and brown, with the carrots cut too big the way his father always cut them.

Satoshi had eaten alone for 9 years. Konbini bento at 1 in the morning in front of a screen, the plastic lid on the desk, the chopsticks still in their paper because he'd forgotten them. Some weeks he hadn't said a word out loud to anybody until Wednesday. He knew how quiet that room had been, because he could hear it now, here, under the news and his mother's chair scraping and Mio humming something from school.

He put his hands together.

"Itadakimasu," he said.

He said it properly. He heard himself do it: both hands, a little bow of the head, the whole word, every syllable.

Across the table, Mio stopped with her spoon in the air.

She looked at him. He picked up his own spoon and started eating. After a while she started eating too, but she kept looking.

"So," said his mother. "Who was it? With the ball?"

"A 1st year. Hinata. And another 1, Kageyama, who served it. And a 3rd year, Sugawara-san. He gave me the tissues." He ate a piece of potato. "They're the volleyball club."

"That was nice of him."

"Yeah." He put his spoon down. "I'm going to join."

The TV went on talking about rain in Kyushu.

His father had been about to say something. Sho saw it before it happened: his father's mouth started to open and his eyes came up from his plate, and then he closed his mouth again and looked at Sho.

His mother put her own spoon down.

"Since when," said Mio, "do you like volleyball?"

"Since today."

"You'll quit," Mio said. She said it as a fact, the way she said the bus came at 7:40. "By Golden Week."

"I won't."

"You did kendo for 4 months."

"I know."

"You said kendo was your thing. You said it at dinner. You said it right there." She pointed with her spoon at his chair. "And then you stopped going and you said it was stupid."

"It was," said Sho. "For me. It was stupid for me."

"And swimming."

"That was 10."

"And soccer."

"Mio," said their mother.

"I'm just saying." Mio put her spoon down too, now, so that all 3 of them had, and only their father was still eating. "I'm saying he'll quit by Golden Week."

Sho looked at her. She was sitting up very straight on her chair with her chin out, and her ears had gone pink at the tops. She looked about as sure as anybody had ever looked about anything.

He'd quit kendo in July. He remembered that now. He remembered her standing in the hallway in her summer pajamas when he came back from the last practice he went to, and him walking past her up the stairs, and her asking if he'd won, and him saying he wasn't going anymore. She'd been 6. He hadn't thought about it once since.

"Bet," he said.

Mio blinked. "What?"

"Your pudding cups." She had a row of them in the fridge, the little ones with the caramel at the bottom, 4 of them, lined up on the door shelf where everybody could see them and nobody was allowed to touch them. "If I'm still going after Golden Week. All of them."

She thought about it. She thought about it hard. He could see her doing it.

"Deal," she said. "And if you quit, I get your room."

"Okay."

"Okay?" She looked at their mother, as if their mother might stop it. Their mother was looking at Sho. "It's a bet, then. You can't take it back."

"I won't."

Mio got down from her chair. She went to the fridge, where the calendar hung on the door, the free 1 from the bank with the whole year on 1 sheet and a photo of Matsushima across the top. She took a magnet off the corner of it, round and red, with the bank's logo in white. She found May, and found Golden Week, and put the magnet on it, right in the middle, and pressed it flat with her thumb so it wouldn't slide.

Then she came back and sat down and picked up her spoon and went on eating.

Their mother watched her do it. Then she watched Sho, and Sho ate his curry.

"Volleyball," said his father, after a while. "That's the 1 with the net."

"Yes."

"Mm." His father ate a carrot. "Over the net. With your hands."

"Yes."

"Mm."

He went back to the news. He was eating slowly, the way he ate when he was thinking about work, and every so often his eyes came off the TV and went to Sho's nose.

Sho put the edamame down and looked at his rice.

His mother bought it from the same shop every time, from a man in Natori whose family grew it, and she always made too much, and Sho had eaten it every day of his life. The bowl was warm in his hand. The steam had made the rim wet.

"Is it bad?" his mother said. "Your nose?"

He looked up.

"No," he said. "It's good. Everything's good."

She looked at him a bit longer than she usually did. Then she nodded, and picked up her spoon, and asked Mio about her math test, and Mio said it was fine.

When the curry was finished and his father had got up to put the pot in the sink, Sho said, "Dad."

His father turned round with the pot in both hands.

"If it's all right," Sho said, "I'm going to need shoes. Volleyball ones. The kind with gum soles, for the gym floor. And knee pads." He stopped. "If it's all right. I'll use them. I'm going to use them."

His father looked at him over the pot.

"How much are they?"

"I don't know yet. I'll look."

"Mm." His father put the pot in the sink and ran the water into it. "Saturday," he said to the window.

"We'll see on Saturday," said his mother.

Mio was looking at the magnet on the fridge.

---

He couldn't sleep.

He lay in bed with the light off and both lives going round in his head, the clock at 3:14 and the clock on his own desk, which said 10:31, then 10:36, then 10:40. At 10:40 he gave up and got up and went downstairs in his socks.

The hall cupboard had the vacuum in it and the umbrellas and the box of things that didn't have a place. Under the box, flat and soft and gray with dust, was a ball. A volleyball, an old soft 1, from a beach trip when he was 7. He remembered his father throwing it to him in the shallow water and him missing every time and crying, and his father laughing and saying it didn't matter.

He found the bike pump by the back door and pumped it until it was hard and took it out to the side of the house, where the wall had no windows, only the drainpipe and the gas meter and the light from the kitchen coming round the corner.

Wall passes. The most boring drill there was. You stood 3 meters from a wall and passed the ball against it and passed it again when it came back, and you did it 1,000 times, and every coach in Japan made every beginner in Japan do it until they hated walls.

The 1st 1 went into the hedge.

He knew why. His elbows had bent at the last instant, like Hinata's. Satoshi's half of his head had the correction ready before the ball landed: lock your arms, thumbs together, angle the platform, move your feet 1st so your arms don't have to. Sho's arms heard all of that and did whatever they liked.

The 2nd 1 hit the gutter and came down on his head.

The 3rd came back to him clean. Off the wall and into his arms and back up, a little arc, the height he'd sent it, and he nearly shouted.

He kept going. He did 200. Maybe 40 were clean. The ball got away from him and he chased it into the hedge and out of the hedge and once under his father's car, and his forearms went red, then blotchy, then hot, the skin on the inside of his wrists stinging.

He wasn't breathing hard. This body hadn't done anything harder than the stairs to the 3rd floor in 3 years, and it should have been dying, and his legs felt like they'd been for a short walk. If anything they wanted more.

At 11:15 the window over the kitchen slid open.

"Kiyose Sho," said his mother, "it is a SCHOOL NIGHT."

"5 more."

"Now, Sho."

The window slid shut.

He did 5 more, and when he turned to pick up the ball from the hedge, the window above his mother's was open too. Mio's. She had her arms folded on the sill and her chin on her arms, and she was watching him.

He had no idea how long she'd been there.

"What are you doing," she said.

"Passing."

"To who?"

"The wall."

She looked at the wall. She looked at him, with his red arms and the ball under 1 of them, standing in the light from the kitchen at 11:20 on a school night.

"So weird," she said, and pulled her head in, and drew the curtain.

It stayed open a little at the side. He picked the ball out of the hedge and went in by the back door.

In the bathroom he washed his arms in the sink and looked at himself in the mirror.

A 15-year-old face. His own. Puffy across the bridge of the nose, a little purple under the left eye, a bit of dried blood still in the corner of 1 nostril. He'd seen this face every morning, bored, brushing his teeth. He was looking at it differently now, and it took him a while to see how: he was looking at it the way Satoshi looked at tape.

He dried his hands. His left hand went down, all on its own, and rested on his left knee, on the kneecap, and stayed there.

He took it away.

---

Upstairs, he sat at his desk and took the entry form out of his blazer and smoothed it flat with both hands.

*Name:* Kiyose Sho.

*Class:* 1-5.

*Club:* he wrote it carefully. Volleyball.

At the bottom there was a line that said *Position (if known)*, and he sat with his pen over it.

He thought about a setter yelling a name that belonged to somebody dead, and the ball hanging there, high and soft, waiting for him. About an orange-haired kid going up past the top of the net with nothing above him but the roof.

*I'm going to be Karasuno's ace,* the kid had said. Once to Kageyama, and once on the step, to Sho, so he'd know.

A team had 1 of those.

He wrote it anyway, small, in the box at the end of the line.

*Ace.*

Then he folded the form in 4 and put it in his bag where he'd see it in the morning, and set his alarm for 5:45, and turned off the light.

## Chapter 2: The wig

Sho got to the Second Gymnasium at 6:50 a.m. with his form, a red nose, and a plan.

The plan was simple. Be polite to everyone older than him. Agree with everything. Get on the court.

The door was already open. Inside, 3 people in practice clothes stood near the net, and 2 more stood in front of them, having yesterday's argument again.

"I'm the one who's going to beat you!"

"You can't even receive!"

Hinata and Kageyama. Apparently they just did this, like birds at dawn.

Sugawara spotted him and waved. "Kiyose! You came!" He turned to the broad-shouldered third year beside him. "Daichi, this is the one I told you about. The face."

"The face," Sho agreed.

Sawamura Daichi, captain, looked like someone had built a person out of a load-bearing wall. He had a calm, friendly smile that was somehow scarier than yelling. He took the form, read it, and stopped at the bottom.

"'Position: Ace,'" he read aloud.

"Aim high," Sho said.

The third person, a second year with a shaved head and the posture of a stray dog guarding a sandwich, leaned in to read over Daichi's shoulder. Then he leaned toward Sho until their faces were very close.

"Oi. First year. You think you can walk in here and be the ace?"

Sho looked at him. 29 years of life had taught him a lot of things, and one of them was this: some people are easy.

"Sorry," Sho said, with great sincerity. "I didn't know you'd already taken it. You've got the aura, senpai."

The second year froze.

"The... aura?"

"Ace aura. You can't fake it. I saw you and thought, ah. That's the guy who scores when it matters."

"I'm Tanaka," he said, ears going pink. "Tanaka Ryunosuke. Second year. Wing spiker." He coughed. "...I'm not technically the ace."

"Yet," Sho said.

Tanaka turned to face the wall, folded his arms, and whispered "yet" to himself like a prayer.

Sugawara put a hand over his mouth. Daichi looked at Sho for a long second, the way a man looks at a dog that has just opened the fridge.

"Any experience?" Daichi asked.

"In a past life," Sho said.

Daichi laughed. Sho let him.

---

What happened next, Sho would later describe to Mio as a Gintama episode with no laugh track.

Daichi had turned back to Hinata and Kageyama, who were still at it. Kageyama wanted proof that Hinata could receive his serve. Hinata wanted to give it. Daichi wanted them both to stop, and for a moment it looked like he might get his wish.

Then the side door opened, and a balding man in a suit walked in. The vice principal, according to Tanaka's sudden whisper, who had opinions about the volleyball club.

Kageyama served anyway.

Sho watched it go. A good serve, the same flat topspin as yesterday. Hinata's elbows were bent again. Satoshi's brain had time to file a complaint.

The ball came off Hinata's forearms, flew in a perfect arc across the gym, and struck the vice principal on the top of his head.

The top of his head lifted off.

It rotated once in the air, slowly, like a small, sad UFO.

It landed on Daichi's head.

Nobody breathed.

Hinata had gone the color of the ceiling. Kageyama looked like a statue of a boy regretting something. Tanaka was shaking with the effort of staying silent, and a single tear ran down his cheek. Sugawara stared at a spot on the floor with his whole soul.

Sho kept his face perfectly blank.

He had sat through 9 years of team meetings where a general manager said something unbelievable and everyone had to nod. He could have kept a straight face at his own funeral. He had, technically, missed it.

Daichi lifted the hairpiece from his head with both hands, like a cake, and returned it to its owner with a deep bow. Whatever the vice principal said next, Sho chose not to hear. It included the word "Sawamura" several times. Then the side door shut, and the gym went quiet.

Daichi turned around.

He was smiling.

"You two," he said to Hinata and Kageyama, in the kindest voice Sho had ever heard. "Out."

"But..."

"Captain, we..."

"Until you two can act like teammates, you're not joining this club." The smile didn't move. "Out."

They went. Hinata looked back twice. Kageyama didn't look back at all, but his shoulders said plenty.

The door slid shut. Daichi breathed out through his nose, turned to the rest of them, and clapped once.

"Right. Warm-up."

Sho decided he liked this captain a lot.

---

The warm-up was fine. Sho could run. Sho could stretch. His body did both without complaint.

Then Daichi started the receiving drill, and things went downhill fast.

It was the simplest drill in the sport. Daichi stood at the net and hit easy balls at whoever was in line, and you sent them up to Sugawara at the setter's spot. Satoshi had done this drill maybe 50,000 times. He could have done it asleep. On crutches. Dead, even.

The first ball Daichi sent him went into the ceiling, clanged off a light fixture, and dropped on Tanaka.

"Oi!"

"Sorry, senpai."

Sho knew exactly what had happened. He'd swung his arms at it. You never swing at a ball; you give it a platform and let your legs do the work. Satoshi's brain sent the correction instantly. Sho's arms received the memo, read it, and fed it to a shredder.

The second ball went off his thumb.

The third went straight back at Daichi, who caught it without looking impressed.

"Your feet are late," Daichi said.

"I know," Sho said. "And my platform tilts left because I'm reaching instead of moving. And my elbows bend at contact."

Daichi paused. "...You know all that?"

"Theoretical experience."

He knew everything. That was the problem. Knowing was the easy part, and it was worth exactly nothing until his body agreed. Every shank felt like watching tape of himself at 12, except the tape was live and everyone in the gym had a seat.

Tanaka laughed every time, delighted, like Sho was a dog trying to climb stairs.

On the 11th ball, Sho's feet got there first. Arms locked. Platform flat. The ball went up in a soft arc and dropped into Sugawara's hands.

"Nice receive!" Sugawara called.

Sho stood very still. It was a boring, normal, beginner's pass.

He wanted to run a lap of the gym screaming.

"That one," he told Tanaka, "was a set. For a very tall spiker."

"That's not how sets work!"

---

Then came spiking, and for about 3 seconds Sho thought it was going to be his day.

Sugawara tossed. Sho ran the approach the way Satoshi had run it 10,000 times: left, right-left, arms swinging back, plant, go.

The floor dropped away.

He went up so fast and so high that his stomach stayed on the boards. This body could jump. He hadn't known. Kiyose Sho had spent 3 years avoiding stairs, and his legs had been sitting on this the whole time.

He was up there early. Way too early. He hung at the top, swung at empty air, and came down in time for the ball to land on his head.

Bonk.

Tanaka fell over.

"Testing the ball's density," Sho said.

On the second one he waited too long, and the ball dropped past his hand while he was still rising.

The third one, he caught right. His palm met the ball at the top of his reach, and it left his hand like a gunshot. It smashed into the floor near the end line, kicked up, and hit the back wall so hard the sound echoed.

It was about a meter out.

The gym had gone quiet anyway.

Tanaka had stopped laughing. He was looking at the back wall, then at Sho, with an expression Sho knew from a hundred locker rooms. Every hitter had it. It meant: I want to hit one harder than that.

"Again," Tanaka told Sugawara, already walking to the line.

Sugawara tossed. Tanaka took 3 hard steps and went up, and his arm came through like a whip. Clean cross-court, inside the line, into the floor with a crack.

He landed, turned, and grinned at Sho with every tooth.

"That's how it's done, rookie."

Sho's first reaction was envy. His second was Satoshi's, older and more practical: that arm swing was beautiful. Late snap, high contact, wrist turning over at the last instant. You couldn't learn that from a video.

You could steal it, though.

"Teach me," Sho said.

Tanaka blinked. "Huh?"

"That cross. The wrist thing at the end. Teach me." There wasn't an ounce of pride in it. "I'll buy you a meat bun."

"...2 meat buns."

"Done."

Tanaka looked at Daichi like he'd just been handed a puppy. "Captain! He wants ME to teach him!"

"I heard," said Daichi. He was giving Sho the fridge look again.

---

The last part of practice was running.

By lap 15, Tanaka was swearing at the floor. By lap 20, a quiet second year named Ennoshita was lying flat on his back. By lap 25, even Daichi was breathing through his mouth.

Sho finished, looked around, and said, "Are we doing more?"

Everyone stared at him.

He wasn't trying to be a jerk. He just wasn't tired. His legs felt warm and loose and his lungs felt fine, which was honestly a little creepy. Satoshi's old body would have been in the nurse's office.

"You said you quit every sport," Sugawara said slowly.

"Yeah."

"So how are you in this kind of shape?"

Sho had no idea. His body was a sports car somebody had left in a garage for 15 years, and the keys had turned up yesterday.

"Spite," he said.

---

After practice, he begged.

Shamelessly. Sincerely. With a full bow.

"20 more minutes. I'll mop the whole floor. I'll put the net away. I'll wash the ball cart. I'll carry your bag to the station, Captain."

"I don't take the train," Daichi said.

"I'll carry it anyway. As a gesture."

In the end, Daichi gave him 20 minutes if he mopped, and Sugawara stayed to feed him balls, because Sugawara was apparently a saint. Sho got 1 good pass in 6. Then 1 in 4. On the last one, his feet moved before he told them to, and the ball floated up like it had been there all along.

He mopped the floor with a big, stupid grin.

---

Behind the gym, in the last of the light, he found Hinata and Kageyama.

They hadn't gone home. Kageyama was tossing, high and clean, and Hinata was chasing every ball across the gravel, scraping his knees and bouncing back up. They were still arguing. They were also still playing. Sho wasn't sure they knew the difference.

Hinata saw him first. "Ball-to-the-face guy! You made the team?!"

"Provisionally," Sho said.

"We'll be on it too!" Hinata pointed at the gym like it owed him money. "We're going to make the captain let us play a match. And then we're going to win!"

Kageyama tossed again. Hinata ran under it and jumped.

There it was again, that absurd jump. Sho watched the kid's hand go higher than it had any right to, and Satoshi's brain did something it hadn't done in years. It stopped taking things apart and just watched, because the thing in front of it was too strange to sort.

If someone ever gave that kid a set that matched that jump...

Sho walked home with his hands in his pockets and his heart going too fast.

Whatever match those 2 were planning, he wanted to be on the other side of the net.

## Chapter 3: Dawn reps

Sho's alarm went off at 4:30 a.m., and for 3 full seconds he hated Matsumoto Satoshi.

Satoshi had been a morning person. Satoshi had liked the gym at dawn, when the floor was cold and nobody was watching. Sho, until 2 days ago, had believed 4:30 a.m. was a rumor.

He got up anyway.

He crept downstairs in his socks with his bag over his shoulder, planning to be gone before anyone stirred. The kitchen light was on.

His mother stood at the counter in her cardigan, pressing rice between her palms. 2 onigiri already sat on a plate, wrapped in plastic. A 3rd was taking shape.

"Mom. It's 4:40."

"I know what time it is." She didn't look up. "Salmon or umeboshi?"

"...How did you know?"

"You set your alarm to the loudest one. The whole street knows." She wrapped a strip of nori around the rice and set it with the others. "Salmon, then. You hate umeboshi."

He did hate umeboshi. Sho had hated it since he was 4. Satoshi used to eat it straight from the jar. For a second the 2 lives ground against each other like gears with the wrong teeth, and then they settled, and he was just a kid in a kitchen while his mother made him breakfast in the dark.

She put all 3 in a cloth bundle and tied the corners. She still hadn't asked what he was doing at school at 5 a.m.

"Ittekimasu," he said at the door.

"Itterasshai." A pause. "Wear the jacket. It's cold."

It was 6 degrees. He wore the jacket.

---

The convenience store on the corner had 2 nikuman left in the warmer, the last of the season, a bit wrinkled from sitting there all night. Sho bought both. Then he ran.

The streets were blue and empty. Petals from the cherry tree by the shrine stuck to the wet road. His breath fogged. His legs didn't care about any of it; they wanted to go faster, so he let them, and he reached the Second Gymnasium at 4:58, barely breathing hard.

Hinata and Kageyama were already there, sitting on the concrete step outside the locked door.

Hinata shot to his feet. "Ball-to-the-face guy?!"

"Kiyose," Sho said. "Sho is fine."

"What are you doing here?!"

"Training. What are you doing here?"

"Tanaka-senpai said morning practice starts at 7," Hinata said, "so we came at 5!"

"So we'd get 2 hours," Kageyama added, like it was obvious.

Sho looked at the locked door. Then at the 2 of them, red-nosed with cold, with a ball between them they'd clearly been passing in the dark already.

"Did anyone tell you who has the key?"

Silence.

Kageyama's face did something complicated. Hinata's mouth opened and slowly closed.

"...Oh," said Hinata.

At 5:04, footsteps slapped up the path. Tanaka appeared in a tracksuit, yawning so wide his jaw cracked, a key dangling from one finger.

"I knew it," he said. "I knew you idiots would come at 5." Then he saw Sho. "Why are YOU here?!"

"My lesson," Sho said, and held out the plastic bag. "Cross-court. The wrist thing. Payment in advance."

Tanaka looked into the bag. 2 nikuman, still steaming faintly in the cold.

He stared at them for a long moment.

"Where did you even get these at 5 a.m.?"

"Konbini by the shrine. Last 2. I fought an old man for them." Pause. "I didn't. He let me have them. He said I looked desperate."

Tanaka's ears went pink. He shoved the key into the lock with a lot more force than it needed.

"...Senpai doesn't need bribes," he muttered.

"It's a bribe for later," Sho said. "I'm investing in a senpai."

"I'm NOT an investment."

He ate the first bun in 3 bites while he wrestled the door open. The second one he tucked carefully inside his jacket, which Sho decided not to mention.

---

Inside, the gym smelled like cold wood and somebody's old knee pads. Hinata bowed at the door and shouted "Onegaishimasu!" at the empty court. Kageyama bowed too, shorter and stiffer. Sho found himself bowing before he'd decided to. Satoshi had done it for 15 years, and the habit had come back with everything else.

They set up the net in the blue light. Hinata stood on the post base to reach the top and nearly fell off. Kageyama fixed the antenna 3 times. Tanaka sat on the edge of the stage and supervised, which mostly meant pointing.

Then the trouble started.

"Toss to me!" Hinata said.

"No," said Kageyama.

"Why not?!"

"Because you can't receive." Kageyama turned the ball over in his hands. "In a match, I toss to whoever we need to win. That's not you."

Hinata went red to the ears. "I'll get better!"

"Then get better first."

Sho, stretching his hamstrings against the wall, watched Kageyama's hands while he said it. Big hands, long fingers, very still. Setter's hands. Satoshi had seen a hundred kids like this on middle school tape: so good so young that nobody had ever taught them other people were still learning.

"Toss to me, then," Sho said.

Both of them turned.

"You?" said Kageyama.

"I'm tall. I can jump. I'm basically the attacker you just described." Sho stood up. "Prove your point. Use me as the example."

Hinata looked at him like he'd been betrayed by a stranger he'd known for 2 days. Kageyama thought about it, nodded once, and picked up a ball.

"Fine. Watch, Hinata. This is who I toss to."

The set was beautiful. It rose off Kageyama's fingers in a clean line and stopped exactly where an ace would want it, high and a little off the net, as if he'd measured Sho in the doorway and built the toss to fit.

Sho ran in, planted, and went up.

Early again. He was at the top of his jump with his hand cocked while the ball was still falling toward him. He waited. Gravity didn't. He came down just as the ball arrived, and it clipped his wrist on the way past and rolled off toward the stage.

Silence.

Tanaka coughed into his sleeve.

"...Your timing," Kageyama said slowly, "is garbage."

"Correct," said Sho.

"Then why did you ask?!"

"To see the toss." Sho rubbed his wrist. "It was a really good toss. I've..." Seen better, he almost said, because Satoshi had watched national team setters on tape. At 15, though? Nowhere close. "I've never seen one like that."

Kageyama opened his mouth. Closed it. A faint red crept up his neck.

"...Don't say weird things," he muttered, and went to get the ball.

Behind him, Hinata had started laughing, a helpless, hiccuping sound. "You're bad too!"

"Terrible," Sho agreed. "Want to be terrible together?"

Hinata grinned so wide it took up half his face. "Yeah!"

---

Sugawara found them at 5:40.

He came through the door in a scarf with his hands in his pockets, looked at the 4 of them, and sighed through his nose.

"Tanaka. You asked Daichi for the key yesterday with the worst poker face I've ever seen."

"Suga-san!" Tanaka nearly dropped the ball. "It's not... We're..."

"I won't tell him." Sugawara slipped off his shoes, set them neatly by the wall, and walked onto the court. "But if you're going to sneak, at least get some real receive practice. Hinata, Kiyose. Over here."

So the 2 of them stood side by side while Sugawara fed them easy balls, and they were, together, terrible.

Hinata's elbows bent. Sho's feet were late. Hinata dove for balls he didn't need to dive for. Sho stood perfectly still and let balls hit him in the chest while his brain narrated the correct footwork in great detail.

"Kiyose, you're thinking too much," Sugawara said.

"I know."

"Hinata, you're thinking too little."

"I know!"

By 6:30, Sho's forearms had gone from pink to red to a blotchy purple, and he was landing maybe 1 clean pass in 3. Hinata was about the same. Every time one of them got a good one, the other yelled "NICE!" like it was match point at Nationals. Sugawara kept his eyes on the ball cart, and the corner of his mouth kept twitching.

---

At 6:45, Sho went to the vending machine outside for water. Kageyama came with him, because Kageyama apparently needed milk the way other people needed air.

They stood at the machine. Sho fed in his coins. Around the corner of the gym, voices drifted on the cold air: Sugawara and Hinata, sitting on the step.

Sho didn't mean to listen. He stopped moving because Kageyama had stopped moving.

Sugawara was saying he was the team's setter. That he'd toss to Hinata, if Hinata wanted.

Hinata said no.

He said taking Sugawara's tosses now would feel like losing to Kageyama. He talked about last year: his only official match, against Kageyama's team, and how badly he wanted to beat him. Sugawara said something low, and Sho caught only the end of it. Something about the person Hinata most wanted to beat maybe also being the one who could make him strongest.

Kageyama stood in front of the vending machine with a coin in his hand and didn't put it in.

Sho looked at the side of his face. He'd seen that look on tape before. A 20-year-old starter benched mid-season, watching from the warm-up area. Someone who'd always been the best person in the room, finding out late that the room had other people in it.

Sho had 3 jokes ready.

He pressed the button for milk instead. The carton thunked into the tray. He took it out and held it out to Kageyama.

Kageyama looked at it. "...What."

"Milk. You were going to buy milk."

"I have money."

"I know you have money." Sho kept holding it out. "Take it. It's an investment. You're going to be a ridiculous setter, and I want to get in early, before I have to go through your agent."

Kageyama took it like it might go off.

"Why do you keep saying weird things," he said to the carton.

"Shamelessness," Sho said. "It's my best quality."

They walked back without talking. When they turned the corner, Hinata looked up from the step, his eyes a little red. Kageyama stopped in front of him, drank half the milk in one go, and said nothing at all.

Then, to the carton again: "Get up. We've got 15 minutes."

Hinata got up.

Over their heads, Sugawara caught Sho's eye. He didn't say anything either. He nodded once, like he was writing something down.

---

At afternoon practice, 2 new first years arrived.

Sho felt the room change before he saw them. Tanaka went stiff. Daichi and Sugawara looked up. The 2 boys in the doorway had their gakuran jackets over their arms and the easy posture of people who'd never once been the shortest in a room.

The taller one was blond, with glasses and headphones around his neck and a face that said he'd been bored by this conversation in a previous life. He had to be close to 190. The other was shorter and freckled, and he hovered at the blond one's elbow.

"Chiwassu," said the blond one, and bowed to the third years at exactly the right angle with none of the feeling.

"Tsukishima Kei," Daichi read off the form. "Yamaguchi Tadashi. Welcome."

Sho looked up at Tsukishima from 6 centimeters below and felt a little offended on his body's behalf.

So, naturally, he went over.

"You're huge," Sho said. "Blocking must come free with that."

Tsukishima looked at him. It took about a second. Sho watched him read the whole thing: the friendly smile, the angle, the angle behind the angle.

"Flattery's free too," Tsukishima said pleasantly. "Doesn't make it worth anything."

"Nice one, Tsukki!" said Yamaguchi.

"Shut up, Yamaguchi."

"Sorry, Tsukki!"

Sho stared at them. Then he started grinning, and he couldn't stop.

He'd spent 2 days flattering people and watching them melt. Tanaka melted. Hinata melted. Even Kageyama went pink around the edges. And here was somebody who'd looked straight through him and handed the flattery back like a bad coin.

"I like you," Sho said.

Tsukishima's face did a small, involuntary thing. "...Please don't."

---

Daichi waited until warm-ups were done. Then he called everyone in.

"Saturday," he said, "we're playing a match. 3 on 3. Hinata, Kageyama and Tanaka against our new first years."

Tanaka pumped a fist. Kageyama went still. Hinata's whole body vibrated.

"If Hinata and Kageyama win, they're in. If they lose..." Daichi's smile didn't move. "Kageyama doesn't play setter while I'm on this team."

Kageyama's jaw went tight. Hinata looked at him, then away.

"Tsukishima and Yamaguchi make 2," Sugawara said. "They need a 3rd."

"I was going to play," Daichi said. He turned his head. "Kiyose."

Sho had been mid-stretch. He froze.

"You want to play, right?"

Sho's hand was already up. It had gone up on its own, the way Hinata's whole body did whenever a ball was nearby.

"Captain, I would do terrible things to play."

"Don't do terrible things." Daichi looked at him for a long moment, the fridge look again, with something else under it that Sho couldn't read. "I want to see you in a real match. And I want to watch this one from outside." He nodded at the far side of the net. "Take my spot. You're with Tsukishima and Yamaguchi."

"WHAT?!" said Tanaka. "He's on the enemy team?!"

"Sorry, senpai." Sho bowed. "The lesson still counts. I'm going to use it against you."

Tanaka stared. Then a slow, terrible grin spread across his face, all teeth.

"Oh, you little... Good. GOOD. I'll crush you, Kiyose!"

"Please do your best, senpai."

"STOP BEING POLITE ABOUT IT."

Behind Sho, Tsukishima said to no one in particular, "Great. A loud one."

---

That evening, Sho walked into the confrontation instead of the other way around.

He'd stayed late to mop, which had turned into a race, end to end, against Yamaguchi, who was fast and extremely competitive about mops. When he came out, Hinata and Kageyama were practicing on the dirt behind the gym under the one working light, and Tsukishima and Yamaguchi were strolling toward them.

Sho stopped in the shadow of the bike racks. He told himself he was tying his shoe.

## Chapter 5: Eyes closed

Kageyama's hands touched the ball.

Sho watched the toss leave them.

It didn't go up. That was the first wrong thing. A toss is supposed to rise and hang and give the hitter a moment. This one went sideways, fast and flat, a short hard line toward the right side of the net, toward a spot in the air where nobody was standing.

Except someone was already there.

Hinata had started his run before the pass. Now he was in the air, high, impossibly high, rising past the tape with his right arm already swinging, and the ball was still on its way to him.

The gym went quiet.

It happened between one heartbeat and the next. The squeak of shoes, the second years on the bench, Tanaka's breathing, all of it dropped away like someone had pulled the plug on a speaker. Sho could hear his own pulse in his ears.

And Hinata changed.

His body stayed where it was, and Sho saw it as joints. Shoulder, elbow, wrist, hips, knees: a skeleton made of points of light, and the brightest was the right forearm, burning white. A pale shape peeled off it and moved ahead of the rest. A ghost of the arm, one blink early, swinging down and across toward the deep left corner of Sho's side.

Sho's legs moved.

He didn't tell them to. They went anyway, left and back, toward the corner where the ghost was pointing.

They were too slow.

The ball hit Hinata's palm with a crack like a door slamming in an empty house. It went straight down past Tsukishima's fingertips and smacked the floor 2 meters in front of Sho's outstretched arms.

20-17.

The sound came back all at once. Shoes. Breathing. Someone on the bench saying, "Eh?"

Sho stood in the corner with his arms still out, looking at the spot where the ball had landed. It was exactly where the ghost had pointed. Exactly.

Then he looked at Hinata.

Hinata had landed. He was staring at his right hand like it belonged to somebody else. And Sho, who'd been looking at his face the whole time without meaning to, without even knowing how he'd seen it so clearly, said it out loud before he could stop himself.

"His eyes were closed."

Tsukishima turned his head. "What?"

"Hinata. When he hit it. His eyes were shut."

Kageyama had gone very still. He turned slowly and stared at Hinata.

"You closed your eyes," he said.

"You said you'd bring it!"

"I said don't LOOK at it. I didn't say close your..." Kageyama stopped. His face did something complicated, as if a hundred things were trying to get out at once and had all jammed in the door.

"It felt..." He couldn't find the word. "It hit right here. Right in the middle."

Nothing had happened since. No light, no ghost. Twice Hinata had gone up and twice Sho had watched him with everything he had, and twice he'd seen a short kid jumping too high and nothing more. Whatever had happened at 20-16 was gone, and it hadn't left a note.

So was he imagining it? A good guess. A tired brain filling in a gap. Satoshi had read the papers back in his analyst days, about how the brain stretches a frightening moment afterward, making it longer and clearer in memory than it ever was.

That was probably it.

Except the ball had landed exactly where the ghost pointed.

He came back from his 3rd trip with a look of grim determination and the news that he'd met the tall boy, who was called Kindaichi and who had told him Kageyama only saw people as pieces. Sho listened to all this while stretching, and said nothing. It wasn't his story.

But when they lined up at the net to bow before the first set, he was standing across from Kindaichi.

Kindaichi looked him up and down. Tall, new, no number on the jacket yet. Nobody.

"Kiyose," Sho said pleasantly. "Remember it. It'll be on the scoresheet a lot."

Kindaichi's eyebrows went up. Beside him, a pale boy with a bored face and half-closed eyes glanced over at Sho, then away, as if Sho had been mildly interesting for a full second and that was the limit.

"Onegaishimasu!"

They went to their positions. Sho looked back once at the bench.

Sugawara and Ennoshita sat side by side, in their jackets, hands on their knees. 2 seniors pushed out by first years. Neither of them was looking at the other. Both of them were looking at the court.

Sho turned around and didn't look back again.

He'd pay that later. With interest.

---

Set 1 was a disaster, and most of it was Hinata.

On the very first rally, Hinata cut in front of Daichi's receive and the ball went sideways off both of them. Tanaka's spike got stuffed by Kindaichi. Hinata missed a block by a meter, then jumped for a ball that was Tsukishima's, then forgot which rotation they were in.

Kageyama yelled. Kindaichi, across the net, watched the yelling with an expression of quiet, grim satisfaction, like a man watching a prediction come true.

Sho's own problems were quieter.

The first time Kageyama set him, he was late.

He'd spent a week learning to wait. Wait for the ball to peak, count 1, go. That worked beautifully on Yamaguchi's rainbows. Kageyama's set to the right side came fast and flat, already dropping by the time Sho's brain said go, and he reached the ball late and low and hit it straight into Kindaichi's hands.

"Mine. My bad," Sho said, before Kageyama could open his mouth.

Kageyama shut it. He looked thrown, as though he'd loaded a shout and had nowhere to put it.

On the next dead ball, Sho walked over to him.

"Their middle," he said, low. "The tall one. Does he jump on your tempo or the hitter's?"

Kageyama answered instantly. Kindaichi read the setter. He'd always read the setter. In middle school, he'd watched Kageyama's hands, and he'd gotten good at it, and if Kageyama gave away the direction early, Kindaichi would be there. Kageyama said all this in about 9 seconds, with more words in a row than Sho had ever heard from him.

Then he stopped, like he'd surprised himself.

"Got it," Sho said. "So he watches you. Does anybody watch me?"

Kageyama looked at him. "Nobody knows who you are."

"Excellent," said Sho. "Let's keep it that way."

It didn't save the set. Seijoh's servers found the seam between Sho and Daichi twice, and Sho, hiding at opposite, still ended up passing 1 of them into the stands. His timing on Kageyama's sets got better, one swing at a time, and he hit 2 decent balls in the whole set, and it didn't matter.

13-24.

Hinata went back to serve.

Sho could see his hands shaking from the front row. He saw Hinata toss the ball before the whistle had even finished, and swing at it in a blind panic, and he knew, he absolutely knew, where it was going about a quarter-second before it got there.

The ball hit Kageyama square in the back of the head.

Thunk.

The gym went silent, in the bad way.

The ball rolled gently off toward the sideline. Kageyama didn't move. Hinata's face went the color of paper. On the Seijoh side, Kindaichi had both hands over his mouth.

Then Tanaka made a strangled noise and folded in half, and Tsukishima turned around and put his face in the crook of his elbow, and his shoulders started shaking.

Kageyama turned around very, very slowly.

"Great aim," Sho said into the silence. "Wrong target."

Kageyama's eyes snapped to him and stayed there, black and flat, for 2 full seconds. Sho met them pleasantly. He was 44 years old, technically, and he'd been glared at by a general manager who'd once made a scout cry.

Then Kageyama looked away from him and walked over to Hinata.

He reached up and slapped the back of his own head, hard, right where the ball had hit.

"Is there anything," he asked Hinata, "scarier than what you just did?"

Hinata thought about it. Sho watched him actually think about it, the panic draining out of his face as he did.

"...No?"

"Then there's nothing left to be scared of. So play normally."

13-25.

---

The break between sets was 3 minutes long. Tanaka used all of them.

He sat down next to Hinata and talked, with his elbows on his knees, not too loud. Everyone already knew Hinata wasn't good yet, he said. He didn't need to prove anything to anybody. Nobody was going to take him off for messing up. If he messed up, someone would cover him, because that was what a team was.

Sho stood a few steps away with a towel over his shoulder and kept his mouth shut. This was Tanaka's, all of it.

When Tanaka finished and stood up, and Hinata was sitting a little straighter, Sho walked over, took the onigiri out of his jacket pocket, and set it on Hinata's knee.

Hinata looked at it. Then up at Sho.

Sho didn't say anything. He went back to the bench.

Someone pushed a water bottle into his hand. He took it, twisted the cap, and found it had already been loosened.

Ennoshita didn't look at him. He was looking at the court, at the Seijoh side, where the tall middle blocker was drinking from a bottle of his own.

"Kindaichi," Ennoshita said quietly. "Their tall middle. After long rallies, he drifts toward Kageyama. Every time. He wants to be the one who blocks him."

Sho went very still.

He'd been on court for the whole set, watching that blocker with Satoshi's brain, and he hadn't caught it. You couldn't see everything from inside. Ennoshita had been sitting on the bench with nothing to do but watch, and he'd watched, and he'd seen it.

"Thank you, Ennoshita-san," Sho said.

"Mm." Ennoshita still didn't look at him. "Use it."

---

Set 2 started badly, too.

Tsukishima's receive went off his arms at an angle and Kageyama had to chase it, and his toss to Hinata on the run came out high, and Hinata swung at air.

On the Seijoh side, Kindaichi said something to Hinata through the net. Sho didn't catch it, but it was obvious from the face: now he's going to scream at you.

Kageyama walked over to Hinata.

"That one was too high," he said. "My fault. I'll fix it."

On the other side of the net, Kindaichi's face went completely blank. The bored boy beside him, Kunimi, actually opened his eyes all the way.

The next quick landed. Hinata came down from it with his hands up and his whole face lit, and Sho, standing at the 3-meter line, saw something on Hinata's face that he hadn't seen on the 3v3 day. Hinata looked like somebody who'd just found out he was part of something.

After that, Hinata started running, and he didn't stop.

He ran at every set. Fast, eyes shut, all the way. And every time he ran, Seijoh's blockers went with him, because they had to, because if they didn't he'd score. Tanaka came in behind him and found an open net. Kageyama set Tanaka. Tanaka scored. Kageyama set Hinata. Hinata scored.

10-9 to Karasuno.

Then Sho got his chance.

A long rally. 6 touches, 7, the ball going back and forth over the net until everyone was breathing hard. Hinata went charging in toward Kageyama again, and Sho watched Kindaichi's feet, and there it was. Kindaichi drifted toward the middle. Toward the King. Just like Ennoshita had said.

Which left the right side with 1 blocker. The bored one. Kunimi.

Kageyama saw it too. The ball came out to the right, fast and flat, and this time Sho had already started, a half-beat early, because he'd been counting Kageyama's tempo all set.

Kunimi went up. Late, and not very high, and not very interested.

Sho hit it high off his fingertips, hard, and it went out of bounds over the Seijoh bench.

11-9.

Sho landed. He didn't celebrate. He pointed back over his shoulder at the Karasuno bench without turning around.

On the bench, Ennoshita's mouth twitched.

Kunimi shook out his hand and looked at Sho with the same faint, bored interest as before.

"You're coasting," Sho told him through the net, cheerfully.

Kunimi considered this. "...And?"

Sho grinned. He liked this one. Somebody who'd worked out the exact minimum and refused to go a gram over it. Satoshi had known pros like that. The scary ones were always the ones who could turn it off, because it meant they knew exactly when to turn it on.

---

The middle of the set got messy.

Tsukishima and Kageyama started bickering about a block. Kageyama said Tsukishima had jumped late. Tsukishima said Kageyama's set had been to the wrong place, so he'd had to cover the wrong hitter. They were both a little right and both completely unwilling to say so.

"Gentlemen," Sho said, stepping between them with his hands up. "Please. Let's settle this the way civilized people do."

They both looked at him.

"Rock paper scissors," Sho said.

"No," said Tsukishima.

"No," said Kageyama.

"Finally," said Sho. "Something you agree on."

Tsukishima looked at him for a long moment. "Hitting with that face?" he said. "Very cool."

"Thank you. I practice it in the mirror."

Tsukishima's mouth tightened. It had nothing to bite on.

On the next rally, Iwaizumi, Seijoh's acting captain, came in on the left with a ball set high and outside. Sho read him the old way. Approach from outside the sideline: cross-court. He watched the hitting hand pull back and vanish behind Iwaizumi's head: full swing.

Kageyama and Tsukishima went up together and put a wall in front of the line. Sho slid 2 steps left into the cross-court lane before the ball was hit.

Iwaizumi hit it hard and cross. It smacked into Sho's forearms, right in the middle, and popped up high and clean toward the net.

No silence. No light. No ghost. Just a read, and his feet getting there first.

He'd take it.

"NICE RECEIVE!" Hinata screamed, and ran in, and Kageyama set Tanaka, and Tanaka hit it so hard the ball bounced into the balcony.

Iwaizumi landed, looked at Sho for a second, and frowned. It was an honest frown, a player's frown: who is that.

---

Daichi called a timeout at 18-16. In the huddle, Hinata talked fast.

"In middle school, I didn't have a team. Not really. So I had to do everything myself. But here..." He looked around at them, at Tanaka and Daichi and even Tsukishima. "Here I can just jump. And trust you guys to do the rest."

Daichi smiled. "That's a real weapon, Hinata."

Sho, at the back of the huddle, dabbed his forehead with a towel and didn't say anything. He was busy watching Kageyama's face while Hinata talked. Kageyama was looking at his own hands.

---

24-22.

Set point. Kageyama set Hinata, eyes shut, and the ball hit the floor behind Kindaichi's outstretched arms before anyone else moved.

25-22.

One set each.

Hinata did a lap of their half of the court with his arms out. Tanaka chased him. Daichi let them.

Then the screaming started.

It came from the balcony. Girls, maybe 10 of them, all at once, leaning over the railing and waving. Sho looked up, confused, and then followed their eyes down to the doorway.

A tall boy in a Seijoh jacket had just walked in. Brown hair, perfectly arranged. He waved up at the balcony with both hands and a huge smile, and the screaming got louder.

Then he walked straight to the Seijoh bench and bowed to the coach, an older man with a calm face, who told him he was welcome back, and not to overdo it like last time, and to take a long warm-up.

"Who," said Tanaka, with deep loathing, "is that nice guy?"

Kageyama answered without taking his eyes off him.

"Oikawa-san. My senpai in middle school. He's their real setter." A pause. "I learned serving from watching him. Blocking too." Another pause. "His personality is worse than Tsukishima's."

"Hey," said Tsukishima.

Hinata stared across the gym at Oikawa with enormous eyes. "So he's like... the King's king. The Great King."

Nobody corrected him.

Oikawa stretched by the Seijoh bench, chatting, smiling, waving at the balcony now and then. Then, while he was bent over his own knee, his eyes came up and went across the net.

They went to Kageyama first. They stayed there for a long moment, and the smile on his face didn't change at all, and that was somehow worse.

Then they moved. Hinata. Tanaka. Daichi. Tsukishima.

They slid over Sho, and moved on.

Then they came back.

Only for a second. A small, polite, curious second, like a man who'd glanced at a menu and noticed a dish he didn't recognize.

Sho looked straight back at him.

For exactly one beat, the back of his neck prickled.

Then Satoshi's brain opened a fresh page, and wrote at the top: Oikawa. Setter. Watches people.

And underneath that, in smaller letters: He watches like I do.

## Chapter 8: Grand King

Oikawa warmed up for a long time.

That was the first thing Sho noticed about him in set 3. The coach had told him to take it slow, and he was taking it slow, the way a cat takes it slow when it's watching a bird. He stretched on the sideline behind the Seijoh bench. He jogged. He bounced a ball, caught it, bounced it again. He waved at the balcony every few minutes and the balcony screamed every time.

And the whole time, his eyes were on Karasuno.

Sho saw it on the 4th rally. Daichi passed a serve, and Oikawa's eyes went to Daichi's arms. Tanaka passed one, and they went to Tanaka's arms. Hinata dove for one and shanked it into the net, and Oikawa smiled.

He was shopping.

Sho, hidden at opposite where he didn't have to pass anything, felt the cold part of his brain click over. Oikawa was building a list of who couldn't receive. Tsukishima would be on it. Hinata would be on it. And Sho would be on it too, except Daichi had hidden him, so Oikawa had never seen him try.

If Daichi had put him in serve receive, Sho would be at the top of that list.

He kept that thought and played.

---

Set 3 went their way, mostly.

Kageyama had stopped being surprised by Hinata, and Hinata had stopped being scared of anything. The quick landed, missed, landed again. Tanaka scored off the gaps it left. Tsukishima and Kageyama put up a wall on Iwaizumi that made the Seijoh bench groan. Sho got 2 balls on the right side and hit them both off Kindaichi's hands and out, because Kindaichi kept drifting toward Kageyama, just like Ennoshita had said, and Sho saw no reason to stop using a gift.

24-21.

Match point.

The Seijoh coach raised his hand. Kunimi walked off the court without any visible feeling about it.

Oikawa walked on.

---

He took a ball from the line judge and walked back behind the end line, and he kept walking. 4 big steps. He stopped, turned, and bounced the ball 3 times.

Then he lifted one hand and pointed across the net.

At Tsukishima.

"You can have all the attacking power in the world," Oikawa said pleasantly, to nobody and everybody. "But if the receive doesn't connect, it doesn't mean anything."

Tsukishima's face didn't change. His hands, Sho noticed, went flat against his thighs.

The whistle blew.

Oikawa tossed with one hand, high, out in front of his right shoulder. 4 steps, getting longer and faster. He went up into the air behind the end line and hit the ball at the top of his jump with his whole body behind it.

The sound was a flat crack, like somebody slapping a table.

The ball came over the net with topspin, low and fast, and dove at the last second, and Tsukishima got his arms on it, and it went off them sideways and out.

Oikawa landed a meter inside the court and was already smiling.

24-22.

"That serve," Sugawara said quietly on the bench, "is stronger than Kageyama's. And he can put it wherever he wants."

Sho, at the net, didn't turn around. He was replaying it.

Toss: 1 hand, right hand, about 2 meters above his head, just in front of the hitting shoulder. Approach: 4 steps. Contact: top of the jump, ball in front. Landing: well inside the line.

He'd seen that serve before. Satoshi had seen it 1,000 times on tape from the best servers in the V.League. He'd just never seen a high school kid do it this clean.

Oikawa got the ball back and walked to the same spot. 3 bounces. He pointed at Tsukishima again.

This time Sho watched only the toss.

Same hand. Same height. Same spot in the air. The ball came over, hit Tsukishima's forearm, and went somewhere useless.

24-23.

The gym was loud now. The balcony was screaming. The Seijoh bench was on its feet. On the Karasuno side it was very quiet, and Sho could hear Tsukishima breathing.

He didn't look at Tsukishima. Tsukishima would hate being looked at right now. Sho knew this the way he knew his own height.

Hinata looked.

"Hey!" Hinata shouted across the net. "Great King! Aim at me instead!"

"Shut up," said Tsukishima, not turning his head.

"No! Everybody on this side of the net is your ally!" Hinata looked back over his shoulder at Tsukishima, red-faced and absolutely serious. "That's what Tanaka-senpai said!"

Tanaka made a choked sound, and when Sho glanced over, he had his hand pressed to his chest like he'd been shot.

Tsukishima's jaw tightened. He didn't say anything else.

Sho had a suggestion ready. He'd had it since the first serve. Spread out. Make Oikawa's target smaller so he has to take power off to hit it.

He opened his mouth.

Daichi got there first.

"Spread out," Daichi said, calm. "Hinata, Tsukishima, Tanaka, Kiyose. Wider. I'll take more court."

They spread out. Daichi slid over until he was covering almost half the back of the court by himself.

Sho closed his mouth.

He noticed something else while he did. He'd been about to say it. Daichi had said it 2 seconds earlier, without any tape, without any dead man's 9 years of analysis. Just a captain who looked at his team and knew where the hole was.

Sho filed that under things to respect.

Oikawa looked at the new formation from behind the end line. Then he smiled, a different smile, the smile of somebody who'd been asked a harder question than he expected and liked it.

He served.

It was softer. Not by much. Enough. To get the ball to Tsukishima past Daichi's arms, he'd had to take some of the power off, and Tsukishima got the ball up. It went high and floated, and drifted back over the net, slow and easy.

A free ball. For Seijoh.

"Chance ball!" someone on their side yelled.

It went to the setter, and the setter went to Kindaichi, and Kindaichi came in hard and swung.

Hinata was already at the net. Sho didn't know how; he'd been in the back row a second ago. He jumped and got a fingertip on it, and the ball slowed and dropped and Tanaka dug it up, ugly and high.

Hinata hit the floor and kept going. He sprinted the whole width of the court, from the left side to the right, through the gap between Sho and Tsukishima.

Kageyama was under the ball. He didn't even look at Hinata.

The set went out fast and flat to the right side, and Hinata was there, already in the air, eyes shut.

The ball went past Oikawa's left ear and into the floor.

25-23.

---

Sho took a step back.

He didn't mean to. His body did it the way it had done it in college, when a teammate made the winning play and the right thing was to give them the court.

At the edge of his vision, Hinata was screaming with his arms up, and Tanaka was screaming with him, and Kageyama stood very still with his mouth fighting something it was losing.

Sho looked across the net instead.

Oikawa hadn't moved. He was standing where he'd landed after the dig attempt, turned halfway toward the spot where the ball had hit the floor.

And he was smiling.

This one was small and sharp and real, nothing like the one he gave the balcony, and it was aimed at Kageyama.

Sho filed that too.

---

They lined up and bowed. "Arigatou gozaimashita!"

Then the Karasuno team ran across and bowed to the Seijoh bench and the coach, and ran back and bowed to their own bench, and Sho found himself standing next to Ennoshita in the line. He stepped half a pace back, so that Ennoshita stood in front of him. He didn't explain. Ennoshita didn't ask.

Sugawara, passing behind them, reached out and flicked Sho in the forehead.

"Ow."

"Don't make that face at Ennoshita," Sugawara murmured. "He'll be fine." Then he was gone, cheerful, helping Daichi carry the ball bag.

Takeda-sensei made a speech in the hallway. He talked about watching something incredible start to grow. He compared Hinata and Kageyama to a chemical reaction, 2 substances that are ordinary alone and turn into something new when you put them together, and halfway through his own sentence he went pink and apologized for being too poetic.

"No, that was good, sensei," said Hinata, who hadn't understood a word.

Sho had understood all of it. He watched Takeda put his notebook away and noticed that on the open page, underlined twice, was the word coach.

---

Tsukishima was at the water bottles alone.

Sho went over to get his own. Tsukishima didn't look at him. He drank, set the bottle down, and said, before Sho could open his mouth:

"Don't."

"I was going to say your shoelace is untied."

Tsukishima looked down at his shoe.

The shoelace was fine.

He looked back up at Sho with an expression of deep, weary contempt. Sho smiled back. Neither of them said anything else, and after a second Tsukishima picked up his bag and walked off, and Sho noticed he'd left his water bottle on the bench beside Sho's like a placeholder.

Sho picked both of them up and carried them out.

---

Oikawa was waiting at the gate.

He was leaning on the gatepost with his jacket over his shoulders, as if he'd just happened to be there, and when Karasuno came out he pushed off it and smiled at all of them.

Hinata and Tanaka immediately puffed up like 2 cats. Kageyama stopped walking.

"Hey, hey. Don't look at me like that," Oikawa said. "I came to say good game."

He said good game to Hinata, specifically. He said Hinata's jump was amazing and his speed was amazing and the quick was amazing. Hinata started to glow.

Then Oikawa said Hinata's receive was terrible, and so was Tsukishima's, and if Karasuno didn't fix that before the Inter-High prelims, there wasn't much point in turning up. He said it in exactly the same pleasant voice. He said there wasn't much time.

Then he looked at Kageyama.

"Tobio-chan," he said. "Next time it's official, I'll crush you properly."

Kageyama said nothing. His hands were fists.

Daichi stepped forward. He didn't argue. He agreed, in fact, that their receive needed work. Then he said, very calmly, that their receiving was about to get a lot better, because one of their members was coming back soon.

"Our Guardian Deity," Daichi said.

Hinata's head snapped around. "Our WHAT?"

Oikawa smiled at Daichi like he'd been told a fun secret, and seemed about to leave.

Sho stepped forward.

He had decided to do this somewhere around 24-22, while he was watching the toss for the second time. He'd weighed it. It was embarrassing, it had no chance of working, and it would cost him nothing he cared about.

He bowed. A little too deep.

"Teach me your jump serve," he said.

The whole Karasuno team went silent.

Oikawa looked at him. For a second, the smile stayed exactly where it was, but something behind his eyes shut, like a door closing in a lit house. It was there and then it wasn't.

Sho saw it. He kept his face friendly and didn't let on that he'd seen it.

"I'll pay," Sho added. "In praise. I'm very good at praise. Ask anyone."

"He is," said Tanaka, nodding seriously.

Oikawa tilted his head. "And you are... Karasuno's..." He looked at Sho's jacket, which had no number on it. "Tall-kun?"

"Kiyose."

"Kiyose-kun." Oikawa said it like he was holding it up to the light and finding it a bit cheap. "I'll have forgotten that by dinner."

"That's fine," said Sho. "I'll remind you at the prelims."

Oikawa's smile widened. It was the sort of smile you'd want to see on a shark if you had to see one at all.

"Then watch closely at the prelims, Kiyose-kun," he said. "I'll show you my serve. From very, very close."

It was a threat dressed up as a present, and he said it beautifully.

"OIKAWA."

A voice from up the path. A broad, short-haired boy in a Seijoh jacket was striding toward them with a face like a closed fist. Iwaizumi. He reached Oikawa, took him by the back of the collar without breaking stride, and started walking him away.

"Iwa-chan! I was being nice!"

"You were being you." Iwaizumi looked back over his shoulder at Sho for a second, then at Oikawa, then at Sho again. "Great. There's 2 of them now."

Then, to the rest of Karasuno, with a short, real bow: "Sorry about him."

They went up the path. Oikawa was still complaining. Iwaizumi didn't let go.

Kageyama turned to the team. "I'm sorry about that," he said stiffly. "About what he said."

Daichi waved it away.

Tanaka turned to Sho, eyes wide. "You just ASKED him? To his FACE?"

"He said no," Sho said.

"He didn't say no."

"He said no very creatively." Sho picked up his bag. "That's the best kind. It means he thought about it."

---

On the bus, Sho sat next to Ennoshita.

He'd bought 2 melon breads from the vending corner at Seijoh. He tore one in half, a bit unevenly, and held out the bigger half without saying anything. Ennoshita took it without saying anything either.

They ate for a while. The bus pulled out. The light outside was going orange.

"What else did you see?" Sho asked. "From the bench."

Ennoshita looked at him sideways.

"In set 3. Anything. I was on the court, so I couldn't see anything that wasn't on top of me."

Ennoshita chewed, swallowed, and looked out the window for a moment. Then he started talking.

He'd seen that Seijoh's libero cheated toward the line every time Tanaka was in the front row. He'd seen that Kageyama always looked at Hinata before a quick, even when the set was going somewhere else, and that the Seijoh blockers hadn't figured that out yet but would. He'd seen that Sho, when he was nervous about a set, started his approach with his left foot turned out.

Sho stopped chewing.

"Do I?"

"Mm."

Sho thought about that for a long time. Satoshi had watched other people's feet for 9 years. Nobody had ever watched his.

"Thank you, Ennoshita-san," he said.

Ennoshita shrugged and went back to his melon bread. But he kept talking, on and off, all the way to the city limits, and Sho kept listening.

Somewhere around the river, Takeda-sensei turned around in his seat at the front, notebook open.

## Chapter 9: Guardian deity

A 3rd-year wing spiker. The one Nishinoya had fought with. The one Sugawara couldn't smile about. The one the libero refused to play without. A team with no libero and no ace was a team with 2 holes, and those 2 holes were the same story.

"Don means don, ssu."

He knew what he was doing wrong. He knew every single time. The ball came, and he watched it, and his brain did the work: line, speed, spin, where it would land. By the time the answer arrived, the ball was already past his best moment, and his feet were late, and the platform came up a beat behind, and the ball came off it sideways.

On the 21st shank, Nishinoya walked over and slapped him on the back hard enough to make him stagger.

"You're thinking with your eyes!"

Sho looked down at him. "I'm what?"

"Your eyes are going like this." Nishinoya made a frantic tracking motion with his fingers. "You're watching it, and watching it, and watching it, and THEN your feet go. Your feet are late. You're waiting to be sure!"

"I need to be sure where it's going."

"No you don't!" Nishinoya looked genuinely baffled. "Then you're too late! You just go! If you're wrong, you fix it on the way!"

Sho opened his mouth to explain why that was wrong, and stopped.

Because he'd just watched Nishinoya receive 30 balls in a row. And Nishinoya's feet moved before Daichi's hand touched the ball. Every time. Before the hit. And he was right almost every time, and when he was wrong, he adjusted in mid-step, small, and was right by the time the ball arrived.

He was reading. He was reading faster than words.

And Sho thought, with a sudden cold drop in his stomach, about the 3v3. About the eye. It had never fired when he was trying. It had fired when the play outran his brain, when there was no time to be sure, when he'd stopped narrating and just moved.

It had fired in exactly the place Nishinoya lived all the time.

Okay, Sho thought. Okay. That's data.

He went back to the line.

"Shu," he said to himself. "Don."

Daichi hit the ball. Sho didn't wait for his brain to finish. His feet went, and they went slightly wrong, and on the way he felt them correct, a small step left, and he got low and put his platform out and gave the ball a short shove forward instead of swinging.

Thup.

The ball went up and came down a meter from the setter's spot.

Nishinoya didn't say anything nice. He just grabbed the next ball out of the cart and slapped it into Sho's chest.

"AGAIN!"

He bowed, stiffly. "Nishinoya-san. Onegaishimasu."

Nishinoya grinned and got into position. Kageyama served. Thup. Kageyama served again. Thup. Kageyama served a 3rd time, harder, with a look on his face like he was taking it personally, and Nishinoya dove and got it and rolled over his shoulder and came up on his feet shouting "ROLLING THUNDER!"

"...Kidding," Sho added. "Mostly."

"You write a lot."

Nishinoya and Tanaka had crossed the gym. They stood in front of him, one very short, one bald, both with their arms folded.

"Kiyose," said Tanaka. "A question."

"Yes, senpai."

"What," said Nishinoya, "do you think of Kiyoko-san?"

"Like the sun," he said. "You don't look directly at it."

Nishinoya and Tanaka looked at each other. Then they turned back to Sho and nodded, slowly and gravely, like 2 elders accepting a new member into a village.

"Correct," said Tanaka.

"Correct," said Nishinoya. "But you're not in the guard. The guard is full."

"I accept," said Sho.

---

At the very end, Sho asked Sugawara to toss him a few spikes before they put the net away.

Sugawara said of course. He tossed 5 to Sho on the left side, high and outside, the kind of set you give an ace when everything else has gone wrong.

On the 3rd one, his hands hesitated.

It was tiny. A fraction of a beat at the top, the ball held a hair too long before it left his fingers, as if his hands remembered somebody else being at the end of this set and weren't sure who'd be there now.

Sho hit it. He landed. He didn't say anything.

He could have. Satoshi had spent 9 years noticing exactly this kind of thing and saying it out loud in team meetings. Your setter hesitates on the high outside ball. Here's the clip.

And Satoshi had seen what happened to analysts who said things like that to a team that wasn't ready to hear them.

"Thank you, Sugawara-san," Sho said, and went to help with the net.

---

Nishinoya left last, except for Sho.

He'd said 3 times today that he wasn't rejoining. He'd stayed until 7:30 anyway. He stood at the door now, pulling on his shoes, and Sho, coiling the net cable, said, "Nishinoya-senpai."

"Yeah?"

"Asahi-san. What's he like?"

Nishinoya went still with one shoe half on.

He didn't answer for a while. When he did, his voice had gone quiet, which was so unlike him that Sho stopped coiling.

"He's big," Nishinoya said. "Really big. His spikes are so heavy your arms go numb." He pulled the shoe on. "And he got blocked. A lot. In one match. Every ball. And after that..." He stood up. "After that he stopped asking for the ball."

He opened the door.

"You'll see," he said, without turning around. "If he comes back."

The door shut.

Sho stood in the empty gym with the net cable in his hands.

He knew that feeling. He knew it better than he knew anything. Satoshi had stopped asking for the ball once, too, in the last 2 months before the knee went, because his knee hurt and he was scared of what it meant and he didn't want anyone to see. He'd hidden. He'd let someone else take the swings.

And then he'd never gotten to take them again.

He finished coiling the cable. He turned off the lights. On the walk home, under the streetlights, he opened the notebook and wrote 2 lines by the light of a vending machine.

> **Asahi.** Ace. Got blocked until he stopped asking for the ball.
>
> **Receiving.** Move first. Correct after.

He looked at the 2 lines together for a long time.

Then he underlined both.

"Who's Nekoma?" said Hinata.

Tanaka turned to the first years with the face of a man about to tell a ghost story. Way back, he said, Karasuno's old coach and Nekoma's old coach had been rivals. The 2 schools used to play each other all the time. The whole town used to come and watch. People called it the Battle at the Garbage Dump.

"Because crows and cats both go through the garbage," said Tanaka, with enormous pride.

"That's a terrible name," said Tsukishima.

"It's a LEGENDARY name."

Sugawara was frowning slightly. "We haven't played them in years, though. Why now, sensei?"

Takeda smiled and pushed his glasses up. "Let's just say," he said, "I'm hoping it gets someone's attention."

Sho, coiling the net cable, looked at Takeda's face and filed it. That was bait.

On the other side of the gym, Hinata had turned to Kageyama, bouncing. "Kageyama! Nekoma! Cats! We'll beat them, right? And the ace will be back by then, right? Asahi-san?"

Nobody answered him.

Hinata looked around at the faces. He stopped bouncing.

"Right," he said, quieter. "Okay. Then I'll go ask him."

---

He went the next day, at lunch.

Sho went with him as far as the stairwell to the 3rd-year floor. Then he stopped on the landing and leaned on the railing.

"You're not coming?" said Hinata.

"3rd-year air costs extra," said Sho. "I'm poor."

The real reason was that this was Hinata's visit. Hinata wanted to meet the ace. Sho wanted to watch the ace, which was a different and much worse thing to bring into a classroom.

Hinata shrugged and went up. Kageyama, who'd come along without anyone asking him, went up after him. Sho stayed on the landing and ate half a meat bun and listened.

The 3rd-year floor was louder than the 1st. He heard Hinata's "Shitsurei shimasu!" at a classroom door, much too loud. He heard a classmate call out, "Azumane! There's a first year here for you!" He heard Sugawara's voice, surprised and warm, making introductions.

Then he heard a voice he didn't know. Low, soft, a little hesitant, the voice of somebody big who'd spent years trying to take up less room.

He couldn't hear all of it. He caught pieces. Hinata saying he wanted to be an ace. Hinata saying like you. Then a pause, much longer than it needed to be.

Then the soft voice again, and this part Sho heard clearly.

"I'm not the ace."

Footsteps. A door. Somebody walking away fast down the corridor, in the other direction.

Sho finished his half of the meat bun. He folded the paper sleeve in half and put it in his pocket.

Hinata and Kageyama came back down 5 minutes later. Hinata looked like somebody had told him Santa Claus wasn't real. Kageyama looked like he was thinking very hard about something he didn't have the words for.

"He said he's not the ace," said Hinata.

"I heard."

"He just. Left."

"I heard that too."

Hinata stared at the floor for a second. Then his chin came up.

"Then I'll be the ace," he said.

Sho looked at him. All 163 centimeters of him.

"Okay," said Sho.

Hinata blinked. "You're not going to say I'm too short?"

"You know you're short. You don't need me to tell you." Sho pushed off the railing. "Go eat. You'll faint in 5th period."

They went down. Sho waited on the landing a little longer. He had the other meat bun in his bag, the one he'd been saving for after practice.

He didn't know why he waited. He'd stopped asking himself questions like that around the time he'd died.

Ten minutes before the bell, somebody came down the stairs.

He was huge. That was the first thing. Taller than Sho by a few centimeters and much wider, with long dark hair tied back and a bit of stubble on his chin, so that he looked like a college student who'd wandered into the wrong building. He came down the stairs with his shoulders hunched in, his eyes on his feet.

He saw Sho on the landing and stopped. Sho watched him read the color of Sho's indoor shoes, which said 1st year, and then read his face, and then flinch slightly, as if Sho might also want to tell him he was an ace.

Sho took the meat bun out of his bag and held it out.

"Bought one too many," he said.

The big 3rd year looked at the meat bun. Then at Sho.

"...Sorry," he said, and took it.

Sho nodded and walked down the stairs ahead of him, and didn't look back.

In the 2 months before Satoshi's knee went, nobody had asked him why he'd stopped calling for the ball. He wouldn't have told them anyway. The most anyone could have done was let him know he'd been seen, without making him explain it.

That was all Sho gave him. It was all there was to give.

---

Sugawara told them the rest that afternoon, sitting on the edge of the stage while the others stretched.

Asahi was the tallest on the team. The strongest. So, last year, he'd become the ace without anyone really deciding it. When things got desperate, the ball went to him. When the team couldn't score, it was Asahi's job to score. Every opponent knew it, so every opponent's block went straight for him.

Then they'd played Date Tech, in the prelims. Date Tech had a block they called the Iron Wall.

"Every spike," Sugawara said. "Every single one he hit. Blocked."

He said it quietly, and then he was quiet for a moment.

"After that he started to hate volleyball, I think. A little. And he and Nishinoya fought about it. And then he stopped coming."

Hinata was listening with his whole body. Kageyama was listening with his arms folded. Tsukishima was pretending not to listen and had stopped stretching.

Sho was looking at Sugawara's hands. Sugawara's hands were holding a ball on his knee, and his thumbs kept moving over the panels, the way a setter's thumbs move before a set.

---

The meat buns at Sakanoshita were still 120 yen. Sho had stopped haggling, mostly, and started going for the conversation.

On Friday afternoon he walked in and found Takeda-sensei bowing at the register.

A deep bow, papers clutched to his chest, head down.

"Please," Takeda was saying. "Just once. Just come and look."

"No," said the blond guy behind the counter, around an unlit cigarette.

"Ukai-kun, I'm asking..."

"And I'm saying no. 5th time you've said it this week. 5th time I've said no. We've got a rhythm."

Sho stopped in the doorway. Ukai. He knew that name. Kageyama had said it on the walk home. The famous old coach. The one who'd collapsed.

The blond guy's last name was Ukai.

The papers slipped out of Takeda's arms as he straightened up and fanned out across the floor. Sho crouched without thinking and gathered them, squaring the edges against his knee. The top sheet said Karasuno High School Boys' Volleyball Club across the top in Takeda's neat handwriting, with a list of names. His own was the last one.

He handed the stack back to Takeda without a word.

Takeda flushed. "Kiyose-kun. Ah. Thank you."

The blond guy, Ukai, looked at Sho. Then at Takeda. Then back at Sho, with growing suspicion.

"Is this one of yours?"

"I'm a regular customer," said Sho. "Sensei, if I may." He turned to Ukai. "The trick is, don't ask him to coach. Ask him to just watch. 10 minutes. He doesn't even have to put out his cigarette."

"I don't smoke in here," said Ukai.

"Then he doesn't even have to light it."

Ukai stared at him for a long moment.

"Both of you," he said. "Out."

They went out. Takeda was still smiling, somehow. On the pavement outside, he looked at the shop window, where Ukai had already gone back to his magazine, and said, half to himself, "6th time tomorrow."

"Sensei," said Sho. "Why him?"

Takeda looked at him. Then he looked back at the shop.

"He was a setter here. At Karasuno. His grandfather was our coach, back when we were strong." He pushed his glasses up. "I don't know volleyball, Kiyose-kun. I read about it every night and I still don't know it. You all deserve someone who does."

He bowed to Sho, a small one, and walked off toward the station with his papers held tight.

Sho stood outside Sakanoshita and thought about a man who'd bowed 5 times in a week to a guy who kept saying no, for a team that wasn't even his sport.

Then he went back inside and bought a meat bun at full price.

Ukai looked up. "What."

"Nothing," said Sho. "120, please."

Ukai gave him the biggest one in the warmer. Again.

---

On Monday, Takeda walked into the gym with Ukai behind him.

Ukai was wearing a tracksuit instead of his shop apron, and his hair was held back with a band, and he had the face of a man who had been tricked into something and was going to make everyone pay for it.

Takeda had told him about Nekoma, Sho found out later. About Nekomata, Nekoma's old coach, coming out of retirement. About Nekoma's current coach, who'd been their setter back when Ukai was in high school. Somewhere in that list, Ukai's face had changed, and he'd said, very irritably, fine, but only until the Nekoma match.

Takeda had won it with nothing but bows. Sho had squared some papers.

"This is Ukai Keishin-kun," Takeda announced, glowing. "Our new coach!"

The team bowed. "ONEGAISHIMASU!"

Sho bowed with them. Then he straightened up and looked at Ukai with a face of total, theatrical betrayal.

"Shop-niisan?! You stalker. You followed my wallet here."

The whole team turned to look at him.

Ukai didn't blink. "In here, it's Coach," he said. "And you still owe me 40 yen."

"For what?"

"Thursday. You were short. I let it go." He pointed at Sho without looking at him. "I keep a tab."

Sho closed his mouth.

He had been 40 yen short on Thursday. He'd forgotten. Ukai hadn't said a word, just handed over the bun.

"Yes, Coach," said Sho.

---

Ukai ran them through drills for an hour and barely said anything. He stood at the side with his arms crossed and watched. Every so often he'd mutter something to Takeda, who wrote it down.

Then Kageyama set Hinata a quick, eyes shut, the ball hitting the floor before anyone else moved, and Ukai's arms came uncrossed.

"What," he said, to nobody. "What the hell was that."

"A quick," said Kageyama.

"I know it's a quick. Do it again."

They did it again. Ukai watched it with his mouth slightly open, the unlit cigarette hanging off his lip, and for a few seconds he looked like a kid who'd just seen a magic trick.

Sho, at the back of the line, watched Ukai watch it, and didn't say anything. That one belonged to them.

A few minutes later, during spike approaches, Ukai said, "Oi. Opposite."

Sho looked around. Ukai was looking straight at him.

"Your left foot," Ukai said. "On your first step. Why are you babying it?"

The gym kept going around them. Hinata was still talking. Somebody's shoe squeaked. But for one second, Sho couldn't hear any of it.

Ennoshita had said, on the bus: when you're nervous, your left foot turns out on the first step. Sho had written it down and thought it was a nerves thing. Ukai had just named the real cause in one look. Satoshi's left knee had been the one that went. For 2 months before it went, he'd been stepping off it soft, turning the foot out to spare it, and his body had done it so long that the habit had come back with everything else.

Into a body with 2 perfectly good knees.

One beat. That was all he gave it.

"Old injury," Sho said. "From a past life."

Ukai looked at him like he was a rude customer.

"Tch. Smart-ass." He jerked his chin at the line. "Fix it."

That was it. He turned away to yell at Tanaka about something else.

Sho went back to the end of the line. He took 3 approaches with his left foot pointed straight, and it felt wrong, like writing with the wrong hand. On the 4th, it felt a little less wrong.

He'd write it down later. He didn't need to. He wasn't going to forget it.

---

At water break, Hinata appeared at his elbow with an expression of outrage.

"Kiyose! Tanaka-senpai said you wrote ACE on your form!"

"I did."

"I'M going to be the ace!"

"I also wrote that I was 190 centimeters," said Sho. "Forms are a manifesto. They're about the person you'll become."

"You wrote you were 190?!"

"I'll grow."

"Both of you," said Kageyama, from the water cooler, "are dumbasses."

Nishinoya, who had been half-listening from the stage, hopped down. He'd come back to practice every day since his return, and every day he'd said he wasn't playing in matches, and every day he'd stayed until the lights went off.

"A first year writing ACE?" he said, grinning up at Sho. "I like your guts!"

Then the grin went, and his voice went flat.

"But Karasuno's ace is Asahi-san."

Sho looked down at him. There was no point arguing, and there was no part of him that wanted to.

"Then I'll be the opposite," he said. "Literally. It's my position."

Nishinoya blinked. Then he laughed, short and surprised, and slapped Sho on the arm, and went back to the stage. When they lined up again, Sho noticed he left a gap in the line next to him. Just big enough for one more person.

Nobody stood in it.

---

Ukai called them in.

"I need to see how you play," he said. "Not drills. A real game. So tonight, 6:30, you're playing a match."

"Against who?" said Daichi.

"My team. Neighborhood Association. Karasuno old boys, mostly. Guys who work." He glanced at his phone. "I've got 4 coming. I need a setter, 1 more spiker, and a libero."

He looked at the team. "And you've got 2 setters. They can't be on the same side. One of you comes with me."

Kageyama and Sugawara looked at each other.

"I'll go," said Sugawara.

He said it lightly. Ukai nodded, already moving on, and Kageyama watched Sugawara walk over to the other side of the net.

A few minutes later, Sho saw Kageyama go over to him. He saw Sugawara say something, and Kageyama's face change.

He didn't hear all of it. He wasn't trying to. But the gym was quiet in that moment and Sugawara's voice carried, low and honest.

He said that after the Date Tech match, he'd been scared to toss to Asahi. Scared that every set he gave him would get blocked, that it was his fault. He said that when Kageyama came to Karasuno, part of him had been relieved. Relieved that he wouldn't have to be the one setting anymore.

Then he said: but I still want to toss to him again. One more time.

Kageyama didn't say anything. He bowed, short and stiff, and walked away.

When Sugawara turned around, Sho was the closest person to him. Sugawara looked at him. Sho saw him realize what Sho had heard.

"You noticed, didn't you," Sugawara said quietly. "The high sets. The pause."

"I didn't say anything."

Sugawara looked at him for a long moment. "...Why not?"

"It was yours to say."

Sugawara laughed. It was a small laugh, a little wet around the edges. Then he reached out and patted Sho on the back, too hard, the way you do when you don't want the other person to see your face.

"Go warm up, Kiyose," he said.

Sho went.

---

The Neighborhood Association guys arrived at 6:20 in work clothes and changed in the club room. One ran a supermarket. One had his own electrical shop. They were loud and friendly and called Ukai "Kei-chan," which made him visibly want to die.

Ukai turned to Nishinoya, who was standing by the door with his bag on his shoulder.

"You're the libero. You're with us."

"I'm not playing matches," said Nishinoya.

"It's not a match. It's a game with my friends from the shopping street."

"I'm not..."

"Just tonight."

Nishinoya hesitated. Sho watched him look at the court, at the net, at the side where the old boys were warming up. His hand tightened on his bag strap.

"We're still 1 short," said Ukai, irritated, counting heads. "I need a spiker. Anybody."

Then Hinata, who had been staring out the high window by the door for no reason at all, pointed and yelled.

"ASAHI-SAN!"

Everyone turned.

He was standing outside, in the dusk, on the path by the gym. Huge and hunched and caught, holding a school bag in both hands like a shield. He must have been watching through the open door. Sho had no idea for how long.

He took a step back.

Ukai didn't know who he was. Ukai didn't care. He saw a very tall boy in a Karasuno uniform and a team that was 1 short.

"You!" Ukai barked. "Tall guy! Do you play?"

"I... I used to, I..."

"Great. Get changed. You're with us."

"I can't, I'm not..."

"We're short a player. Get in here."

There was a long, terrible moment where the big 3rd year stood in the door and nobody moved.

Sho looked at Nishinoya. Nishinoya wasn't moving either. He was staring at Asahi with his jaw locked, his whole body pointed at him, and his hand on his bag strap had gone white at the knuckles.

Then Asahi came in.

He came in slowly, the way a man walks into cold water. He went past the Karasuno side without looking at any of them. Past Sho, who had given him a meat bun on a stairwell 4 days ago and said nothing. He went to the other side of the net, where Ukai and the old boys and Sugawara were, and stood there, very large and very still.

Nishinoya put down his bag.

He walked onto the court and took his place in the back row of the Neighborhood side, right behind Asahi. He didn't look at him. He didn't say anything. He just stood there, low, ready, the way he'd stood behind every ball he'd ever received.

On the Karasuno side, Daichi was quietly counting off positions. Kageyama was flexing his fingers. Hinata was vibrating.

Sho took his spot at opposite, at the net.

Across from him, through the tape, was the ace who'd stopped asking for the ball.

And Sho thought, very calmly: I'm going to block you as hard as I can.

Because that was the only honest thing to do.

## Chapter 11: Neighborhood

The Neighborhood Association changed in the club room, and they came out looking exactly like what they were: guys who'd worked all day.

One was thin, with glasses and black hair, and he was still wearing a polo shirt with SHIMADA MART stitched on the chest. The second had dyed blond hair and an easy grin and kept calling Ukai "Kei-chan" to watch him suffer. The third was quieter and broad through the shoulders. A 4th was stretching against the wall with a groan that sounded like a door hinge.

"I did inventory all day," the one in the polo announced to the gym. "So go easy on me, kids."

"Shimada-san," Tanaka told the first years, in a respectful whisper. "And that's Takinoue-san. They were on the team. Like, back when."

Sho did the math. They had to be around 26. That made them 3 years younger than Satoshi had been when he died.

Kids, he thought, and almost said Shimada-kun out loud.

Ukai stood on the sideline in his tracksuit with his arms folded and the unlit cigarette behind his ear. He'd told them he needed a setter. Sugawara had walked over to their side of the net, and Ukai had looked at Sugawara, then at the court, and stayed where he was.

"Suga sets," he said. "I watch. I've seen enough of these idiots play to last me."

"Rude, Kei-chan," said Takinoue.

On the Karasuno side, Yamaguchi had been on the starting list until Ukai crossed his name out and wrote Kiyose instead. Yamaguchi took it with a small, quick nod, and sat down on the bench next to Ennoshita, and when Sho passed him on the way to the net, he said, "Do your best, Kiyose," and meant it, which was almost worse.

Sho bowed to him. Then he went to his spot.

Across the net, Asahi stood in the back row in a borrowed practice shirt that was too tight across the shoulders, and looked at the floor.

Nishinoya stood behind him. Low, ready, his eyes on the back of Asahi's head.

"Onegaishimasu!"

---

The first point went to Sugawara.

The Neighborhood side passed the serve, a little ugly, and Sugawara went under it, and for one moment Sho saw his hands go still. Then Takinoue came running in for a quick, and Sugawara put the ball right where he needed it, soft and fast and accurate, and Takinoue hit it into the floor between Tsukishima and Hinata.

Hinata stared at Sugawara like he'd seen a ghost. "Suga-san can do that?"

"He's a real setter," Daichi said, and there was something in his voice that sounded a lot like pride.

On the other side of the net, Nishinoya slapped Sugawara on the back. "Suga-san! That was cool!"

Sugawara laughed. Sho, watching, saw that he was also looking, very briefly, at the back of Asahi's head.

Then Kageyama set Hinata, eyes shut, the ball hitting the floor between Takinoue's feet before Takinoue had even bent his knees, and the gym went quiet in the good way.

Takinoue looked at the floor. Then at Shimada.

"...Is that allowed?"

"I don't think there's a rule against it," said Shimada slowly.

On the sideline, Ukai had uncrossed his arms again.

And in the back row on the Neighborhood side, Sho saw Asahi watching Hinata. He watched Hinata land, and whoop, and spin around to Kageyama. He watched for a long time, and his face did something Sho couldn't read from 9 meters away.

Sho filed it.

---

Asahi's first attack came at 2-3.

It was a good set. Sugawara put it high and outside on the left, where an ace wants it, and Asahi came in on his approach, and he was big, and he was fast, and he was beautiful to watch for exactly 3 steps.

On the 4th step, he slowed down.

It was tiny. A hitch on the last step, the speed draining out of his legs like he'd changed his mind halfway through. His arm swing came up short. And at the top, his hand opened before it reached the ball.

Sho saw all of it. Satoshi's brain flagged it before the ball had even left Sugawara's fingers, because Satoshi had done that exact thing, that exact hitch on the last step, about 300 times in the 2 months before his knee went.

He didn't think about it. He called it.

"Tip! Short!"

Asahi tipped. Daichi was already moving. He scooped it up a meter behind the block, and Kageyama set Tanaka, and Tanaka buried it.

3-3.

Sho landed and didn't look at Asahi. He shook out his hands and put them back up.

On the sideline, Ukai said, to no one, "Oi. Kiyose. How'd you know that?"

"He told me," Sho said.

Ukai squinted at him. Then at Asahi. Then he looked at Asahi for a long time, and didn't say anything else.

---

Asahi tipped the next one too.

This time Tsukishima called it. "Tip." Quiet, bored, a half-beat before Sho opened his mouth. He didn't look over. Sho didn't say anything about it. Daichi dug it.

When they reset, Tsukishima glanced sideways at him.

"You jump like it's personal."

"Everything's personal," said Sho. "I'm extremely petty."

Tsukishima looked away. The corner of his mouth moved, maybe.

Across the net, Tanaka was shouting. Not at his own team. At the other one.

"ASAHI-SAN! Keep asking for it! You're the ACE! KEEP ASKING FOR THE BALL!"

Daichi, beside him, didn't tell him to stop.

On the dead ball, Tanaka turned and grabbed Sho by the front of the shirt, just hard enough to mean it.

"You," he said, low. "You called his tip. Out loud."

"Yes, senpai."

"Why'd you do that?"

Sho looked at him. "Because if I go easy, he'll know."

Tanaka held onto his shirt for another second. Then he let go.

"...Then block him harder," Tanaka said. "Damn it."

---

Sho tried to hit through Nishinoya once and learned his lesson.

Kageyama set him on the right side, and Sho went up with his left foot pointed straight for once, and hit a sharp cross, hard, at the back corner.

Nishinoya was already there. He'd been there before Sho's hand touched the ball. Thup.

"Too honest, Sho!" Nishinoya yelled, grinning, while Suga set Shimada to finish the rally on the other side.

"I was testing your eyesight, senpai."

"My eyesight is PERFECT!"

Sho took 2 more rallies to set it up.

He watched Nishinoya instead of the ball. Noya moved first. That was the whole lesson, the first thing he'd ever taught Sho: you don't wait to be sure, you just go. So Noya would go. He'd read the swing, and he'd take one big confident step toward where the ball should land, and he'd correct on the way.

Which meant that for a fraction of a second, the space right behind that first step was empty.

The next time Kageyama set him, Sho swung his whole arm like he was going to hit the same hard cross. He watched Noya's weight shift. And at the last instant he turned his wrist over and rolled the ball, soft and short, over the block and into the empty floor behind Noya's first step.

It dropped. Noya, halfway through his step, twisted, lunged, and missed it by a hand.

4-4.

Nishinoya lay on the floor for a second. Then he slapped the boards with both palms, delighted and furious at the same time.

"WHO taught you THAT?"

"Some short guy," said Sho.

Nishinoya sat up, pointed at him, and laughed, a huge laugh that echoed around the gym. Then he got up, still grinning, and went back to his spot behind Asahi, and Sho noticed that the next time Noya got ready, his first step was a little smaller.

He'd adjusted. In 1 rally.

Of course he had.

---

Sugawara served at Sho on purpose. Sho could see the decision in his toss.

A float, nothing special, aimed at the seam between Sho and Daichi. Two weeks ago it would have gone off Sho's arm into the stage.

Shu, he thought. Don.

His feet went first. He didn't wait to be sure. He was a little wrong, and he fixed it on the way, a small step left, and he got low and put out his platform and gave the ball a short push forward instead of a swing.

It went up clean and dropped to Kageyama.

Sho didn't say anything. He didn't look at Sugawara.

But out of the corner of his eye he saw Sugawara's right hand lift, just a little, maybe 2 centimeters off his thigh. Almost a wave. Then it went back down.

---

Between rallies at 4-5, while Shimada was retying his shoe, Sho saw Asahi turn around to Nishinoya.

They were too far away to hear. But Sho could see Asahi's mouth move. He could see that it was short, and hard to say. He could see Nishinoya's face while he listened. It went from flat to something else. Something that looked a lot like the face Hinata made right before a quick.

Nishinoya said something back. Even shorter.

Then he slapped Asahi on the back, hard, and went back to his spot.

## Chapter 12: Call for the ball

"Receive."

Then he made them do it. Serve receive in rotations: each player stayed on the court until he got 5 good passes to the target. 5 clean ones, to the target, or they didn't count.

Hinata stayed on the court for a very long time.

Sho stayed on almost as long, because he'd given himself a new problem on purpose.

Neck checks. Satoshi had watched it on tape for years, the way the great passers did it: a glance before the ball came, to see the setter, the blockers, the gaps. A snapshot of the whole court, so that when the ball arrived you already knew where everything was. Satoshi had always been able to see it on a screen, from above. Sho wanted it in his own head, on the floor.

So he tried it.

Ball served. Check the setter. Check the blockers. Back to the ball.

The ball was already past him.

Ball served. Check left. Check the net. Ball. It hit his shoulder and went into the bleachers.

"Kiyose!" Ukai barked. "Where are you LOOKING?"

"Everywhere, Coach."

"Look at the BALL."

"You're thinking with your eyes again!" Nishinoya yelled from the next court.

"On purpose this time, senpai."

"That's WORSE!"

He got 2 clean passes in a row. Then he tried the checks again and shanked 3 in a row, his eyes jumping around like a puppy in a room full of tennis balls.

At the water break, he sat on the bench with the notebook.

> Too many checks. Wrong time. Looked away while the ball was coming. Lost it.

He looked at it. Then he crossed out "too many" and wrote "wrong time" twice.

Before the toss, he could look anywhere. Once the server hit it, the ball was coming to him, and it was his until he passed it. The time to look away was when the ball was going to somebody else. When somebody was about to touch it, look back.

He tried it that way in the afternoon. It was worse for an hour. Then, for 3 balls in a row, it was a little better.

He wrote that down too.

---

Ukai pulled him out of the receive line 1 rep early on the 2nd day.

"Water," Ukai said.

"I'm not thirsty, Coach."

"Water."

Ukai was looking at the far wall. Sho glanced down. His left foot was turned out on the first step again, just slightly, the way it had been the day of the Neighborhood match. He hadn't felt it happen.

He went and got water.

Neither of them said why.

On the way back, because he couldn't help himself, Sho said, "Coach, you're very attentive. It's one of your best qualities. Have you ever thought about doing this professionally?"

"20 more," said Ukai, without looking at him. "On top of your 5."

Sho did 20 more. He made the rest of his jokes under his breath after that.

---

They cooked curry on the first night. Takeda had a recipe printed out from the internet for 20 people, and he read it aloud like a poem while the first years chopped.

Sho was put in charge of the rice cooker.

"I've never lost to a rice cooker," he announced.

Then he lost to the rice cooker. He used the wrong cup. The rice came out with the texture of a wet sponge.

Asahi looked at it, scooped it into a bowl, and started a 2nd pot. He measured the water with his finger, a knuckle above the rice, the way Sho's mother did. When it was done, he put the good rice in front of everyone else and the sponge rice in front of Sho, and Sho ate all of it and said it was delicious, and Asahi's mouth twitched.

"Itadakimasu!"

They ate like animals. Ukai had said that eating was training, and nobody had needed to be told twice. Tanaka went back for seconds 3 times. Hinata went back 4 times and then lay on the tatami holding his stomach, announcing that he could see the Sanzu River.

"It's curry," said Kageyama.

"It's a RIVER of curry."

Sho lay on the tatami next to him. Every muscle in his body was warm and heavy and finished. "If I die tonight," he said to the ceiling, "my notebook goes to Yamaguchi. My debts go to Tanaka-senpai."

"Why do I get your DEBTS?!"

"You're the only one strong enough to carry them, senpai."

Tanaka considered this, and seemed to accept it as a compliment.

Later, Asahi came by with an onigiri wrapped in plastic, put it down next to Sho's head without a word, and walked on.

Salmon.

Sho lay there and did the math. 2 meat buns, given. 1 onigiri, received. That left 1 meat bun outstanding.

He decided he'd never collect it.

---

The first years bathed in turns, after the seniors. Hinata and Kageyama raced each other into the tub without washing first and were both shouted at by Daichi through the door.

Sho took the stool at the far end and washed slowly. The water from the tap was cold. He ran it over his left knee for a while, out of a habit that wasn't his. The knee under his hand was fine. He let the water run anyway.

Then Hinata screamed.

He'd gone out into the hallway in a towel and come back with his face white. "There's a KID in the hallway! A little kid! With a towel!"

It was Nishinoya. His hair was wet and flat against his head, and without the spikes he was, to be fair, about the size of a middle schooler.

"WHO ARE YOU CALLING A KID?!"

Then Asahi walked past the door behind him, very tall, with his long wet hair down and a towel over his head like a ghost, and everyone in the bath screamed again, and out in the hall, so did Tanaka.

"Shut UP," Daichi called from the other room.

---

Lights out was at 10. Takeda turned the lights off himself and said goodnight to each of them by name, which was very kind and also took a long time.

The futons were in 2 long rows. Sho had Kageyama on his left and Tsukishima on his right, which he suspected was Sugawara's doing.

For about 5 minutes nobody said anything.

Then Tanaka's voice, from the far end, in the dark: "So. Who does everybody like?"

Groans.

"Hinata!"

"Uh... I don't... volleyball?"

"That's not a PERSON. Kageyama!"

Silence. Then, very firmly: "No."

"No WHAT?"

"No."

"Kiyose!"

Sho thought about it seriously. "The rice cooker," he said. "We have unfinished business."

Somebody threw a pillow. It hit Tsukishima, who put it under his own head and said, "Thanks."

"Lights out means lights out," said Daichi, quietly, and the room went still.

After a while the breathing got slow. Tanaka started to snore. Then Nishinoya.

Hinata's voice, very quiet, from the other side of Kageyama.

"Kiyose. You awake?"

"No."

"Why'd you start volleyball? Sugawara-san said you used to quit everything."

Sho lay on his back and looked at the dark ceiling.

There were a lot of true answers. Most of them he couldn't say out loud in a room full of people who had never died.

"A ball hit me in the face," he said. "And I remembered I liked it."

Silence.

"That's so weird," Hinata whispered.

"I know."

"I like it, though." A pause. "Good night."

"Good night."

Hinata was asleep in about 30 seconds. He breathed like he played, all at once.

---

At 4:15 the next morning, Sho opened his eyes.

His body had been doing it for a month. It didn't need the phone anymore.

He sat up.

Beside him, at exactly the same moment, Kageyama sat up too.

They looked at each other in the grey dark.

"Lie down," said Daichi, from across the room, without opening his eyes.

They both lay back down. At exactly the same speed. Like a mirror.

After a long time, Kageyama whispered, "In the 3v3. When I set Hinata the first time. You moved before he hit it."

Daichi had said the same words to him once, under a towel.

Sho kept looking at the ceiling. "I guessed."

"You don't guess."

Sho was quiet for a second.

"Tsukishima was drifting left," he said finally. "His shoulders were open. A quick that fast and that low only has one lane. So I went there. Too slow."

Silence.

"...You talk like a coach," Kageyama said.

Something in Sho's chest went still for exactly 1 beat. Then it started again.

"I watch a lot of volleyball," he said.

It was a good answer. Satoshi had given answers like it to head coaches for 9 years. The rest of it, the ghost of Hinata's arm a blink early and his own legs leaving without him, he kept.

Kageyama didn't say anything else. His breathing slowed. Sho lay awake until 6, logging the conversation line by line, the way he'd log a rally, and when Daichi finally said "Up," he was already sitting.

---

On the 3rd night, Sho called home from the green payphone in the hall, feeding it 10-yen coins, because Daichi still had his phone.

His mother answered. She asked if he was eating. She asked if he was sleeping. She asked if the bath was clean, which he couldn't answer honestly.

"Oh, and," she said. "Mio moved the magnet."

"What magnet?"

"On the calendar. On the fridge. She had a magnet on Golden Week. Since April. Yesterday she moved it." A pause. "To August."

Sho didn't say anything.

There was a scuffle on the other end of the line, and then Mio's voice, much too close to the phone.

"Is the food bad?"

"The rice was a sponge."

"Don't get fat," said Mio, and handed the phone back before he could answer.

His father came on next. He asked how practice was. Then he asked, carefully, "Did they give you a number?"

"The morning of the match."

"Ah." A pause. "Tell me then."

"I'll mail you."

"Okay," said his father. "Good."

Sho hung up. He stood in the hallway with his hand still on the receiver until the phone gave his last coin back.

---

On the last morning's run, Hinata raced Kageyama up the hill, took a wrong turn, and came back 20 minutes late, red-faced and full of news.

"I met a guy! He was sitting by a fence playing a game! He plays volleyball! He's a SETTER!"

"From where?" said Daichi.

"I don't know! He didn't say!" Hinata was bouncing. "He said he doesn't like or hate volleyball. Then a tall guy with weird hair came and yelled 'Kenma!' and took him away. And he said 'See you, Shoyo.' He called me SHOYO!"

Sho, stretching his calves on the step, filed it. A setter, lost near Karasuno, the day before the Nekoma match.

He kept it to himself. Hinata would find out tomorrow, and the look on his face then would be worth more than anything Sho could guess now.

---

On the last afternoon, Sho got his 5 good passes in 9 balls. On the first morning it had taken him 31.

He wrote both numbers down and underlined neither.

That evening, Ukai read the lineup off a sheet of paper.

"Sawamura. Azumane. Tanaka. Tsukishima. Hinata. Kageyama. Nishinoya, libero."

He folded the paper.

"Kiyose." He didn't look up. "Bench. You go in when I say. Be ready from the 1st serve."

"Yes, Coach."

Sho's left foot started to turn out. He set it straight.

They laid out the futons after that, and Nishinoya made Asahi apologize to Ennoshita.

"For what?" Asahi asked, already half-bowing.

"For taking his spot! Against Nekoma!" Nishinoya pointed at him. "You came back and you just walked in. Apologize!"

Asahi turned to Ennoshita and bowed, enormous, hands flat against his thighs. "Ennoshita, I'm sorry, I didn't think..."

"It's fine," said Ennoshita. He was smoothing out a sheet. He said it very easily. "Really. I ran away once. So I can't say anything."

Hinata and Kageyama both stopped what they were doing.

"You ran away?" said Hinata.

Ennoshita didn't look up from the sheet. Narita and Kinoshita, a few futons down, had both gone still.

Sho was laying out the futon beside Ennoshita's. He kept smoothing his own sheet, head down, and listened.

Ennoshita told it like a story about somebody else.

The club used to be fun, he said. Relaxed. Then last year, the old Coach Ukai came back. Keishin's grandfather. Practice got hard. Nationals hard. Every day, until you couldn't stand up.

"So the 3 of us stopped coming," Ennoshita said, and smiled at the sheet. "Me, Narita, Kinoshita. We just... didn't go."

"We came back," said Narita, from down the row. "Later."

"We came back," Ennoshita agreed. "Because it turned out we still wanted to play. But by then he'd collapsed. He was gone. So we never got to tell him." He tucked the corner of the sheet under the futon. "We ran. Pathetic, right?"

Nobody laughed.

Narita threw a pillow at him. Ennoshita caught it and put it at the top of his futon without comment.

"Anyway," said Ennoshita. He looked at Hinata and Kageyama, very calm, with his sleepy face. "We're not getting left behind this time. So. We'll be right behind you."

Hinata straightened up. "Okay!"

Sho finished his futon and sat back on his heels.

Satoshi had a whole speech about quitting and coming back. He had it ready. He owed Ennoshita a starting spot. He put the speech away.

"Ennoshita-san," he said instead. "After camp. Will you feed me receive balls? After practice. 20 minutes."

Ennoshita turned and looked at him.

"I'm still bad," Sho said. "And you see things I don't."

Ennoshita looked at him for a long moment.

"Mm," he said. "Fine."

The next morning, there was a melon bread sitting at the head of Ennoshita's futon when he woke up. No note.

Narita saw it and looked around the room. "Where's ours?"

"Earn your bread," said Sho.

Kinoshita snorted into his pillow.

Kiyoko brought the jerseys after breakfast, folded and clean, smelling of laundry soap.

She handed them out in order. Daichi, 1. Sugawara, 2. Asahi, 3. Nishinoya, 4, in the libero's different color, which he held up to the light like a flag. Tanaka, 5. Ennoshita, 6. Narita, 7. Kinoshita, 8.

"Kageyama. 9."

Kageyama took his and held it as if it might be taken back.

"Hinata. 10."

Hinata looked down at his jersey. His face fell.

"10? It's 2 digits. Kageyama got 1 digit."

"9," Sho said helpfully, from behind him. "Ku. As in kurushii. Suffering. Very fitting."

"Shut up," said Kageyama.

"Tsukishima, 11. Yamaguchi, 12."

Then she held out the last one to Sho.

"Kiyose. 13."

Black, with orange trim, KARASUNO across the chest and a big white 13 on the back.

He waited for it to mean something. Satoshi had worn 5 in college, and 5 had meant everything for 2 years. 13 was the number after 12. It had nothing in it yet. He'd put things in it.

"13," he announced. "A prime number. Main-character number."

"That's not a thing," said Tsukishima.

"It's a thing in my heart."

Hinata was still frowning at his 10.

Then Daichi said, mildly, "You know who wore 10, Hinata?"

Hinata looked up.

"The Little Giant," said Daichi. "At Nationals. Karasuno's number 10."

Hinata went completely still.

He looked down at the jersey in his hands for a long time, and Sho, standing right behind him, took a step back.

Ukai, at the door, cleared his throat.

"The Little Giant's team was the strongest Karasuno's ever had," he said. "They went to Nationals." A pause. "They never beat Nekoma. Not once."

He looked at all of them.

"So beat them," said Ukai, and walked out.

Sho put on the jersey. He went out into the hallway with the phone Daichi had finally given back and typed a mail to his father. It took him a while. He kept deleting things.

In the end he sent 2 characters.

> 13

The reply came back 30 seconds later. His father must have been holding the phone.

> 13.

Sho looked at it for a long time. Then he put the phone away and went to the gym.

Nekoma arrived at 9.

They came in wearing red jackets, and the first thing that happened was that a big guy with a buzz cut and a guard dog's face walked straight up to Hinata and loomed.

"Oi. What're you looking at?"

Hinata squeaked.

Tanaka was there in under a second, chest out, face doing the thing. The 2 of them stood nose to nose, growling, like 2 cats on a wall.

"Tanaka," said Sugawara.

"Yamamoto," said a short, sharp-eyed 3rd year from Nekoma.

Both of them were hauled back by the collar. Sugawara and the Nekoma 3rd year looked at each other, bowed, and apologized with perfect manners, as if they did this every week.

Then Kiyoko walked past with a clipboard.

The big Nekoma guy, Yamamoto, went the color of a tomato. He stared at her. Then he turned around and walked very fast out of the gym without saying anything.

2 Nekoma players behind him exchanged a look and a high five. Sho heard the words "yakisoba bread."

Hinata was staring past them.

At the back of Nekoma's group, holding a handheld game, with his shoulders hunched and his hair 2 colors, was a small, quiet boy who looked like he wanted to be anywhere else.

"KENMA!"

The boy flinched.

"You're NEKOMA'S setter?!"

"...Yeah," said Kenma.

"Why didn't you SAY?"

Kenma looked at the floor and didn't answer.

Hinata ran to Kageyama, pointing. "That's him! The setter! The one I met!"

Kageyama looked across the gym at Kenma. Sho watched his face change. It was the face Kageyama made whenever he heard the word setter attached to anyone. The face of somebody who'd just found out there was a new kind of fish in the river.

"Him?" said Kageyama.

"Him!"

---

The captains shook hands in the middle of the court.

"Sawamura," said Daichi, smiling. "Looking forward to it."

"Kuroo," said Nekoma's captain, smiling back. He was tall, with black hair that stuck up on one side as if he'd slept on it badly for 17 years, and a smile that was very friendly and very, very interested. "Me too."

They both kept shaking hands for slightly too long. Sho, watching from the end of the line, thought it looked like 2 men each checking whether the other's hand had a knife in it.

Then Kuroo's eyes left Daichi and swept along the Karasuno line, easy, lazy, one face after another. They reached Sho. And they stopped.

Sho had been watching Nekoma since they walked in. Who stood where. Who talked to who. Which first year carried the ball bag. Who the small sharp 3rd year watched when the others weren't looking. He'd done it without moving his head, the way Satoshi had learned to watch a team in hotel lobbies and away gyms for 9 years.

Kuroo had caught him anyway.

"Hey, Sawamura," Kuroo said, still smiling, tilting his head at Sho. "Your team's got a guy taking notes."

Daichi glanced back.

Sho met Kuroo's eyes and smiled pleasantly.

"Thank you," Sho said. "I'm also very handsome."

Kuroo's smile widened by exactly 1 millimeter. Something behind it moved and sat back down, the way a cat's ears move at a sound in the next room.

"Noted," said Kuroo.

He walked back to his team.

---

Sho sat down on the bench, stretched his calves, and didn't look at Kuroo again.

When he looked up, Kenma was looking at him. Hinata was 3 meters away, waving, and Kenma was looking at Sho.

It lasted a second. A quiet, flat, careful look from under 2-colored hair. It went over Sho the way a scanner goes over a barcode.

Then Kenma looked down at his game, as if Sho had been mildly interesting for exactly 1 second and he'd already filed him and closed the drawer.

Sho sat very still.

He knew that look. He'd worn it in a dark room in front of a monitor for 9 years.

He took out the notebook and wrote 2 lines.

> Nekoma. Kuroo, captain. Caught me watching. Didn't mind.
>
> Kenma, setter. Watches like I do. That's 2.

He closed it. On the court, Nekoma had started their warm-up, and somebody in red was moving the ball around as if the court were a board he'd played on before.

## Chapter 14: Garbage Dump

Before the first whistle, a tall Nekoma first year with short spiky hair and a very honest face looked down at Hinata across the net.

"You're the middle blocker? You're short."

"Don't look down on me!"

"I'm not," said the first year, sincerely. "I just noticed."

On the bench, Takeda leaned over to Sho with the face of a man who'd done his reading.

"Do you know what they call this match up? Karasuno and Nekoma. Crows and cats." He held up a finger. "The Battle at the Garbage Dump."

"That's a terrible name, sensei."

"Isn't it wonderful?"

Sho wrote it in the margin anyway.

---

Kenma served first. It was a soft, careful serve, the kind that didn't look like anything, and it went straight at Asahi's weak side. Asahi got it up, a little off the net.

Kageyama ran under it anyway. Hinata was already in the air.

Bang.

The ball hit the floor on Nekoma's side before any of the cats had moved. For a second the gym went quiet the way gyms do when something doesn't make sense.

Then Tanaka was screaming, and Nishinoya was screaming, and on Nekoma's side the tall first year, Inuoka, was staring at his own hands as if they'd failed him personally.

Sho watched Kenma.

Kenma looked at the spot where the ball had landed. Then he looked at Hinata. Then at Kageyama. Then he looked at nothing at all, and his face did exactly what it had done before, which was nothing.

Sho wrote the time in the corner of the page.

---

From the bench, the court was easy.

He'd spent 9 years watching volleyball from above, and the bench was the closest thing to the camera booth this life had given him. 12 players. 1 ball. Every head, every shoulder, every first step, all in front of him at once.

Ennoshita sat down beside him with a towel on his knee. He didn't turn his head when he talked.

"Their libero cheats left on his first step," Ennoshita said. "The short one. 3rd year."

Sho looked. Yaku, small and sharp, was taking a half step to his left before every serve, so small it barely counted.

"Ennoshita-san," Sho said, "you're a scout."

"I'm bench." Ennoshita moved his towel to the other knee. "We have time."

Sho wrote it down. Then he wrote the rest of it, the things the bench let him see. Kuroo's hands at the net, open early, steering instead of stopping. Kenma's sets, low and quiet and always a little different. The way Nekoma's whole back row shifted 1 step every time Kenma touched the ball, as if they were tied to him with string.

Asahi swung at a ball on the left and Yaku got nothing on it. The next time, 4 points later, Yaku got a hand on it.

"Huh," said Tsukishima, coming off the court for Nishinoya's rotation. "He touched it. He's seen that spike once."

Sugawara was watching Sho's neck, which was turning left, right, left, right, every few seconds like a desk fan.

"Your neck's going to be sore tomorrow," said Sugawara.

"Worth it, Suga-san."

"Mm." Sugawara put a water bottle next to his foot. "Drink this anyway."

Hinata missed a serve. Tanaka hit a ball past Yamamoto so hard that Yamamoto yelled at it. Karasuno went up 12 to 9, and the old man in Nekoma's chair raised a hand for a timeout.

---

Sho watched Nekoma's timeout from across the gym.

Kenma talked. Nobody else did. He talked to the floor, mostly, in short pieces, and when he finished he pointed at Inuoka once, and Inuoka nodded so hard his whole body went with it.

"He's giving that one a job," Ennoshita murmured.

"Just one," said Sho. "Look how happy he is."

The coach in Nekoma's chair, a small old man with a face like a dried plum, said something short. Inuoka nodded again.

When play came back, Inuoka stopped watching the ball. He watched Hinata. Only Hinata. Every run, every jump, he went with him like a dog after a thrown stick.

The first time, he went with Hinata's decoy and Kageyama set somebody else.

The second time, he got his fingertips on the quick.

Sho's pen stopped.

Kenma had found the problem in 1 timeout and handed it to the one player who would chase it without getting bored. Satoshi had watched head coaches take a full week of tape to do that.

"Kiyose."

Ukai said it without looking away from the court.

"Tanaka's rotation. Receive first, then swing."

"Yes, Coach."

His left foot started to turn out on the first step. Ukai's eyes flicked down to it. Sho set it straight, and Ukai's eyes went back to the court.

---

Tanaka came off at 13 to 11 and slapped Sho's hand hard enough to sting.

"Don't get comfy," Tanaka said. "That's my spot."

"Just keeping it warm, senpai."

"Keep it warm for 5 MINUTES."

Then he sat down on the bench, and Sho heard him yell "KIYOSE, NICE!" before Sho had done anything at all.

Across the net, Kuroo was waiting at the antenna with his hands on his hips.

"Oi, notebook," he said pleasantly. "Do I come out handsome?"

"8 out of 10," said Sho. "The hair costs you 2."

Kuroo laughed once, short. His eyes stayed on Sho the whole time.

---

The first ball came to him on the next rally. A float serve, wobbling, from Yamamoto.

Before the toss, Sho looked everywhere. Setter. Middle. Kuroo's hands. Yaku's feet. Then the server hit it, and the ball was his until he passed it, and he didn't look at anything else.

It went to Kageyama's hands like it had been mailed there.

"NICE RECEIVE!" Nishinoya yelled from behind him.

Kageyama set Asahi, and Kuroo got both hands on it. 13 to 12.

Yamamoto served again. Sho passed again, clean, and this time Kageyama set him, a high ball behind the 3-meter line. Sho went up with his left foot straight and hit it down the line on Yaku's right, the side Yaku had just cheated away from. It went in.

14 to 12.

"KIYOSE!" Tanaka was on his feet on the bench. "THAT'S MY SPOT YOU'RE BEING GOOD IN!"

"Sit down," said Ukai.

Karasuno rotated. Sho slid to the back left and breathed. His neck turned, left, right, left. Every snapshot went into the drawer and the drawer was filling up nicely.

On the other side of the net, Kenma was looking at him.

It was the same look as before the match. Flat, quiet, a scanner over a barcode. Only this time it didn't move on.

---

Sho noticed the change 2 rallies later, and by then it was too late.

Kenma's sets got slower. 1 beat. Maybe less. He'd catch the ball in his hands and hold it just a little longer than he needed to, his eyes down, and in that little extra time Sho's head would turn to check the hitters.

Then the ball would go where Sho had just been looking away from.

The first time, it was a dump. Kenma tipped it over with 2 fingers into the gap behind the block, the exact spot Sho had left to check Yamamoto's approach.

14 to 13.

The second time, Kenma set the back row, right as Sho's head came back from the net. Sho got a hand on it and sent it into the bleachers.

14 to 14.

"Shake it off!" Daichi called.

Sho shook it off. He tightened the checks. More of them. Faster. He'd see everything. If he saw everything, there'd be nowhere left for Kenma to put it.

The next rally went long.

Daichi dug Yamamoto. Kageyama set Asahi. Kuroo got a hand on it and it came back high, and Nishinoya scrambled it up, and Hinata hit a desperate free ball over, and then Yaku passed it, perfectly, of course, straight to Kenma.

Sho's head was turning.

Setter. Middle. Kuroo. Yamamoto's run. Inuoka. Yaku. Setter again. 12 snapshots in about 5 seconds, every one of them sharp and every one of them saying something.

Then the eye fired.

He saw Kenma's hands push the ball toward Yamamoto on the left. A ghost, clear as glass, a blink before it happened.

But his last snapshot had Kenma's shoulders square to the net. The snapshot said dump. The ghost said left.

2 pictures. Both of them his. Both of them true a moment ago.

His feet stopped.

He stood there on a varnished floor that squeaked under every other pair of shoes in the gym, and watched the ball go exactly where the ghost had said, to Yamamoto on the left, and watched Yamamoto hit it into the empty floor 2 meters to his right, where he should have been.

The whistle went.

14 to 15.

Somewhere behind him Nishinoya said, "Kiyose?"

Sho looked down at his feet. A clean base, set 2 meters from where the ball had gone.

"Kiyose," said Ukai, from the bench. His voice stayed level. "Out."

---

Tanaka came back in at a jog. He hit Sho on the back as he passed, once, not hard, and went to his spot.

Sho sat down on the bench.

He put his hands flat on his knees.

For exactly 1 breath he didn't do anything at all. The gym was very loud. Somebody in red was yelling "NICE KENMA!" and Kenma was pretending he hadn't heard it.

Then the breath was over.

Takeda sat down beside him and held out 2 things: a drink, and a sheet of paper covered in small neat marks.

"I marked every touch in your rotations," said Takeda. "In case the notebook wants company."

Sho took the drink. He took the sheet. He looked at the marks. Takeda had written them like a man who'd learned what a dig was 3 weeks ago and had decided to take it seriously, and every single one was right.

"Thank you, sensei."

He opened the notebook and found an empty page.

He wrote about the machine.

> 12 checks in 5 seconds. Too many. Last one stale by the time the set came.
>
> Eye fired on top of the scan. 2 feeds. Different pictures. Body picked neither.
>
> Kenma saw the checks and set into them. He holds the ball for 1 beat to make me look.

He read it back. Then, under it, slowly:

> Don't run both.
>
> See less, earlier. 2 checks max. Setter's hands, then the hitter. Then feet, whatever the eyes say.

He looked at the last line for a while. The eye was the faster of the 2 and he couldn't turn it on or off. The scan was slower and he could. So the scan was the one that had to get out of the way.

It fit on 1 line. He drew a box around it.

He closed the notebook and looked up.

Sugawara was watching him from the other end of the bench, quiet, not asking.

"Report," Sho said, to the bench in general. "I've found my limit. Please clap."

Sugawara clapped. Once, softly.

Ennoshita, without turning his head, said, "Your feet stopped before your eyes did."

Sho turned and looked at him.

"I saw it from here," said Ennoshita. "You were looking at 3 things and your feet were waiting to be told."

Sho wrote that down too.

---

On the court, the set went the way Kenma wanted it to.

Hinata started reading Kenma's sets, because Hinata could read anything if it ran fast enough. So Kenma stopped setting. He dumped the ball himself, soft little tips into the holes, again and again, until Karasuno's front row didn't know whether to jump or wait.

18 to 18.

Then 20 to 21. Then 22 to 24.

Set point for Nekoma.

At the net, Hinata said something to Kageyama, leaning in like the ball owed him money. Kageyama said something back.

Kageyama gave him the next one.

Inuoka was already there. He'd stopped watching anything else 10 points ago. He went up with Hinata, matched him, and put both hands over the top of the quick, clean.

The ball came straight down on Karasuno's side.

22 to 25.

The teams switched ends. Nekoma's short libero passed the Karasuno bench with a ball under his arm and slowed down for half a second.

"Hey," said Yaku, to Sho. "Drink something. You look pale."

"Thank you, senpai of the other school."

Yaku frowned at him. "Don't be weird." Then he kept walking.

Sho drank something.

Across the gym, Kenma was sitting on Nekoma's bench with a towel over his head. Kuroo was crouched beside him, saying something. Kenma said 1 word back. Then, from under the towel, his eyes came out and went straight to Karasuno's bench.

They found Sho. They stayed there for half a second.

Then they went to Kuroo, once, and went back under the towel.

Sho knew exactly what that meant. He'd done it himself, 100 times, in a dark room. You find the weak spot and you tell the person who can use it.

He'd been filed. And now he'd been passed along.

He looked down at his notebook. The last line still said Setter's hands, then the hitter. Then feet.

Ukai walked past him on the way to the end line, and stopped.

He kept his eyes on the court, the way he always did.

"Set 2," said Ukai. "Same rotation. Same call. You go in when I say."

He walked off.

He wrote 1 word in it.

> Kenma.

## Chapter 15: Not a Team Yet

Inuoka blocked Hinata 3 times in the first 5 points of set 2.

After the 3rd one, Hinata landed, looked at the ball on the floor, looked at Inuoka, and grinned so wide that Inuoka took a small step back.

"I hate getting blocked," Hinata told him, through the net, still grinning.

"...Sorry?" said Inuoka.

On the bench, Ennoshita passed Sho a towel without turning his head.

"Setter's hands first," Ennoshita said. "Your feet will wait now."

"Ennoshita-san, are you my coach?"

"I'm bench." He shifted on the seat. "We have time."

Sho wrote 2 lines on a clean page. Setter's hands. Then the hitter. Under that, smaller: Then feet.

He'd copied it out 4 times since the end of set 1. Satoshi used to do the same thing with game plans the night before a final, until the words stopped being words.

---

Nekoma went up 10 to 5, and Ukai called the timeout.

He talked to Hinata and Kageyama first, short and sharp, and Kageyama's face went flat the way it did when he was swallowing an argument. Then Ukai crouched in front of Hinata and told him a story.

His grandfather had coached a small spiker once. Karasuno's number 10. When the blockers got too tall, the Little Giant hit the ball off their hands.

Sho listened with his pen still.

He knew the move. Every pro he'd ever filmed knew the move. The hands of a good blocker were the safest place on the court to hit, if you were brave enough to aim at them.

Hearing it as a story about a boy in this jersey, in this gym, made it sound new.

Hinata went back out. 2 rallies later he swung cross-court, hard, off Inuoka's fingers, and Yaku dove and couldn't hold it.

Sho closed the notebook and didn't write anything about that one.

---

Karasuno climbed.

Yamamoto pounded one through and shouted that he was the ace, and Tanaka spiked the next one off Yamamoto and Inuoka both and then he and Nishinoya did a chant that made Yamamoto go purple. Hinata got a touch on Inuoka. Then a quick glanced over the top. 17 to 15, Karasuno.

Kuroo rotated to the front row and called Hinata "Shrimpy," and Kageyama answered him with the fastest quick of the day.

18 to 16.

"Kiyose."

Ukai didn't look away from the court.

"Azumane's spot. New rotation, same call. Kuroo's in front. Show him something different."

"Yes, Coach."

His left foot stayed straight. Ukai didn't look down at it.

Asahi came off and stopped in front of him, enormous and a little sweaty. He opened his mouth, closed it, and then held out his hand, palm up, and Sho slapped it.

"Go get him," Asahi said, very quietly, as if it were a secret.

"I'll bring him back for you, senpai."

---

Kuroo saw him coming.

"Oi, notebook," Kuroo said, at the net, one elbow on the tape. "Back for more?"

"I forgot my pen."

The first ball Kageyama set him was a high one on the left. Sho took 2 looks. Kageyama's hands, the set. Then Kuroo's hands, at the top of his approach.

Kuroo was showing him the line. His hands sat a little inside, leaving the sideline open like a door.

Sho swung cross.

Kuroo's hands closed on it like a trap and the ball came straight back down at Sho's feet.

18 to 17.

"Thank you," said Kuroo, and tapped the tape.

Sho went back to the end line.

He'd seen exactly what Kuroo wanted him to see. Then he'd done exactly what Kuroo wanted him to do.

The next time Kageyama set him, he took the same 2 looks. Setter's hands. Kuroo's hands.

Line again. Open door.

This time he swung at the door, at Kuroo's outside hand, flat and hard, and wiped the ball off the tips of Kuroo's fingers toward the antenna. It went off his pinky into the stands.

19 to 17.

Kuroo looked at his hand. Then he looked at Sho over the tape.

"You stopped looking around," he said.

"Budget cuts, senpai."

Kuroo laughed, once, real. "Nice swing. Do it again so I can steal it."

"Stealing is my line."

---

Nekoma didn't let it stay nice for long.

They started sending everyone at once. Yamamoto, Kuroo and the quiet 3rd year all ran at the net together, and the ball went to whoever Karasuno didn't jump with. Fukunaga came out of nowhere from the back row and hit one straight into Hinata's face. Kuroo ran a quick, then stopped halfway through his approach while all 3 of Karasuno's blockers went up in front of him, and then he went up alone and hit it into the floor.

Sho went up with the block and bit the fake like everyone else. He'd seen it on tape. He'd never been fooled by it in person.

19 to 20. Nekoma's lead again.

Kenma served. Sho stood at the left edge of the receive line, in Asahi's spot.

He took his 2 looks. He got his feet under him. Then he did something he'd promised himself not to do.

He asked.

Come on. Fire. Show me.

He waited for the ghost. For the world going quiet and the ball arriving before it arrived.

Nothing came. The gym stayed loud. The ball came at its normal speed, the way balls did for everyone else, and he passed it with his own 2 arms, a little high, and Kageyama had to chase it. Nekoma finished the rally. 19 to 21.

So the eye didn't take requests.

He filed that.

The next rally, with the ball in the air on Nekoma's side and his head nowhere near ready, it fired on its own. A ghost of Yamamoto's arm, cutting cross. Sho was already moving. His feet had been ready for both.

He dug it. It went up, ugly, and Daichi saved it, and Hinata put it away.

20 to 21.

He'd asked, and nothing came. Then it came when he didn't ask, and he'd still been ready. He wrote both down in his head in the gap between rallies, side by side, like 2 clips for the head coach.

Nekoma took the next one. At 20 to 22, Ukai brought Asahi back.

Sho jogged off. Asahi slapped his hand going in, harder this time, and didn't say anything, which from Asahi was a speech.

---

The rest of set 2 belonged to other people.

With Nekoma still 2 up, the old man in Nekoma's chair leaned forward and asked his team, conversationally, if they were scared.

"NO," said Nekoma, all together.

The points went back and forth after that, 1 each, nobody breaking.

Hinata glanced one off the block. Kenma dumped one into a hole that only Kenma had seen. Daichi hit a ball straight down the line, so clean that even Yaku just watched it.

23 to 24.

The last rally lasted forever. Hinata hit, Yaku dug, the ball went into the net and came back out. Kai missed one, and somebody saved it with his foot. Takeda whispered the old coach's saying to Sho, very fast: the team that let the ball touch the floor first lost. Sho nodded without looking at him.

The ball went high. It came down in Karasuno's back court, in the empty space between Nishinoya and Hinata, who both dove and both missed.

It touched the floor.

23 to 25.

---

"ONE MORE!" Hinata said, before anyone had stood up. "Please! One more game!"

The old man in Nekoma's chair chuckled.

"It wouldn't be a practice match," Nekomata said, "without a rematch."

They played 2 more.

Karasuno lost both, straight sets, every one of them close and every one of them gone.

Sho played in pieces of all of them. He took his 2 looks every time. He froze zero times. Kuroo blocked him twice more and he tooled Kuroo once more, and Kuroo said "Again?" with a hand flat on his chest.

In the last game, Kageyama set him a high ball on the left and he put it away, and on the way back to the line Kageyama muttered, "Your 2nd step was late. Fix it."

"Yes, Kageyama-kun."

---

They lined up afterward.

Ukai stood in front of them with his arms folded. He looked at them for a long moment, all 13, sweaty and quiet.

"Some sets you made fewer mistakes than they did," he said. "They still took all 4."

Nobody said anything.

"That's the difference," said Ukai. "You've got some very strong players. They've got a team."

Kageyama said, quietly, to no one, "If that had been an official match, we'd be done."

"Yeah," said Ukai. "Prelims start in less than a month. You lose there, you're done. You want to play these guys again, you can't lose."

He walked down the line. He stopped in front of Sho, and his eyes stayed on the court behind him.

"You stopped spinning your head," Ukai said. "Held it for 3 games."

"Yes, Coach."

"Feet were ready." A pause. "Keep them ready."

That was all. He moved on to Daichi.

Sho stood where he was. He had a joke ready. He'd had one ready the whole time Ukai was walking. He didn't use it.

---

Kenma and Hinata ended up at the end line together while everyone was packing.

Hinata was talking. Of course he was. Kenma said something short, and Hinata went very still, and then said something back, much louder, with his whole body. Next time. Fun. Kenma looked at the floor in a way that, on Kenma, might have been a smile.

Sho saw it from across the gym, and looked away, and packed his bag.

That one was theirs.

The 2 teams shook hands in a long line. Daichi and Kuroo laughed while they did it, and gripped too hard, and both said they'd win next time, and Sugawara and the Nekoma libero looked at each other over their captains' heads like 2 mothers at a playground.

Kenma reached Sho in the line and stopped 1 step too long.

"You stopped looking," Kenma said.

"Mostly."

"...Mm."

Kenma's eyes went down, once, to Sho's feet. Then they went away again, and he moved on to Tsukishima, and that was it.

Sho kept his face exactly where it was. He didn't make a joke. Somewhere in the back of his head, Satoshi made a note in very small letters that the best scout he'd met in this life was 16 and wanted to go home and play video games.

Kuroo was last.

"Win your prelims, notebook," he said, shaking Sho's hand. "We'll see you in Tokyo."

"Reserve me a front-row seat."

Kuroo grinned. "Earn a court first."

---

On the bus back to the school gate, Tanaka sat down hard in the seat beside Sho and stared out the window.

"That line shot," Tanaka said, to the window. "Off Kuroo's hand. Show me the foot thing."

"Tomorrow, senpai."

Tanaka was quiet for a while. The bus went over a bump.

"When you froze," he said, gruffer, "I thought, good. My spot." He scratched his head. "Then you went back in and tooled their captain and I yelled anyway. Don't tell anyone."

"Senpai," Sho said, "I'm writing it down."

"DON'T."

Sho took the notebook out, very slowly, and Tanaka grabbed for it, and Nishinoya climbed over a seat to help, and Daichi said "Bus," in the voice, and they all sat down at once.

Sho put the notebook away. He hadn't written anything. He didn't need to.

---

Dinner was on the table under cling wrap when he got home. His father was behind his newspaper. He didn't lower it.

"Lost?" said his father.

"To a very good team."

"Ah." The newspaper turned a page. "Did you play?"

"Pieces of 4 games."

The newspaper went still for a second. Then it turned another page.

Mio was in the doorway in her pajamas, holding a pudding cup.

"You lost," she said.

"To a very good team."

"It's not summer yet." She frowned at him. "You haven't quit."

"No."

"So the bet's still on."

"It's still on."

She walked over and put the pudding cup down next to his plate.

"This one's free," she said. "Don't get used to it."

She went back upstairs. Sho looked at the pudding for a while. His father turned another page of the newspaper he wasn't reading.

---

Later, in his room, with the desk lamp on and the house quiet, Sho opened the notebook.

He wrote it all down, the way he'd log a match. Every rally he'd played, every touch, Kuroo's hands, Kenma's look. Then, at the bottom of the page, the part that mattered.

> Observation Haki. Fired once in set 2, uninvited. Showed me a cross that was right.
>
> Asked for it at 19-20. Nothing.
>
> It doesn't take requests.

He looked at that for a long time.

A thing in him that came and went on its own schedule. That he couldn't turn on, couldn't turn off, and couldn't explain to anyone in this house or that gym. Satoshi would've wanted to measure it. Measuring would take time, and the preliminaries started in less than a month.

So he wrote the only rule he could actually use.

> Don't ask it. Be ready for both.

He drew a box around it.

Then, at the very bottom, he wrote 1 more word, and underlined it once.

> Inter-High.

He turned off the lamp.

Through the wall, very faintly, he could hear the clock in Mio's room ticking, and somewhere under it, the sound of a ball he'd already hit 10,000 times in another life, still bouncing.
