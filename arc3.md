# Second Serve: Arc 3 chapters (Tokyo expedition and summer camp)

## Chapter 30: Red pen

Tanaka was already on the step when Sho came up the hill on Monday, sitting on it with the key on its red string hanging from 1 finger and his chin on his fist, watching the bottom of the path.

When Sho came round the corner by the shrine Tanaka stood up and put the key in the lock and turned it. *Clack.*

It was 4:56. The air was already warm, the 1st properly warm morning of the summer, and the sky over the school had gone pale and clean, with 1 long thin cloud lying over the hills behind the gym.

"Early," said Sho.

"EARLIER THAN YOU," said Tanaka, and pushed the door open with his whole arm.

They got the net out of the storage room. It was a 2-person job, and for a week the net had stayed rolled up on its pole in the corner, because Tanaka had been on his own. Tanaka took the left post and Sho took the right, and they walked it out across the floor between them, the net sagging in the middle like a hammock. The left post's crank stuck halfway, the way it always did, and Tanaka hit it with the heel of his hand and it went. Sho held the post steady while Tanaka cranked. The cable came up tight. Tanaka slapped the tape with his flat hand, and it hummed.

"Light," said Tanaka. "Coach said light."

"Light."

They passed. Back and forth across the net, slow, the ball going up and down between them in the blue light from the high windows. Tanaka in his FIGHTING SPIRIT shirt with the backward R, yelling "NICE!" when Sho passed it to him and "MINE!" when it came back, as if there were anybody else in the gym it could belong to.

After a while Tanaka started hitting it at him instead, slow down-balls, 1 a little to Sho's left, then a little to his right, then 1 short, dropping in front of him. Sho got his feet to all of them and his arms under all of them, and the ball went up, 1 after another, and came down in Tanaka's hands on the other side of the net.

At 5:40 Tanaka caught the ball and stood holding it.

"Your eyes."

"Fine."

"Fine fine? Or fine like you said with the nose?"

"Fine fine."

Tanaka gave him the intimidation face, which on Tanaka also meant he was thinking. Then he nodded once, hard, threw the ball up, and spiked it at the floor by Sho's feet, a little harder than before. Sho was already there. It went straight up off his arms, high and clean, and came down into Tanaka's hands.

"Okay," said Tanaka. "Good."

---

At the start of practice that afternoon, before warm-up, Ukai stood in front of the club with his clipboard.

"Monday," he said. "Exams. Who's in trouble."

Nobody moved.

"I said Monday. It's Monday. Who's in trouble."

Slowly, from the middle of the half circle, Hinata's hand went up. A beat later, from a different part of the half circle, Kageyama's hand went up.

Then Noya's. Then, after a long silence, Tanaka's, at shoulder height, as if he were hoping it wouldn't count if it didn't go all the way.

"Which subjects," said Ukai.

"All of them," said Hinata.

"English," said Kageyama. "And the rest."

"English," said Noya. "And math. And Classical. And maybe history."

Ukai looked at Tanaka.

"Classical," said Tanaka, to the floor. "I got 33 last time."

Down the half circle, somebody made a small sound. Tanaka's head came round toward the back of the room, toward Sho, with his eyes narrowed. Sho kept his face exactly where it was.

"33's a pass," said Ukai. "Barely. Who are you studying with."

"Noya. At my place." Tanaka scratched his head. "My sister says she'll make us. She says if we don't pass she'll drive us to Tokyo herself, and we'll have to sit in the back with the dog."

"Fine. And you 2." Ukai looked at Hinata and Kageyama, a few places apart, both staring at the floor. "Who are you studying with?"

Hinata opened his mouth. Kageyama opened his. They both shut them again.

"Sort it out," said Ukai. "By tomorrow." He turned a page on his clipboard. "Warm up."

---

The receive drill was the one they did every day. Ukai on a chair on the far side of the net with a cart of balls, serving floats. The receivers coming through in a line, Daichi, Noya, Asahi, Tanaka, Ennoshita, Narita, Kinoshita, round and round, and the pass going up to Suga at the net, who caught it and dropped it in a basket.

"Kiyose." Ukai pointed at the floor beside the end line, behind the receivers. "There. You call. Who passed, and if it's good. Every 3rd turn you take 1 yourself, light."

"Yes."

Ukai served. Daichi passed.

"Daichi-san. Good."

Noya. "Noya-san. Good."

Asahi. "Asahi-san. A bit high."

Tanaka. The ball came off his arms, and Sho heard it go wrong before he saw it, the flat hard note it made when it hit the bone, and his eyes went to Tanaka's left arm, which was lower than his right, and then to the ball, which was going short.

"Tanaka-senpai. Left. Short."

Suga had to take a step in for it.

"I KNOW," said Tanaka.

"Keep calling," said Ukai, from the chair.

Ennoshita. "Good." Narita. The sound came, stiff and straight-armed, the same sound Asahi made, and Sho's eyes went to the arms that had made it, and it was Narita. "Narita-san. Good." Kinoshita. "Kinoshita-san. Short."

The line went round again, and again. Somewhere in the 3rd time round Sho noticed that Noya, after each of his own passes, was turning his head a fraction toward the back of the court. Waiting for the call. When it came, "Noya-san, good," he punched the air, small, by his hip.

Daichi kept his head still. After Sho said "Daichi-san, a bit fast," Daichi's next pass came up off his arms softer, with more air under it, and dropped into Suga's hands like something being set down on a table.

Tanaka, after his 3rd "Left," stood in the line and lifted his left arm and looked at it as if it belonged to someone else. When his turn came round again he bent lower, and the ball came off both arms evenly, and Sho said "Tanaka-senpai, good," and Tanaka yelled "HA!" so loud that Ukai's next serve went into the net.

"Oi."

"Sorry, Coach!"

Every 3rd turn Sho went to the front of the line and took a pass himself.

The 1st was easy, a float straight at him, and he passed it up to Suga, a little toward the antenna. The 2nd was the same. Then, on his 3rd turn, Ukai looked at him over the cart of balls a beat longer than usual before he tossed.

Sho watched him.

Ukai's toss went up, a little further to his right than the others had, and his shoulder opened as he swung, turning out toward the sideline. His hand came through the ball flat, and Sho's feet were already going, 1 step back and to the right, before the ball had crossed the net. The ball came over flat and fast toward the seam between him and Noya, dipping in the last 3 meters, and he was there, and it came into his arms, and he let it go up, high, soft, in the middle of the court.

Suga caught it without moving his feet.

"Nice," said Suga.

On the chair, Ukai had the next ball in his hand and wasn't tossing it.

"You went before it crossed," said Ukai.

"His shoulder opened, Coach. Yours. You turned it out."

Ukai looked at his own shoulder, as if it had told on him.

"Hm," he said. He tossed the next ball. "Again."

Sho went to the back of the line. At the end of the drill Ukai got down off the chair and came round the net with his clipboard. He'd been keeping a column of little ticks the whole time, Sho saw, between serves.

"How many'd you call wrong?"

"3. Maybe 4."

"I had you at 4." Ukai tucked the clipboard under his arm. "Tomorrow, same. Ears and eyes. You hear it, you look where it tells you."

He walked away across the court, and at the far end Sho heard him say, not loudly, to Takeda, who'd come in during the drill with a stack of papers, "Kid reads servers like a book," and Takeda say, "Is that good?" and Ukai say, "It's IQ. You can't teach it," and blow his whistle.

---

On Tuesday at lunch Sho went down to the 1st-year corridor to buy bread and stopped by the noticeboard halfway along it, because at the far end, by the window outside class 1-4, Tsukishima was leaning on the sill with his headphones round his neck and a carton of milk tea, and Yamaguchi was beside him eating an onigiri, and Hinata had just come up the stairs at the other end of the corridor at a run.

Hinata saw Tsukishima. He slowed down.

From the vending machines in the alcove halfway along, Kageyama came out with a carton of milk. He saw Tsukishima too, and stopped.

He saw Hinata. Hinata saw him.

Both of them turned and looked at Tsukishima.

Then they both started walking toward the window, Hinata from the stairs and Kageyama from the alcove, at almost exactly the same speed, each with his eyes fixed on Tsukishima and nowhere else.

Sho read the poster on the noticeboard. It was about the swimming pool.

Hinata got there 1 step ahead. He stopped in front of Tsukishima and bowed, deep, the whole way down. Kageyama arrived a step later, a meter away from Hinata, and stood there with the carton of milk in his hand and his mouth slightly open, and didn't bow.

"Please teach us!" said Hinata, to the floor. "For exams! Please!"

Tsukishima looked down at the top of Hinata's head. Then at Kageyama, who was still standing up straight.

"And the King?" said Tsukishima. "Is he asking too, or is he just here for the view?"

Kageyama's face went through several things. His jaw worked. The carton in his hand made a small crumpling noise.

"...Please," said Kageyama. It came out of him like a cork coming out of a bottle. Then he bowed too, abruptly, from the waist, a little too far, so that the milk carton nearly hit the floor. "Teach us. English. And the rest."

Yamaguchi had put his onigiri down on the windowsill. His shoulders were shaking.

"Tsukki," he said, not quite steadily. "They're asking nicely."

Tsukishima drank some of his milk tea. He looked out of the window at the field for a while, at the baseball team's net sagging in the heat.

"Library," he said at last. "After practice. Every day till exams." He looked down at the 2 bowed heads. "And if you 2 start fighting in the library, I'm leaving, and you can fail on your own."

"Yes!" said Hinata.

"...Yes," said Kageyama.

They came up out of the bow together, and both noticed, and both looked away from each other, and walked off in opposite directions, Hinata toward the stairs and Kageyama back to the vending machines, where he stood for a long time staring at the buttons as if he'd forgotten what milk was.

Sho went on down the corridor to buy his bread. As he passed the window Tsukishima's eyes moved to him, briefly, over the top of the milk tea, and moved away again.

---

On Wednesday after practice the library was full.

It was on the 3rd floor at the end of the corridor, and in exam season it was the most crowded room in the school. Every table was taken. The windows were open because the radiators were off and the heat had come in early, and outside in the cherry trees the 1st cicadas were starting, a dry rattling sound that came and went.

Suga had the last table by the window. He'd put his bag on the chair opposite to keep it, and moved it when Sho came in.

At a table on the far side of the room, under the clock, Tsukishima sat with a red pen in his hand and 2 practice tests in front of him, 1 from Hinata and 1 from Kageyama, which Hinata and Kageyama had pushed across to him from opposite ends of the table. Tsukishima was marking them. The red pen went down each page, and each time it moved Hinata flinched, and Kageyama didn't, and Yamaguchi, sitting next to Tsukishima, watched the pen and winced on their behalf.

"Center Exam," said Suga, tapping the thick blue book in front of him. "English. This one's going to kill me." He tapped the other book, older and thinner, with a paper cover gone soft. "And this one's yours."

*Classical Japanese Grammar: Complete Review.*

"I got 72 in Classical last year," said Suga. "So. English for Classical."

Sho sat down.

They did English 1st, because Suga had a practice test the next morning and said he was about 3 grammar points away from crying. Sho looked at the paper. Long reading passages, the kind he'd read a thousand of in the other life: scouting reports from Italy and Poland and Brazil run through bad translation software, which he'd then had to rewrite by hand into 3 lines a coach could read before a match. The grammar in Suga's book was ordinary. The trick, he'd learned in a room with the lights off, had never been the grammar.

"Don't read all of it straight off," said Sho. "Find the sentence that's doing the work."

"How do you find it?"

"It's usually the 1st or the last. Or the one with *however* in it. The rest is getting there." He put his finger on a paragraph about recycling in Sweden. "This one. *However, the program has struggled to...* That's the paragraph."

Suga read the sentence twice. He looked at the question under it, circled an answer, and turned to the answer key at the back.

"Huh," he said.

"Do the next one."

Suga did the next one. He read the paragraph through once, quickly, with his pencil moving down the margin, and stopped at a sentence near the end and read that one again, slowly, and circled his answer. Right. The one after that, he read and stopped and frowned, and read the 1st sentence again, and the last, and chose, and got it wrong, and swore at the book under his breath in the polite way he swore at things, and the 1st year at the next table looked round.

"*Although*," said Sho, pointing. "Not *however*. Same job."

Suga wrote it in the margin. *Although = however.* He did 6 more. He got 5.

"I watch a lot of movies," said Sho, before Suga could ask.

"Hm," said Suga, and slid the paper-covered book across the table.

Classical was worse.

Suga explained it with his hands. The verb endings that changed if the sentence was a question. The particle that meant 2 different things depending on what came after it. The way the subject vanished from sentences for whole paragraphs, and you were meant to know, from the honorifics on the verbs, whether it was the emperor or a lady-in-waiting doing the crying.

"This ending," said Suga, tapping it with his pencil, "means somebody important. So it's the emperor. It tells you who. You just have to hear it."

Sho looked at the sentence. *Ima wa mukashi...* He read it again under his breath, the endings, the shape of the sound of them.

"...The emperor's crying."

"The emperor's crying." Suga sat back. "Yes."

They went on. The light outside went from white to gold. Across the room Tsukishima finished marking and pushed the 2 tests back across the table, 1 to each end, and Hinata looked at his and put his forehead down on the table, and Kageyama looked at his and went very still, and Yamaguchi said "Tsukki, that's a lot of red," and Tsukishima said "That's their red. I just found it."

At 6:30, when the librarian started turning off the lights at the far end of the room, Suga took a sheet of paper out of the Classical book. It was a practice test, 1 page, photocopied, the kind the teachers handed out in exam week.

"Do this one," said Suga. "15 minutes. I'll mark it."

Sho did it. He did it the way he'd been doing the sentences all evening, out loud under his breath, listening to the endings. Some of them he heard. Some of them, halfway through a sentence, the subject slid away from him, and he couldn't tell whether it was the emperor or the lady or a monk who'd come in 2 lines ago, and he guessed.

At 15 minutes Suga held out his hand, and Sho gave him the paper.

Suga took a red pen out of his pencil case. He marked it the way Tsukishima had marked Hinata's, steadily, page down, the pen moving and stopping and moving. A tick. A tick. A line through a whole answer. Another line. A tick. A small circle round a verb ending, with a red arrow and a word next to it.

At the bottom of the page he wrote a number and put the pen down and turned the paper round.

*38.*

In red, circled.

Sho looked at it.

"It's 3 lower than last time," said Suga. "It's not a bad paper. This one's harder than the last test. But." He tapped the number, gently, with the back of the pen. "The line's 30. That's 8 away."

8 away. 8 points, out of 100, between him and a make-up test on a Saturday morning in Miyagi, while a bus went south to Tokyo without him.

He thought about the Nekoma page at the back of his notebook. *What does he see now?* He thought about a gym he'd never been in, and Kenma in it with no expression at all, waiting, holding the ball 1 beat too long for somebody who wasn't going to be there to look.

Across the room, Hinata had lifted his forehead off the table and was looking at his own red marks, and then, very slowly, picking up his pencil.

"Same time tomorrow," said Sho.

Suga raised an eyebrow. "It's Wednesdays."

"Tomorrow too," said Sho. "Please. And Friday. I'll do your English every day."

Suga looked at him, and at the red 38, and at him again.

"You really want Tokyo," said Suga.

"Yes," said Sho.

Suga capped the red pen.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Same table. Bring the emperor."

## Chapter 31: Villager B

On Thursday at lunch Kiyoko came down the 1st-year corridor, looking into the classrooms.

Sho was coming back up from the school store with a melon bread and a carton of milk, and he saw her as he came off the stairs. She came from the far end, from 1-1, slowly, with her bag on her shoulder and the light from the windows catching her glasses, and at each classroom door she paused and looked in, and moved on.

The corridor went quiet around her as she came. 3rd years didn't come to the 1st-year floor. Shimizu Kiyoko of the volleyball club was known by sight to every 1st year in the school, and none of them had ever expected her to look at them.

1-2. 1-3. At 1-4 Tsukishima, at the window with his milk tea, raised his eyebrows a fraction and lowered them again. Yamaguchi stood up straighter.

At 1-5 Kiyoko stopped. She looked past the door, down the corridor toward Sho, at 3 girls coming back from the vending machines with juice boxes. The 2 on the outside were talking. The 1 in the middle was listening, and laughing a beat after the others: a small girl with light hair tied up on 1 side, holding her juice box in both hands.

Yachi Hitoka. She sat in the front row of 1-5, by the door, and wrote everything down in small, neat handwriting, and had the top mark in the class on the last math test, and jumped when people said her name. In May she'd dropped her pencil case in this corridor and Sho had picked up 3 of the pens for her, and she'd said thank you so many times he'd started to wonder what he'd done.

Kiyoko stepped into the girls' path.

The 2 on the outside stopped. Yachi walked another half step before she noticed, and then stopped so suddenly the juice box squelched in her hands.

"Excuse me," said Kiyoko. "Are you a 1st year?"

"Y-yes?"

"Are you in a club?"

"N-no. Not yet. I mean, no. I'm not."

"Would you like to be a manager? For the boys' volleyball club."

Yachi looked up at her with her mouth a little open and the juice box forgotten. Her 2 friends had each taken a small step backward.

"Yes," said Yachi.

A beat later her face caught up with what she'd said.

"I mean... That is... What does a manager...?"

"Come and see," said Kiyoko. "After school. The Second Gymnasium." She inclined her head, very slightly. "Thank you."

She walked back up the corridor the way she'd come. When she'd turned the corner by the stairs the noise came back all at once, and Yachi's friends grabbed her by both arms.

"Hitoka! That was *Shimizu-senpai*!"

"I said yes." Yachi's voice had gone thin and high. "Why did I say yes? I don't know anything about volleyball. I don't know what a manager *does*. What if they make me carry things? What if I drop them? What if I drop something important and it breaks and they lose and they make me pay for it and I have to sell my..."

Her friends steered her into the classroom. On the way past, her eyes landed on Sho, standing aside in the doorway with his bread and his milk, and stopped.

"Kiyose-kun. You're in the volleyball club."

"Yes."

"Are they..." She held the juice box to her chest. "Are they scary?"

Sho thought about it.

"They're loud," he said. "Tanaka-senpai makes faces. He means them nicely. Hinata shouts about everything. Noya-senpai is short and shouts louder than Hinata." He thought some more. "Kiyoko-san's the quietest one there. When she says something, everybody stops."

Yachi looked at him for a while.

Then she bowed, quickly, a small bob from the waist, and let her friends pull her to her desk.

---

She came to the gym after school with Kiyoko, holding the strap of her school bag in both hands, and sat on the edge of the stage where Kiyoko pointed.

Sho saw her come in from the end line. He was where Ukai had put him on Monday, behind the receive line, calling the passes and taking every 3rd 1 himself.

"Noya-san. Good. Tanaka-senpai. Good."

On the stage Yachi jumped every time Tanaka yelled. Her eyes went from the receive drill to the far court, and stayed there.

On the far court, Hinata and Kageyama had a cart of balls between them.

They still weren't speaking. Ukai had put them on the far net together anyway, and Kageyama tossed and Hinata ran, and neither of them said a word. The 1st 2 went the old way. Hinata in the air before the ball left Kageyama's hands, his eyes shut, the ball arriving at his palm as if it had always been going there, the crack of it on the floor. On the stage Yachi leaned forward with her mouth open.

On the 3rd, Hinata kept his eyes open.

Sho saw it from the far end of the gym, because he'd been waiting for it. Hinata's eyes stayed wide all the way up, on the ball, and he was a fraction late, the quick already past the place where his hand arrived. He swung at the air. The ball went off the end of his fingers and over the antenna. He landed and stood looking at his own palm.

Kageyama took the next ball out of the cart without a word, and tossed it, the old way, fast. Hinata looked at it go past him. Then he ran for the next one, eyes shut, and it landed.

Yachi flinched at the crack and leaned forward again. To her it had been 2 of the most beautiful things she'd ever seen and a 3rd that went over the antenna, and she didn't know the difference yet.

Sho's turn came round. He went to the front of the line.

Ukai, on his chair on the far side of the net, picked a ball out of the cart and held it.

Before the toss Sho looked once at the other side of the net, all of it at once. Ukai's chair. The cart. Suga at the net with the basket by his feet. Ennoshita at the side, picking up a stray ball. The empty court where the blockers would be. He took it in 1 look and let it go, and then he was looking only at Ukai: the ball in his left hand, the right arm drawn back.

The toss went up, a little left of the last one. Ukai's chest stayed square. The arm came through straight.

*Straight down the middle. At me.*

Sho didn't move his feet. He let his weight sink and kept his arms together and still, and the ball came over the net flat and dipping, and it came to him, exactly where he was standing, and went up off his arms in a soft high curve into Suga's hands.

"Nice," said Suga.

"Daichi-san," said Sho, going back to the end line, because Daichi was already passing the next one. "Good."

At the water break Ennoshita came over with the basket of loose balls.

"Kiyose."

"Yes."

"You go still," said Ennoshita. "Right before. Every time."

Sho held the ball he'd been about to put in the basket.

"Someone said that once," he said.

"Thought so." Ennoshita took the ball out of his hand and put it in the basket. "I watch a lot, from the line."

He carried the basket off toward the cart.

Sho stood at the end of the court.

Oikawa had said it at the sink, after Seijoh. *You'd go still and look at them, and you'd already be moving.* But that had been the day the eye fired, the day of the strange one, with Oikawa's serve coming at him and his whole head full of light.

Today the eye hadn't fired. Today it had been an ordinary drill, an ordinary float, and he'd gone still anyway, every time, enough for Ennoshita to see it from the side of the court.

It was him now. His body had learned to stop and wait for the read, and it did it whether the eye came or not, and anybody who watched him for long enough could see it.

He went to the stage, to his bag at the far end from Yachi, and got the notebook out, and turned to the back, under the Nekoma box, under *What does he see now?*

*I go still before every read. With the eye or without. Every time. Ennoshita saw it from the line.*

And under that:

*So Kenma will.*

---

He did Classical with Suga every day after that, in the library, at the window table, the way he'd asked.

On Thursday it was 44. On Friday it was 47. Suga marked them in red and pushed them back across the table, and they did English for Suga after, every day, the trade going both ways, and Suga's practice scores went 8 out of 10, then 9.

On Friday, at lunch, Hinata came running down the 1st-year corridor shouting "YACHI-SAN!" at the top of his voice.

Yachi went out into the corridor white as paper, and the 2 of them stood by the window outside 1-5 for most of lunch: Hinata talking with both hands, Yachi listening with both hands clutched to her chest. Sho was at his desk at the back by the window, with his Classical book open and his bread, and Hinata came through the open door the way he came through most walls. Mostly Sho heard the shape of it, rising and falling, very fast.

Once a single word came through clearly, because Hinata shouted it.

"*VILLAGER!*"

Sho closed his Classical book, picked up his bread, and went to the library.

On Monday morning there was a poster on the noticeboard in the main corridor.

It was the size of 2 sheets of drawing paper side by side. A black crow with its wings wide, cut out of shadow against white, and behind it the outline of Karasuno's gym in thin, clean lines, and along the bottom in neat block letters *KARASUNO HIGH SCHOOL VOLLEYBALL CLUB*, and under that, smaller: *Please support our journey.* There was a donation box by the office and a list of the dates of the 2 trips.

People stopped to look at it all morning. Teachers stopped. Sho stopped on his way to homeroom and stood in front of it.

Every feather in the crow's wings had its own edge. Somebody had sat up late with a sharp knife.

In 1-5, Yachi was at her desk in the front row with her head down on her arms, and there was a small bandage on her left thumb. Her 2 friends were standing over her saying *Hitoka, everybody's looking at it, everybody*, and Yachi was saying into her arms, "Don't. Please. Thank you. Don't."

At practice that afternoon she came in with Kiyoko in a tracksuit a size too big for her, holding a clipboard to her chest. Before the warm-up she went to Takeda, who was by the door with his own notebook, and held out a sheet of paper with both hands, bowing over it.

It was the club registration form. At the bottom, in the box for a parent's seal, there was a red seal, pressed carefully, a little crooked.

Takeda took it in both hands and bowed back.

Then Daichi called everyone in and said, "This is Yachi Hitoka-san. She's going to be a manager," and Yachi bowed so low and so fast her clipboard hit her knees.

"I'm Yachi! I'll do my best! Please take care of me! I don't know anything! Sorry!"

Tanaka and Noya, side by side in the front row, looked at each other. Then at Yachi. Then at Kiyoko, standing beside her with her hands folded.

Their eyes filled up together.

"A 2nd... manager..." said Noya.

"Kiyoko-san brought us... a 2nd manager..." said Tanaka.

"Don't cry at her," said Daichi.

The exams started on Tuesday: 3 days of them, the 1st-semester end-of-term exams, 2 or 3 subjects every morning and then home at noon. The desks were pulled apart into rows. The blinds were half down against the July sun. The gym was locked all week.

On Tuesday Sho woke at 4:14 anyway. He lay in the dark and listened to the house, the water heater ticking, a car going past at the bottom of the street, and then got up and did 30 minutes of verb endings at his desk under the lamp. When he went downstairs his mother was already at the counter in her cardigan making onigiri, and there was a cup of tea poured and going cold at his place.

Math and English were on Tuesday. He finished both early and went through them twice and put his pencil down and looked out of the window at the hills behind the gym, which had turned the dark heavy green of real summer.

Classical was Thursday, 2nd period.

On Wednesday the exams finished at noon, and at 1 o'clock Suga was at the window table in the library with the old paper-covered book. His own exams were the same days as Sho's. His English was Thursday, the same as Sho's Classical. He'd come in anyway, on his own revision afternoon.

"Last one," said Suga, and slid a practice paper across. "Then you go home and sleep, and I go home and cry over English."

Sho did it. 15 minutes. Suga marked it in red.

*51.*

"Better," said Suga. "Still guessing when the subject slides." He turned the paper round and tapped a sentence. "This one. Who's speaking?"

Sho looked. "The monk."

"The lady. Look at the verb."

He looked at it. *Mōsu*. A humble verb, for speaking to someone above you.

"She's speaking to him," said Sho slowly. "She's lower than him. So she uses the humble one."

"So when the subject goes, look at the verb, and ask who's lower," said Suga. "Most of the time, the one using the humble word is the one doing it. Listen for who's bowing."

Sho looked at the sentence a while longer.

"Who's bowing," he said.

"Who's bowing." Suga capped the red pen and pushed his own blue book across the table. "Now. 10 minutes. Make me pass English."

---

The Classical exam was in 1-5's own room, with Ishikawa-sensei at the front with the papers face down in a stack and the blinds down on the left side, so that the light came in long gold bars across the floor and the backs of the desks.

Sho sat at the back by the window. In the front row by the door, Yachi had her pencil case open and 3 pencils lined up in a row, all sharpened to the same length, her bandaged thumb resting on the edge of the desk.

"Begin."

The papers turned over all round the room with a sound like birds getting up off a field.

The grammar section, he did the way Suga had taught him, the way he'd learned to hear a pass. He read each one under his breath with his lips barely moving, and listened to the shape of it, and the ending told him what it was. Mostly. 15 of them; 11 he was sure of; he put a small dot beside the other 4.

The passage was long. A courtier from the capital travelling to a temple in the mountains in winter, snow on the pines, a conversation with an old priest. In the 3rd sentence the subject slid away from him. Somebody was speaking to somebody, and the sentence didn't say who.

He sat with his pencil over the paper.

*Who's bowing.*

He read the verb again under his breath. *Mōshikeru.* The humble one. Somebody lower, speaking to somebody higher. In the world of the passage the courtier was a man from the capital, and the priest was an old man in a hut on a mountain.

So the priest was answering the courtier.

He wrote it down.

It went on like that for 40 minutes. Sometimes he heard it, right there, the way he heard Daichi's passes, solid and clean and the same every time. Sometimes the subject went and he listened for who was bowing, and sometimes that told him and sometimes it didn't, and then he guessed, and put a dot, and went on.

At 40 minutes he went back to his dots. He changed 2 of them.

"Pencils down."

The papers went forward along the rows, hand to hand. In the front row Yachi had already put her 3 pencils back in their case, side by side. She turned round in her chair and looked along the window row until she found him, and mouthed something.

He couldn't read it from there. She turned back round and put her hands in her lap.

The results came back on Friday, the last day of term, the day before the bus.

Math, 94. English, 98. Ishikawa-sensei came last, after lunch, and read out the names, and you went up and took yours and walked back to your desk with it folded so nobody could see the number.

He sat down at his desk by the window and unfolded it.

*58.* In red, in the top right corner, circled. And under it, in Ishikawa-sensei's small handwriting: *Subjects in the passage much improved.*

Who's bowing.

He folded it and put it in the inside pocket of his bag next to the permission form in its clear plastic folder, where Dad had zipped it shut nearly 2 weeks ago, and zipped the pocket shut again.

At the end of the day the door of 1-5 banged open and Tanaka came in, which 2nd years did not do, waving a test paper over his head like a flag.

"KIYOSE! 31!" He slapped it down on Sho's desk. *31*, in red, circled. "CLASSICAL! 31! I PASSED!"

Half of 1-5 had turned round to look at him. In the front row Yachi had gone rigid in her chair.

"Congratulations, senpai," said Sho.

"Noya got 34 in English! We're BOTH going! Saeko-nee made us do it till 2 in the morning!" Tanaka picked his paper back up and held it at arm's length, beaming at it. "31. Beautiful. What'd you get?"

"58."

Tanaka lowered the paper.

"...That's private," he said, and turned round and walked out again.

Sho picked up his bag and followed him out into the corridor. Tanaka was already gone, down the stairs, still yelling. The corridor was full of 1st years with their bags, the end-of-term noise, and Sho was at the stairs end of it when the sound started, from the doorway of 1-1, right beside him.

It was Hinata. It was a long, rising, falling wail, like a siren going past.

Hinata was standing in the doorway of 1-1 holding a test paper by 1 corner, the way you'd hold something dead. There was a number in the top corner of it, small and red and circled twice.

Further down the corridor, Kageyama came out of 1-3 with a paper in his hand, folded, held down by his side. He was walking slowly. He saw Hinata in the doorway of 1-1. Hinata saw him.

"...English," said Hinata.

"English," said Kageyama.

They stood there 10 meters apart, holding their papers, while everybody else went past them toward the stairs.

Sho stood at the top of the stairs with his bag on his shoulder and did the arithmetic, because he couldn't not.

Tomorrow at 6 in the morning the bus left for Tokyo. Tomorrow at 9, in this building, Hinata and Kageyama would be sitting a make-up test.

Tomorrow morning, against Fukurodani, or Nekoma, or whoever came 1st, Suga would set. There'd be no quick. There'd be no Hinata pulling 2 blockers across the net every time he ran. There'd be 2 empty places on the court in the front row, and Ukai would have to fill them with somebody.
